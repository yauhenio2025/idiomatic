[SIDE]
TITLE: The stem machine
SVG: pt_future-subjunctive_c1b.svg

Ear training first: the formation machine. Take the completed-past third-person plural and remove the last two letters. What remains is the future subjunctive:
TL: tiveram; tiver
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: fizeram; fizer
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: disseram; disser
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: foram; for
[PAUSE:1200]
Then the personal endings — hear one full high-frequency pattern:
TL: fizer, fizeres, fizer, fizermos, fizerdes, fizerem
[PAUSE:1500]
Regular verbs hide this machinery — their result looks like the infinitive. The irregulars are the diagnostic forms, so own them as sound. That's the ear training done — move on to the next card.

[CARD]
TITLE: The gate: pending, not unlikely
SVG: pt_future-subjunctive_c2f.svg

The rule this card teaches: the gate marks a future event as pending — not as unlikely. You can be certain it will happen; it just hasn't happened yet. The extreme case:
TL: Quando o sol nascer, a cidade vai acordar.
SHOW: sunrise — certain, and still pending
[PAUSE:1500]
But future meaning alone opens no gate. A standalone prediction stays ordinary:
TL: Amanhã vou voltar.
SHOW: plain prediction — no gate
And a direct question is not a dependent clause:
TL: Quando você vai voltar?
SHOW: question — no gate
[PAUSE:1200]
The moment that return becomes the circumstance for something else, the gate opens:
TL: Quando você voltar, me avise.
[PAUSE:1500]
When you're ready, flip the card for three views of the same clause.

[SIDE]
TITLE: One conjunction, three statuses
SVG: pt_future-subjunctive_c2b.svg

The same conjunction, three temporal statuses. A repeated habit takes the present indicative:

SHOW: habit — indicative
[PAUSE:1000]
A completed occasion takes the past indicative:
TL: Quando tive tempo, li o relatório.
SHOW: completed — past indicative
[PAUSE:1000]
A pending future occasion takes the future subjunctive:
TL: Quando tiver tempo, vou ler o relatório.
SHOW: pending — future subjunctive
[PAUSE:1200]
Your second recorded error sits exactly there, and its repair shows the machinery:
TL: quando eu vou ter mais tempo, vou testar
SHOW: ✗
TL: quando eu tiver mais tempo, vou testar
SHOW: ✓ — *tiver*, from the past-plural stem
[PAUSE:1500]

TITLE: The second gate: open conditions
SVG: pt_future-subjunctive_c3b.svg

The second gate is a genuine condition. Your record preserves the Spanish-shaped version:
TL: se eu vou viajar em janeiro
SHOW: ✗ — from your record
An open future condition takes the future subjunctive, with the spoken future free in the main clause:
TL: Se eu viajar em janeiro, vou comprar a passagem em outubro.
SHOW: ✓ — open condition
[PAUSE:1200]
A remote or counterfactual hypothesis changes machines — imperfect subjunctive plus conditional:
TL: Se eu viajasse em janeiro, ficaria mais tempo.
SHOW: remote — a different machine
[PAUSE:1200]
And the same little word can mean "whether" — an indirect question, not a condition:
TL: Não sei se ele vem amanhã.
SHOW: "whether" — no gate
TL: Se ele vier amanhã, vamos conversar.
SHOW: real condition — gate open
[PAUSE:1500]
Same spelling, different job. Move on to the next card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The boundary: not every future
SVG: pt_future-subjunctive_c4b.svg

The boundary this side draws: future meaning alone does not choose this tense. Some conjunctions take the present subjunctive even for future actions:
TL: Antes que você publique, revise os dados.
SHOW: "before" — present subjunctive
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Caso você tenha tempo, leia o anexo.
SHOW: "in case" — present subjunctive
[PAUSE:1200]
And a habit keeps the plain present, even under a trigger word you've seen open gates:
TL: Sempre que tenho uma dúvida, escrevo para a editora.
SHOW: habit — indicative
[PAUSE:1200]
So the safe lesson is: learn the gates as constructions, not as trigger words. The three strong ones are pending time clauses, open conditions, and open future relatives.
SHOW: pending time · open condition · open relative
Boundary drawn — move on to the next card.
