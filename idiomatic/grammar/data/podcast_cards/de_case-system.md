## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Who does what to whom
SVG: de_case-system_c1f.svg

[MUSIC:intro]
This card is about German case — the system that tags every noun phrase with its job: who acts, what changes hands, who receives. Your Russian already thinks this way; the task is to transfer the reflex, not the endings. Here is the three-person scene this whole episode lives in:
TL: Die Redakteurin gibt dem Autor den Vertrag.
SHOW: editor → contract → author
[PAUSE:1500]
The editor is nominative — the subject. The author is dative — the recipient. The contract is accusative — the thing transferred. Now watch the jobs survive a word-order shuffle:
TL: Den Vertrag gibt die Redakteurin dem Autor morgen.
SHOW: same jobs, new order — the articles carry them
[PAUSE:1500]
That is exactly the intuition to transfer from Russian: roles live in the forms, not the positions. Keep the scene in your ear — and flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The audible landmark: der, den, dem, des
SVG: de_case-system_c1b.svg

Ear training first: German concentrates the case signal on the determiner, and the masculine row is the best landmark because all four forms differ. Nominative, accusative, dative, genitive:
TL: der, den, dem, des
[PAUSE:1200]
Now on a real noun:
TL: der Bericht
TL: den Bericht
TL: dem Bericht
TL: des Berichts
[PAUSE:1500]
The other rows repeat cells — listen to the feminine row:
TL: die, die, der, der
SHOW: cells repeat — a form is evidence, not an answer
[PAUSE:1500]
So the ending alone can't tell you the job; the job tells you the ending. That's the ear training done — move on to the next card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Gate one: the subject is nominative
SVG: de_case-system_c2f.svg

The rule this card teaches: the case decision runs through gates, and gate one is sentence structure. The grammatical subject is nominative — even when it isn't doing anything deliberate:
TL: Der neue Plan gefällt der Redaktion.
SHOW: the plan is the subject — nominative; the team experiences — dative
[PAUSE:1500]
A rough "doer equals nominative" rule fails here; the verb hands the experiencer the dative. And case never spreads by contagion. Your record preserves this line:
TL: Warum dieser Frau so viel Geld zur Verfügung hatte
SHOW: ✗ — from your notes
[PAUSE:1000]
The woman is the subject of that clause, so she is nominative:
TL: Warum diese Frau so viel Geld zur Verfügung hatte
SHOW: ✓ — each noun phrase has its own job
[PAUSE:1500]
When you're ready, flip the card for gate two — the verb.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Gate two: the verb decides
SVG: de_case-system_c2b.svg

Gate two: some verbs assign a case you must learn with the verb. Listen to seven common dative-selecting verbs:
TL: helfen, danken, folgen, gehören, gefallen, entsprechen, widersprechen
[PAUSE:1200]
TL: Die Software hilft der Redaktion.
SHOW: helping → dative
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Server gehört dem Ministerium.
SHOW: belonging → dative
[PAUSE:1200]
But semantic feel can betray you. Your record has a city that felt like a recipient:
TL: Damit konnte er seiner Stadt mit Strom versorgen
SHOW: ✗ — from your notes
The supplying verb uses a different frame — the supplied person is accusative:
TL: jemanden mit etwas versorgen
TL: Damit konnte er seine Stadt mit Strom versorgen.
SHOW: ✓ — the verb overrides the feeling
[PAUSE:1500]
Roles propose; the verb confirms or overrides. Gate two closed — move on to the next card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The give-and-tell rhythm
SVG: de_case-system_c3b.svg

This side is about the ditransitive rhythm — verbs of giving, showing and telling:
TL: geben, zeigen, schicken, erklären, erzählen, sagen
[PAUSE:1000]
With these, the recipient or addressee is dative; the thing given or said is accusative. Two of your clearest recorded errors sit exactly on this boundary:
TL: Du kannst den Programm sagen, was es hervorheben soll
SHOW: ✗ — the addressee is dative, and the noun is neuter
TL: Du kannst dem Programm sagen, was es hervorheben soll.
SHOW: ✓
[PAUSE:1200]
TL: Sie tun damit jemanden einen Gefallen
SHOW: ✗ — the favor is accusative, its receiver dative
TL: Sie tun damit jemandem einen Gefallen.
SHOW: ✓
[PAUSE:1200]
Store the boundary as a rhythm, not a rule:
TL: jemandem einen Gefallen tun
TL: der Redaktion den Entwurf schicken
[PAUSE:1500]
Rhythm stored — move on to the next card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Gate three: the preposition
SVG: de_case-system_c4b.svg

Gate three: when a preposition is present, check it first — it may settle the case immediately. This group governs dative:
TL: mit, nach, aus, zu, von, bei
This group governs accusative:
TL: durch, für, gegen, ohne, um
And formal prose keeps three genitive ones:
TL: während, trotz, wegen
TL: wegen des Berichts
[PAUSE:1500]
Then the two-way set — location takes dative, a goal takes accusative. Being somewhere:
TL: Wir arbeiten in der Türkei.
SHOW: location — dative
Heading somewhere:
TL: Wir fliegen in die Türkei.
SHOW: goal — accusative
[PAUSE:1500]
Learn each place as the pair, location and direction — that also locks in the article Russian never asks for. Gate three closed — move on to the next card.
