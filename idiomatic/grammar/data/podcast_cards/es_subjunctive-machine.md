TITLE: The switch is a vowel
SVG: es_subjunctive-machine_c1b.svg

Ear training first: the subjunctive switch is mostly one vowel. Verbs whose present ends in A flip it to E. Listen:
TL: publicas, publiques
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: funciona, funcione
[PAUSE:1000]

TL: viene, venga
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: tiene, tenga
[PAUSE:1200]

TL: sea, esté, vaya, haya, sepa, dé

[CARD]
TITLE: The machine: stance in, subjunctive out
SVG: es_subjunctive-machine_c2f.svg

The rule this card teaches: Spanish marks a subordinate clause that is not being asserted. The main clause carries a stance; the clause after que carries the event; the machine flips its mood. The first stance family is influence — wanting, recommending, preventing:
TL: Quiero que revises el guion.
SHOW: wanting
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: La editora recomienda que cambiemos el título.
SHOW: recommending
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: El contrato impide que vendan los derechos.
SHOW: preventing
[PAUSE:1500]
Don't memorize each verb as a magic word. Hear what the main clause does: it puts pressure on the event, so the event is no longer a neutral report.
SHOW: pressure on the clause → subjunctive
When you're ready, flip the card for the machine's compression rule.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The compression rule
SVG: es_subjunctive-machine_c2b.svg

The compression rule: when one person controls both actions, Spanish collapses the second clause into an infinitive — no machine needed.
TL: Quiero publicar el episodio.
SHOW: one actor — infinitive
[PAUSE:1000]
Change the actor, and the full machine becomes visible:
TL: Quiero que publiques el episodio.
SHOW: new actor — que + subjunctive
[PAUSE:1500]
So the practical question is never "is this verb on the list". It is: do I have a second clause, with its own subject, under somebody's stance?
SHOW: second clause + own subject + stance?
Compression rule pocketed — move on to the next card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reaction, doubt — and the trap
SVG: es_subjunctive-machine_c3b.svg

This side widens the machine's inputs. Reaction and evaluation place a clause under judgment — even when the fact is real:
TL: Me alegra que el episodio tenga éxito.
SHOW: reacting, not doubting
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Es importante que el audio sea claro.
[PAUSE:1200]
So "subjunctive means uncertainty" is too small. The next input is withheld commitment — and here, listen to meaning, not the word no:
TL: Creo que el plan funciona.
SHOW: committed — indicative
TL: No creo que el plan funcione.
SHOW: commitment withheld — subjunctive
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: No dudo de que el plan funciona.
SHOW: "no doubt" = confident assertion — indicative
[PAUSE:1500]
The machine reads the whole stance, not a nearby particle. Inputs widened — move on to the next card.

TITLE: Pending, unidentified — and a firewall
SVG: es_subjunctive-machine_c4b.svg

The machine's last two inputs. First, pending time: an event still ahead is not yet a report. Hear a habit against a pending occasion:
TL: Cuando termino, guardo una copia. Cuando termine, te llamaré.
SHOW: habit — indicative · pending — subjunctive
[PAUSE:1500]
Second, the not-yet-identified person or thing:
TL: Conozco a una editora que habla portugués.
SHOW: known person — indicative
TL: Busco una editora que hable portugués.
SHOW: still unidentified — subjunctive
[PAUSE:1500]
And one firewall, aimed straight at your Portuguese: an open condition keeps the indicative, a remote one takes the imperfect subjunctive — and a present subjunctive after conditional "if" is never right:
TL: Si tengo tiempo, lo hago. Si tuviera tiempo, lo haría.
SHOW: si + present subjunctive — never
[PAUSE:1500]
Two inputs and a firewall — move on to the next card.
