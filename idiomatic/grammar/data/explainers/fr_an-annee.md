## SCRIPT

This fossil is not really about two interchangeable words. It is about two ways of viewing time: as a counting unit, or as a stretch of life and calendar. The record contains these forms:

TL: cet an

TL: chaque an

TL: dans les ans 90

The expected forms were:

TL: cette année

TL: chaque année

TL: dans les années 90

Here is the memory rule. Use the short form when time is a number on a counter: age, a bare measurement, or a fixed counting expression. Use the longer form when you are inside the period, evaluating it, filling it with events, or naming a calendar cycle.

Contrast one: age is counting, but the current calendar period is lived experience.

TL: Elle a trente ans.

TL: Cette année a été difficile.

Contrast two: a repeating numerical interval uses the counter, while every calendar cycle uses the longer form.

TL: Le festival a lieu tous les deux ans.

TL: Le festival change de thème chaque année.

Contrast three shows the same lens with days. A counted date or unit uses the short form. A whole day treated as a container for activity uses the longer form.

TL: Le trajet prend trois jours.

TL: Nous avons travaillé toute la journée.

Contrast four extends the pattern to parts of the day. A point on the schedule takes the short form; an event or a filled block takes the longer form.

TL: Je pars demain matin.

TL: La réunion occupera toute la matinée.

TL: Nous dînerons ce soir.

TL: Nous avons passé une excellente soirée.

This is a strong tendency, not a claim that every numerical phrase has only one possible word. In some contexts, both forms are grammatical and change the viewpoint. In neutral modern French, store the three recorded frames this way:

TL: cette année; chaque année; dans les années quatre-vingt-dix

The short alternatives with a demonstrative or distributive can occur in elevated or literary French. Here, they are register mismatches rather than universally impossible strings. Decades reliably use the longer form. When in doubt, ask: am I merely counting units, or describing what the period contains or feels like?

Question one. Say, “This year, sales increased.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Cette année, les ventes ont augmenté.

Question two. Say, “She is thirty years old.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Elle a trente ans.

Question three. Say, “We worked all morning.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Nous avons travaillé toute la matinée.
