## SCRIPT

This is the most repeated single pattern in your French record: one hundred and thirty-eight direct matches. The trap is keeping an article after a quantity word. One recorded sentence was:

TL: J'apprends beaucoup des langues.

For the ordinary meaning “a lot of languages,” the correction was:

TL: J'apprends beaucoup de langues.

Here is the memory rule: quantity first, then one plain link, then the noun. The link never agrees with the noun. Before a vowel sound, it simply contracts.

TL: beaucoup de livres

TL: beaucoup d'idées

Contrast one: plural does not bring the plural article back. Whether there is one kind of thing or many individual things, an indefinite quantity uses the same link.

TL: Elle lit beaucoup d'articles.

TL: Elle reçoit beaucoup de messages.

Contrast two: an uncountable noun behaves the same way. This other recorded sentence retained the partitive article:

TL: Il gagne beaucoup de l'argent.

In the intended, general reading, the correction is:

TL: Il gagne beaucoup d'argent.

Contrast three: the same rule follows other ordinary quantity words. Think “quantity plus link,” not “quantity plus article.”

TL: Nous avons trop de réunions.

TL: Ils ont assez d'expérience.

TL: Elle publie peu de commentaires.

Contrast four is the useful exception. A definite subset keeps its definite article. The following does not mean “many articles” in general; it means “many of the particular articles that you sent.” The apparent plural form is really the contraction of the link with the definite plural article.

TL: J'ai lu beaucoup des articles que tu m'as envoyés.

So listen for the meaning. Indefinite quantity: use the plain link. A defined set, normally followed by a phrase identifying that set: keep the definite article. This is why the safe default is the short pattern, but the longer form is not forbidden in every sentence.

Question one. Say, “She has a lot of ideas.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Elle a beaucoup d'idées.

Question two. Say, “We have too many meetings.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Nous avons trop de réunions.

Question three. Say, “I read many of the reports that you selected.”

[PAUSE]

TL: J'ai lu beaucoup des rapports que tu as sélectionnés.
