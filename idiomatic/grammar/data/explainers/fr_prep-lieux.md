## SCRIPT

The place-preposition cluster contains fifty-six recorded rows. Thirty-six match the signature pattern: using the country preposition before a city. Here is an attested sentence:

TL: J'habite en Berlin.

For an ordinary city, the repair is:

TL: J'habite à Berlin.

The memory rule is: a city normally gets one small marker; a country shows its grammatical shape. The same markers normally work for both location and movement, so you do not need one system for “in” and another for “to.”

Contrast one: compare a city with the country around it.

TL: Elle travaille à Rome.

TL: Elle travaille en Italie.

That difference is structural, not a translation choice. The first name is a city; the second is a feminine country.

Contrast two: feminine countries normally use the same marker as most vowel-initial countries, including masculine ones.

TL: Le livre a été publié en France.

TL: Le journaliste travaille en Iran.

Contrast three: a masculine country beginning with a consonant takes the contracted masculine form. This recorded sentence used the wrong one:

TL: Il travaille en Chili.

The corrected sentence is:

TL: Il travaille au Chili.

The same pattern gives:

TL: Elle vit au Brésil.

Plural country names take the plural contraction.

TL: Ils étudient aux États-Unis.

Contrast four: a few place names must be learned as complete chunks. Some cities retain an article, and some countries or islands are used without one. Do not force the broad rule onto them.

TL: La conférence se tient au Caire.

TL: Le sommet se tient à Cuba.

So make the decision in this order. First, identify city or country. For an ordinary city, choose the city marker. For a country, recall its lexical gender and number: feminine or vowel-initial, masculine with consonant, or plural. Finally, check whether the name belongs to the smaller set with a fixed lexical pattern. Learning the place together with its marker is more reliable than translating the English word “in.”

Question one. Say, “The conference takes place in Berlin.”

[PAUSE]

TL: La conférence a lieu à Berlin.

Question two. Say, “She works in Brazil.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Elle travaille au Brésil.

Question three. Say, “They study in the United States.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Ils étudient aux États-Unis.
