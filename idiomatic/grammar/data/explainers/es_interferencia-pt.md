## SCRIPT

Cross-language interference is the largest category in the Spanish record: twenty-six of sixty-two verbatim pairs. Roughly fourteen point toward Portuguese. The most persistent item appeared sixteen times across six calendar years, including two recorded errors. The recorded intrusion was:

TL: inserir

The Spanish target is:

TL: insertar

This is not a beginner’s lack of vocabulary. It is a retrieval race between two closely related languages, and Portuguese sometimes reaches the microphone first. The memory rule is: treat instant familiarity as an alarm. Pause, retrieve the whole Spanish word or chunk, and do not merely adjust a Portuguese ending.

Contrast one is a near look-alike. The record gives this wrong form:

TL: exemplo

The Spanish form changes its central consonant:

TL: ejemplo

Anchor the Spanish form in a frequent discourse chunk.

TL: Por ejemplo, el informe incluye tres escenarios.

Contrast two requires a different word, not a spelling repair. First, the recorded Portuguese-shaped adjective:

Contrast three begins like a cognate but needs the Spanish initial vowel pattern. The recorded intrusion was:

The Spanish target is:

TL: El equipo no puede trabajar aislado.

Contrast four is grammatical, not lexical. The recorded past-tense form is valid Portuguese, which makes it especially seductive:

TL: quisemos

Spanish uses a different first-person-plural preterite ending:

TL: quisimos

TL: Quisimos insertar el ejemplo en el informe.

The profile also records a pronoun imported whole:

These contrasts suggest three checks. First, listen for a suspiciously effortless cognate. Second, ask whether Spanish changes the spelling or replaces the entire word. Third, for an inflected verb, retrieve the Spanish paradigm rather than importing a Portuguese cell. The goal is not to distrust cognates; it is to verify the handful that repeatedly cross the border.
