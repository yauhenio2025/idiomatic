## SCRIPT

This fossil is a traffic problem between Romance languages. Italian supplies one tempting connector, and Spanish supplies another. Portuguese does not want either connector in the patterns recorded here. Across four years, the profile found thirty verb-regime errors, including nine instances with the first verb in this list and five each with the second and fourth.

TL: tentar, conseguir, decidir, ir

When one of these verbs directly governs another verb in the relevant meaning, picture a zero-width bridge. The infinitive follows immediately. Zero means zero: do not insert a preposition.

The most persistent recorded pattern imported the Italian connector. Compare the attempt with its repair.

TL: tentei de instalar

TL: tentei instalar

The same zero bridge holds after the verb meaning “to manage.” The notes record this contrast.

TL: consegue a procurar

TL: consegue procurar

Decision works the same way when it is followed directly by the action decided upon.

TL: decidi de mandar

TL: decidi mandar

The spoken future is another major trap. Spanish inserts a connector between the conjugated verb and the infinitive; Brazilian Portuguese does not. This full sentence is recorded in the profile.

TL: Eu vou a passar quatro dias lá.

TL: Eu vou passar quatro dias lá.

Do not turn this into a rule that Portuguese never uses prepositions after verbs. Many verbs require one, and each regime is lexical. The narrow rule is that these four constructions take a bare infinitive in these meanings. Also separate the future auxiliary from literal movement. A preposition can still introduce a destination when the verb describes actual travel, while an infinitive expressing the future action remains bare.

The practical habit is to learn a verb together with its bridge: a specific preposition, or a deliberate zero. Before speaking, ask whether the next element is a destination, a noun complement, or the action governed by this verb. For these recorded action patterns, go straight to the infinitive.
