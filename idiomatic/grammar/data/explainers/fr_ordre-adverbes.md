## SCRIPT

The recurring error is English-style placement: subject, then adverb, then the finite verb. The broader adverb-placement cluster has more than sixty rows, including thirty-three direct matches for this order. One recorded sentence was:

TL: Je déjà connais ce mot.

The neutral French order is:

TL: Je connais déjà ce mot.

Here is the memory rule for this fossil: find the conjugated carrier, then let a short, common adverb ride just after it. In a simple tense, that carrier is the main verb. In a compound tense, it is the auxiliary, so the adverb often lands between the auxiliary and the past participle.

Contrast one uses a simple tense. The adverb follows the conjugated verb.

TL: Elle comprend déjà le problème.

TL: Nous voyageons souvent ensemble.

Contrast two uses a manner adverb in a neutral sentence. French does not insert it between the subject and the finite verb.

TL: Le gouvernement veut clairement changer de cap.

Longer manner adverbs are more mobile and often come later, so do not turn the memory rule into a law for every adverb. It is a reliable repair for the repeated short-adverb pattern.

Contrast three uses compound tenses. The first conjugated word is now the auxiliary.

TL: J'ai déjà lu ce rapport.

TL: Ce plan a aussi échoué.

The participle stays after the short adverb. This is why looking only for the “main meaning” verb can mislead you.

Contrast four combines placement with negation. This recorded sentence put the negative adverb before the future verb:

TL: On ne jamais arrivera à ce point.

The conjugated verb belongs between the two negative elements:

TL: On n'arrivera jamais à ce point.

With a compound tense, the same negative adverb follows the auxiliary and precedes the participle.

TL: Ils ne l'ont jamais rencontré.

French can move an adverb for contrast or style, and a focused phrase has its own pattern. But for an unmarked statement, anchor the adverb to the conjugated carrier. That gives you the useful rhythm: subject, finite verb, short adverb; or subject, auxiliary, short adverb, participle.

Question one. Say, “I already know this result.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Je connais déjà ce résultat.

Question two. Say, “They have never met him.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Ils ne l'ont jamais rencontré.

Question three. Say, “The government also wants to act.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Le gouvernement veut aussi agir.
