## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: The infinitive names an action without locating it in time
REF: 19.1, 19.2

The infinitive is the dictionary form ending in -ar, -er, or -ir. It can behave like an English to-form or an -ing form, depending on its job in the sentence.
TL: Viajar ensina muito. · Prefiro cozinhar em casa.
EN: Travelling teaches you a lot. · I prefer cooking at home.
[PAUSE:900]
It also follows future ir, modal verbs, adjectives, and impersonal expressions. The finite verb carries tense and person while the infinitive supplies the action.
TL: Vou ligar amanhã. · Podemos entrar? · É bom descansar.
EN: I will call tomorrow. · May we come in? · It is good to rest.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: noun-like action · auxiliary/modal complement · impersonal expression -> infinitive
Flip for the feature that makes Portuguese infinitives unusually flexible.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A personal infinitive can introduce its own subject
REF: 19.1.2, 19.3

Portuguese can give an infinitive a subject distinct from the subject of the main clause. A noun or subject pronoun normally stands immediately before it.
TL: É melhor vocês esperarem aqui. · Ela saiu sem eu perceber.
EN: It is better for you to wait here. · She left without me noticing.
[PAUSE:900]
This often replaces the longer conjunction-plus-finite-clause pattern that English or another Romance language might lead you to expect.
TL: Antes de o trem chegar, vamos tomar café.
EN: Before the train arrives, let us have coffee.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: preposition/expression + subject + infinitive -> compact subordinate event

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose the personal infinitive when its subject needs to be visible
REF: 19.3.1, 19.3.3, 19.3.4

Compare subjects first. If the understood subject remains the same as the main verb, the plain impersonal infinitive is normally enough.
TL: Saí sem fechar a janela.
EN: I left without closing the window.
[PAUSE:900]
If the subordinate action has a different or independently stated subject, use the personal construction. First- and third-person singular have no audible extra ending, so the stated subject does the work.
TL: Saí sem eles perceberem. · Isto é para eu fazer hoje.
EN: I left without them noticing. · This is for me to do today.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: same understood subject -> plain infinitive · independent subject -> personal infinitive
Flip to form the endings without confusing them with another mood.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Personal endings attach to the whole infinitive
REF: 19.3.2

Keep the infinitive stem intact and add the personal endings. The most useful overt forms in Brazilian Portuguese are -mos for nós and -em for vocês, eles, elas, or a plural noun.
TL: falar -> falarmos, falarem · fazer -> fazermos, fazerem
EN: for us to speak, for them to speak · for us to do, for them to do
[PAUSE:900]
The full pattern also has -es for tu and -des for vós, though vós is rare in Brazil. Do not substitute the irregular future-subjunctive stem.
TL: para nós fazermos · quando nós fizermos
EN: for us to do · when we do
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: infinitive stem + zero, -es, zero, -mos, -des, -em · fazermos, not fizermos

[CARD]
TITLE: Prepositions make compact infinitive clauses
REF: 19.4.1, 19.4.2

Depois de, antes de, sem, apesar de, em, and por can introduce an infinitive clause. Add a subject and personal ending whenever the subordinate actor must be clear.
TL: Telefonaram antes de nós sairmos. · Ela viajou sem os filhos saberem.
EN: They called before we left. · She travelled without her children knowing.
[PAUSE:900]
In careful writing, do not contract de, em, or por with an article or pronoun that is the infinitive's subject. The boundary between preposition and subject remains visible.
TL: antes de o avião partir · o fato de eles recusarem
EN: before the plane leaves · the fact that they refuse
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: de/em/por + infinitive subject -> keep separate in careful writing
Flip for two prepositions whose meanings decide the relation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Para marks purpose; por can supply a reason
REF: 19.4.3, 19.4.4

Use para plus an infinitive for the purpose of the main action. A separate subject turns it into for someone to do or so that someone can do.
TL: Baixei a voz para não acordar o bebê. · Abri a porta para eles entrarem.
EN: I lowered my voice so as not to wake the baby. · I opened the door for them to come in.
[PAUSE:900]
Por plus an infinitive can explain the cause. Inflect it when the reason belongs to a plural subject distinct from the main-clause subject.
TL: Ela melhorou por fazer exercício. · Fomos elogiados por termos ajudado.
EN: She improved because she exercises. · We were praised because we helped.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: para + infinitive = purpose · por + infinitive = cause

[CARD]
TITLE: Learn a verb together with the link before its infinitive
REF: 19.5.1, 19.5.2, 19.5.3, 19.5.4, 19.5.5

Modals and many common verbs take no preposition. Other verbs govern a, de, em, or para, and that link is part of the construction rather than a free stylistic choice.
TL: quero sair · aprendeu a nadar · parou de fumar · pensa em mudar · pediu para entrar
EN: wants to leave · learned to swim · stopped smoking · thinks of moving · asked to enter
[PAUSE:900]
An object may sit before the governed link. Memorize the entire frame so the roles remain clear.
TL: convenceu o amigo a ficar · impediu o carro de passar · falou para eu esperar
EN: persuaded the friend to stay · stopped the car from passing · told me to wait
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: verb + zero/a/de/em/para + infinitive · learn the whole frame
Flip to see how these frames shift between formal writing and conversation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Formal reporting often compresses a finite clause into an infinitive
REF: 19.5.1, 19.5.5

In careful reporting, verbs such as dizer, afirmar, negar, and alegar can introduce an infinitive. The infinitive shares the reporting subject and sounds more compact and formal.
TL: O diretor afirma desconhecer o problema. · A empresa nega ter recebido o aviso.
EN: The director says he is unaware of the problem. · The company denies receiving the notice.
[PAUSE:900]
Conversation normally expands the message with que plus a finite verb. Requests and commands likewise favor para plus an expressed subject.
TL: O diretor diz que não conhece o problema. · Pedi para eles voltarem amanhã.
EN: The director says he does not know about the problem. · I asked them to return tomorrow.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: formal report -> verb + infinitive · conversation -> que-clause / para + subject + infinitive

[CARD]
TITLE: Perception and causative verbs allow two grammatical viewpoints
REF: 19.6.1, 19.6.2, 19.6.3, 19.6.4

After ver, ouvir, sentir, deixar, fazer, and mandar, a plural noun can be treated as the first verb's object or as the infinitive's subject. That permits an uninflected or inflected infinitive.
TL: Vi os atletas cruzar a linha. · Vi os atletas cruzarem a linha.
EN: I saw the athletes cross the line. · I saw the athletes cross the line.
[PAUSE:900]
An unstressed object pronoun requires the impersonal infinitive. Spoken eles or elas used as an object instead favors the personal form.
TL: Nós os mandamos esperar. · A gente mandou eles esperarem.
EN: We told them to wait. · We told them to wait.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: unstressed object pronoun -> plain infinitive · spoken eles/elas -> personal infinitive
Flip for the especially visible let me contrast.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Deixe-me and deixa eu encode the register difference
REF: 19.6.4, 19.7

Careful written style uses an attached object pronoun followed by an impersonal infinitive. Everyday Brazilian speech uses a subject pronoun and the personal construction.
TL: Deixe-me explicar. · Deixa eu explicar.
EN: Let me explain. · Let me explain.
[PAUSE:900]
With the same plural subject as the main verb, inflection can also appear when the infinitive phrase comes first or stands far from its controller. Close complements normally stay uninflected.
TL: Sem fazermos barulho, entramos. · Começamos a trabalhar.
EN: Without making noise, we went in. · We began to work.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: written object pattern -> deixe-me fazer · spoken subject pattern -> deixa eu fazer

[CARD]
TITLE: Pronoun position changes with syntax and register
REF: 19.8.1, 19.8.2, 19.8.3

At the start of a written clause, attach an unstressed pronoun to the infinitive. A preceding subject, negative word, interrogative, or adverb normally draws the pronoun before it.
TL: Proteger-se é essencial. · É melhor eles se protegerem.
EN: Protecting oneself is essential. · It is better for them to protect themselves.
[PAUSE:900]
After a preposition, Brazilian speech and neutral informal writing favor pronoun plus infinitive. Formal writing may attach me, te, se, nos, or lhe; third-person o, a, os, and as attach as -lo, -la, -los, and -las.
TL: antes de se mudar · antes de mudar-se · para encontrá-la
EN: before moving · before moving, formal · in order to meet her
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: clause-initial written -> infinitive-pronoun · informal after preposition -> pronoun + infinitive
Flip before using an English -ing form as a signal for the gerund.

[SIDE]
TITLE: English -ing does not automatically mean a Portuguese gerund
REF: 17.1, 19.1, 19.2

Portuguese commonly uses the infinitive as an action-name, after a preposition, and after another verb where English uses -ing. The gerund instead profiles an action in progress, manner, or accompaniment.
TL: Fumar faz mal. · Saí sem avisar. · Está chovendo. · Saiu correndo.
EN: Smoking is harmful. · I left without warning. · It is raining. · He ran out.
[PAUSE:900]
Diagnose the construction, not the English ending. Ask whether the action is being named or linked, or whether it is unfolding and modifying another event.
TL: Gosto de caminhar. · Ela passou a tarde caminhando.
EN: I like walking. · She spent the afternoon walking.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: action-name/link -> infinitive · progress/manner/concurrent action -> gerund

[CARD]
TITLE: The infinitive extends into several concise written patterns
REF: 19.9.1, 19.9.2, 19.9.3, 19.9.4, 19.9.5

Adjectives and nouns often take de before an infinitive. Por can mean remaining to be done, while ao introduces a written when-doing relation.
TL: difícil de abrir · hora de sair · trabalho por terminar · ao chegar
EN: difficult to open · time to leave · work left to finish · on arriving
[PAUSE:900]
A plus an infinitive can carry a passive or conditional flavor in formal style. These compact patterns belong more to written usage than ordinary conversation.
TL: os pontos a discutir · a julgar pelos resultados · a persistirem os sintomas
EN: the points to be discussed · judging by the results · if the symptoms persist
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: de = complement · por = pending · ao = on doing · a = passive/conditional written pattern
Flip to place one infinitive action before another reference point.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ter plus a participle forms the perfect infinitive
REF: 19.10

Use ter plus an invariable past participle when the infinitive's event is already complete before the main event or viewpoint. English often uses a plain -ing form instead.
TL: Ele pediu desculpas por ter chegado tarde. · Ela nega ter visto a mensagem.
EN: He apologized for arriving late. · She denies having seen the message.
[PAUSE:900]
The perfect infinitive can also be personal. Inflect ter, not the participle, when an independent plural subject needs expression.
TL: Obrigado por vocês terem vindo. · Saíram sem terem terminado o trabalho.
EN: Thank you for having come. · They left without having finished the work.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: earlier non-finite event -> ter/terem + invariable participle

[CARD]
TITLE: An infinitive can replace a subjunctive clause when roles stay clear
REF: 19.3, 20.8

An expressed infinitive subject often lets Portuguese compress que plus a subjunctive into a shorter construction after an evaluation, emotion, or preposition.
TL: É importante que vocês participem. -> É importante vocês participarem.
EN: It is important that you participate. -> It is important for you to participate.
[PAUSE:900]
The same compression works after para, sem, até, and related links. Preserve the subject and time relation; use the perfect infinitive if the subordinate event is earlier.
TL: Esperei até eles chegarem. · Lamento você não ter recebido o convite.
EN: I waited until they arrived. · I am sorry you did not receive the invitation.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: que + finite subordinate clause -> subject + personal infinitive when clear
Flip for a final selection path.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build an infinitive construction in five checks
REF: 19.2, 19.3, 19.4, 19.5, 19.10

One: identify the infinitive's job. Two: if a governing verb is present, recall whether it takes zero, a, de, em, or para. Three: compare the main and infinitive subjects.
[PAUSE:850]
Four: if the infinitive has an independent plural subject, add -mos or -em where required or natural. Five: if its event happened earlier, choose ter plus the participle.
[PAUSE:900]
Then check register and pronoun placement. A compact construction may be formal, conversational, or neutral even when its basic meaning is unchanged.
TL: quero sair · para nós sairmos · por termos saído · deixa eu sair
EN: I want to leave · for us to leave · because we left · let me leave
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: job -> governed link -> subject -> time relation -> register/pronoun position
