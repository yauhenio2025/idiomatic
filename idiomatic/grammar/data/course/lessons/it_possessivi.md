## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Italian chooses a possessive construction, not just a possessive word
REF: 10.1, 10.2, 10.4, 10.13

English possessive forms can correspond to several Italian structures. Name an external owner with di, use a possessive adjective when ownership must be explicit, and often use only an article when context already identifies the possessor.
TL: Il progetto di Giulia è pronto, ma lei non trova la sua copia.
EN: Giulia's project is ready, but she cannot find her copy.
[PAUSE:900]
Body parts and closely associated belongings often use an article plus a pronoun that marks the affected person. The sentence, not the English apostrophe or possessive adjective, determines the Italian pattern.
TL: Mi hanno controllato il passaporto e poi mi hanno aperto la valigia.
EN: They checked my passport and then opened my suitcase.
SHOW: named external owner -> di · ownership must be explicit -> possessive adjective · context supplies owner -> article or affected-person pronoun
Flip for the agreement rule shared by nearly all ordinary possessives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The possessed noun controls agreement
REF: 10.4

Mio, tuo, suo, nostro, and vostro agree with the thing possessed, not with the person who possesses it. The possessor's gender does not choose suo versus sua.
TL: Luca cerca le sue chiavi; Marta cerca il suo passaporto.
EN: Luca is looking for his keys; Marta is looking for her passport.
[PAUSE:900]
Loro is the exception: it never changes. The article and noun still show gender and number.
TL: I tecnici hanno presentato la loro analisi e le loro conclusioni.
EN: The technicians presented their analysis and their conclusions.
SHOW: masculine singular -> mio/tuo/suo · feminine plural -> mie/tue/sue · any possessed noun with plural possessor -> loro

[CARD]
TITLE: Use di to name the possessor
REF: 10.1

The neutral way to attach a named owner is possessed noun plus di plus the possessor. Contract di with an article when the possessor noun requires one.
TL: La copertina del manuale è rovinata, ma gli appunti di Elena sono intatti.
EN: The manual's cover is damaged, but Elena's notes are intact.
[PAUSE:900]
Di chi asks whose, and essere di can state ownership without repeating the possessed noun.
TL: Di chi sono questi guanti? Sono di Paolo.
EN: Whose gloves are these? They are Paolo's.
SHOW: owner's noun -> noun + di + owner · di + il -> del · whose? -> di chi? · belong to -> essere di

[SIDE]
TITLE: Omit the possessive when the owner is already obvious
REF: 10.2, 10.3

Italian commonly uses the definite article alone for a person's body parts, clothes, routine belongings, home, or close relations when the subject and context identify the owner.
TL: Non trovo il portafoglio e ho lasciato la giacca in ufficio.
EN: I cannot find my wallet, and I left my jacket at the office.
[PAUSE:900]
For a characteristic attribute, Italian often recasts English possession as avere plus article plus noun. Keep an explicit possessive when the relationship is not inferable or is contrastive.
TL: Davide ha gli occhi scuri e la barba corta.
EN: Davide has dark eyes and a short beard.
SHOW: inferable personal item -> article only · characteristic attribute -> avere + article + noun · unclear or contrastive owner -> explicit possessive

[CARD]
TITLE: Build the possessive adjective from two coordinates
REF: 10.4

Choose the possessor row first: mio, tuo, suo, nostro, vostro, or loro. Then inflect the form for the possessed noun's gender and number.
TL: Abbiamo aggiornato i nostri indirizzi e le nostre password.
EN: We updated our addresses and our passwords.
[PAUSE:900]
Suo can mean his, her, or its. Loro means their and remains invariant, although the surrounding article changes.
TL: La loro proposta riguarda i loro dipendenti.
EN: Their proposal concerns their employees.
SHOW: 1sg mio/mia/miei/mie · 2sg tuo/tua/tuoi/tue · 3sg suo/sua/suoi/sue · 1pl nostro... · 2pl vostro... · 3pl loro
Eleven possessive-form completions follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The article is normal, but some positions omit it
REF: 10.4

Before an ordinary noun, a possessive normally comes with a definite article. The possessive usually precedes any other adjective.
TL: Ho incontrato la tua nuova collega e il suo giovane assistente.
EN: I met your new colleague and her young assistant.
[PAUSE:900]
A bare possessive appears predicatively and in certain fixed expressions, including casa mia or casa tua. Adding the article to casa creates the ordinary noun phrase.
TL: Questa scrivania è mia; ci vediamo a casa tua, non nella mia casa.
EN: This desk is mine; we will meet at your place, not in my house.
SHOW: ordinary noun phrase -> article + possessive + noun · predicate -> essere + bare possessive · home expression -> casa mia/tua

[CARD]
TITLE: Singular close-family nouns usually reject the article
REF: 10.6

With an unmodified singular close relative, place the possessive directly before the noun: mia sorella, tuo padre, nostro zio. This is a special family-noun pattern, not a general article rule.
TL: Mia sorella lavora con tuo cugino.
EN: My sister works with your cousin.
[PAUSE:900]
The ordinary article returns with plural relatives and with loro.
TL: I miei fratelli conoscono la loro zia.
EN: My brothers know their aunt.
SHOW: possessive + singular close relative -> usually no article · plural relative -> article · loro + relative -> article
Eleven printed-key family-form decisions follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Modification and lexical exceptions restore the article
REF: 10.6

An adjective, diminutive, or other modification makes the family noun an ordinary defined noun phrase, so the article returns.
TL: Il mio fratello maggiore vive vicino alla nostra cara nonna.
EN: My older brother lives near our dear grandmother.
[PAUSE:900]
Step-relations and similar terms generally take the article. Mamma and babbo vary in usage, so learn their pattern from context rather than extending the core exception mechanically.
TL: La sua matrigna ha telefonato al mio padrino.
EN: Her stepmother telephoned my godfather.
SHOW: modified relative -> article · diminutive or affectionate form -> article · step-parent/godparent -> usually article · mamma/babbo -> variable

[CARD]
TITLE: A possessive pronoun carries an article
REF: 10.7

When the possessed noun is omitted, the definite article plus an agreeing possessive stands for it. The agreement still belongs to the missing noun.
TL: La mia penna non scrive: posso usare la tua?
EN: My pen does not write: may I use yours?
[PAUSE:900]
For a named owner, use an agreeing form of quello plus di. The form of quello points back to the omitted item, not to its owner.
TL: Preferisco il progetto di Sara a quello di Marco.
EN: I prefer Sara's project to Marco's.
SHOW: mine/yours/hers -> article + possessive · a named person's one -> quello/quella/quelli/quelle + di + person

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mia asserts ownership; la mia selects the owned one
REF: 10.8

After essere, a bare possessive behaves like an adjective and simply attributes ownership.
TL: Quella bicicletta è mia.
EN: That bicycle belongs to me.
[PAUSE:900]
Article plus possessive is a pronoun that identifies one candidate among alternatives: it is the one that is mine.
TL: Quale bicicletta prendi? Quella rossa è la mia.
EN: Which bicycle are you taking? The red one is mine.
SHOW: essere + mia -> assert who owns it · essere + la mia -> select my one from a set

[CARD]
TITLE: Ne can replace a non-reflexive di possessor
REF: 10.10

When di introduces an external person or topic, ne can carry that relation and attach to the verb.
TL: Conosco le ricerche di Ada e ne apprezzo il rigore.
EN: I know Ada's research and appreciate its rigor.
[PAUSE:900]
The same clitic replaces di plus a topic after verbs such as parlare. It does not agree with the possessed noun.
TL: Parli spesso dei tuoi colleghi? Sì, ne parlo ogni giorno.
EN: Do you often talk about your colleagues? Yes, I talk about them every day.
SHOW: di lui/lei/loro or di a topic -> ne · ne + finite verb or infinitive attachment · no gender-number agreement
Thirteen printed-key ne decisions follow, including the meaningful cases where no replacement is possible.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ne cannot mean the subject's own
REF: 10.10, 10.11

Ne points away from the subject. If the possessor is the subject itself, keep a reflexive possessive such as proprio or an unambiguous ordinary possessive.
TL: Ada difende la propria tesi; il relatore ne apprezza la precisione.
EN: Ada defends her own thesis; the supervisor appreciates its precision.
[PAUSE:900]
In the first clause, ne would wrongly suggest somebody else's thesis. In the second, ne correctly points back to Ada's thesis.
TL: Pietro parla dei suoi dubbi, ma noi non ne conosciamo la causa.
EN: Pietro talks about his doubts, but we do not know their cause.
SHOW: subject's own relation -> proprio/suo/loro · external di relation -> ne

[CARD]
TITLE: Proprio marks the subject's own relation
REF: 10.11

Proprio can stand for my own, your own, his or her own, our own, or their own. It usually precedes the noun and agrees with the possessed noun.
TL: Ogni candidata deve controllare i propri dati e la propria firma.
EN: Every candidate must check their own details and signature.
[PAUSE:900]
It is especially useful with indefinite subjects, where suo might suggest a separate third person. First- and second-person possessives may combine with proprio for extra emphasis.
TL: Vogliamo lavorare nel nostro proprio laboratorio.
EN: We want to work in our own laboratory.
SHOW: subject's own -> proprio/propria/propri/proprie · indefinite subject -> proprio avoids an external-owner reading · emphatic 1st/2nd person -> mio/tuo/nostro/vostro + proprio
Eleven proprio-and-altrui transformations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Altrui means someone else's in a formal register
REF: 10.12

Altrui is invariant, normally follows the noun, and belongs mainly to formal or elevated language.
TL: Non è lecito divulgare dati altrui senza consenso.
EN: It is not permissible to disclose other people's data without consent.
[PAUSE:900]
As a pronoun, use l'altrui or, more commonly, an agreeing form of quello plus altrui. Contrast it with proprio to oppose one's own interests and those of others.
TL: Bisogna difendere la propria libertà senza limitare quella altrui.
EN: One must defend one's own freedom without limiting other people's.
SHOW: someone else's + noun -> noun + altrui · someone else's one -> quello/quella + altrui · own versus others' -> proprio versus altrui

[CARD]
TITLE: Suo and loro may hide who the possessor is
REF: 10.5

Suo identifies the number and gender of the possessed item, not the possessor. A sentence with two plausible people can therefore be ambiguous.
TL: Luca ha telefonato a Paolo dopo il suo esame.
EN: Luca telephoned Paolo after his exam.
[PAUSE:900]
If the intended owner matters, replace the possessive with di plus a name or with di lui, di lei, or di loro after the noun.
TL: Luca ha telefonato a Paolo dopo l'esame di Paolo.
EN: Luca telephoned Paolo after Paolo's exam.
SHOW: ambiguous suo/loro -> noun + di + explicit owner · his/hers/theirs after a noun -> di lui/di lei/di loro

[SIDE]
TITLE: Explicit owner phrases also form contrastive pronouns
REF: 10.5, 10.7

Use quello di lui, quella di lei, and their plural forms when English contrasts his, hers, or theirs and ordinary suo would remain unclear.
TL: Anna e Marco hanno mostrato le fotografie di lei e quelle di lui.
EN: Anna and Marco showed her photographs and his.
[PAUSE:900]
The agreeing demonstrative stands for the possessed item; the pronoun after di names its owner. In neutral standard usage, this explicit structure is cleaner than stacking suo and di lui together.
TL: Ho scelto la proposta di lei, non quella di lui.
EN: I chose hers, not his.
SHOW: her noun -> noun + di lei · his one -> quello + di lui · agree quello with the missing possessed noun

[CARD]
TITLE: An affected possessor is an indirect object
REF: 10.13

For body parts and things closely associated with a person, Italian often makes the person an indirect object and uses an article on the affected thing.
TL: Il dentista controlla i denti al paziente.
EN: The dentist checks the patient's teeth.
[PAUSE:900]
Replace the person with an indirect-object clitic. Do not add a possessive adjective to the body-part noun.
TL: Gli controlla i denti e gli cura una gengiva.
EN: The dentist checks his teeth and treats one of his gums.
SHOW: action affects person's part -> article + part + a + person · a lui/a lei -> gli/le · no possessive adjective on the affected part
Twenty advanced contrastive translations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The dative can cover belongings, home, and close relations
REF: 10.13

The affected-person construction extends beyond body parts to clothing, habitual belongings, a home, and sometimes family members.
TL: Mi hanno rubato il telefono e mi hanno danneggiato la bicicletta.
EN: They stole my phone and damaged my bicycle.
[PAUSE:900]
The clitic tells whose sphere is affected; the article identifies the thing. Use an explicit possessive when the relationship is merely descriptive or contrastive rather than affected.
TL: Hanno riparato la sua bicicletta, non la mia.
EN: They repaired her bicycle, not mine.
SHOW: affected owner -> indirect-object clitic + article · descriptive/contrastive owner -> possessive adjective or pronoun

[CARD]
TITLE: Reflexive routines use a reflexive pronoun plus an article
REF: 10.14

When the subject acts on a part of their own body, the affected-person pronoun becomes reflexive. The body-part noun keeps its article.
TL: Mi lavo le mani e Paolo si pettina i capelli.
EN: I wash my hands, and Paolo combs his hair.
[PAUSE:900]
In the third person, the pronoun distinguishes one's own body from somebody else's.
TL: Marco si è rotto il polso; durante la partita gli hanno rotto un dito.
EN: Marco broke his own wrist; during the match somebody broke one of his fingers.
SHOW: subject acts on own part -> mi/ti/si/ci/vi/si + verb + article · external person's part -> gli/le + verb + article

[SIDE]
TITLE: Some body movements need only the article
REF: 10.14

When a movement is understood as originating in the body part itself, Italian normally uses no reflexive pronoun: close the eyes, raise the hand, shake the head.
TL: Chiuse gli occhi, alzò la mano e scosse la testa.
EN: He closed his eyes, raised his hand, and shook his head.
[PAUSE:900]
A reflexive can reappear when the subject uses an external means to act on the part.
TL: Con le dita riuscì ad aprirsi l'occhio gonfio.
EN: With his fingers he managed to open his swollen eye.
SHOW: internally originating movement -> verb + article + body part · external manipulation of own part -> reflexive pronoun + verb + article

[CARD]
TITLE: Perception can present a person's feature as a dative
REF: 10.15

With perception, Italian can mark the person as an indirect object and present the perceived feature with an article.
TL: Gli sentivo il respiro affannoso dalla stanza accanto.
EN: I could hear that his breathing was labored from the next room.
[PAUSE:900]
Formal acknowledgement verbs can use the same affected-person organization. In ordinary contexts, an explicit possessive or di phrase may be clearer.
TL: Alla candidata riconosciamo una notevole precisione.
EN: We acknowledge that the candidate has remarkable precision.
SHOW: perceive person's feature -> indirect object + article + feature · formal acknowledge a quality -> riconoscere + dative + quality

[SIDE]
TITLE: Route possession by meaning
REF: 10.1, 10.4, 10.7, 10.10, 10.13

First ask whether you must name the owner. Use di for an explicit external owner and a possessive adjective or pronoun for genuine, contrastive ownership.
TL: La relazione di Marta è pronta; la mia è ancora incompleta.
EN: Marta's report is ready; mine is still incomplete.
[PAUSE:900]
If context already identifies the person, prefer the article. Add an indirect-object or reflexive pronoun when an action affects that person's body, belongings, or close sphere; use ne only for a non-reflexive di relation.
TL: Mi sono tolto gli occhiali e ne ho pulito le lenti.
EN: I took off my glasses and cleaned their lenses.
SHOW: explicit owner -> di · contrastive ownership -> possessive · inferable owner -> article · affected owner -> dative/reflexive · external di relation -> ne
