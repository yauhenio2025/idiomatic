## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: The infinitive names an event without anchoring it
REF: 12.1

An infinitive identifies a process but does not itself mark person, number, or a finite tense. Its ending makes the form visible: -er, -ir, -re, or -oir in the traditional groups.
TL: Réparer cette erreur demandera du temps.
EN: Correcting this error will take time.
[PAUSE:900]
Réparer names the event; demandera supplies the future anchor for the sentence. English may translate a French infinitive with to plus a verb or with an -ing form.
SHOW: infinitive → event without person or finite tense
Flip for an event completed before another one.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A past infinitive looks backward
REF: 12.1

Build the past infinitive with avoir or être in the infinitive plus a past participle. It presents the infinitive event as already complete relative to the governing event.
TL: Nora regrette d'avoir fermé le dossier trop tôt.
EN: Nora regrets having closed the file too early.
[PAUSE:800]
Reflexive verbs use s'être, and ordinary auxiliary and agreement rules still apply.
TL: Après s'être préparée, Nora est sortie.
EN: After getting ready, Nora went out.
SHOW: avoir / être + past participle → earlier infinitive event

[CARD]
TITLE: Find the understood subject
REF: 12.1, 12.2

An infinitive has no person ending, but its action still has an understood performer. With no intervening participant, that performer is normally the subject of the governing verb.
TL: Malik promet de revenir demain.
EN: Malik promises to return tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Malik both promises and returns. If the intended performers differ, French may introduce the second participant as an object or use a finite clause.
TL: Malik promet que Nora reviendra demain.
EN: Malik promises that Nora will return tomorrow.
SHOW: no new participant → matrix subject controls the infinitive
Flip when an object supplies the performer.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An object can control the infinitive
REF: 12.2

Some governing verbs place the infinitive after a direct or indirect object. Learn the object's form and the infinitive linker as separate parts of the verb's frame.
TL: Léa encourage Marc à répondre; elle ordonne à Marc de rester.
EN: Léa encourages Marc to answer; she orders Marc to stay.
[PAUSE:900]
The first frame is encourager quelqu'un à faire quelque chose. The second is ordonner à quelqu'un de faire quelque chose. English to does not reveal either French pattern.
SHOW: governor + object pattern + infinitive linker → complete frame

[CARD]
TITLE: A licensed group takes no linker
REF: 12.3

Modal verbs and many verbs of intention or attitude take a bare infinitive. The infinitive follows the finite verb directly.
TL: Nous devons partir; vous pouvez attendre; elle veut comprendre.
EN: We must leave; you can wait; she wants to understand.
[PAUSE:900]
Movement verbs can introduce their purpose the same way.
TL: Je descends chercher le courrier.
EN: I am going downstairs to collect the post.
SHOW: devoir / pouvoir / vouloir · movement + purpose → bare infinitive
Flip for perception and causation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Perception, faire, and laisser also take a bare infinitive
REF: 12.3.8, 12.3.9

After a perception verb, the object is understood as performing the infinitive event. Faire and laisser likewise join directly to an infinitive, with causative or permissive meaning.
TL: J'entends les voisins rentrer; elle laisse les enfants choisir.
EN: I hear the neighbours coming home; she lets the children choose.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nous faisons vérifier les chiffres.
EN: We are having the figures checked.
[PAUSE:800]
Treat these as licensed constructions, not as permission to omit a preposition after any verb.
SHOW: perception / faire / laisser + bare infinitive

[CARD]
TITLE: The governing verb selects the bridge
REF: 12.2, 12.3, 12.4, 12.5, 12.11

Between two verbs, French may require no linker, à, de, or occasionally par. The choice belongs to the governing expression and must be learned with it.
TL: espérer réussir · parvenir à répondre · décider de partir · finir par accepter
EN: to hope to succeed · to manage to answer · to decide to leave · to end up accepting
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not translate English to into a French preposition. Store the whole frame and verify an unfamiliar one in a reliable dictionary or the chapter index.
SHOW: governor → Ø / à / de / par → infinitive
Forty hygiene-gated linker choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Similar linkers can signal different event shapes
REF: 12.4.1, 12.5.17

Commencer and continuer allow à or de with little difference in their basic start-or-continue reading. Par, however, marks the first or eventual step in a sequence.
TL: Elle commence à lire; elle commence par vérifier la date.
EN: She starts reading; she begins by checking the date.
[PAUSE:800]
Finir de means finish an activity, while finir par means eventually reach an outcome.
TL: Il finit de rédiger; il finit par envoyer le rapport.
EN: He finishes writing; he eventually sends the report.
SHOW: commencer à/de · finir de → activity boundary · commencer/finir par → sequence

[CARD]
TITLE: À often frames effort, progress, or a goal
REF: 12.4

Many verbs of beginning, effort, aspiration, success, training, and encouragement select à. These meaning groups are useful clues, but the individual frame remains decisive.
TL: Elle s'applique à répondre; nous parvenons à avancer.
EN: She applies herself to answering; we manage to move forward.
[PAUSE:900]
With a second participant, common frames include aider quelqu'un à, encourager quelqu'un à, and obliger quelqu'un à.
TL: Le guide aide les visiteurs à choisir.
EN: The guide helps the visitors choose.
SHOW: effort / progress / goal · aider / encourager / obliger + object → à + infinitive
Flip for the broad de family.

[SIDE]
TITLE: De belongs to many decisions, requests, and reactions
REF: 12.5

Deciding, refusing, trying, and stopping commonly select de. Other de frames introduce what someone is told, prevented, thanked, or blamed for doing.
TL: Nous décidons de rester; il refuse de répondre; elle essaie de comprendre.
EN: We decide to stay; he refuses to answer; she tries to understand.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Dites à Nora de patienter; empêchez Marc de sortir.
EN: Tell Nora to wait; prevent Marc from leaving.
[PAUSE:800]
Meaning narrows the search, but a memorized whole frame prevents analogy from choosing the wrong linker.
SHOW: décider / refuser / essayer / cesser · dire à qn / empêcher qn → de + infinitive

[CARD]
TITLE: Keep small words attached to the infinitive
REF: 12.1

Object and reflexive pronouns that belong to an ordinary infinitive normally stand immediately before it. Negation surrounds that pronoun-infinitive group with ne pas before the infinitive.
TL: Je vais le lui expliquer; Nora promet de ne pas se plaindre.
EN: I am going to explain it to him; Nora promises not to complain.
[PAUSE:900]
In a compound infinitive, negation and pronouns precede the auxiliary.
TL: Il regrette de ne pas l'avoir prévenue.
EN: He regrets not having warned her.
SHOW: linker + ne pas + pronoun + infinitive or infinitive auxiliary
Flip when the matrix object is deliberately left general.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A generic object can remain unspoken
REF: 12.6

When the omitted participant is indefinite and means people in general, French can leave the object of certain governing verbs unexpressed.
TL: Une première réussite encourage à continuer.
EN: An initial success encourages one to continue.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Cette expérience enseigne à rester prudent.
EN: This experience teaches one to remain cautious.
[PAUSE:800]
Do not omit a participant whose identity matters. Name that object or expand the thought into a finite clause.
SHOW: genuinely generic participant → object may be omitted

[CARD]
TITLE: An impersonal adjective takes de
REF: 12.7.1

When il or ce merely introduces an evaluation of an action, the adjective is impersonal and the infinitive takes de.
TL: Il est difficile de vérifier ces chiffres.
EN: It is difficult to check these figures.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: C'est agréable de marcher ici le matin.
EN: It is pleasant to walk here in the morning.
[PAUSE:800]
The real content being evaluated is the infinitive event, not a person or thing described by the adjective.
SHOW: impersonal il / ce + adjective + de + infinitive
Flip when the grammatical subject participates in the infinitive event.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A personal subject can switch the linker to à
REF: 12.7.2, 12.7.3, 12.7.4

With adjectives such as facile or difficile, a personal subject understood as the infinitive's object takes à.
TL: Ces chiffres sont difficiles à vérifier.
EN: These figures are difficult to check.
[PAUSE:800]
Other personal adjectives follow lexical groups: manner or readiness often takes à, while most remaining adjective complements take de.
TL: Nora est prête à partir; elle est heureuse de participer.
EN: Nora is ready to leave; she is happy to take part.
SHOW: infinitive's object as subject → à · prêt/disposé → à · heureux/fier/capable → de
Twelve clean adjective-linker choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Related nouns often inherit a preposition
REF: 12.8.1

A noun related to a verb or adjective often keeps the same infinitive linker. This gives useful families that can be learned together.
TL: inviter à participer → une invitation à participer
EN: to invite someone to participate → an invitation to participate
[PAUSE:800]
TL: désireux de réussir → le désir de réussir
EN: eager to succeed → the desire to succeed
[PAUSE:800]
Inheritance is a strong clue, not a license to guess when the expression is unfamiliar.
SHOW: related verb or adjective frame → related noun often keeps à or de
Flip for the relationship between a noun and its event.

[SIDE]
TITLE: À links a noun that participates in the event
REF: 12.8.2, 12.8.3, 12.8.4

Use à when the noun is understood as an object, instrument, occasion, or other participant in the infinitive event.
TL: un document à signer; une machine à réparer
EN: a document to sign; a machine to repair
[PAUSE:800]
Fixed expressions such as avoir du mal à also take à. Most other noun complements take de, including besoin de, envie de, occasion de, and manière de.
TL: Nous avons du mal à choisir; c'est l'occasion de recommencer.
EN: We are having difficulty choosing; it is an opportunity to begin again.
SHOW: noun involved in event → à · ordinary content or opportunity → often de
Fourteen clean noun-linker choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: An infinitive can fill a noun-like slot
REF: 12.9

An infinitive phrase can serve as the subject or complement of a sentence. French keeps the infinitive even where English naturally uses an -ing form.
TL: Attendre ne résoudra rien.
EN: Waiting will solve nothing.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Notre priorité est de protéger les données.
EN: Our priority is to protect the data.
[PAUSE:800]
The phrase can still contain objects and modifiers; only its verb lacks a finite person-and-tense anchor.
SHOW: infinitive phrase → subject or complement of a finite clause
Flip for a reduced time or circumstance relation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A preposition can open a reduced clause
REF: 12.1, 12.9

Avant de, après, and sans can introduce an infinitive phrase when its understood subject is recoverable from the main clause. Use a past infinitive after après to mark prior completion.
TL: Avant de répondre, Nora relit la question.
EN: Before answering, Nora rereads the question.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Après avoir répondu, elle ferme le dossier sans attendre.
EN: After answering, she closes the file without waiting.
[PAUSE:800]
If the reduced clause needs a different explicit subject, use an appropriate finite construction instead.
SHOW: same understood subject → preposition + infinitive clause

[CARD]
TITLE: The infinitive gives instructions to a general audience
REF: 12.10

Signs, recipes, labels, manuals, and exam directions often use the infinitive instead of an imperative. The missing addressee is anyone who reads the instruction.
TL: Vérifier l'adresse avant l'envoi. Appuyer sur le bouton vert.
EN: Check the address before sending. Press the green button.
[PAUSE:900]
This style is impersonal and compact. A direct command to a particular listener normally uses an imperative instead.
SHOW: non-specific written audience → infinitive instruction
Eight clean instruction completions follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Negate the instruction before the infinitive
REF: 12.10

Put ne pas before the infinitive for a negative public instruction. Object pronouns remain next to their infinitive.
TL: Ne pas ouvrir avant midi. Ne pas le retourner.
EN: Do not open before noon. Do not turn it over.
[PAUSE:800]
A sequence of infinitives can present a whole procedure without repeatedly naming the reader.
TL: Mélanger, couvrir, puis laisser refroidir.
EN: Mix, cover, then leave to cool.
[PAUSE:800]
Recognize the register: these forms are instructions, not dictionary labels or finite narrative verbs.
SHOW: ne pas + infinitive · ordered infinitives → impersonal procedure
