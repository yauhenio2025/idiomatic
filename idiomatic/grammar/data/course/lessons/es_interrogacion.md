## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Interrogative words keep their accent in every real question
REF: 28.1

Write an accent on an interrogative or exclamatory word whether the question is direct or embedded inside another clause.
TL: ¿Quién cerró la ventana?
EN: Who closed the window?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Averigua quién cerró la ventana.
EN: Find out who closed the window.
[PAUSE:1000]
The punctuation changes, but the stressed word and its accent do not.
SHOW: direct ¿quién ...? · indirect quién ... · accent retained
Flip to contrast these words with unstressed connectors.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An accent can change the grammatical job
REF: 28.1

Accented qué, cómo, cuándo, cuál, cuánto, dónde, and quién request missing information or add exclamatory force. Their unaccented counterparts link or modify a clause without asking for that information.
TL: Entendí qué proponían y confirmé que aceptaba.
EN: I understood what they were proposing and confirmed that I accepted.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Dime cómo lo resolviste; hazlo como prefieras.
EN: Tell me how you solved it; do it however you prefer.
[PAUSE:1000]
Hear the stress, then identify the function before choosing the spelling.
SHOW: qué asks · que connects · cómo asks · como links
Flip for the shape of a focused question.

[CARD]
TITLE: Build the question around the information gap
REF: 28, 28.2

First decide exactly what the answer should supply: a person, thing, place, time, cause, purpose, manner, or amount. Replace only that information with the matching interrogative phrase.
TL: Clara dejó el informe en recepción. ¿Dónde dejó Clara el informe?
EN: Clara left the report at reception. Where did Clara leave the report?
[PAUSE:900]
When the interrogative phrase opens the clause, the finite verb normally comes before an expressed subject.
SHOW: locate the answer → replace it → verb before expressed subject
Flip for prepositions and punctuation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Carry the preposition into the question
REF: 28, 28.2

If the missing element is governed by a preposition, keep that preposition before the interrogative word.
TL: Hablamos con la directora. ¿Con quién hablasteis?
EN: We spoke with the director. Who did you speak with?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: El artículo trata de la memoria. ¿De qué trata el artículo?
EN: The article is about memory. What is the article about?
[PAUSE:1000]
Opening marks begin where the question or exclamation begins, even when it sits inside a larger sentence.
TL: Si ya lo sabías, ¿por qué no avisaste?
EN: If you already knew, why did you not warn us?
SHOW: con quién · de qué · ¿ begins with the questioned span
Ten clean question-building drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Qué names a kind; cuál selects a member
REF: 28.3.1, 28.4.1

Use qué before a noun to ask what kind or which item is meant. Use cuál or cuáles as a pronoun when the answer selects from an understood set.
TL: ¿Qué ruta tomamos? ¿Cuál prefieres?
EN: Which route shall we take? Which one do you prefer?
[PAUSE:900]
A preposition stays before either form.
TL: ¿A qué propuesta te refieres? ¿A cuál de ellas te refieres?
EN: Which proposal do you mean? Which of them do you mean?
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: qué + noun · cuál solo · cuál de + set
Flip when the English prompt begins with what is.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Qué es asks for a definition; cuál es identifies a value
REF: 28.3.2, 28.4.1

Use qué es when the requested answer explains the nature or definition of something. Use cuál es when it identifies the relevant answer, reason, address, result, or other value.
TL: ¿Qué es la fotosíntesis?
EN: What is photosynthesis?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: ¿Cuál es la contraseña nueva?
EN: What is the new password?
[PAUSE:1000]
English what is does not decide the Spanish form by itself; the expected answer does.
SHOW: definition → qué es · identification → cuál es
Flip for regional use before a noun.

[CARD]
TITLE: The safest general pattern is qué plus noun
REF: 28.3.3, 28.4.1

Across the Spanish-speaking world, qué can directly modify a noun. In much of Latin America, cuál also appears before nouns when selecting among alternatives, but this is uncommon in European Spanish.
TL: ¿Qué modelo compraste?
EN: Which model did you buy?
[PAUSE:800]
For a broadly neutral contrast, keep qué before the noun or move cuál away from it.
TL: ¿Cuál de los modelos compraste?
EN: Which of the models did you buy?
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: neutral qué modelo · neutral cuál de los modelos · regional cuál modelo
Flip for people and agreement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quién points to people; cuál still agrees in number
REF: 28.3.1, 28.5

When the missing participant is a person, quién or quiénes is usually clearer than cuál. Preserve any governing preposition and choose singular or plural from the answer.
TL: ¿Quién coordina el proyecto? ¿Con quiénes se reunió?
EN: Who coordinates the project? Who did she meet with?
[PAUSE:900]
Cuál also has a plural when the selected answer contains more than one member.
TL: ¿Cuáles son los documentos obligatorios?
EN: Which documents are required?
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: person → quién/quiénes · selection → cuál/cuáles
Eleven clean qué-versus-cuál choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: An indirect question keeps the interrogative machinery
REF: 28, 28.1

An indirect question loses the question marks when the whole sentence is a statement, but its interrogative word stays accented.
TL: No recuerdo dónde aparcamos.
EN: I do not remember where we parked.
[PAUSE:800]
In standard Spanish, the question word does not itself trigger the subjunctive. Choose the verb form required by time and meaning.
TL: Me preguntaron cuándo llegaba la técnica.
EN: They asked me when the technician was arriving.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: no outer question marks · accent retained · mood follows meaning
Flip for qué and lo que.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Before an infinitive, use qué rather than lo que
REF: 28.4.2

With a finite verb, qué and lo que can both introduce an embedded object meaning what. Immediately before an infinitive, the standard compact pattern is qué plus infinitive.
TL: Entiendo qué buscas. Entiendo lo que buscas.
EN: I understand what you are looking for. I understand what you are looking for.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: No decidimos qué comprar.
EN: We did not decide what to buy.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not extend the longer form directly into the infinitive pattern.
SHOW: qué/lo que + finite verb · qué + infinitive
Flip for amount, duration, and degree.

[CARD]
TITLE: Cuánto changes with a noun but not as an adverb
REF: 28.6.1

As a pronoun or determiner, cuánto agrees with the quantity being counted. As an adverb measuring a verb, it remains cuánto.
TL: ¿Cuántas plazas quedan? ¿Cuánta harina necesitas?
EN: How many places remain? How much flour do you need?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: ¿Cuánto pesa la caja? ¿Cuánto tardará el trámite?
EN: How much does the box weigh? How long will the procedure take?
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: cuántos/cuántas + noun · cuánto + verb
Flip for dimensions and degree.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Spanish asks dimensions and degree with its own frames
REF: 28.6.2, 28.7, 8.2.2

For a measurement, use cuánto with the measuring verb or a phrase such as de ancho or de largo.
TL: ¿Cuánto mide de ancho el pasillo?
EN: How wide is the corridor?
[PAUSE:800]
For degree, cómo es de is widespread in Spain and the Southern Cone; qué tan is common across much of Latin America. Lo plus adjective plus que often expresses the same scale indirectly.
TL: ¿Cómo es de exigente el curso? No imaginaba lo exigente que era.
EN: How demanding is the course? I did not imagine how demanding it was.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: cuánto mide · cómo es de · qué tan · lo + adjective + que
Two clean multi-clause translations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Dónde locates; adónde highlights a destination
REF: 28.8, 28.9

Use dónde for location. With motion toward a destination, adónde makes the direction explicit, although dónde is also possible with a motion verb.
TL: ¿Dónde está el archivo? ¿Adónde llevaron las cajas?
EN: Where is the file? Where did they take the boxes?
[PAUSE:900]
Cuándo works in both direct and indirect questions and keeps its accent in either role.
TL: ¿Cuándo publicarán los resultados? Nadie sabe cuándo los publicarán.
EN: When will they publish the results? Nobody knows when they will publish them.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: location → dónde · destination → adónde/dónde · time → cuándo
Flip from time and place to cause and purpose.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Por qué seeks a cause; para qué seeks a purpose
REF: 28.10

Write por qué as two words with an accent when asking why. The answer connector porque is one unaccented word.
TL: ¿Por qué cancelaron la visita? Porque faltaba un permiso.
EN: Why did they cancel the visit? Because a permit was missing.
[PAUSE:900]
Use para qué when the expected answer gives an aim or intended use; para que introduces the purpose clause itself.
TL: ¿Para qué sirve esta palanca? Para que la puerta no se cierre.
EN: What is this lever for? So that the door does not close.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: cause → por qué / porque · purpose → para qué / para que
Flip for exclamatory force.

[CARD]
TITLE: Exclamations use the same accented words
REF: 28.1, 28.4.4

Qué can intensify an adjective or adverb, or introduce an exclamatory noun phrase. Before the noun, Spanish does not insert the indefinite article used by English what a.
TL: ¡Qué propuesta tan sensata! ¡Qué despacio avanza el trámite!
EN: What a sensible proposal! The procedure is moving so slowly!
[PAUSE:1000]
The opening exclamation mark begins exactly where the exclamatory span begins.
TL: Aunque era tarde, ¡qué animado seguía el público!
EN: Although it was late, the audience was still so lively!
SHOW: qué + noun without un/una · qué + adjective/adverb
Flip for other exclamatory scales.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Cuánto counts intensity; cómo presents the scene
REF: 28.6.1, 28.7

Exclamatory cuánto agrees when it modifies a noun and stays invariable when it measures a verb. Cómo can present the striking manner or development of an event.
TL: ¡Cuántas dudas surgieron! ¡Cuánto has cambiado!
EN: So many doubts arose! How much you have changed!
[PAUSE:900]
TL: ¡Cómo ha crecido el barrio!
EN: How the neighborhood has grown!
[PAUSE:1000]
These are genuine exclamatory uses, so the accent remains even without a request for an answer.
SHOW: ¡cuántas dudas! · ¡cuánto cambió! · ¡cómo creció!
The workbook's alternative-rich exclamation set is conservatively omitted.

[CARD]
TITLE: Prefer current neutral forms before marked alternatives
REF: 28.4.3, 28.6.2, 28.7

Some question forms carry regional or stylistic color. Cuán before an adjective survives mainly in elevated styles, while qué tan is especially common in much of Latin America.
TL: Me sorprendió lo lejos que quedaba la estación.
EN: I was surprised by how far away the station was.
[PAUSE:800]
For everyday conversation, qué tal asks about condition or character, and cómo politely requests a repetition.
TL: ¿Qué tal salió la entrevista? Perdona, ¿cómo dices?
EN: How did the interview go? Sorry, what did you say?
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose a form that fits the region, register, and kind of answer you need.
SHOW: neutral scale · regional option · formal option · conversational idiom
Flip for the final construction check.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Audit a question in four passes
REF: 28, 28.1, 28.2

First identify the missing information. Then choose the interrogative phrase and retain its preposition. Next arrange the clause around that focus. Finally check accents and the opening and closing marks.
TL: ¿Con cuántas personas habló la inspectora?
EN: How many people did the inspector speak with?
[PAUSE:900]
For an indirect question, perform the same checks but use punctuation for the whole sentence.
TL: Quisiera saber con cuántas personas habló la inspectora.
EN: I would like to know how many people the inspector spoke with.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: gap → word and preposition → clause order → accents and marks
Review the contrasts before returning to the drills.
