## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A preposition packages a relationship
REF: 13.1, 13.59

French chooses a preposition for the relationship between two elements: location, direction, source, duration, means, cause, or another link. An English preposition is therefore a prompt to identify the relationship, not a word to substitute mechanically.
TL: Nora est à Paris; elle travaille dans un laboratoire; elle part pour Lyon.
EN: Nora is in Paris; she works in a laboratory; she is leaving for Lyon.
[PAUSE:900]
English in and for do not uniquely determine the French forms. The type of place and the purpose or direction of the movement do.
SHOW: identify the relationship → choose the French construction
Flip for the contractions built into à and de.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Contract à and de before a definite article
REF: 13.2, 13.15

À plus le becomes au, and à plus les becomes aux. De plus le becomes du, and de plus les becomes des. There is no contraction before la or an elided l apostrophe.
TL: Nous allons au musée et aux archives; nous revenons du musée et des archives.
EN: We are going to the museum and the archives; we are coming back from the museum and the archives.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Elle reste à la gare; il sort de l'hôtel.
EN: She is staying at the station; he is leaving the hotel.
SHOW: à + le / les → au / aux · de + le / les → du / des

[CARD]
TITLE: Match a destination to its place name
REF: 13.2.2, 13.26.1

Use à with towns and cities. Use en with feminine countries and most countries beginning with a vowel, au with other masculine countries, and aux with plural country names.
TL: à Lille · en France · en Iran · au Japon · aux Pays-Bas
EN: in or to Lille, France, Iran, Japan, and the Netherlands
[PAUSE:1000]
Many regions and islands follow learned geographical patterns rather than one universal rule, so store unfamiliar names with their preposition.
SHOW: city → à · feminine or vowel country → en · masculine country → au · plural → aux
Flip to mark the same places as origins.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Origins reorganize the geographical pattern
REF: 13.15.1, 13.26.1

Use de before a city and before most feminine country names, du before a masculine country, and des before a plural country name. A vowel-initial country commonly uses d apostrophe.
TL: Elle vient de Lyon, de France, du Canada et des États-Unis.
EN: She comes from Lyon, France, Canada, and the United States.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Il revient d'Iran demain.
EN: He is returning from Iran tomorrow.
SHOW: origin → de / d' / du / des according to the place name

[CARD]
TITLE: À locates; dans contains
REF: 13.2.3, 13.14.1

À can give a simple location at an institution or familiar place. Dans presents something as inside a bounded space or a defined setting and normally introduces a determiner.
TL: Nora travaille au laboratoire; ses clés sont dans le laboratoire.
EN: Nora works at the laboratory; her keys are inside the laboratory.
[PAUSE:900]
The first clause identifies Nora's workplace. The second treats the laboratory as the area containing the keys.
SHOW: simple point or activity location → à · bounded interior → dans
Flip for names, streets, and specifically framed regions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Proper names and framed places need their own shapes
REF: 13.2.2, 13.14.1, 13.26.1

A named town normally takes à, while a named street can stand directly without a preposition after an address-like location. A region presented with a determiner or a historical description commonly takes dans.
TL: Elle habite à Nantes, rue des Carmes.
EN: She lives in Nantes, on rue des Carmes.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ce changement a commencé dans la France des années trente.
EN: This change began in the France of the nineteen thirties.
SHOW: city → à · named street → often zero · determined setting → dans

[CARD]
TITLE: Clock time, delay, and completion use different lenses
REF: 13.2.1, 13.14.3, 13.26.5

Use à for a clock time. Dans plus an interval places an event that far ahead of now. En plus an interval measures the time needed to complete an event.
TL: Le train part à neuf heures; il reviendra dans deux heures.
EN: The train leaves at nine; it will return in two hours.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nora a terminé le rapport en deux heures.
EN: Nora finished the report in two hours.
SHOW: clock point → à · event after interval → dans · completion within interval → en
Sixty clean choices among à, dans, de, and en follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Place calendar expressions in their learned frames
REF: 13.2.1, 13.14.3, 13.26.5

Months and years normally take en. Clock times take à, as do fixed phrases such as à l'aube and au printemps. Decades commonly take dans plus the plural article.
TL: en janvier · en 2026 · à l'aube · au printemps · dans les années quatre-vingt
EN: in January, in 2026, at dawn, in spring, in the nineteen eighties
[PAUSE:900]
These compact calendar frames are conventional chunks. Learn the whole expression rather than deriving every choice from English in.
SHOW: calendar noun + conventional frame → one stored time expression

[CARD]
TITLE: De links source, content, and defining relation
REF: 13.15

De can mark origin, possession, authorship, content, measure, material, or cause. The noun relationship determines the reading.
TL: un roman de Duras · une tasse de thé · une table de deux mètres
EN: a novel by Duras, a cup of tea, a table two metres long
[PAUSE:900]
With quantities, de also follows expressions such as plus de and moins de.
TL: Le trajet coûte moins de cinquante euros.
EN: The journey costs less than fifty euros.
SHOW: source / possessor / content / measure / cause → de
Flip for en as a material, state, or mode.

[SIDE]
TITLE: En presents a material, state, language, or transport mode
REF: 13.26

En can classify what something is made of, the state or role in which someone appears, the language used, or an unbounded mode of transport.
TL: une bague en argent · parler en français · voyager en train
EN: a silver ring, to speak in French, to travel by train
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il est venu en ami et il était en civil.
EN: He came as a friend and he was in civilian clothes.
[PAUSE:800]
Transport expressions are lexical: à pied and à cheval contrast with en train and en voiture.
SHOW: material / state / language / transport mode → often en

[CARD]
TITLE: Par traces a route, means, agent, or distribution
REF: 13.41

Par can mark passage through a route or opening, the means used, the agent of a passive, a cause, or a repeated distribution.
TL: entrer par la fenêtre · envoyer par courriel · un texte écrit par Nora
EN: to enter through the window, to send by email, a text written by Nora
[PAUSE:900]
TL: deux fois par semaine · un par un
EN: twice a week, one by one
SHOW: route / means / passive agent / distribution → par
Flip for the goal-oriented work done by pour.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Pour points toward a goal
REF: 13.47

Pour can identify a beneficiary, purpose, destination, intended duration, exchange, or viewpoint. It presents what an action is directed toward.
TL: Ce cadeau est pour Léa; nous partons pour Marseille.
EN: This gift is for Léa; we are leaving for Marseille.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il loue la voiture pour trois jours; pour moi, le prix est raisonnable.
EN: He is renting the car for three days; in my view, the price is reasonable.
[PAUSE:800]
Contrast par jour, a rate per day, with pour trois jours, an intended span.
SHOW: beneficiary / purpose / destination / intended span / viewpoint → pour

[CARD]
TITLE: Depuis connects a starting point to a continuing interval
REF: 13.19, 13.25, 13.46

Depuis gives the point or interval from which a situation continues. Pendant presents a bounded duration without that continuing link, while durant is its more formal relative.
TL: Nora travaille ici depuis lundi; elle a travaillé pendant six heures hier.
EN: Nora has worked here since Monday; she worked for six hours yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
French commonly uses a present with depuis when the situation still holds now.
SHOW: continuing since or for → depuis · bounded duration → pendant / durant
Flip for starting and ending boundaries.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mark the first point and the last point separately
REF: 13.21, 13.36, 13.39, 13.44

Dès means from as early as a stated point. À partir de establishes a starting point from which a rule or situation applies. Jusqu'à marks the endpoint. Lors de locates something on the occasion of an event.
TL: Dès demain, le bureau ouvrira de huit heures jusqu'à dix-huit heures.
EN: Starting tomorrow, the office will open from eight until six.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: À partir de lundi, ce tarif s'appliquera lors de chaque visite.
EN: From Monday onward, this rate will apply on every visit.
SHOW: earliest onset → dès · effective start → à partir de · endpoint → jusqu'à

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose between two points and a surrounding group
REF: 13.27, 13.42

Entre locates or relates items between distinct endpoints or members. Parmi selects or locates something within a larger group. D'entre combines de with entre when an item is selected from a defined set.
TL: La pharmacie est entre la gare et la mairie.
EN: The pharmacy is between the station and the town hall.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nora est parmi les invités; plusieurs d'entre eux la connaissent.
EN: Nora is among the guests; several of them know her.
SHOW: distinct endpoints or members → entre · within a group → parmi · selected from group → d'entre
Flip for the basic vertical and facing relations.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build a spatial map around the reference point
REF: 13.20, 13.22, 13.23, 13.24, 13.30, 13.53, 13.56

Sur and sous place something on or under a reference. Devant and derrière place it in front or behind. Au-dessus de and au-dessous de compare vertical position without requiring contact, while en face de marks an opposite-facing position.
TL: Le dossier est sur la table, sous la lampe et devant l'écran.
EN: The file is on the table, under the lamp, and in front of the screen.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Le café est en face de la gare; l'horloge est au-dessus de la porte.
EN: The café is opposite the station; the clock is above the door.
SHOW: contact / vertical level / front-back / facing relation → choose the matching spatial frame

[CARD]
TITLE: Compound prepositions preserve a precise shape
REF: 13.8, 13.12, 13.16, 13.17, 13.30, 13.34, 13.38, 13.48

Many precise relations are multiword units ending in de. Learn the whole unit and then contract de with any following definite article.
TL: à côté du bureau · au bout de la rue · le long des quais
EN: beside the office, at the end of the street, along the quays
[PAUSE:800]
TL: en dehors de la ville · au-delà des montagnes · près du pont
EN: outside the town, beyond the mountains, near the bridge
SHOW: fixed prepositional unit + de + noun phrase
Twenty clean choices from the wider inventory follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Across and through split by path and obstruction
REF: 13.41.1, 13.57

À travers presents movement across or through a space. En travers de places something crosswise, often as an obstruction. Par commonly marks the route or opening used.
TL: Nous marchons à travers les champs; un arbre est tombé en travers de la route.
EN: We are walking across the fields; a tree has fallen across the road.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La lumière entre par la fenêtre.
EN: The light comes in through the window.
SHOW: traversed space → à travers · crosswise obstacle → en travers de · route or opening → par

[CARD]
TITLE: Cause, help, and concession need distinct links
REF: 13.9, 13.18, 13.31, 13.33, 13.40, 13.54

À cause de identifies a cause, often an unwelcome one; grâce à presents enabling help or a welcome cause. Faute de means for lack of. Par suite de introduces a consequence-producing cause.
TL: Le vol est annulé à cause du brouillard; nous sommes arrivés grâce à Nora.
EN: The flight is cancelled because of the fog; we arrived thanks to Nora.
[PAUSE:900]
Malgré takes a noun directly, while en dépit de includes de.
TL: Malgré la pluie, le match continue; en dépit du retard, ils restent calmes.
EN: Despite the rain, the match continues; despite the delay, they remain calm.
SHOW: adverse cause → à cause de · enabling cause → grâce à · concession → malgré / en dépit de
Flip for relationships directed toward people.

[SIDE]
TITLE: People can be places, companions, or targets of attitude
REF: 13.7, 13.10, 13.28, 13.43, 13.49, 13.52

Chez locates someone at the home, premises, practice, or characteristic world of a person or group. Avec marks accompaniment or an instrument. Envers introduces the target of an attitude or behaviour.
TL: Je vais chez le médecin avec Nora.
EN: I am going to the doctor's with Nora.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il s'est montré patient envers les enfants.
EN: He was patient toward the children.
[PAUSE:800]
Selon introduces a source or standard, quant à shifts the topic, and de la part de identifies the person from whom a message or action comes.
SHOW: premises → chez · accompaniment → avec · attitude target → envers

[CARD]
TITLE: English at, in, and on branch by meaning
REF: 13.59

At may become à for a point or time, en for a state or field, dans for an interior, or chez for a person's premises. In likewise branches among en, dans, and à. On may be sur for a surface, à for a page or activity, or en in a fixed expression.
TL: à sept heures · fort en français · dans le tiroir · chez Nora
EN: at seven, good at French, in the drawer, at Nora's
[PAUSE:900]
TL: sur la table · à la page dix · en route
EN: on the table, on page ten, on the way
SHOW: English form → intended relationship → French frame
Flip for by, from, and to.

[SIDE]
TITLE: English by, from, and to also divide their work
REF: 13.59

By may be par for agent or means, de for authorship, or à for recognition by a feature. From may be de for origin, depuis for a continuing starting point, à partir de for an effective start, or d'après for a source of information.
TL: écrit par Nora · un livre de Camus · reconnaître quelqu'un à sa voix
EN: written by Nora, a book by Camus, to recognize someone by their voice
[PAUSE:800]
TL: venir de Lille · attendre depuis lundi · d'après ce rapport
EN: to come from Lille, to have waited since Monday, according to this report
SHOW: agent / author / clue · origin / continuing start / information source → different French forms

[CARD]
TITLE: English with and as ask what role the noun plays
REF: 13.7, 13.15.2, 13.26.8, 13.59

With may be avec for accompaniment or instrument, de for a filling, covering, or cause, à for a distinguishing feature, or en for a material. As may be en for a temporary role, comme for a comparison or accepted capacity, and en tant que for an explicit official capacity.
TL: venir avec Léa · trembler de froid · un homme à la barbe grise
EN: to come with Léa, to tremble with cold, a man with a grey beard
[PAUSE:800]
TL: travailler en interprète · parler en tant que présidente
EN: to work as an interpreter, to speak in one's capacity as president
SHOW: companion / cause / feature / material / role → choose by function
Flip for a final strategy that makes the system learnable.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Store prepositions as part of a phrase
REF: 13.1, 13.59

Productive spatial and temporal contrasts help you predict many choices, but lexical combinations still have to be learned intact. Store the governor, preposition, and complement together.
TL: penser à un problème · parler de son travail · compter sur un ami
EN: to think about a problem, to talk about one's work, to rely on a friend
[PAUSE:900]
When English offers one short preposition, ask what it means here, test the productive pattern, and then verify the complete French chunk.
SHOW: meaning first → productive contrast → stored lexical frame
Sixty-six clean English-to-French relationship choices follow.
