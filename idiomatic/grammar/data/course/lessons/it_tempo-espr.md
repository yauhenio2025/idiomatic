## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Give every time expression an anchor
REF: 21

Time expressions locate an event, measure its extent, or say how often it recurs. Before choosing a form, identify the anchor: the clock, the calendar, the speaking moment, or another event in the story.
TL: La scadenza cade venerdì, ma il lavoro durerà tutta la settimana.
EN: The deadline falls on Friday, but the work will last all week.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Tra due ore inizierà la seduta; per ora la sala è vuota.
EN: The session will begin in two hours; for now the room is empty.
[PAUSE:900]
Venerdì places a point on the calendar. Tutta la settimana measures a span. Tra due ore counts forward from now, while per ora describes the present situation.
SHOW: choose the anchor -> point, span, recurrence, or sequence
Flip to distinguish when from how long.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Separate location from duration
REF: 21.1, 21.2, 21.3

A normally introduces a clock time. Da and fino a mark the beginning and end of an interval. Per measures an intended or completed duration, while in can describe the time needed to complete something.
TL: L'incontro è alle nove e dura fino a mezzogiorno.
EN: The meeting is at nine and lasts until midday.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Abbiamo controllato tutti i fascicoli in tre ore.
EN: We checked all the files in three hours.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not translate an English time preposition in isolation. First decide whether it names a point, a boundary, elapsed time, or time taken to completion.
SHOW: at a point -> a · boundary -> da / fino a · duration -> per · completion time -> in

[CARD]
TITLE: Make the hour agree with ora
REF: 21.1

Italian treats clock hours as feminine because the understood noun is ora. One o'clock is singular, but every greater hour is plural. Minutes past follow e; minutes before follow meno.
TL: È l'una e un quarto.
EN: It is a quarter past one.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Sono le sette meno dieci.
EN: It is ten to seven.
[PAUSE:900]
The article and verb move together: è l'una, but sono le due. Half past may be e mezzo or e mezza, with mezza agreeing with the understood ora.
SHOW: one -> è l'una · two onward -> sono le · past -> e · to -> meno

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use a plus the hour for scheduled time
REF: 21.1

To say when something happens, combine a with the feminine article: all'una, alle due, alle ventuno. Mezzogiorno and mezzanotte normally stand after a without an article.
TL: Il collegamento inizierà alle ventuno e trenta.
EN: The connection will begin at nine thirty p.m.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il museo chiude a mezzogiorno e riapre alle quattordici.
EN: The museum closes at midday and reopens at two p.m.
[PAUSE:900]
Official schedules often use the twenty-four-hour clock and may insert ore. Everyday speech more often relies on context and the twelve-hour clock.
SHOW: all'una · alle otto · a mezzogiorno · alle ore ventuno

[CARD]
TITLE: Let the period of day resolve ambiguity
REF: 21.2

Mattina, pomeriggio, sera, and notte can locate a clock time or name a broader part of the day. Stasera points to this evening; stanotte foregrounds the night-time period and can refer to the night just passed or the one ahead, as context decides.
TL: Ci sentiamo domani mattina, non stasera.
EN: We will speak tomorrow morning, not this evening.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Stanotte ha nevicato, ma ieri sera il cielo era sereno.
EN: It snowed last night, but yesterday evening the sky was clear.
[PAUSE:900]
Italian boundaries between pomeriggio and sera vary by region. Use the conventional expression for the context rather than calculating a universal cut-off hour.
SHOW: this evening -> stasera · night-time focus -> stanotte · context fixes the day

[SIDE]
TITLE: A bare weekday names one occasion
REF: 21.2

A weekday without an article normally identifies the relevant single day. The definite article or di turns the same name into a habitual schedule.
TL: Venerdì presenterò il progetto.
EN: I will present the project on Friday.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il venerdì lavoro da casa.
EN: I work from home on Fridays.
[PAUSE:900]
All weekday names are masculine except domenica. The contrast is not singular versus plural: venerdì can mean one Friday, while il venerdì or di venerdì means Fridays as a recurring class.
SHOW: one relevant day -> venerdì · habitual day -> il venerdì / di venerdì

[CARD]
TITLE: Distinguish a period from the experience within it
REF: 21.2, 21.3

The basic nouns giorno, mattina, sera, and notte label calendar periods. Their forms in -ata often focus on the duration, events, or felt quality inside that period.
TL: Questa sera resteremo a casa.
EN: We will stay home this evening.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Abbiamo trascorso una serata piacevole con i vicini.
EN: We spent a pleasant evening with the neighbours.
[PAUSE:900]
That is why Buonasera is a greeting, while Buona serata wishes someone an enjoyable remainder of the evening. Annata can similarly evaluate a year as a season of activity or production.
SHOW: calendar label -> sera · experienced span -> serata

[SIDE]
TITLE: The -ata family is lexical, not automatic
REF: 21.3

Giornata, mattinata, serata, and nottata regularly highlight duration. Annata often describes a year's yield, quality, or organized activity, while mesata has the specialized meaning of a month's wages.
TL: Dopo una nottata di lavoro, hanno concluso la verifica in mattinata.
EN: After a night of work, they completed the review during the morning.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Per il vigneto è stata un'annata eccezionale.
EN: It was an exceptional year for the vineyard.
[PAUSE:900]
Treat the pattern as a meaning contrast, not permission to create any form in -ata. Each established noun carries its own range of uses.
SHOW: duration or lived content -> -ata · specialized meanings stay lexical

[CARD]
TITLE: Put months, seasons, and years in their frames
REF: 21.2

Months are masculine and normally follow in or a when they locate an event. Seasons have conventional forms: in primavera, d'estate, in autunno, and d'inverno. A named year takes the masculine article after a preposition.
TL: Il corso comincia in ottobre e termina a marzo.
EN: The course begins in October and ends in March.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: D'inverno il rifugio apre solo nei fine settimana.
EN: In winter the refuge opens only at weekends.
[PAUSE:900]
With a year, say nel 2018, not a bare translation of English in 2018. Decades are framed as negli anni Sessanta, with the decade commonly capitalized.
SHOW: month -> in / a · season -> conventional frame · year -> nel + number

[SIDE]
TITLE: Read centuries on the Italian scale
REF: 21.2

Italian can name a century with an ordinal plus secolo. From the thirteenth through the twentieth century, it can also name the hundreds contained in its dates.
TL: Il palazzo risale al Seicento.
EN: The building dates from the seventeenth century.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il ventunesimo secolo è iniziato nel 2001.
EN: The twenty-first century began in 2001.
[PAUSE:900]
Il Seicento refers to the years beginning with sixteen, so it corresponds to the seventeenth century. Keep this one-step offset explicit when moving between the two systems.
SHOW: il Seicento -> 1600s -> seventeenth century

[CARD]
TITLE: Build dates with article, day, month, year
REF: 21.4

An Italian date normally uses the masculine singular article, a cardinal day number, the month, and then the year. Only the first day of a month uses the ordinal primo.
TL: Oggi è il ventitré maggio.
EN: Today is the twenty-third of May.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il contratto entrerà in vigore il primo luglio 2027.
EN: The contract will enter into force on the first of July 2027.
[PAUSE:900]
The same form works after the verb and as an event date. Di may appear between the day and month, but it is not required.
SHOW: il + cardinal + month · exception -> il primo

[SIDE]
TITLE: Keep the year outside the month contraction
REF: 21.4

When a month is located inside a particular year, nel introduces the month, but the year does not take a second contracted preposition.
TL: La mostra aprirà nel maggio 2027.
EN: The exhibition will open in May 2027.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Spero di consegnare il rapporto prima del primo giugno.
EN: I hope to submit the report before the first of June.
[PAUSE:900]
Formal letter headings may show special conventions, but ordinary prose follows the core date frame. To ask the date, Qual è la data oggi? is transparent; Quanti ne abbiamo oggi? is idiomatic and elliptical.
SHOW: nel maggio 2027 · prima del primo giugno

[CARD]
TITLE: State age with avere, not essere
REF: 21.5

Italian says that a person, building, or institution has a number of years. Compiere presents the birthday or anniversary as the completion of that number.
TL: Mia nipote ha diciassette anni.
EN: My niece is seventeen years old.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il museo compirà cent'anni l'anno prossimo.
EN: The museum will turn one hundred next year.
[PAUSE:900]
With a preposition, age expressions often show the plural article, as in ai sessant'anni or tra i cinque e i sette anni.
SHOW: age as state -> avere X anni · reaching the age -> compiere X anni

[SIDE]
TITLE: Approximate or package an age
REF: 21.5

A numeral in -ina can indicate an approximate age: sulla quarantina, una ventina d'anni. The suffix -enne creates an adjective or noun for someone of a stated age, especially in journalistic language.
TL: Il nuovo responsabile è sulla quarantina.
EN: The new manager is about forty.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Una trentenne ha coordinato l'intervento.
EN: A thirty-year-old woman coordinated the operation.
[PAUSE:900]
The number usually loses its final vowel before -enne: trenta becomes trentenne. Learn irregular-looking boundaries as established forms rather than improvising them.
SHOW: approximate age -> sulla quarantina · age label -> trentenne
Eleven clean calendar, duration, date, and age choices follow; the malformed twelfth prompt is omitted.

[CARD]
TITLE: Count recurrence with volta or ogni
REF: 21.6

Volta counts repetitions. The period that contains them normally carries a definite article, optionally fused with a: due volte al giorno or due volte il giorno. Ogni directly introduces the interval between occurrences.
TL: Controlliamo i sensori due volte al giorno.
EN: We check the sensors twice a day.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il traghetto parte ogni quaranta minuti.
EN: The ferry leaves every forty minutes.
[PAUSE:900]
Choose the frame from the viewpoint: number of repetitions inside a period, or one event at each interval.
SHOW: repetitions per period -> volte al giorno · interval -> ogni quaranta minuti

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ask and alternate with ogni
REF: 21.6

Ogni quanto asks for the interval between recurrences. Alternation can be made explicit with un giorno sì e uno no or un giorno sì, l'altro no.
TL: Ogni quanto aggiorni il registro?
EN: How often do you update the log?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Vado in ufficio un giorno sì e uno no.
EN: I go to the office every other day.
[PAUSE:900]
English often compresses frequency into a modifier. Italian frequently spells out the repeated unit and the interval.
SHOW: how often? -> ogni quanto? · alternate days -> un giorno sì e uno no

[CARD]
TITLE: Anchor last and next to now or then
REF: 21.7

Scorso and prossimo normally look backward or forward from the speaking moment. Precedente, prima, successivo, and seguente relate one period to another reference point inside the narrative.
TL: La settimana scorsa abbiamo chiuso il bilancio.
EN: Last week we closed the accounts.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il mese prossimo cambieremo sede.
EN: Next month we will move offices.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not choose only from the English word. Ask whether the axis is now or a time already established in the discourse.
SHOW: relative to now -> scorso / prossimo · relative to then -> precedente / seguente

[SIDE]
TITLE: Keep ultimo separate from scorso
REF: 21.7

Ultimo identifies the final or latest member of a series. It does not simply replace scorso for the calendar period immediately before now.
TL: Dissero che li avevano incontrati la settimana precedente.
EN: They said that they had met them the week before.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: L'ultimo treno è partito alle ventitré.
EN: The last train left at eleven p.m.
[PAUSE:900]
The first sentence is anchored to the time of saying, not to the present. The second selects the final train in the day's sequence.
SHOW: previous relative period -> precedente · final or latest member -> ultimo

[CARD]
TITLE: Let tense resolve deictic expressions
REF: 21.2, 21.7

Expressions built with questo stay close to the speaking moment but can point backward or forward. Verb tense and context resolve the direction. L'altro giorno points vaguely to a recent day other than yesterday.
TL: Quest'inverno abbiamo consumato meno energia.
EN: This winter we used less energy.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Quest'estate lavoreremo da remoto.
EN: This summer we will work remotely.
[PAUSE:900]
L'altr'anno may mean last year or the year before last, so add context when precision matters.
SHOW: questo = near now · tense chooses backward or forward · altro stays deliberately loose

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mark the temporal viewpoint explicitly
REF: 21.7

Adverbs such as attualmente, precedentemente, and successivamente state where an event sits on the discourse timeline. The invariable adjective allora identifies a role held at that earlier time.
TL: Attualmente non accettiamo nuove richieste.
EN: At present we are not accepting new requests.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: L'allora direttrice aveva già segnalato il rischio.
EN: The then director had already reported the risk.
[PAUSE:900]
Formal Italian can place fu before a name or title to mean late or deceased. Treat it as a marked register choice, not as the ordinary past tense inside the phrase.
SHOW: current viewpoint -> attualmente · narrative sequence -> precedentemente / successivamente · former role -> allora

[CARD]
TITLE: Compose time expressions from the outside in
REF: 21.1, 21.2, 21.4, 21.6, 21.7

Complex schedules become manageable when you establish the reference period first, then the date or day, then the clock time, and finally any frequency or sequence relation.
TL: Il gruppo si riunirà il primo lunedì del mese prossimo.
EN: The group will meet on the first Monday of next month.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Arriviamo venerdì alle diciotto e ripartiamo il lunedì successivo.
EN: We arrive on Friday at six p.m. and leave again the following Monday.
[PAUSE:900]
Each element answers a different question. Keeping those layers separate prevents article, preposition, and reference-point errors.
SHOW: reference period -> day or date -> clock time -> recurrence or sequence

[SIDE]
TITLE: Run the four-question time check
REF: 21.1, 21.2, 21.3, 21.4, 21.5, 21.6, 21.7

Ask four questions: What is the anchor? Is this a point or a span? Does it happen once or recur? Is the reference point now or then?
TL: Per tre giorni controlleremo i dati due volte al giorno.
EN: For three days we will check the data twice a day.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Fra una settimana ne discuteremo un'altra volta.
EN: In a week we will discuss it another time.
[PAUSE:900]
The first sentence combines duration with frequency. The second combines a future offset with a separate repetition. Once the functions are clear, the Italian frame follows.
SHOW: anchor + extent + recurrence + viewpoint
Twelve Italian-production drills now combine frequency with past, next, and narrative-relative time.
