## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Name the relation before choosing the link
REF: 19

Italian connectors package a logical or conversational relation. Addition, correction, reason, purpose, result, condition, concession, and time each ask a different question. Start with the intended relation rather than an English word.
TL: Il rapporto è breve, ma documenta tutti i passaggi.
EN: The report is short, but it documents every step.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il rapporto è breve perché elimina le ripetizioni.
EN: The report is short because it removes repetition.
[PAUSE:900]
The first link contrasts two facts. The second supplies a cause. The clauses may look similar, but the connector changes the argument.
SHOW: meaning first -> clause relation -> connector
Flip to add mood, subject, and register to the decision.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A connector controls more than vocabulary
REF: 19, 19.12, 19.14, 19.16, 19.18

After identifying the relation, check whether the clauses share a subject, whether the link selects the indicative or subjunctive, and whether it belongs to ordinary speech or formal writing.
TL: Ho telefonato per chiarire il malinteso.
EN: I called in order to clear up the misunderstanding.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ho telefonato affinché tutti capissero la modifica.
EN: I called so that everyone would understand the change.
[PAUSE:900]
The shared subject favors per plus infinitive. A different expressed subject requires a finite purpose clause, here with the subjunctive.
SHOW: relation + subject + mood + register

[CARD]
TITLE: Coordinate additions and alternatives symmetrically
REF: 19.1, 19.2, 19.3, 19.4, 19.6, 19.7

E joins words, phrases, or complete clauses. Anche and pure add or emphasize an element, while formal nonché can add the last member of a list. Correlative links make the balance explicit.
TL: Il comitato ha esaminato sia i costi sia i benefici.
EN: The committee examined both the costs and the benefits.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Puoi inviare il modulo oggi oppure consegnarlo domani.
EN: You can send the form today or hand it in tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Keep the linked elements grammatically parallel. Common paired frames include e ... e, sia ... sia or sia ... che, tanto ... quanto, and o ... o.
SHOW: addition -> e / anche / pure · balanced pair -> sia ... sia · alternative -> o / oppure

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build negative coordination with né
REF: 19.5, 19.6, 19.7

Né coordinates rejected alternatives. It can link clauses, noun phrases, adjectives, or adverbs, and it often appears inside a larger negative frame.
TL: Non abbiamo né approvato né respinto la proposta.
EN: We have neither approved nor rejected the proposal.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Né Marta né Luca hanno ricevuto l'avviso.
EN: Neither Marta nor Luca received the notice.
[PAUSE:900]
Use o or oppure for open alternatives. Use né when the alternatives are all inside the scope of negation; after senza, Italian often uses né where English may use or.
SHOW: open alternatives -> o ... o · all rejected -> né ... né

[CARD]
TITLE: Contrast, correction, and reversal are different
REF: 19.8

Ma presents a broad contrast. Bensì replaces something explicitly denied, so it cannot normally stand alone at the start of a new sentence. Anzi goes further: it corrects or strengthens the preceding claim.
TL: Il progetto non è stato cancellato, bensì rinviato.
EN: The project was not cancelled but postponed.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La verifica non sarà breve; anzi, richiederà due giorni.
EN: The review will not be brief; on the contrary, it will require two days.
[PAUSE:900]
Ask whether both statements remain true, whether the second replaces a rejected description, or whether it reverses the direction of the first.
SHOW: contrast -> ma · correction after negation -> bensì · stronger reversal -> anzi

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mark an unmet expectation or a parallel contrast
REF: 19.8

Eppure, però, tuttavia, and nondimeno signal that the second fact survives despite an expectation raised by the first. Invece commonly introduces the contrasting case or replacement, while mentre can place two situations side by side.
TL: Avevamo controllato ogni dato; eppure mancava un allegato.
EN: We had checked every datum, yet an attachment was missing.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Giulia preferisce discutere subito; Marco, invece, vuole aspettare.
EN: Giulia prefers to discuss it at once; Marco, by contrast, wants to wait.
[PAUSE:900]
Però is mobile and often follows the constituent it qualifies. Mentre is ambiguous between temporal overlap and contrast, so context must supply the intended relation.
SHOW: expectation defeated -> eppure / però / tuttavia · contrasted case -> invece / mentre

[CARD]
TITLE: Reformulate, identify, or confirm
REF: 19.6, 19.9

Cioè, ossia, and vale a dire restate or identify preceding material. Cioè is the everyday choice and can also function as a hesitation marker; ossia and vale a dire are more at home in explanatory prose.
TL: Serve una copia autenticata, cioè una copia con valore ufficiale.
EN: An authenticated copy is required, that is, a copy with official validity.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il termine è perentorio, vale a dire che non può essere prorogato.
EN: The deadline is mandatory; in other words, it cannot be extended.
[PAUSE:900]
Ovvero can mean either or or introduce a formal paraphrase. Decide from whether it opens an alternative or renames the same content.
SHOW: restatement -> cioè / ossia / vale a dire · formal alternative or gloss -> ovvero

[SIDE]
TITLE: Infatti confirms; in realtà corrects
REF: 19.9

Infatti supports or confirms an expectation already created. It does not normally contradict the preceding claim, so translating it mechanically as English in fact is risky.
TL: Il server è di nuovo disponibile; infatti, tutti i controlli ora passano.
EN: The server is available again; indeed, all the checks now pass.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Sembrava un errore di rete; in realtà mancava una credenziale.
EN: It seemed to be a network error; in fact, a credential was missing.
[PAUSE:900]
In effetti can acknowledge what is genuinely the case. Invero also confirms, but belongs to an elevated or old-fashioned register.
SHOW: evidence or confirmation -> infatti · expectation corrected -> in realtà / invece

[CARD]
TITLE: Draw a conclusion without confusing it with cause
REF: 19.10

Dunque, quindi, perciò, and pertanto present a consequence or conclusion from what precedes. Pertanto is especially formal. Per cui commonly begins a result clause in informal discourse.
TL: Il termine è scaduto; pertanto la domanda non è ammissibile.
EN: The deadline has expired; therefore the application is inadmissible.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il treno è stato soppresso, per cui arriveremo domani.
EN: The train was cancelled, so we will arrive tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Cause points backward to a reason; a conclusive connector moves forward to what follows from it.
SHOW: reason -> claim · claim -> conclusion
Eighteen hygiene-gated reformulation and conclusion choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Guide the next move in the discourse
REF: 19.10

Allora can introduce a consequence or the next practical step. Ebbene frames a response or resolution; ora moves an argument or narrative onward. Ecco che presents an outcome as emerging, often with little surprise.
TL: Abbiamo tutti i documenti. Allora possiamo iniziare la verifica.
EN: We have all the documents. So we can begin the review.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ebbene, dopo tre revisioni il testo è pronto.
EN: Well then, after three revisions the text is ready.
[PAUSE:900]
These forms can organize conversation as well as encode strict logic. Listen for whether the speaker is concluding, resuming, or opening the next stage.
SHOW: conclusion · transition · narrative next step

[CARD]
TITLE: Keep cause, purpose, and result in separate lanes
REF: 19.13, 19.14, 19.17

A causal clause explains why an event holds. Perché commonly follows the main clause, while siccome, dato che, visto che, and poiché often present a reason as background before it.
TL: Abbiamo rinviato la riunione perché mancavano due relazioni.
EN: We postponed the meeting because two reports were missing.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Siccome mancavano due relazioni, abbiamo rinviato la riunione.
EN: Since two reports were missing, we postponed the meeting.
[PAUSE:900]
Poiché and giacché favor writing. In everyday commands, che can introduce the practical reason: Vieni, che dobbiamo parlare.
SHOW: new answer to why? -> perché · background reason -> siccome / dato che / poiché

[SIDE]
TITLE: Purpose is intended; result is observed
REF: 19.14, 19.17

Purpose names an intended outcome. With the same subject, use per plus infinitive; with a different subject, perché or affinché introduces a subjunctive clause. Result reports what actually followed.
TL: Ho semplificato il grafico per rendere chiaro il confronto.
EN: I simplified the chart in order to make the comparison clear.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ho ingrandito il testo affinché tutti potessero leggerlo.
EN: I enlarged the text so that everyone could read it.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il testo era così piccolo che nessuno riusciva a leggerlo.
EN: The text was so small that nobody could read it.
[PAUSE:900]
Result frames include così or tanto ... che, talmente ... che, cosicché, sicché, and da plus infinitive when the subjects match.
SHOW: intended outcome -> purpose · actual consequence -> result

[CARD]
TITLE: A condition opens a possibility; a concession resists it
REF: 19.11, 19.12, 19.16

Se gives the general conditional frame. Purché, sempre che, a patto che, and a condizione che narrow it to a requirement and take the subjunctive.
TL: Ti presto il fascicolo, purché tu lo restituisca domani.
EN: I will lend you the file, provided that you return it tomorrow.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Possiamo partire alle otto, sempre che il tempo migliori.
EN: We can leave at eight, assuming the weather improves.
[PAUSE:900]
Purché often presents the minimum condition under someone's control. Sempre che adds reservation; a patto che sounds negotiated, and a condizione che makes the requirement firmer.
SHOW: ordinary hypothesis -> se · restrictive requirement -> purché / sempre che / a patto che
Seven hygiene-gated condition-versus-concession choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Concession preserves the main claim
REF: 19.16

A concessive clause acknowledges a fact that fails to cancel the main proposition. Benché, sebbene, ancorché, and nonostante che normally select the subjunctive; anche se readily introduces a finite clause in ordinary speech.
TL: Benché il termine sia breve, completeremo il lavoro.
EN: Although the deadline is short, we will complete the work.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Pur conoscendo il rischio, hanno approvato il piano.
EN: Although they knew the risk, they approved the plan.
[PAUSE:900]
Pur and seppure can introduce a gerund or participle. A costo di plus infinitive says that the main action will proceed even at a stated cost.
SHOW: conceded fact -> main claim still holds

[CARD]
TITLE: Time links encode boundaries and overlap
REF: 19.18

Quando locates an event; ogni volta che marks recurrence, and se can mean whenever in habitual contexts. Mentre marks overlap, while man mano che expresses gradual parallel development.
TL: Ogni volta che aggiorniamo il sistema, salviamo una copia.
EN: Whenever we update the system, we save a copy.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Man mano che arrivavano i dati, il quadro diventava più chiaro.
EN: As the data came in, the picture became clearer.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not confuse temporal se with a hypothetical condition: a repeated, factual pattern normally uses the indicative.
SHOW: occurrence -> quando · recurrence -> ogni volta che / se · overlap -> mentre · progression -> man mano che
Nine clean source time choices follow; punctuation-only and merged-answer defects are omitted.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Match the connector to the subject and boundary
REF: 19.18

Before takes prima che plus subjunctive with a separate subject, or prima di plus infinitive when the subject is understood. After uses dopo che plus a finite clause or dopo or dopo di plus a compound infinitive.
TL: Chiudi il file prima che inizi l'aggiornamento.
EN: Close the file before the update begins.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Dopo aver verificato la firma, abbiamo archiviato il modulo.
EN: After checking the signature, we filed the form.
[PAUSE:900]
Finché non marks the endpoint up to which something continues; finché without non can mean for as long as. Appena marks immediate succession, and da quando supplies a starting point.
SHOW: before -> prima che / prima di · endpoint -> finché non · duration -> finché · since -> da quando

[CARD]
TITLE: Discourse markers manage the conversation
REF: 19.19

Discourse markers organize stance, attention, topic, and response rather than merely linking propositions. They are commonly separated by a pause or comma.
TL: A proposito, hai ricevuto la versione corretta?
EN: By the way, did you receive the corrected version?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Guarda, il problema non è il costo, ma il tempo.
EN: Look, the problem is not the cost but the time.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Insomma, dobbiamo scegliere entro stasera.
EN: In short, we must choose by this evening.
[PAUSE:900]
Guarda or senti draws attention, a proposito changes topic, appunto confirms, and insomma summarizes or manages impatience.
SHOW: attention · topic shift · confirmation · summary

[SIDE]
TITLE: Interjections encode stance, not a fixed proposition
REF: 19.19, 19.20

Beh can soften an answer or open a conclusion. Mah and boh signal doubt or uncertainty, while ahi marks pain and accidenti surprise or frustration. Peccato evaluates an outcome as regrettable.
TL: Beh, possiamo riprovare domani.
EN: Well, we can try again tomorrow.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Boh, nessuno mi ha spiegato il cambiamento.
EN: Who knows; nobody explained the change to me.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Accidenti, ho allegato il documento sbagliato!
EN: Damn, I attached the wrong document!
[PAUSE:900]
Register, intonation, and context carry much of the meaning. Treat an interjection as a speech action, not as a word with one permanent translation.
SHOW: beh -> response · mah/boh -> uncertainty · ahi -> pain · accidenti -> surprise or irritation

[CARD]
TITLE: Run a five-question connector check
REF: 19.1, 19.20

First, name the relation: addition, alternative, correction, reason, purpose, result, condition, concession, or time. Second, decide whether the link coordinates or subordinates. Third, compare the subjects. Fourth, select the required mood. Fifth, check register and discourse force.
TL: Poiché il dato è incompleto, rinvieremo la decisione.
EN: Since the datum is incomplete, we will postpone the decision.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Rinvieremo la decisione affinché il gruppo possa verificare il dato.
EN: We will postpone the decision so that the group can verify the datum.
[PAUSE:900]
The first subordinate clause supplies a reason with the indicative. The second supplies an intended outcome with the subjunctive.
SHOW: relation -> clause type -> subject -> mood -> register

[SIDE]
TITLE: Resolve ambiguous English links by logic
REF: 19.8, 19.9, 19.10, 19.13, 19.14, 19.17

English but may become ma, bensì, invece, eppure, or anzi. So may announce a result, a conclusion, or merely the next conversational move. Because and so that separate cause from purpose.
TL: Non è un riassunto, bensì una valutazione.
EN: It is not a summary but an assessment.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La valutazione è completa, quindi possiamo pubblicarla.
EN: The assessment is complete, so we can publish it.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La pubblichiamo perché tutti conoscano il risultato.
EN: We are publishing it so that everyone knows the result.
[PAUSE:900]
Translate the relation and the grammar together. A connector is correct only when its logic, clause shape, mood, and register all fit.
SHOW: do not translate the link alone
