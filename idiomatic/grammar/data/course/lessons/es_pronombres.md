## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A subject pronoun is a discourse signal
REF: 12.2.1

Spanish verb endings usually identify the subject, so an ordinary sequence does not repeat yo, él or ella at every turn. Omission is the unmarked choice when the same subject remains in focus.
TL: La directora presentó el informe y respondió a todas las preguntas.
EN: The director presented the report and answered every question.
[PAUSE:1000]
Add the pronoun when speakers or viewpoints contrast, or when the subject itself is the point of the message.
TL: Marta revisará el contrato; yo prepararé el anexo.
EN: Marta will review the contract; I will prepare the appendix.
SHOW: recoverable subject → omit · contrast or emphasis → state the pronoun
Flip for the places where the pronoun carries information.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Identification and ambiguity can require the subject
REF: 12.2.1

A pronoun stands alone in an identifying answer and follows ser in the pattern soy yo, eres tú, es ella.
TL: —¿Quién autorizó el cambio? —Yo.
EN: Who authorized the change? I did.
[PAUSE:900]
It can also disambiguate verb forms whose endings do not distinguish first from third person, especially in imperfect and subjunctive contexts.
TL: El comité pidió que yo presentara los datos y que ella explicara el método.
EN: The committee asked me to present the data and her to explain the method.
[PAUSE:1000]
An unnecessary pronoun can imply a contrast the speaker did not intend. Treat presence and absence as meaningful choices.
SHOW: standalone answer · contrast · emphasis · ambiguous ending

[CARD]
TITLE: Address forms are a regional social system
REF: 12.3.2, 12.3.3, 12.3.4

Tú is the familiar singular form across much of the Spanish-speaking world. Usted marks polite or social distance and always selects third-person grammar.
TL: Tú conoces el procedimiento, pero usted puede solicitar una explicación formal.
EN: You know the procedure, but you may request a formal explanation.
[PAUSE:1000]
In Spain, vosotros is the familiar plural and ustedes is formal. In Latin America, ustedes normally serves as the everyday plural regardless of familiarity.
TL: Vosotros podéis esperar aquí; ustedes pueden pasar a la sala siguiente.
EN: You can wait here; you can go through to the next room.
SHOW: familiar singular: tú · polite singular: usted · plural: vosotros in Spain, ustedes across Latin America
Flip for voseo.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Vos changes the pronoun-and-verb package
REF: 12.3.1

In voseo regions, vos replaces familiar tú. Its object form is te, its possessive is tu, and its prepositional form remains vos.
TL: Vos tenés razón, pero no te das cuenta de que estoy hablando de tu propuesta.
EN: You are right, but you do not realize that I am talking about your proposal.
[PAUSE:1000]
Verb forms vary geographically; the Buenos Aires pattern is prominent but not universal. Keep one regional system coherent rather than mixing vos with unrelated familiar forms.
TL: Si vos querés, hablamos mañana con más calma.
EN: If you like, we can talk more calmly tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
The Peninsular counterpart would use tú quieres. Register and relationship matter as much as morphology.
SHOW: vos + regional verb · object te · possessive tu · after a preposition vos

[CARD]
TITLE: Most prepositions select a dedicated pronoun form
REF: 13.1

After an ordinary preposition, yo becomes mí and tú becomes ti. Mí carries an accent; ti does not. The remaining personal pronouns normally keep their subject forms.
TL: Guardaron una copia para mí y otra para ti.
EN: They kept one copy for me and another for you.
[PAUSE:900]
Repeat the preposition before coordinated pronouns when each is its complement.
TL: Prepararon credenciales para ella y para nosotros.
EN: They prepared credentials for her and for us.
[PAUSE:1000]
In voseo, vos itself follows the preposition.
TL: Dejaron un mensaje para vos en recepción.
EN: They left a message for you at reception.
SHOW: yo → mí · tú → ti · vos → vos · other forms remain visible
Flip for a small but important exception class.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Entre, excepto and según take subject forms
REF: 13.1

Entre, excepto, salvo, menos, incluso, según, and hasta meaning even select ordinary subject forms rather than mí or ti.
TL: Esto queda entre tú y yo.
EN: This stays between you and me.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Según tú, todos aceptarán la propuesta menos yo.
EN: According to you, everyone will accept the proposal except me.
[PAUSE:1000]
When excepto or incluso is followed by a separate preposition such as a, that new preposition governs its own pronoun.
TL: Excepto a ti, no dejaron entrar a nadie.
EN: Except for you, they did not let anyone in.
SHOW: entre tú y yo · según tú · menos yo · but excepto a ti
Ten prepositional-form corrections follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Con fuses with the first two singular pronouns
REF: 13.2

Use conmigo and contigo, never con mí or con ti in standard Spanish.
TL: Si quieres revisar las cifras, ven conmigo.
EN: If you want to review the figures, come with me.
[PAUSE:900]
Voseo keeps the analytic form con vos. Other personal pronouns also remain separate after con.
TL: Clara habló con él y después discutió el asunto con vos.
EN: Clara spoke with him and then discussed the matter with you.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not generalize the fusion beyond the forms that license it.
SHOW: con + mí → conmigo · con + ti → contigo · con vos · con él
Flip for the reflexive member of the same family.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Sí and consigo point back to the subject
REF: 13.3

Sí is the third-person reflexive form after a preposition; consigo is its fused form after con. Mismo often makes the reflexive link explicit.
TL: La investigadora guardó una copia para sí misma y llevó el original consigo.
EN: The researcher kept a copy for herself and took the original with her.
[PAUSE:1000]
For reciprocal reference, entre sí is compact, while entre ellos or entre ellas can be more natural when specific people are already established.
TL: Los socios discutieron entre sí antes de votar.
EN: The partners discussed the matter among themselves before voting.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use a nonreflexive form when the complement points to someone other than the subject.
SHOW: reflexive after a preposition: sí · after con: consigo · nonreflexive: él/ella
Ten con-form decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Object clitics encode participants, not English cases
REF: 14.1, 14.2.1, 14.2.2

The unstressed series is me, te, lo or la or le, nos, os, los or las or les. First- and second-person forms do not distinguish direct from indirect objects.
TL: La directora me vio y luego me envió el informe.
EN: The director saw me and then sent me the report.
[PAUSE:1000]
Spanish indirect objects can receive, lose, or simply be affected by something. The verb and context determine the relationship that the clitic leaves unstated.
TL: Les retiraron el permiso por incumplir las condiciones.
EN: Their permit was withdrawn for failing to comply with the conditions.
SHOW: me/te/nos/os = role from context · third person adds lo/la versus le
Flip for the third-person baseline.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Direct and indirect roles divide the third person
REF: 14.2.2, 14.2.3, 15.2, 15.3, 15.4

As a dependable international baseline, use lo, la, los and las for direct objects, with gender and number agreement. Use le and les for indirect objects.
TL: Vi a Lucía, la saludé y después le entregué las llaves.
EN: I saw Lucía, greeted her, and then handed her the keys.
[PAUSE:1000]
Usted and ustedes use third-person object forms even though they address the listener.
TL: Señora, la llamaré mañana y le explicaré el procedimiento.
EN: Madam, I will call you tomorrow and explain the procedure to you.
[PAUSE:1000]
The personal a does not by itself make an object indirect; the verb still controls the role.
SHOW: direct: lo/la/los/las · indirect: le/les · usted uses third-person forms
Ten object replacements follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: A clitic cluster has one fixed internal order
REF: 14.2.4

When several unstressed pronouns meet, their order is rigid: se first, then te or os, then me or nos, with a third-person object last.
TL: No se me ocurrió contártelo antes.
EN: It did not occur to me to tell you earlier.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Te lo enviaré en cuanto nos lo confirmen.
EN: I will send it to you as soon as they confirm it to us.
[PAUSE:1000]
The meanings can be dense, but the cluster is indivisible. Conjunctions cannot join clitics, and an adverb cannot split them from a finite verb.
SHOW: se → te/os → me/nos → le/lo/la/les/los/las
Flip for the strongest repair rule in the system.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Le or les becomes se before an l-clitic
REF: 14.9.1, 14.9.2

Spanish forbids adjacent third-person clitics beginning with l. Before lo, la, los or las, change le or les to se.
TL: Le expliqué la decisión y luego se la expliqué a sus colegas.
EN: I explained the decision to him and then explained it to his colleagues.
[PAUSE:1000]
Because se hides the recipient's number and identity, add a él, a ella, a usted, a ellos or a ustedes when context is not enough.
TL: Se lo enviamos a ustedes ayer.
EN: We sent it to you yesterday.
[PAUSE:1000]
Colloquial se los dije for standard se lo dije a ellos is widespread in Latin America, but the internationally safe agreement follows the thing said.
SHOW: le/les + lo/la/los/las → se + lo/la/los/las
Eight clitic-order repairs follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Finite verbs attract clitics to their left
REF: 14.3.1, 14.3.2

With an ordinary finite verb, place the whole clitic cluster immediately before the verb. In a compound tense, it precedes haber, not the participle.
TL: Todavía no se los hemos enviado.
EN: We still have not sent them to you.
[PAUSE:900]
An affirmative imperative reverses the direction and attaches the cluster. A negative imperative keeps it before the verb.
TL: Envíamelo hoy, pero no me lo envíes sin revisar.
EN: Send it to me today, but do not send it to me without checking it.
[PAUSE:1000]
Attachment may require a written accent to preserve the original stress.
SHOW: finite: me lo envías · affirmative command: envíamelo · negative: no me lo envíes
Flip for infinitives, gerunds and participles.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Nonfinite forms do not all behave alike
REF: 14.3.3, 14.3.6, 14.3.7

An independent infinitive takes attached pronouns. A gerund normally does too, though a progressive construction can place the cluster before its finite auxiliary.
TL: Estoy explicándotelo con detalle; también te lo estoy mostrando por escrito.
EN: I am explaining it to you in detail; I am also showing it to you in writing.
[PAUSE:1000]
A past participle never hosts modern object clitics. In a compound tense, they stand before haber.
TL: Ya se lo habían explicado dos veces.
EN: They had already explained it to him twice.
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat the cluster as one unit wherever it lands.
SHOW: infinitive: explicártelo · gerund: explicándotelo · perfect: te lo he explicado
Ten imperative pairs follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Pronoun climbing offers two equivalent positions
REF: 14.3.3, 14.3.4

With many finite-verb plus infinitive combinations, the clitic can attach to the infinitive or climb before the finite verb. Wanting, being able, having to, and going to commonly allow both patterns.
TL: Quiero contártelo hoy; te lo quiero contar antes de la reunión.
EN: I want to tell it to you today; I want to tell you before the meeting.
[PAUSE:1000]
All the clitics move together. Never strand one on each verb.
TL: Tenemos que devolvérselas o se las tenemos que devolver.
EN: We have to return them to her.
SHOW: quiero decírtelo = te lo quiero decir · move the whole cluster
Flip for the boundaries of climbing.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Syntax can block the climb
REF: 14.3.5

If the finite verb already owns a clitic, do not pull the infinitive's object into that cluster. Keep each pronoun with the predicate that licenses it.
TL: Me interesa leerlo antes de decidir.
EN: I am interested in reading it before deciding.
[PAUSE:900]
An intervening negation or other independent word can also force the clitic to stay attached to the infinitive. Hay que and parecer do not license climbing in careful usage.
TL: Prefiero no comentarlo todavía, pero hay que corregirlo hoy.
EN: I prefer not to comment on it yet, but it has to be corrected today.
[PAUSE:1000]
A positive command likewise keeps the infinitive's pronoun attached.
TL: Procura recordarlo mañana.
EN: Try to remember it tomorrow.
SHOW: licensed verbal unit → optional climb · separate predicate or intervening word → no climb
Ten controlled clitic movements follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: An affected-person clitic adds a viewpoint
REF: 14.8

Spanish can mark someone emotionally or practically affected by an event even when English has no direct equivalent. Accidents and unwanted changes often use se plus this affected person.
TL: Se me averió el coche justo antes de la entrevista.
EN: My car broke down on me just before the interview.
[PAUSE:900]
The construction presents the event as happening within that person's sphere rather than as a deliberate action by them.
TL: Se nos perdieron dos expedientes durante la mudanza.
EN: Two files went missing on us during the move.
SHOW: se + affected person + event · viewpoint without agency
Flip for possession and topical duplication.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Spanish often states the participant twice
REF: 14.10.1, 14.10.2, 14.10.4

When a definite object moves before the verb as a topic, a matching clitic is normally required.
TL: Este informe lo redacté ayer; aquella versión la corrigió Marta.
EN: I drafted this report yesterday; Marta corrected that version.
[PAUSE:1000]
An indirect-object phrase commonly co-occurs with le or les even after the verb. The noun phrase identifies the participant; the clitic integrates its role into the predicate.
TL: Le envié el informe definitivo a Marta.
EN: I sent the final report to Marta.
[PAUSE:1000]
Indefinite fronted direct objects behave differently and often omit the duplicate.
TL: Café no tomo, pero el té sí lo acepto.
EN: I do not drink coffee, but I will have the tea.
SHOW: definite topic + clitic · named recipient often with le/les · indefinite contrast may omit it
Ten affected-person choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Start le versus lo or la from argument structure
REF: 15.1, 15.2, 15.3, 15.4

For a stable cross-regional system, ask what the verb does. A directly perceived, moved, praised, or otherwise affected person or thing takes lo, la, los or las; a recipient, beneficiary, source, or experiencer takes le or les.
TL: A Marta la vi en la estación y luego le mandé un mensaje.
EN: I saw Marta at the station and then sent her a message.
[PAUSE:1000]
Gender belongs to the direct-object series. Le and les mark number but not gender.
TL: Los documentos los archivaron; a la directora le reservaron una copia.
EN: They filed the documents; they reserved a copy for the director.
SHOW: direct object agrees in gender/number · indirect object le/les
Flip for the variation hidden by the simple rule.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Leísmo is patterned variation, not random exchange
REF: 15.5, 15.6.1, 15.6.2, 15.6.3, 15.6.4

Le for a singular male human direct object is standard in much of Spain. The lo baseline remains accepted internationally and is safest when no regional target is intended.
TL: Al director lo vi ayer; en buena parte de España también se oye le vi.
EN: I saw the director yesterday; in much of Spain one also hears le vi.
[PAUSE:1000]
Le is also favored across regions with some human experiencers, impersonal se, and verbs whose meaning conventionally selects it. Learn these as argument patterns, not as permission to replace every lo or la.
TL: A Julia le preocupa el retraso y al nuevo empleado le enseñaron el procedimiento.
EN: Julia is worried by the delay, and the new employee was taught the procedure.
[PAUSE:1000]
Keep the contrast lexical where it distinguishes meanings.
TL: Lo tiraron a la basura, pero le tiraron de la manga.
EN: They threw it in the trash, but they pulled his sleeve.
SHOW: international baseline first · regional human leísmo · lexical le with selected verbs
Fourteen le, lo and la judgments follow.
