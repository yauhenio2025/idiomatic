## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Read an Italian verb as a stack of information
REF: 14.1, 14.19

A finite verb combines a lexical root with material that identifies person, number, tense, and mood. A thematic vowel often signals the conjugation between the root and the ending.
TL: Cantavamo insieme ogni domenica.
EN: We used to sing together every Sunday.
[PAUSE:900]
In cantavamo, cant- carries the lexical meaning, -a- identifies the first conjugation, -va- marks the imperfect, and -mo marks first-person plural.
SHOW: root + thematic vowel + tense or mood + person and number
Flip for the contrast between one-word and compound forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Synthetic forms compress; analytic forms divide the work
REF: 14.1, 14.19

A synthetic form carries its grammar in one word. An analytic form uses an auxiliary for finite information and a participle for the lexical event.
TL: Vengo presto; sono venuta presto.
EN: I am coming early; I came early.
[PAUSE:900]
Only the auxiliary is finite in the compound form. The participle may also show agreement when essere is the auxiliary.
TL: Avremmo finito il lavoro, ma siamo partiti.
EN: We would have finished the work, but we left.
SHOW: synthetic -> one inflected word · analytic -> finite auxiliary + participle

[CARD]
TITLE: Three thematic vowels organize the regular system
REF: 14.2

The infinitive classes are conventionally organized around -are, -ere, and -ire. Their thematic vowels recur in forms such as the imperfect, gerund, and second-person plural.
TL: parlare -> parlavate -> parlando
EN: to speak -> you were speaking -> speaking
[PAUSE:900]
TL: temere -> temevate -> temendo · dormire -> dormivate -> dormendo
EN: to fear -> you were fearing -> fearing · to sleep -> you were sleeping -> sleeping
SHOW: first -> a · second -> e · third -> i

[SIDE]
TITLE: Identify the whole form, not just its final letter
REF: 14.2

The same ending can belong to different analyses. Date can be present indicative or an imperative, while a stressed final vowel can distinguish a passato remoto form such as finì.
TL: Date il modulo alla segretaria; ieri Lucia finì il rapporto.
EN: Give the form to the secretary; yesterday Lucia finished the report.
[PAUSE:900]
Use the stem, ending, stress, and sentence context together. Third-conjugation verbs in -isc- add that element only in the root-stressed present forms.
TL: Definisco il problema, ma definiamo insieme la soluzione.
EN: I define the problem, but we define the solution together.
SHOW: stem + ending + stress + context · -isc- in root-stressed present forms

[CARD]
TITLE: Learn irregular verbs as a compact form network
REF: 14.3

One isolated present form rarely predicts every other irregular form. Store a small network: infinitive, first-person present, passato remoto, past participle, and future stem.
TL: venire -> vengo -> venni -> venuto -> verr-
EN: to come -> I come -> I came -> come -> future stem
[PAUSE:900]
High-frequency verbs may draw their forms from visibly different stems.
TL: essere -> sono -> fui -> stato -> sar-
EN: to be -> I am -> I was -> been -> future stem
SHOW: infinitive · present anchor · remote-past anchor · participle · future stem
Forty bidirectional irregular-form recalls follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Keep function attached to every irregular form
REF: 14.3

A form is useful only when its grammatical identity is clear. Compare a finite present, an imperative, a gerund, and a participle from familiar irregular verbs.
TL: Lei esce; date il segnale; cuocendo lentamente; un documento nascosto.
EN: She goes out; give the signal; cooking slowly; a hidden document.
[PAUSE:900]
Accent and context can separate forms that look similar on the page or share a spelling.
TL: Lui dà una risposta; date una risposta; ebbero una risposta.
EN: He gives an answer; give an answer; they had an answer.
SHOW: retrieve form + person + tense or mood + syntactic role

[CARD]
TITLE: Many derived irregulars inherit a family model
REF: 14.4

Prefixes normally preserve the core irregular pattern. Once a model is known, the ending of a new infinitive can identify a useful family.
TL: porre -> pongo -> posto · sottoporre -> sottopongo -> sottoposto
EN: to put -> I put -> put · to subject -> I subject -> subjected
[PAUSE:900]
The same principle links venire with intervenire and trarre with estrarre, although spelling and meaning still belong to each lexical item.
TL: vengo / intervengo · traggo / estraggo
EN: I come / I intervene · I draw / I extract
SHOW: recognize family -> preserve prefix -> apply model -> verify lexical exceptions
Nineteen hygiene-gated family and mixed-conjugation forms follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Compiere and the -fare family mix patterns
REF: 14.5

Compiere combines second-conjugation-looking forms with a third-conjugation future and conditional.
TL: compio, compiono, compiuto · compirò, compireste
EN: I complete, they complete, completed · I will complete, you would complete
[PAUSE:900]
Derivatives in -fare show real variation in modern use. Prefer an established current form and recognize alternatives rather than forcing a single mechanical rule.
TL: soddisfo, soddisfiamo, soddisfatto · rarefatto
EN: I satisfy, we satisfy, satisfied · rarefied
SHOW: compiere -> mixed but stable · -fare derivatives -> family pattern plus usage variation

[CARD]
TITLE: The addressee chooses the imperative frame
REF: 14.8

For tu, first-conjugation verbs use the third-person present shape, while the other conjugations normally use the second-person present shape. The negative tu command instead uses the infinitive.
TL: Parla più piano; temi le conseguenze; non dormire qui.
EN: Speak more softly; fear the consequences; do not sleep here.
[PAUSE:900]
Voi commands use the ordinary second-person plural form. Formal Lei and Loro commands use the present subjunctive.
TL: Aspettate qui; entri, Signora; entrino, Signori.
EN: Wait here; come in, Madam; come in, ladies and gentlemen.
SHOW: tu -are -> 3sg shape · other tu -> 2sg shape · negative tu -> infinitive · Lei/Loro -> subjunctive
Seven hygiene-gated special-command recalls follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Short imperatives absorb their clitics
REF: 14.9

Andare, dare, dire, fare, and stare have short tu commands. Before an attached clitic, va, da, fa, and sta normally double the clitic's initial consonant; di' behaves similarly.
TL: Va' via; dammi il modulo; fallo subito; dimmi la verità.
EN: Go away; give me the form; do it at once; tell me the truth.
[PAUSE:900]
Avere, essere, sapere, and volere use special commands that must be learned directly.
TL: Abbi pazienza; sii prudente; sappiate che è urgente; vogliate firmare qui.
EN: Be patient; be careful; be aware that it is urgent; kindly sign here.
SHOW: va'/da'/fa'/sta'/di' · clitic attachment -> consonant doubling · abbi/sii/sappi/vogli

[CARD]
TITLE: Agreement follows grammatical number
REF: 14.18

Italian formal usage keeps a singular verb with a grammatically singular collective, even when the noun refers to many people.
TL: La maggioranza degli studenti sostiene la proposta.
EN: The majority of the students support the proposal.
[PAUSE:900]
Gente, polizia, squadra, governo, and nessuno are likewise singular heads. Do not copy an English notional plural.
TL: La polizia è intervenuta; nessuno di loro è arrivato.
EN: The police intervened; none of them arrived.
SHOW: find the grammatical head -> singular head, singular verb · plural head, plural verb
Thirteen source agreement choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Coordinated persons follow a hierarchy
REF: 14.18

Coordination is plural, but the person of the verb depends on who is included. Any group containing io or noi uses first-person plural.
TL: Mia sorella ed io andiamo d'accordo.
EN: My sister and I get along.
[PAUSE:900]
Without a first-person member, a group containing tu or voi uses second-person plural; an all-third-person group uses third-person plural.
TL: Tu e tuo fratello verrete domani; Luca e Sara verranno sabato.
EN: You and your brother will come tomorrow; Luca and Sara will come on Saturday.
SHOW: includes 1st person -> 1pl · else includes 2nd -> 2pl · all 3rd -> 3pl

[CARD]
TITLE: Compound forms keep the auxiliary finite
REF: 14.19

Every analytic form combines the required tense or mood of avere or essere with the past participle. Change the auxiliary form to change the temporal viewpoint.
TL: Ho chiuso; avevo chiuso; avrò chiuso; avrei chiuso.
EN: I closed; I had closed; I will have closed; I would have closed.
[PAUSE:900]
With essere, the participle agrees with the subject.
TL: Giulia è partita; i ragazzi erano partiti; le colleghe sarebbero partite.
EN: Giulia left; the boys had left; the colleagues would have left.
SHOW: finite auxiliary -> person + tense or mood · participle -> event + agreement with essere

[SIDE]
TITLE: Choose avere or essere from structure and event shape
REF: 14.20, 14.21

Transitive verbs take avere, while clitic-reflexive verbs take essere. Among intransitives, controlled activity tends toward avere; bounded motion, change, appearance, persistence, and states tend toward essere.
TL: Abbiamo nuotato per due ore; siamo arrivati alle sei.
EN: We swam for two hours; we arrived at six.
[PAUSE:900]
Some verbs change auxiliary with meaning: avere foregrounds the activity, essere the attained location or resulting state. A modal usually takes avere, but it may inherit essere from a tightly integrated infinitive.
TL: Ha corso per un'ora; è corso fino alla stazione. · Ha dovuto lavorare; si è dovuta alzare.
EN: He ran for an hour; he ran as far as the station. · He had to work; she had to get up.
SHOW: transitive/activity -> avere · reflexive/change/endpoint/state -> essere · modal + infinitive -> check integration

[CARD]
TITLE: Participle agreement begins with the auxiliary
REF: 14.22

With avere, the participle normally does not agree with the subject. With essere, it agrees in gender and number with the subject.
TL: Le ragazze hanno cantato; le ragazze sono arrivate.
EN: The girls sang; the girls arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
This essere rule covers ordinary intransitives, reflexives, and canonical passives.
TL: Le finestre sono state chiuse; Marta si è ricordata della chiave.
EN: The windows were closed; Marta remembered the key.
SHOW: avere + participle -> normally invariant · essere + participle -> agree with subject
Ten hygiene-gated agreement completions follow; the already-complete example is omitted.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A preceding object clitic can control agreement
REF: 14.23, 14.24

With avere, the participle must agree with preceding lo, la, li, and le. Agreement with partitive ne is usual, while mi, ti, ci, and vi allow more variation.
TL: Patrizia? Non l'ho ancora vista. · Di copie, ne abbiamo ordinate tre.
EN: Patrizia? I have not seen her yet. · We ordered three copies.
[PAUSE:900]
When an essere construction also contains a third-person direct-object clitic, agreement with that direct object overrides subject agreement.
TL: I moduli? I ragazzi se li sono presi.
EN: The forms? The boys took them for themselves.
SHOW: lo/la/li/le before participle -> object agreement · ne -> usually quantity agreement · essere + direct-object clitic -> object wins

[CARD]
TITLE: Fare plus infinitive creates one causative unit
REF: 14.25, 14.26

Italian uses fare plus a present active infinitive for make, let, or have something done. The infinitive is not passivized and cannot carry its own clitic.
TL: Ho fatto pulire la stanza; la farò pulire domani.
EN: I had the room cleaned; I will have it cleaned tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Clitics attach to fare, and the performer normally follows the infinitive.
TL: Il direttore ha fatto leggere il rapporto a Marta; glielo ha fatto leggere.
EN: The director made Marta read the report; he made her read it.
SHOW: finite fare + active infinitive · clitics attach to fare · performer follows infinitive
Eighteen source causative transformations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A and da reveal the causative viewpoint
REF: 14.27, 14.28, 14.30

If the infinitive has no direct object, its performer follows directly. With a direct object, a presents the performer as the active doer.
TL: Faccio correre Gianni; faccio leggere la lettera a Gianni.
EN: I make Gianni run; I make Gianni read the letter.
[PAUSE:900]
Da backgrounds the performer as the agent of an action done to the object. Reflexive fare also selects da for that agent.
TL: Faccio leggere la lettera da Gianni; mi faccio tagliare i capelli dalla parrucchiera.
EN: I have the letter read by Gianni; I have my hair cut by the hairdresser.
SHOW: no object -> performer direct · active performer with object -> a · backgrounded agent -> da · true reflexive infinitive -> recast with fare sì che

[CARD]
TITLE: A passive promotes only a direct object
REF: 14.31, 14.32, 14.33

The active direct object becomes the passive subject and agrees with the auxiliary and participle. The former subject may be omitted or introduced as an agent with da.
TL: Tutti hanno firmato il modulo; il modulo è stato firmato da tutti.
EN: Everyone signed the form; the form was signed by everyone.
[PAUSE:900]
An Italian indirect object cannot become a passive subject. Keep it as an a phrase and promote the direct object instead.
TL: Hanno promesso un rimborso al cliente; al cliente è stato promesso un rimborso.
EN: They promised the customer a refund; the customer was promised a refund.
SHOW: direct object -> passive subject · agent -> da · indirect object -> remains indirect

[SIDE]
TITLE: Essere, venire, andare, and si choose different perspectives
REF: 14.34, 14.36

Essere is the neutral passive auxiliary. Venire highlights a dynamic or repeated process and is limited to synthetic tenses. Andare can mean that something gets lost or destroyed, or that it ought to be done.
TL: La porta è chiusa; viene chiusa alle otto; va chiusa prima di uscire.
EN: The door is closed; it gets closed at eight; it must be closed before leaving.
[PAUSE:900]
Si passivante backgrounds a normally human agent. Its third-person verb agrees with the subject that undergoes the action.
TL: Si ripara la bicicletta; si riparano le biciclette.
EN: The bicycle is repaired; bicycles are repaired.
SHOW: essere -> neutral/state · venire -> dynamic process · andare -> loss or obligation · si + agreeing verb -> human-agent passive
