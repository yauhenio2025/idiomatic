## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Conjugation is an information stack
SVG: de_konjugation_c01f.svg
REF: 10, 10.1.1

A German verb does more than name an event. Its finite form anchors a clause by carrying agreement, tense and mood. The remaining verb forms can then add lexical meaning, completion, futurity or voice.
TL: Die Gutachterin prüft den Antrag noch einmal.
EN: The reviewer is checking the application once more.
[PAUSE:900]
The ending on the finite verb links the event to a third-person singular subject and places it in the present indicative.
SHOW: finite verb = person + number + tense + mood
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: One finite anchor, several non-finite partners
REF: 10.1.1

A clause may contain a whole verb chain, but only one member is finite. The others are infinitives or participles: fixed forms that do not agree with the subject.
TL: Die Gutachterin wird den Antrag bis Freitag geprüft haben.
EN: The reviewer will have checked the application by Friday.
[PAUSE:1100]
The first verb carries agreement and mood. The participle and final infinitive build the future-perfect meaning around that anchor. Learn to scan every German clause this way: first find the finite verb, then read the non-finite payload.
SHOW: one finite anchor · the rest is non-finite payload
This architecture will keep every longer verb chain manageable.

[CARD]
TITLE: The present endings are one reusable grid
SVG: de_konjugation_c02f.svg
REF: 10.1.1, 10.2.1

The ordinary present-tense grid is stable across weak and strong verbs. Singular forms distinguish speaker, addressee and third person; the plural largely returns to the infinitive shape.
TL: Ich werte die Daten aus.
EN: I evaluate the data.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Du wertest die Daten aus.
EN: You evaluate the data.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Das Team wertet die Daten aus.
EN: The team evaluates the data.
SHOW: singular: -e · -st · -t | plural: -en · -t · -en
Flip the card for the sound-driven repairs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: German inserts or compresses for pronounceability
REF: 10.2.1

The grid survives, but spelling adapts where consonants would collide. After certain roots ending in a dental sound or a consonant cluster, a linking vowel keeps the ending audible. After a sibilant, the familiar singular ending compresses because the root already supplies its first sound.
TL: Du arbeitest heute besonders sorgfältig.
EN: You are working especially carefully today.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ihr öffnet den Sitzungssaal um acht Uhr.
EN: You open the meeting room at eight o'clock.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Du reist schon morgen wieder ab.
EN: You are leaving again tomorrow.
SHOW: protect pronunciation · do not invent a new paradigm
The next card asks what the subject actually requires.

[CARD]
TITLE: Agreement follows the grammatical subject
REF: 10.1.4

Finite agreement follows grammatical structure, not the number of people or ideas you happen to imagine. An entire subordinate clause or infinitive clause counts as one subject and therefore takes singular agreement.
TL: Dass beide Seiten schweigen, erschwert die Vermittlung.
EN: The fact that both sides remain silent makes mediation more difficult.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Jetzt zu widersprechen wäre unklug.
EN: It would be unwise to object now.
SHOW: clause as subject → third-person singular
Flip the card for coordinated and collective subjects.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Coordination adds; collectives stay singular
REF: 10.1.4

Subjects linked additively normally trigger a plural verb. A singular collective noun, by contrast, remains grammatically singular even when it denotes many people. Quantity expressions can fluctuate, especially in speech, but formal prose has a strong reason to respect the head noun.
TL: Der Vorstand und die Rechtsabteilung haben zugestimmt.
EN: The executive board and the legal department have agreed.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Die Polizei ermittelt bereits.
EN: The police are already investigating.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Eine Reihe von Fachleuten hat den Entwurf kritisiert.
EN: A number of specialists have criticized the draft.
SHOW: additive subjects → plural · singular collective → singular
Now turn from agreement to the three principal parts.

[CARD]
TITLE: Weak and strong verbs diverge in the past
SVG: de_konjugation_c04f.svg
REF: 10.1.2, 10.2.1, 10.6

Weak verbs build the past and participle with dental suffixes. Strong verbs change the root vowel and end their participle differently. The useful learning unit is therefore not one infinitive but a compact row of principal parts.
TL: planen – plante – geplant
EN: to plan – planned – planned
[PAUSE:800]
TL: werfen – warf – geworfen
EN: to throw – threw – thrown
SHOW: weak = dental suffix · strong = vowel alternation
Flip the card for the verbs between those poles.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Irregularity comes in families
REF: 10.1.3, 10.6

Some verbs keep the weak suffix but change the stem. Others combine strong vowel alternation with an extra consonant change. For a strong or irregular verb, add the third-person singular present whenever the present stem also shifts.
TL: nennen – nennt – nannte – genannt
EN: to name – names – named – named
[PAUSE:800]
TL: ziehen – zieht – zog – gezogen
EN: to pull – pulls – pulled – pulled
[PAUSE:800]
TL: nehmen – nimmt – nahm – genommen
EN: to take – takes – took – taken
SHOW: learn: infinitive · third-person present · past · participle
The first exercise block tests exactly these compact form rows.

[CARD]
TITLE: Strong present changes live in the singular
REF: 10.2.1, 10.6

Many strong verbs alter their stem vowel in the second- and third-person singular present, while the first-person singular and all plural forms keep the infinitive vowel. Short and long vowels follow recurring families, but the individual verb still has to be learned.
TL: Die Redakteurin liest den Absatz noch einmal.
EN: The editor reads the paragraph once more.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Der Fahrer hält vor dem Seiteneingang.
EN: The driver stops outside the side entrance.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Wir lesen den Absatz gemeinsam und halten jedes Ergebnis fest.
EN: We read the paragraph together and record every result.
SHOW: stem change: second and third singular · plural resets
Flip the card for the singular imperative.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The imperative reveals two different patterns
REF: 10.2.1

Strong verbs with a front-vowel change retain that change in the familiar singular command. Verbs whose present singular uses an umlaut normally drop the umlaut in the command. A strong stem-changing command also has no extra ending.
TL: Lies den Absatz bitte noch einmal.
EN: Please read the paragraph once more.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nimm bitte die vertrauliche Fassung.
EN: Please take the confidential version.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Fahr vorsichtig durch die Baustelle.
EN: Drive carefully through the construction site.
SHOW: front-vowel change stays · present-tense umlaut usually goes
The patterns are small; the lexical inventory is what needs repetition.

[CARD]
TITLE: The irregular auxiliaries are structural hubs
REF: 10.1.3, 10.2.2

The three basic auxiliaries are wholly irregular, but their importance is structural rather than exceptional. Once finite, they carry agreement and tense for perfect, future and passive constructions.
TL: Sie ist längst gegangen.
EN: She has long since left.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Sie hat den Entwurf vollständig überarbeitet.
EN: She has completely revised the draft.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Sie wird den Entwurf morgen vorlegen.
EN: She will present the draft tomorrow.
SHOW: finite auxiliary carries the grammar · final form carries the event
Flip the card for the modal pattern.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Modals borrow weak past endings
REF: 10.1.3, 10.2.2

Modal auxiliaries and the verb for knowing have irregular singular presents, then mostly use weak-style past endings with their own stem changes. With another verb in a perfect construction, a modal normally appears as an infinitive rather than as a participle.
TL: Er kann den Widerspruch nicht erklären.
EN: He cannot explain the contradiction.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Er konnte den Widerspruch nicht erklären.
EN: He could not explain the contradiction.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Er hat den Widerspruch nicht erklären können.
EN: He was not able to explain the contradiction.
SHOW: modal perfect + main verb → double infinitive
Keep this special chain in view when the auxiliaries start stacking.

[CARD]
TITLE: The participle follows stress, not appearance
SVG: de_konjugation_c07f.svg
REF: 10.2.1

The ordinary past participle surrounds a weak root with a prefix and a dental ending; a strong participle usually has the same prefix and a final infinitive-like ending. But the prefix disappears when the first syllable is not stressed, notably with inseparable prefixes and verbs in the productive loan-verb class.
TL: Die Kommission hat den Vorschlag sorgfältig geprüft.
EN: The commission carefully examined the proposal.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Die Zeugin hat den Ablauf präzise beschrieben.
EN: The witness described the sequence of events precisely.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Das Labor hat sämtliche Proben analysiert.
EN: The laboratory analyzed all the samples.
SHOW: stressed start → ge- is possible · unstressed start → no ge-
Flip the card for separable prefixes.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Separable prefixes open, then close
REF: 10.2.1

In a main clause, a separable prefix leaves the finite verb and closes the clause. In a participle, the same prefix rejoins the verb and the participial marker slots between them. In a subordinate clause, the prefix and finite verb remain joined at the end.
TL: Die Referentin stellt die Ergebnisse knapp vor.
EN: The presenter presents the results concisely.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Die Referentin hat die Ergebnisse knapp vorgestellt.
EN: The presenter presented the results concisely.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Wir wissen, dass die Referentin die Ergebnisse knapp vorstellt.
EN: We know that the presenter presents the results concisely.
SHOW: main clause: prefix detached · non-finite or final: rejoined
That same bracket becomes the frame for every compound tense.

[CARD]
TITLE: Four compound tenses, one construction rule
SVG: de_konjugation_c08f.svg
REF: 10.3.1

Compound tenses split the work. A finite auxiliary carries agreement and viewpoint; a participle or infinitive carries the lexical event. Change the auxiliary tense or the final payload, and the same architecture yields four different temporal relations.
TL: Die Arbeitsgruppe hat den Vorschlag geprüft.
EN: The working group has examined the proposal.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Die Arbeitsgruppe hatte den Vorschlag geprüft.
EN: The working group had examined the proposal.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Die Arbeitsgruppe wird den Vorschlag prüfen.
EN: The working group will examine the proposal.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Die Arbeitsgruppe wird den Vorschlag geprüft haben.
EN: The working group will have examined the proposal.
SHOW: perfect: auxiliary + participle · future: auxiliary + infinitive
Flip the card for clause order and past-before-past.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The bracket closes differently by clause type
REF: 10.3.1

In a main clause, the finite auxiliary occupies the finite slot and the non-finite material closes the bracket. In a subordinate clause, the verbal material gathers at the end, with the auxiliary normally last. The pluperfect marks an event already complete at a past reference point.
TL: Die Sachverständige hatte die Unterlagen gründlich ausgewertet, bevor die Anhörung begann.
EN: The expert had evaluated the documents thoroughly before the hearing began.
[PAUSE:950]
TL: Wir wussten, dass die Sachverständige die Unterlagen gründlich ausgewertet hatte.
EN: We knew that the expert had evaluated the documents thoroughly.
SHOW: main clause: finite early · subordinate clause: auxiliary last
Eight past-and-pluperfect sentence builds follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Sein marks a bounded transition
SVG: de_konjugation_c09f.svg
REF: 10.3.2

Most verbs form the perfect with the default auxiliary. The smaller group using the other auxiliary has a semantic center: an intransitive transition across space or into a new state. Verbs of happening and the basic verbs of being and remaining join that group.
TL: Die Delegation ist gestern aus Brüssel zurückgekehrt.
EN: The delegation returned from Brussels yesterday.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Der Konflikt ist unerwartet eskaliert.
EN: The conflict escalated unexpectedly.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Bei der Übertragung ist ein Fehler aufgetreten.
EN: An error occurred during transmission.
SHOW: intransitive path or change of state → sein
Flip the card for the default auxiliary.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Haben is the broad default
REF: 10.3.2

Use the default with transitive verbs, reflexive verbs, stable activities and states, and most impersonal verbs. This is not a mechanical contrast between action and motion; direct objects and event shape matter.
TL: Die Delegation hat den Gegenvorschlag ausführlich erörtert.
EN: The delegation discussed the counterproposal in detail.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Die Vorsitzende hat sich über den Tonfall geärgert.
EN: The chair was annoyed by the tone.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Das Publikum hat zwei Stunden lang gewartet.
EN: The audience waited for two hours.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: In der Nacht hat es heftig geregnet.
EN: It rained heavily during the night.
SHOW: transitive · reflexive · durative · impersonal → haben
Ten perfect-tense transformations reinforce both sides of the choice.

[CARD]
TITLE: The same verb can switch auxiliaries
REF: 10.3.2

Auxiliary choice belongs to the verb's meaning in its sentence, not permanently to the dictionary headword. A motion verb can use the transition auxiliary when it traces a path, but the default when it takes a direct object.
TL: Die Pilotin ist am Morgen nach Wien geflogen.
EN: The pilot flew to Vienna in the morning.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Die Pilotin hat erstmals den neuen Langstreckenjet geflogen.
EN: The pilot flew the new long-haul jet for the first time.
[PAUSE:850]
The first clause presents a journey. The second presents an activity performed on an object.
SHOW: path without direct object → sein · transitive use → haben
Flip the card for event shape without an object.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Destination can turn activity into transition
REF: 10.3.2

With a small class of movement activities, a bounded path favors the transition auxiliary, while unbounded activity favors the default. The same semantic contrast explains the difference between a change of state and an impersonal weather event.
TL: Das Paar ist aus dem Saal getanzt.
EN: The couple danced out of the hall.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Das Paar hat bis Mitternacht getanzt.
EN: The couple danced until midnight.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Der See ist über Nacht gefroren.
EN: The lake froze overnight.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: In der Nacht hat es gefroren.
EN: It froze during the night.
SHOW: bounded transition → sein · activity or weather event → haben
Sixteen clean auxiliary decisions follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: German has an event passive and a state passive
SVG: de_konjugation_c11f.svg
REF: 10.4

The event passive combines the participle with the auxiliary of becoming and presents an action or process. The state passive combines the same participle with the auxiliary of being and presents the resulting condition.
TL: Die Brücke wird derzeit saniert.
EN: The bridge is currently being renovated.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Die Brücke ist seit gestern gesperrt.
EN: The bridge has been closed since yesterday.
SHOW: werden + participle = process · sein + participle = resulting state
Flip the card for the passive verb chains.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Passive chains stack from the right
REF: 10.2.2, 10.4

The finite auxiliary changes for tense while the lexical participle stays in place. In the perfect event passive, the passive auxiliary uses its special participle without the ordinary prefix. The future passive ends with a second copy of that auxiliary in the infinitive.
TL: Die Brücke wurde im Frühjahr saniert.
EN: The bridge was renovated in the spring.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Die Brücke ist im Frühjahr saniert worden.
EN: The bridge was renovated in the spring.
[PAUSE:750]
TL: Die Brücke wird bis Ende 2028 saniert werden.
EN: The bridge will be renovated by the end of 2028.
SHOW: perfect passive: participle + worden · future passive: participle + werden
Read a long passive chain from the lexical participle outward: event first, then voice, then tense.

[CARD]
TITLE: The two subjunctives are form systems, not tenses
SVG: de_konjugation_c12f.svg
REF: 10.5.1, 10.5.2

Apart from the basic verb of being, the first subjunctive builds regularly from the present stem and most visibly differs from the indicative in the third-person singular. Strong verbs do not apply their ordinary present-tense vowel change. Its compound forms simply put the relevant auxiliary into that mood.
TL: Die Sprecherin erklärt, der Ausschuss prüfe den Vorwurf.
EN: The spokesperson states that the committee is examining the allegation.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Sie ergänzt, das Gutachten sei bereits veröffentlicht worden.
EN: She adds that the report has already been published.
SHOW: Konjunktiv I: present stem + subjunctive endings
Flip the card for the second subjunctive.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Konjunktiv II starts from the past system
REF: 10.5.2

Strong and irregular verbs can build a distinctive simple form from their past stem, often with an umlaut. Weak simple forms collide with the ordinary past and are normally replaced by the conditional construction. The compound perfect uses the second-subjunctive form of the appropriate perfect auxiliary.
TL: Ohne die Fristverlängerung käme das Team in Schwierigkeiten.
EN: Without the extension, the team would run into difficulties.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Mit mehr Zeit würde die Arbeitsgruppe den Entwurf überarbeiten.
EN: With more time, the working group would revise the draft.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Hätte die Behörde früher reagiert, wäre der Schaden geringer ausgefallen.
EN: If the authority had reacted earlier, the damage would have been less severe.
SHOW: distinctive simple form where natural · otherwise würde + infinitive
The complete system is now visible: one finite anchor controls every simple or compound chain.
