## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose a relationship before choosing you
REF: 22.1, 22.3

Italian address forms encode number and the relationship between speakers. Tu presents the singular addressee as familiar. Lei keeps social distance. The choice controls the whole clause, not just one pronoun.
TL: Ciao, Marta, vuoi sederti qui accanto a me?
EN: Hi, Marta, would you like to sit here beside me?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Buongiorno, dottoressa, desidera accomodarsi?
EN: Good morning, Doctor, would you like to take a seat?
[PAUSE:900]
The greeting, verb, reflexive pronoun, and tone all point to the same relationship. Mixing systems accidentally can sound careless or intrusive.
SHOW: familiar singular -> tu · distant singular -> Lei
Flip to make a safe first choice.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Default to distance, then negotiate closeness
REF: 22.1, 22.3

With an unfamiliar adult, Lei is the conservative opening. A move to mutual tu is a social negotiation, often proposed by the older or more senior person.
TL: Mi scusi, può dirmi dove si trova l'ufficio informazioni?
EN: Excuse me, can you tell me where the information office is?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Se preferisce, possiamo darci del tu.
EN: If you prefer, we can address each other with tu.
[PAUSE:900]
Lei expresses distance, not kindness by itself. Tu expresses familiarity, not affection by itself. Either form can carry warmth, irritation, or contempt.
SHOW: when unsure -> Lei · mutual change -> darsi del tu

[CARD]
TITLE: Map each address form to its verb person
REF: 22.2, 22.5

Tu takes the second person singular. Lei and elevated Ella take the third person singular. Plural voi takes the second person plural; highly formal Loro takes the third person plural.
TL: Tu conosci già il progetto, mentre voi dovete ancora leggerlo.
EN: You already know the project, while you all still have to read it.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Lei riceverà una copia oggi; Loro la riceveranno domani.
EN: You will receive a copy today; you, formally addressed as a group, will receive it tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Think in grammatical person rather than translating English you. The verb ending is the load-bearing signal.
SHOW: tu -> 2sg · Lei/Ella -> 3sg · voi -> 2pl · Loro -> 3pl

[SIDE]
TITLE: Let the ending identify an omitted subject
REF: 22.2

Italian normally omits an unstressed subject pronoun. An address form may therefore be visible only in the verb and its accompanying clitics.
TL: Maria, che cosa ne pensi?
EN: Maria, what do you think about it?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Avvocato, che cosa ne pensa?
EN: Counsel, what do you think about it?
[PAUSE:900]
Pensi selects tu; pensa selects Lei. Add the subject pronoun only for emphasis, contrast, or clarity.
SHOW: address often lives in the ending, not an explicit pronoun

[CARD]
TITLE: Extend formal Lei through the clitic system
REF: 22.2

Formal Lei behaves syntactically like third person singular. Direct object La, indirect object Le, reflexive si, and possessive Suo or suo must remain aligned with the formal verb.
TL: Professore, La richiamerò domani e Le invierò il documento.
EN: Professor, I will call you back tomorrow and send you the document.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Le restituisco il fascicolo e La ringrazio per l'attesa.
EN: I am returning the file to you and thanking you for waiting.
[PAUSE:900]
The feminine shape of Lei is grammatical history, not a claim about the addressee's sex.
SHOW: formal subject Lei -> object La · recipient Le · reflexive si · possessive Suo/suo

[SIDE]
TITLE: Separate pronoun gender from human agreement
REF: 22.2

Predicate adjectives and participles normally agree with the sex of the person addressed. But a past participle agreeing with formal direct-object La is generally feminine, even when the addressee is a man.
TL: Dottor Riva, è stato molto paziente.
EN: Dr Riva, you have been very patient.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Professor Neri, l'ho vista ieri al convegno.
EN: Professor Neri, I saw you yesterday at the conference.
[PAUSE:900]
In the first sentence, stato describes a male addressee. In the second, vista agrees with the direct object La hidden inside l'.
SHOW: description of person -> natural gender · agreement with La -> feminine

[CARD]
TITLE: Treat capitals as a register choice
REF: 22.2

Formal correspondence may capitalize Lei and its possessive or clitic forms, even when a clitic is attached to a verb. Current general usage also permits and increasingly favours lower case.
TL: Le comunico che la Sua domanda è stata accolta.
EN: I inform you that your application has been accepted.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Se desidera ulteriori chiarimenti, sarò lieto di fornirGlieli.
EN: If you would like further clarification, I shall be happy to provide it to you.
[PAUSE:900]
Capitalization raises the graphic formality; it does not repair a clause whose verb or pronouns belong to the wrong address system.
SHOW: formal writing may capitalize Lei, La, Le, Suo, attached clitics

[SIDE]
TITLE: Convert the entire clause, not one verb
REF: 22.2

Moving between tu and Lei can change the finite verb, imperative, reflexive form, object pronoun, and possessive. Scan the whole clause for dependencies.
TL: Quando arrivi, chiamami e porta con te i documenti.
EN: When you arrive, call me and bring the documents with you.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Quando arriva, mi chiami e porti con sé i documenti.
EN: When you arrive, call me and bring the documents with you.
[PAUSE:900]
The formal version uses third person arriva, formal imperatives chiami and porti, and third person con sé.
SHOW: change the relationship -> rescan verb + imperative + clitics + possessives

[CARD]
TITLE: Use tu for familiarity, not automatic equality
REF: 22.3

Tu is normal in families, friendships, many peer groups, and speech to children. It can also mark authority or contempt, so the same grammar may describe either closeness or a power imbalance.
TL: Giulia, hai finito la relazione che ti avevo chiesto?
EN: Giulia, have you finished the report I asked you for?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Paolo, diamoci del tu: lavoreremo insieme per mesi.
EN: Paolo, let us address each other with tu: we will be working together for months.
[PAUSE:900]
A first name often accompanies tu, but context outranks the name alone. Some workplaces and institutions sustain asymmetric address.
SHOW: tu can mean closeness, shared membership, authority, or contempt

[SIDE]
TITLE: Name the relationship with dare del
REF: 22.3

Dare del tu and dare del Lei describe how one person addresses another. These phrases make reciprocity or asymmetry explicit.
TL: Il direttore dà del tu agli stagisti, ma loro gli danno del Lei.
EN: The director addresses the interns with tu, but they address him with Lei.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Dopo qualche mese abbiamo cominciato a darci del tu.
EN: After a few months we began to address each other with tu.
[PAUSE:900]
The reciprocal darci shows a mutual system. Without reciprocity, keep track of who licenses which form in each direction.
SHOW: dare del tu/Lei a qualcuno · darci del tu = mutual

[CARD]
TITLE: Use Lei to preserve social distance
REF: 22.3

Lei is standard with adult strangers and people treated as socially distant or authoritative. It can also show respect to a subordinate or a familiar shopkeeper, even alongside a first name.
TL: Signor Conti, potrebbe firmare qui, per favore?
EN: Mr Conti, could you sign here, please?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Francesco, mi dà mezzo chilo di pane?
EN: Francesco, will you give me half a kilo of bread?
[PAUSE:900]
Do not reduce the choice to first name versus surname. The ongoing relationship determines whether Francesco receives tu or Lei.
SHOW: Lei = maintained distance · first name does not automatically mean tu

[SIDE]
TITLE: Hear a switch as a change of stance
REF: 22.3

A speaker may abandon Lei for tu mid-utterance to dramatize anger, contempt, intimacy, or a sudden renegotiation of distance.
TL: Guardi, non accetto questa condizione; e tu non provare a minacciarmi.
EN: Look, I do not accept this condition; and do not you dare threaten me.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: All'inizio ci davamo del Lei; poi siamo passati al tu.
EN: At first we addressed each other with Lei; then we switched to tu.
[PAUSE:900]
Treat a deliberate switch as discourse meaning. Treat an accidental switch as an agreement error.
SHOW: stable relationship -> stable system · deliberate switch -> marked stance

[CARD]
TITLE: Let voi cover the ordinary plural
REF: 22.5

Plural voi is the general address form for familiar and formal groups. Unlike singular tu and Lei, Italian normally does not require a distance contrast in the plural.
TL: Ragazzi, avete portato i vostri documenti?
EN: Have you brought your documents, everyone?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Signore e signori, accomodatevi pure: vi raggiungo subito.
EN: Ladies and gentlemen, please take your seats: I shall join you at once.
[PAUSE:900]
The second person plural verb, reflexive, object, and possessive work for both groups. Natural gender still controls adjectives and participles.
SHOW: familiar plural + formal plural -> normally voi

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reserve Loro for conspicuous formality
REF: 22.5

Plural Loro takes third person plural verbs and signals much greater distance than singular Lei. It survives chiefly in elevated, institutional, or deliberately ceremonious language.
TL: Le Signorie Loro riceveranno la comunicazione entro venerdì.
EN: You will receive the notification by Friday.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Entrino pure; il direttore parlerà con Loro tra poco.
EN: Please come in; the director will speak with you shortly.
[PAUSE:900]
For ordinary plural address, return to voi. Do not manufacture a tu-versus-Lei contrast by forcing Loro into neutral conversation.
SHOW: ordinary plural -> voi · marked institutional distance -> Loro

[CARD]
TITLE: Recognize singular voi without adopting it
REF: 22.4

Older Italian used voi as a respectful singular, like French vous. It survives regionally, in some family relationships, in speech to sacred figures, and as an intentionally old-fashioned voice.
TL: Nonna, volete che vi accompagni a casa?
EN: Grandmother, would you like me to accompany you home?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Madonna mia, proteggetemi in questo viaggio.
EN: My Lady, protect me on this journey.
[PAUSE:900]
Although the verb and pronouns are plural, an adjective or participle describing the one addressee stays singular and follows that person's sex.
SHOW: singular voi -> plural verb/pronouns + singular human agreement

[SIDE]
TITLE: Place Ella and honorific nouns at the far edge
REF: 22.3, 22.6

Ella is an extremely elevated singular alternative to Lei. Honorific noun phrases such as la Signoria Vostra belong to formal ceremonial or bureaucratic prose and usually trigger feminine agreement.
TL: Eccellenza, Ella sarà ricevuta nella sala d'onore.
EN: Your Excellency, you will be received in the hall of honour.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La Signoria Vostra è autorizzata ad accedere agli archivi.
EN: Your Lordship is authorized to access the archives.
[PAUSE:900]
These forms are important for reading register, but they are poor defaults for present-day conversation.
SHOW: Ella -> extremely elevated · Signoria Vostra -> ceremonial/bureaucratic

[CARD]
TITLE: Make the salutation match the address system
REF: 22.7

The conventional greeting helps establish the relationship before a verb appears. Ciao belongs with tu. Buonasera belongs with Lei. Buongiorno normally aligns with Lei, though it can greet a familiar person at the start of the day.
TL: Ciao, Luca. Vieni a sederti con noi.
EN: Hi, Luca. Come and sit with us.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Buonasera, signora Belli. Si accomodi.
EN: Good evening, Mrs Belli. Please take a seat.
[PAUSE:900]
Buonanotte and arrivederci can accompany either relationship. Salve often occupies a useful middle ground.
SHOW: ciao + tu · buonasera + Lei · salve = intermediate

[SIDE]
TITLE: Let the farewell confirm the register
REF: 22.7

ArrivederLa explicitly selects formal singular Lei. Arrivederci is compatible with familiar or formal relationships and can address one person or a group.
TL: ArrivederLa, ingegnere. Le telefonerò domani.
EN: Goodbye, Engineer. I shall telephone you tomorrow.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Arrivederci, Chiara. Ci sentiamo presto.
EN: Goodbye, Chiara. We shall speak soon.
[PAUSE:900]
Titles such as professore, dottore, and ingegnere conventionally select Lei. Keep the following verb and clitics in that same system.
SHOW: arrivederLa -> formal singular · arrivederci -> flexible · title -> Lei

[CARD]
TITLE: Build address from the outside in
REF: 22.1, 22.2, 22.3, 22.5, 22.7

First classify the relationship and number. Then choose the verb person. Finally align clitics, possessives, agreement, greeting, title, and capitalization.
TL: Buongiorno, dottoressa Ferri. Le ho portato i documenti che mi aveva chiesto.
EN: Good morning, Dr Ferri. I have brought you the documents you asked me for.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ciao, Davide. Ti ho portato i documenti che mi avevi chiesto.
EN: Hi, Davide. I have brought you the documents you asked me for.
[PAUSE:900]
The informational content is nearly identical. Every grammatical choice consistently encodes a different relationship.
SHOW: relation + number -> person -> pronouns -> agreement -> register

[SIDE]
TITLE: Run the coherence check
REF: 22.1, 22.2, 22.3, 22.4, 22.5, 22.6, 22.7

Ask five questions: one addressee or several? Familiar or distant? Which verb person follows? Which clitics and possessives belong to it? Do the greeting and title tell the same social story?
TL: Professoressa, desidera che Le mandi la bozza?
EN: Professor, would you like me to send you the draft?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elisa, vuoi che ti mandi la bozza?
EN: Elisa, would you like me to send you the draft?
[PAUSE:900]
If every answer points to the same system, the utterance will sound socially and grammatically coherent.
SHOW: number + distance + person + dependent forms + register
