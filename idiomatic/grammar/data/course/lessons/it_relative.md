## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Che, cui, and il quale divide the main relative jobs
REF: 7.1, 7.4, 7.8

Use invariant che when the relative is the subject or direct object of its clause. Use invariant cui after a preposition; use a form of il quale as an agreeing alternative in many prepositional relatives.
TL: Conosco la ragazza che ha scritto il libro che stai leggendo.
EN: I know the woman who wrote the book that you are reading.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Questo è il collega con cui lavoro.
EN: This is the colleague I work with.
[PAUSE:1000]
The gender and number of che and cui never change. Il quale agrees with the antecedent as il quale, la quale, i quali, or le quali.
SHOW: subject or direct object -> che · after a preposition -> cui / il quale · agreement alternative -> il/la/i/le quale/quali
Flip for a contrast with English that affects every relative clause.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Italian normally states the relative marker
REF: 7.2, 7.3

English can omit an object relative or leave its preposition at the end. Standard Italian does neither: state the relative marker and place its governing preposition immediately before it.
TL: È il medico di cui ti avevo parlato.
EN: He is the doctor I had told you about.
[PAUSE:900]
When one relative is shared by coordinated verbs, it need not be repeated before the later verb. Its role must remain clear in both clauses.
TL: Sono libri che legge e poi regala agli amici.
EN: They are books that she reads and then gives to friends.
SHOW: English omitted relative -> restore it · English stranded preposition -> preposition + relative · coordinated verbs -> one shared relative is possible

[CARD]
TITLE: Restrictive and parenthetical relatives keep the same core forms
REF: 7.4

A relative may identify which referent is intended or add parenthetical information about an already identified referent. Che can be the subject or direct object in either use.
TL: Cerco gli studenti che hanno prenotato; Marta, che è già qui, li aspetta.
EN: I am looking for the students who booked; Marta, who is already here, is waiting for them.
[PAUSE:900]
Use commas to mark the parenthetical reading. After superlatives and similar expressions, Italian may use the subjunctive when the relative is presented as exceptional or not simply asserted.
TL: È la persona più gentile che abbia mai incontrato.
EN: She is the kindest person I have ever met.
SHOW: identifies the referent -> no commas · adds parenthetical information -> commas · che remains invariant
Flip to avoid a common conversational shortcut in careful Italian.

[SIDE]
TITLE: All-purpose che is heard in speech but avoided in this course
REF: 7.7

Popular speech sometimes uses che where standard Italian requires a preposition plus cui, and may add a resumptive clitic to show the missing relationship.
TL: Informal: il bar che ci andava ogni giorno.
EN: Informal: the bar that he went to every day.
[PAUSE:900]
In neutral and formal Italian, restore the governing preposition and remove the unnecessary resumptive clitic.
TL: Standard: il bar in cui andava ogni giorno.
EN: Standard: the bar he went to every day.
SHOW: all-purpose che + resumptive clitic -> recognize as informal · careful form -> required preposition + cui
Twenty-two che-versus-cui linking and selection prompts follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Che fills an ungoverned subject or direct-object gap
REF: 7.4

Choose che when the antecedent itself is the subject of the relative verb. The verb agrees with that antecedent, not with the noun nearest to it.
TL: Ho incontrato le persone che organizzano il festival.
EN: I met the people who organize the festival.
[PAUSE:900]
Choose che also when the antecedent is the direct object inside the relative clause. Do not add an object clitic that repeats it.
TL: Ecco le fotografie che Marco ha scattato.
EN: Here are the photographs that Marco took.
SHOW: antecedent performs relative verb -> che + agreeing verb · antecedent receives relative verb directly -> che · no repeated lo/la/li/le
Flip when the missing complement is governed by a preposition.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Cui keeps the preposition selected by the relative clause
REF: 7.3, 7.8

If the verb or expression inside the relative clause selects a preposition, put that preposition before cui. Recover the relation from the complete underlying clause.
TL: La mostra di cui parlavo ha chiuso; la collega con cui uscivo si è trasferita.
EN: The exhibition I was talking about has closed; the colleague I used to go out with has moved.
[PAUSE:900]
Cui can occasionally appear without a before it when it means to whom, but a cui is clearer and more usual for learners. Never use preposition plus che for an ordinary personal or thing antecedent.
TL: Le persone a cui abbiamo scritto hanno risposto.
EN: The people we wrote to have replied.
SHOW: parlare di -> di cui · uscire con -> con cui · scrivere a -> a cui · preposition + che -> replace

[CARD]
TITLE: Di cui and tra cui can introduce a subset
REF: 7.9

After a quantity or group, di cui or tra cui can mean of whom, of which, or including. The following phrase identifies members of the preceding set.
TL: Ho letto cinque romanzi, di cui due in italiano.
EN: I read five novels, two of them in Italian.
[PAUSE:900]
Il motivo per cui and la ragione per cui mean the reason why. In formal document language, di cui sopra can mean mentioned above.
TL: Non capisco il motivo per cui se n'è andata.
EN: I do not understand the reason why she left.
SHOW: group + subset -> di cui / tra cui · reason why -> motivo/ragione per cui · document reference -> di cui sopra
Flip for the small class of prepositions that rejects bare cui.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A few complex prepositions require il quale rather than cui
REF: 7.10

Do not put cui after tranne, eccetto, fuori, in vece di, or di là da. Use the agreeing form of il quale instead.
TL: Erano presenti tutti i candidati, tranne quello del quale avevamo parlato.
EN: All the candidates were present except the one we had discussed.
[PAUSE:900]
The exact form agrees with the antecedent, while any article inside a contracted simple preposition belongs to the relative form.
TL: Ho salutato le colleghe, eccetto le quali nessuno conosceva il progetto.
EN: I greeted the colleagues, apart from whom nobody knew the project.
SHOW: ordinary simple preposition -> cui or il quale · tranne/eccetto/fuori/in vece di/di là da -> il quale
Two hygiene-gated correctness-and-repair prompts follow; three prompt-duplicate solutions are excluded.

[CARD]
TITLE: Il quale shows gender and number after a preposition
REF: 7.12

Il quale agrees with the antecedent: il quale and la quale in the singular, i quali and le quali in the plural. A simple preposition contracts with the article where Italian normally contracts it.
TL: È la proposta della quale discutiamo e sono i dati sui quali lavoriamo.
EN: It is the proposal we are discussing and these are the data we are working on.
[PAUSE:900]
This visible agreement can clarify which of two possible nouns the relative refers to. Cui remains the compact neutral choice when no clarification is needed.
TL: Ho parlato con la figlia di Paolo, alla quale ho consegnato le chiavi.
EN: I spoke with Paolo's daughter, to whom I gave the keys.
SHOW: di + il quale -> del quale · a + la quale -> alla quale · su + i quali -> sui quali · agreement -> antecedent
Flip for environments in which che and il quale are not interchangeable.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Il quale is useful, but it does not replace every che
REF: 7.12

After a preposition, il quale is often an alternative to cui and can be required after certain complex prepositions. As a subject relative it is formal and especially common in definitions or legal-style conditions.
TL: I candidati i quali non si presentano entro le nove saranno esclusi.
EN: Candidates who do not report by nine will be excluded.
[PAUSE:900]
After a personal pronoun such as me, ordinary Italian uses che rather than il quale. Direct-object relatives also normally use che.
TL: Avete visto me, che aspettavo fuori.
EN: You saw me, who was waiting outside.
SHOW: after preposition -> cui / agreeing il quale · formal defining subject -> il quale possible · after personal pronoun or as direct object -> che
Eleven cui-to-il-quale transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Il che refers to a whole preceding proposition
REF: 7.5

Invariable il che means which when its antecedent is an entire fact, event, or clause rather than one noun. The relative clause comments on that proposition.
TL: Hanno cancellato il treno, il che ci ha sorpresi.
EN: They cancelled the train, which surprised us.
[PAUSE:900]
Because il che has no gender or number agreement with a noun, do not use it where ordinary che or il quale refers to one stated noun phrase.
TL: La porta era chiusa, il che significava che erano già partiti.
EN: The door was closed, which meant that they had already left.
SHOW: whole prior proposition -> il che · one noun antecedent -> che/cui/il quale · il che -> invariant
Flip to put the proposition under a preposition.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A preposition combines with il che as a single discourse link
REF: 7.5

Use del che for about or for which, al che for whereupon, col che for with which or thereby, and dal che for from which. These links refer back to the preceding proposition.
TL: Mi sono spiegato male, del che ti chiedo scusa.
EN: I explained myself badly, for which I apologize.
[PAUSE:900]
Il che can also stand in a separate sentence or appear parenthetically inside the sentence containing the proposition it comments on.
TL: Pubblicheremo l'ultimo volume. Col che il lavoro sarà finito.
EN: We will publish the last volume. With that, the work will be finished.
SHOW: apologize for that -> del che · reaction to that -> al che · result/completion -> col che · inference -> dal che
Eleven whole-clause linking prompts follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Possessive cui agrees through the following article
REF: 7.11

To express whose or of which, put a definite article before cui. That article agrees with the possessed noun after cui, not with the antecedent that owns it.
TL: Conosco la scrittrice il cui romanzo ha vinto e i cui racconti sono tradotti.
EN: I know the writer whose novel won and whose stories are translated.
[PAUSE:900]
The antecedent may be any gender or number; only the following possessed noun chooses il, la, i, or le.
TL: È un museo le cui sale restano aperte fino a tardi.
EN: It is a museum whose rooms stay open until late.
SHOW: possessed M singular -> il cui · F singular -> la cui · M plural -> i cui · F plural -> le cui
Flip for word order and a form to leave in older prose.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Article plus cui stays immediately before the possessed noun
REF: 7.11

Keep the sequence article + cui + noun together. Unlike an ordinary possessive adjective, cui does not take a second article before the noun.
TL: Gli studenti le cui domande erano più precise hanno ricevuto risposta.
EN: The students whose questions were more precise received an answer.
[PAUSE:900]
The older pattern il di cui, la di cui, and similar forms is literary and not the neutral model. Rewrite it as article + cui + noun.
TL: Moderno: il filosofo il cui parere fu richiesto.
EN: Modern: the philosopher whose opinion was requested.
SHOW: owner antecedent + [article matching possessed noun] + cui + noun · avoid old-fashioned article + di cui
Twelve possessive-cui rewrites follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Quello che and ciò che mean what or that which
REF: 7.15

When there is no expressed noun antecedent, quello che, quel che, and ciò che can mean what or that which. Add tutto for everything or all that.
TL: Quello che dici è vero; credo a tutto ciò che hai scritto.
EN: What you say is true; I believe everything you wrote.
[PAUSE:900]
Ciò is more formal and text-like; quello is neutral and can inflect when it represents a person or an understood countable noun. Cosa che instead comments on a whole preceding proposition.
TL: Ha mantenuto la promessa, cosa che nessuno si aspettava.
EN: He kept the promise, which nobody expected.
SHOW: what/that which -> quello che / ciò che · all that -> tutto quello/ciò che · prior proposition -> cosa che
Flip for quale and quanto without an article.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quale expresses kind or capacity; quanto gathers all that
REF: 7.14, 7.16

Bare quale can mean such as or in the capacity of. It characterizes a type rather than selecting an ordinary antecedent with the agreeing article.
TL: Vi scrivo quale rappresentante della ditta.
EN: I am writing to you in my capacity as the company's representative.
[PAUSE:900]
Quanto can mean what or all that, and tutto quanto reinforces everything that. In compact formal phrases, essere is often left unexpressed before a participle.
TL: Da quanto affermato deduco che l'accordo è valido.
EN: From what has been stated, I infer that the agreement is valid.
SHOW: such as / in the capacity of -> quale · what/all that -> quanto · everything that -> tutto quanto · quanto affermato -> quanto è stato affermato
Fifteen special free-relative translations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Che has an antecedent; chi supplies its own human antecedent
REF: 7.4, 7.17

Use che after an expressed person or group. Use chi when the relative itself means the person who, anyone who, or those who and no separate antecedent is stated.
TL: Ogni studente che partecipa deve iscriversi; chi partecipa deve iscriversi.
EN: Every student who takes part must register; anyone who takes part must register.
[PAUSE:900]
Any preposition required by the surrounding clause comes before chi. Inside its own relative clause, chi remains a human singular form.
TL: Regalerò il libro a chi arriverà per primo.
EN: I will give the book to whoever arrives first.
SHOW: expressed human antecedent + relative -> che · no antecedent, human meaning -> chi · outer governing preposition -> before chi
Flip for agreement and more explicit formal alternatives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Chi takes singular grammar even when it includes many people
REF: 7.17

Chi is grammatically singular, so its own verb is singular even when the intended group is plural or general. The main clause follows the structure chosen for that free relative.
TL: Chi ha ricevuto la lettera potrà entrare gratis.
EN: Everyone who received the letter will be able to enter free.
[PAUSE:900]
Formal prose can make gender or number explicit with colui che, colei che, and coloro che. Quello che, quella che, and quelli che are less formal alternatives when context supplies the class.
TL: Coloro che hanno prenotato riceveranno una conferma.
EN: Those who booked will receive a confirmation.
SHOW: compact neutral human free relative -> chi + singular · formal explicit -> colui/colei/coloro che · contextual class -> quello/quella/quelli che
Twelve chi-versus-che completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Chiunque means whoever; qualunque cosa means whatever
REF: 7.18

Chiunque is human: whoever or anyone who. Qualunque cosa and qualsiasi cosa are non-human: whatever or anything that. Keep the expression appropriate to the missing participant.
TL: Aiuterò chiunque me lo chieda; accetterò qualunque cosa tu proponga.
EN: I will help whoever asks me; I will accept whatever you propose.
[PAUSE:900]
These relatives commonly present an unrestricted or hypothetical choice and therefore often take the subjunctive. A plain factual statement can follow ordinary tense and mood rules.
TL: Chiunque tu sia, non puoi entrare senza un invito.
EN: Whoever you are, you cannot enter without an invitation.
SHOW: unrestricted person -> chiunque · unrestricted thing -> qualunque/qualsiasi cosa · hypothetical/unknown relative -> commonly subjunctive
Flip for an agreeing alternative and the conservative exercise gate.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quale che sia agrees with the noun it leaves open
REF: 7.18

The formal pattern quale che + essere means whichever or whatever. Quale agrees with the noun, and che stands immediately before the form of essere.
TL: Quali che siano i tuoi progetti, dovrai decidere presto.
EN: Whatever your plans may be, you will have to decide soon.
[PAUSE:900]
Where both qualunque cosa and qualsiasi cosa are printed as acceptable, the course does not force one reconstructed answer. The seven such judgment-dependent workbook items are excluded; the unambiguous chiunque items remain.
TL: Chiunque voi siate, vi ascolteremo.
EN: Whoever you may be, we will listen to you.
SHOW: quale che sia + singular · quali che siano + plural · multiple printed alternatives -> exclude from single-answer drill
Six unambiguous chiunque completions follow.
