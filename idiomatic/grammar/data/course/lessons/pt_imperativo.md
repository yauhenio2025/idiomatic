## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Brazilian commands choose a form for the audience
REF: 21, 21.1, 21.2, 21.3

An imperative tells or invites someone to act. Brazilian Portuguese first distinguishes one familiar addressee, one formally addressed person, and two or more addressees.
TL: Fala comigo. · Fale com o gerente. · Falem mais devagar.
EN: Talk to me. · Speak with the manager. · Speak more slowly.
[PAUSE:900]
The familiar affirmative normally uses the third-person singular present indicative. The formal singular and the plural use present-subjunctive forms.
TL: fala · fale · falem
EN: speak, familiar · speak, formal singular · speak, plural
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: one familiar -> ele-form indicative · one formal -> singular subjunctive · plural -> plural subjunctive
Flip to choose the lane before forming the verb.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Register and number decide more than politeness alone
REF: 21, 21.1.1, 21.2.1, 21.3

Use the familiar form in most everyday conversation with one person and in informal writing. Use the formal form with o senhor or a senhora and in formal written orders and instructions.
TL: Entra! · Entre, senhora Oliveira. · Preencha o formulário.
EN: Come in! · Come in, Ms Oliveira. · Fill in the form.
[PAUSE:900]
When addressing more than one person, use the plural subjunctive form in both familiar and formal situations.
TL: Entrem, por favor. · Preencham os formulários.
EN: Come in, please. · Fill in the forms.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: everyday singular -> familiar · respectful/written singular -> formal · any plural audience -> plural

[CARD]
TITLE: Build the familiar affirmative from the ele form
REF: 21.1.1

For an ordinary familiar command, use the same form as ele or ela in the present indicative. This gives -a with regular -ar verbs and -e with regular -er and -ir verbs.
TL: falar -> fala · comer -> come · abrir -> abre
EN: speak · eat · open
[PAUSE:900]
High-frequency irregular present forms remain irregular. Learn them as spoken command anchors rather than rebuilding them from the infinitive.
TL: vem · põe · vai · diz · faz · traz · tem
EN: come · put · go · say · do · bring · have
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: familiar affirmative = present indicative ele/ela · falar -> fala · vir -> vem
Flip for pronouns and the two verbs that do not follow this familiar pattern.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Familiar pronouns stand before the command
REF: 21.1.1, 21.1.2

In the ordinary spoken familiar imperative, an unstressed object or reflexive pronoun comes immediately before the verb.
TL: Me dá o seu e-mail. · Te cuida. · Me diz a verdade.
EN: Give me your email address. · Take care of yourself. · Tell me the truth.
[PAUSE:900]
Ser and estar do not use familiar indicative-shaped imperatives in this system. Use their formal-subjunctive forms even in a direct command.
TL: Seja paciente. · Esteja aqui às oito.
EN: Be patient. · Be here at eight.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: familiar affirmative -> pronoun + verb · ser -> seja · estar -> esteja

[CARD]
TITLE: Build the formal singular from the present subjunctive
REF: 21.2.1

The formal singular imperative is the third-person singular present subjunctive. Regular -ar verbs take -e, while regular -er and -ir verbs take -a.
TL: falar -> fale · comer -> coma · abrir -> abra
EN: speak · eat · open
[PAUSE:900]
This form serves respectful spoken address and impersonal written instructions. There is normally no overt subject because the context identifies the addressee.
TL: Entre, por favor. · Vire a bicicleta. · Abra o arquivo.
EN: Come in, please. · Turn the bicycle over. · Open the file.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: formal singular = present subjunctive ele/ela · -ar -> e · -er/-ir -> a
Flip to carry irregular present stems into the command.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The eu-present stem predicts many formal commands
REF: 21.2.1

Remove final -o from the first-person present and add the subjunctive ending. This preserves many important irregular stems.
TL: faço -> faça · digo -> diga · trago -> traga · ponho -> ponha
EN: do · say · bring · put
[PAUSE:900]
Other very common verbs have forms worth learning whole.
TL: seja · esteja · vá · dê · venha · tenha · saiba · queira
EN: be · be or stay · go · give · come · have · know · want
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: eu present minus -o often supplies the stem · faço -> faça · ponho -> ponha

[CARD]
TITLE: Affirmative formal commands normally attach pronouns
REF: 21.2.2

In careful formal commands, append an unstressed object or reflexive pronoun to the verb with a hyphen.
TL: Certifique-se. · Deixe-me explicar. · Abra-o agora.
EN: Make sure. · Let me explain. · Open it now.
[PAUSE:900]
Spoken Brazilian Portuguese also allows common hybrid forms with the pronoun before a formally shaped command. This is especially frequent in polite everyday expressions.
TL: Me desculpe. · Me diga uma coisa.
EN: Excuse me. · Tell me something.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: careful affirmative formal -> verb-pronoun · everyday hybrid -> pronoun + formal verb
Flip because negation reverses the pronoun position.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Negative commands use the subjunctive in every register
REF: 21.1.3, 21.2.3, 21.2.4

The normal prohibition uses a present-subjunctive form even with a familiar singular addressee. Place the negative word before the verb.
TL: Não fale tão alto. · Não esqueça de ligar. · Nunca faça isso.
EN: Do not speak so loudly. · Do not forget to call. · Never do that.
[PAUSE:900]
If an unstressed pronoun is present, put it between não or nunca and the verb. A familiar indicative-shaped negative occurs in very colloquial speech, but it is not the neutral default.
TL: Não se preocupe. · Nunca se esqueça disso. · Não esquece, viu?
EN: Do not worry. · Never forget that. · Do not forget, okay? Very colloquial.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: negative word + pronoun + present subjunctive · colloquial familiar indicative is marked

[CARD]
TITLE: Build plural commands with the third-person plural subjunctive
REF: 21.3

Address two or more people with the vocês, eles, elas form of the present subjunctive. Regular -ar verbs end in -em; regular -er and -ir verbs end in -am.
TL: falem · comam · abram
EN: speak · eat · open
[PAUSE:900]
The same form works in an informal group and in a formal group. Irregular stems match the singular subjunctive and take their plural endings.
TL: façam · digam · venham · ponham · sejam
EN: do · say · come · put · be
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: plural audience -> vocês subjunctive · -ar -> em · -er/-ir -> am
Flip for pronouns with plural commands.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Plural pronouns follow the affirmative and precede the negative
REF: 21.3

In an affirmative plural command, append the unstressed pronoun with a hyphen. Reflexive se follows the same pattern.
TL: Sentem-se. · Fiquem à vontade. · Traduzam-nas.
EN: Sit down. · Make yourselves at home. · Translate them.
[PAUSE:900]
In a prohibition, the negative word attracts the pronoun before the imperative form.
TL: Não se levantem. · Não as traduzam ainda.
EN: Do not stand up. · Do not translate them yet.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: affirmative plural -> verb-pronoun · negative plural -> não + pronoun + verb

[CARD]
TITLE: Attached o-forms reflect the verb ending
REF: 21.2.2, 21.3

After a vowel-final formal singular command, attach o, a, os, or as directly with a hyphen.
TL: abra-o · repita-a · tenha-as · elogie-o
EN: open it · repeat it · have them · praise him
[PAUSE:900]
After the -m of a plural command, the third-person direct-object forms appear as -no, -na, -nos, or -nas.
TL: deixem-nos · traduzam-nas
EN: leave them · translate them
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: vowel-final command + o/a -> -o/-a · plural -m + o/a -> -no/-na
Flip to keep direct and indirect objects distinct.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Choose the pronoun by its role before placing it
REF: 21.2.2, 21.3

Use o, a, os, or as for a direct object. Use lhe or lhes for an indirect recipient introduced by a in the expanded wording.
TL: Abra-o. · Dê-lhe uma cópia.
EN: Open it. · Give him or her a copy.
[PAUSE:900]
A command can contain both roles. First decide which participant or thing each pronoun represents, then apply the affirmative or negative placement rule.
TL: Entregue-a ao diretor. · Não lhe entregue a chave.
EN: Hand it to the director. · Do not give him or her the key.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: direct thing/person -> o/a/os/as · indirect recipient -> lhe/lhes · placement follows polarity

[CARD]
TITLE: Written instructions favor the formal imperative
REF: 21.2.1, 21.2.2

Recipes, manuals, notices, and procedural directions normally use the formal singular even when no particular person is being addressed.
TL: Corte a cebola. · Coloque o remendo. · Aguarde cinco minutos.
EN: Cut the onion. · Put on the patch. · Wait five minutes.
[PAUSE:900]
Keep a sequence consistent unless the audience genuinely changes. Infinitives may label controls or terse steps, but a developed instruction sequence naturally uses finite formal commands.
TL: Abra o aplicativo, digite a senha e confirme.
EN: Open the application, enter the password, and confirm.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: developed written procedure -> formal singular sequence · group instructions -> plural sequence
Flip for ways to make a command socially lighter.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Soften the act without changing its grammar
REF: 21, 21.1.1, 21.2.1

Por favor, a title, or a brief explanation can make an imperative polite. The verb form still tracks the intended audience and register.
TL: Entre, por favor. · Esperem um momento, por favor.
EN: Come in, please. · Wait a moment, please.
[PAUSE:900]
A question with poder is an alternative request rather than an imperative. In everyday speech it can sound less direct, while the resulting action may be the same.
TL: Pode fechar a janela? · Feche a janela, por favor.
EN: Can you close the window? · Close the window, please.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: politeness marker changes tone · audience and register still choose the imperative form

[CARD]
TITLE: Build a command in five checks
REF: 21.1, 21.2, 21.3

First identify whether the audience is one person or a group. Second, for one person, choose familiar spoken style or formal and written style. Third, check whether the command is affirmative or negative.
[PAUSE:850]
Fourth form the verb: familiar indicative, formal singular subjunctive, or plural subjunctive. Fifth place any pronoun according to the lane and polarity.
TL: Me passa o sal. · Passe-me o sal. · Não me passe o sal. · Passem-me o sal.
EN: Pass me the salt, familiar. · Pass me the salt, formal. · Do not pass me the salt. · Pass me the salt, plural.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: audience -> register -> polarity -> verb form -> pronoun position
Flip for the compact contrast map.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Let three contrasts prevent the common mix-ups
REF: 21.1.2, 21.2.2, 21.2.3, 21.2.4, 21.3

For one familiar affirmative, use the indicative-shaped verb and put the pronoun before it. For one formal affirmative, use the singular subjunctive and normally attach the pronoun in careful style.
TL: Me conta. · Conte-me.
EN: Tell me, familiar. · Tell me, formal.
[PAUSE:850]
For a normal negative, use the subjunctive and put the pronoun after the negative word. For a group, use the plural subjunctive, attaching a pronoun only in the affirmative.
TL: Não me conte. · Contem-me. · Não me contem.
EN: Do not tell me. · Tell me, plural. · Do not tell me, plural.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: familiar affirmative = pronoun + indicative · formal/plural affirmative = subjunctive-pronoun · negative = não + pronoun + subjunctive
