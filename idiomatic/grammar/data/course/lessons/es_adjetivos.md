## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Agreement starts from the noun
REF: 5.1, 5.2, 5.6

A Spanish adjective normally copies the number of the noun it describes, and many adjectives copy its gender as well. Begin with the noun, then choose the adjective family.
TL: El informe preliminar contiene una propuesta ambiciosa.
EN: The preliminary report contains an ambitious proposal.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Los informes preliminares contienen propuestas ambiciosas.
EN: The preliminary reports contain ambitious proposals.
[PAUSE:1000]
Adjectives in -o usually show all four forms. Many adjectives in -e or a consonant have one singular form for both genders, but still mark the plural.
SHOW: noun first → gender class → singular or plural
Flip for adjectives shared by several nouns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A shared adjective resolves the whole group
REF: 5.6.1, 5.6.2, 5.6.4

A following adjective that describes two nouns is plural. If the nouns have different genders, the conventional shared form is masculine plural.
TL: Revisaron la estructura y el calendario propuestos.
EN: They reviewed the proposed structure and schedule.
[PAUSE:900]
When an adjective comes before a coordinated pair, it usually agrees with the nearest first noun instead.
TL: Admiramos su extraordinaria paciencia y rigor.
EN: We admire her extraordinary patience and rigor.
[PAUSE:1000]
If that compact phrasing could hide which noun the adjective describes, repeat or recast it. Agreement should clarify scope rather than merely satisfy a table.
SHOW: after a mixed group → masculine plural · before a group → usually nearest noun
Twelve agreement decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Four-form and two-form families
REF: 5.2.1, 5.2.2

The four-form family includes adjectives in -o and many nationality or person adjectives ending in stressed consonants. Their feminine adds or exposes -a.
TL: El asesor español trabaja con una consultora española.
EN: The Spanish adviser works with a Spanish consultant.
[PAUSE:900]
The two-form family keeps one singular form across gender. Common endings include -e, -ista and many other consonants.
TL: Un análisis prudente evitó una decisión imprudente.
EN: A prudent analysis prevented an imprudent decision.
SHOW: -o and many person endings → gender changes · -e, -ista, many consonants → shared singular
Flip for the plural.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Plural spelling preserves the sound
REF: 5.2.1, 5.2.2

After an unstressed vowel, the plural normally adds -s. After a consonant, it normally adds -es. A final z changes to c before that plural ending.
TL: una medida eficaz · unas medidas eficaces
EN: an effective measure · effective measures
[PAUSE:800]
Written accents may disappear when the added syllable changes the stress pattern.
TL: un investigador joven · unas investigadoras jóvenes
EN: a young researcher · young researchers
[PAUSE:900]
Treat the plural as a sound-and-spelling operation after the gender form is settled.
SHOW: vowel + s · consonant + es · z becomes c before es
Twelve feminine transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Colour words split into two systems
REF: 5.2.4, 5.2.5

Basic colour adjectives behave like ordinary adjectives and agree with the noun.
TL: Eligieron carpetas rojas y archivadores azules.
EN: They chose red folders and blue filing cabinets.
[PAUSE:900]
A noun borrowed as a colour label is commonly left unchanged, especially in careful usage.
TL: La diseñadora combinó unas cortinas salmón con cojines mostaza.
EN: The designer combined salmon curtains with mustard cushions.
[PAUSE:1000]
Compounds such as a basic colour plus claro or oscuro also commonly remain fixed as a unit.
SHOW: ordinary colour adjective → agrees · noun-colour or compound shade → commonly invariable
Flip for geographical adjectives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Place adjectives are lexical knowledge
REF: 5.7.1, 5.7.2

Spanish has no dependable formula that converts every place name into its adjective. Learn the adjective with the place, then apply its own agreement pattern.
TL: Una empresa hondureña abrió dos oficinas costarricenses.
EN: A Honduran company opened two Costa Rican offices.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El equipo madrileño colaboró con investigadoras lisboetas.
EN: The Madrid team collaborated with researchers from Lisbon.
[PAUSE:1000]
These adjectives normally follow the noun because they classify origin. They can move before it only in marked, expressive contexts.
SHOW: memorize place + adjective · then agree · normally place it after the noun
Twelve colour and origin items follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Isimo intensifies; it does not compare
REF: 5.8.1, 5.8.3

The suffix -ísimo presents a quality as extreme. Remove a final vowel before adding it, and make the result agree like an ordinary adjective.
TL: La propuesta es clarísima, pero sus consecuencias son gravísimas.
EN: The proposal is extremely clear, but its consequences are extremely serious.
[PAUSE:900]
Spelling protects the original sound: rico becomes riquísimo, vago becomes vaguísimo, and feliz becomes felicísimo.
[PAUSE:900]
The suffix expresses intensity, not first place in a comparison.
SHOW: adjective stem + ísimo + agreement = extreme degree
Flip for its limits.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use the intensive selectively
REF: 5.8.1, 5.8.2, 5.8.3

Not every adjective accepts -ísimo naturally. Technical, compound, already absolute, and several long forms usually prefer an adverb such as muy or sumamente.
TL: El resultado es sumamente improbable.
EN: The result is extremely unlikely.
[PAUSE:800]
Some frequent intensives have learned stems and should be stored as whole words.
TL: Es una solución antiquísima, pero el rendimiento actual es pésimo.
EN: It is an extremely old solution, but its current performance is dreadful.
[PAUSE:1000]
In neutral modern prose, muy plus an adjective is often the safer choice. Reserve -ísimo for genuine emphasis.
SHOW: productive form, lexical exceptions, register choice
Nine intensive forms follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Relational adjectives classify a domain
REF: 5.11

A relational adjective often packages a phrase with de plus a noun into one classifier. It answers what kind or what domain, rather than how something feels.
TL: El comité revisó la política energética y el presupuesto municipal.
EN: The committee reviewed energy policy and the municipal budget.
[PAUSE:900]
These adjectives normally stand after the noun and resist ordinary degree comparison.
TL: El laboratorio instaló un nuevo sistema informático.
EN: The laboratory installed a new computer system.
[PAUSE:1000]
The mapping from noun to adjective is lexical, so inventing a transparent-looking form is risky.
SHOW: de + noun domain → established relational adjective after the noun
Flip for adjectives with two readings.

[SIDE]
TITLE: One form can classify or describe
REF: 5.10.2, 5.11

Context can give the same adjective a relational reading or a descriptive one. The relational reading creates a subtype; the descriptive reading attributes a quality.
TL: El centro hospitalario amplió su servicio nocturno.
EN: The hospital center expanded its night service.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La directora tuvo un gesto hospitalario con los visitantes.
EN: The director made a hospitable gesture toward the visitors.
[PAUSE:1000]
Test whether the phrase can be paraphrased as a stable domain or purpose. If so, keep the adjective next to the noun it classifies.
SHOW: classifier = domain or purpose · descriptor = attributed quality
Twelve relational-adjective transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Restrictive adjectives normally follow
REF: 5.10.1, 5.10.2

An adjective is restrictive when it selects a subtype or separates one member from alternatives. That adjective normally follows the noun.
TL: El equipo necesita datos fiables, no estimaciones provisionales.
EN: The team needs reliable data, not provisional estimates.
[PAUSE:900]
Nationality, technical, colour, and relational adjectives are often restrictive because they classify the noun.
TL: Compararon el mercado brasileño con el sector industrial europeo.
EN: They compared the Brazilian market with the European industrial sector.
[PAUSE:1000]
If the adjective carries the contrast that identifies the referent, postposition is the dependable default.
SHOW: subtype or contrast → noun + adjective
Flip for speaker evaluation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Preposition presents an assessment
REF: 5.10.3, 5.10.4

A descriptive adjective can move before the noun when the speaker presents it as an evaluation, an expected property, or an expressive frame for the whole referent.
TL: La preocupante demora obligó a revisar el calendario.
EN: The worrying delay forced a review of the schedule.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Celebraron los excelentes resultados del programa piloto.
EN: They celebrated the excellent results of the pilot program.
[PAUSE:1000]
Moving the adjective does more than decorate the sentence. It changes whether the quality identifies a subset or voices the speaker's stance.
SHOW: after noun = classify or contrast · before noun = frame or evaluate

[CARD]
TITLE: Keep compounds intact before attaching a quality
REF: 5.10.5

In a phrase built with de, first decide whether the two nouns form one established concept. If they do, attach the adjective outside that complete unit.
TL: Compraron una mesa de trabajo resistente.
EN: They bought a sturdy work table.
[PAUSE:900]
If the de phrase is only a loose complement, the adjective usually stays beside the noun it directly describes.
TL: Publicaron un informe detallado de la investigación.
EN: They published a detailed report on the investigation.
[PAUSE:1000]
Word order should reveal whether the adjective modifies the head noun, the dependent noun, or the compound as a whole.
SHOW: identify the compound boundary before placing the adjective
Flip for relational adjacency.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Relational classifiers hug their noun
REF: 5.10.5, 5.11

A relational adjective and its noun form a tight unit. Another descriptive adjective may stand before that unit or follow it, but should not split the classifier from its noun.
TL: Detectaron un peligroso virus informático.
EN: They detected a dangerous computer virus.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Detectaron un virus informático peligroso.
EN: They detected a dangerous computer virus.
[PAUSE:1000]
Both orders preserve virus informático as the classified concept. The descriptive adjective peligroso takes wider scope around it.
SHOW: descriptive adjective + [noun + relational adjective] · or [noun + relational adjective] + descriptive adjective
Nine scope decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Position can select a different meaning
REF: 5.10.6, 5.10.7, 5.10.8

Some frequent adjectives have conventional readings on either side of the noun. Before the noun, antiguo and viejo can mean former or long-standing; after it, they more readily describe age.
TL: Una antigua directora visitó el edificio antiguo.
EN: A former director visited the old building.
[PAUSE:900]
Nuevo before a noun often means another or newly appointed. After the noun it often means brand-new.
TL: El nuevo gerente encargó un ordenador nuevo.
EN: The new manager ordered a brand-new computer.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn these as paired constructions, not as a universal before-versus-after translation.
SHOW: antiguo director = former · edificio antiguo = old · nuevo director = new incumbent · equipo nuevo = brand-new
Flip for shortened forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Short forms belong directly before the noun
REF: 5.5, 5.10.6

Grande becomes gran before a singular noun when it means great. Bueno and malo become buen and mal directly before a singular masculine noun.
TL: Fue un gran avance y un buen comienzo para el proyecto.
EN: It was a major advance and a good start for the project.
[PAUSE:800]
The full form returns after the noun, and it also returns if another word breaks direct contact.
TL: Eligieron un método bueno aunque costoso.
EN: They chose a good though costly method.
[PAUSE:1000]
With these common adjectives, position, form, and interpretation must be chosen together.
SHOW: gran proyecto · buen resultado · mal día · proyecto grande · resultado bueno
Ten position-sensitive items follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Regular comparison builds around que
REF: 6.1, 6.2, 6.10

Most qualities compare with más or menos before the adjective or adverb and que before the comparison target.
TL: La segunda propuesta es más flexible que la primera.
EN: The second proposal is more flexible than the first.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: El nuevo sistema responde menos rápidamente que el anterior.
EN: The new system responds less quickly than the previous one.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use subject pronouns after que when people are compared.
TL: Ana es más paciente que yo.
EN: Ana is more patient than I am.
SHOW: más or menos + quality + que + comparison target
Flip for irregular comparatives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Better and worse need no extra mas
REF: 6.2, 6.8, 6.9, 6.10

Mejor and peor already mean better and worse, so do not add más. As adjectives they mark the plural but have no separate feminine form.
TL: Estas condiciones son mejores, pero ofrecen peores garantías.
EN: These conditions are better, but they offer worse guarantees.
[PAUSE:900]
Mayor and menor cover age, rank, importance, and several abstract scales. Physical size often favors más grande or más pequeño.
TL: La sala es más pequeña, pero su capacidad es mayor.
EN: The room is smaller, but its capacity is greater.
[PAUSE:1000]
Mucho intensifies an adverbial comparison; before a compared noun, it agrees with that noun.
SHOW: mucho mejor · muchas menos dudas · mayor capacidad · más pequeña
Comparison now expands from phrases to quantities and clauses.

[CARD]
TITLE: Que compares; de crosses a threshold
REF: 6.5, 6.6

Use que when two people, things, actions, or qualities are compared. Use de before a number or quantity that functions as a threshold.
TL: El proyecto costó más que la reparación anterior.
EN: The project cost more than the previous repair.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: El proyecto costó más de dos millones de euros.
EN: The project cost more than two million euros.
[PAUSE:1000]
Before a clause, Spanish often needs de lo que, or an agreeing form of del que when an expressed noun supplies gender and number.
TL: El proceso tardó más de lo que esperábamos.
EN: The process took longer than we expected.
SHOW: compare target → que · numerical threshold → de · clause → de lo que or agreeing del que
Flip for superlatives and equality.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Locate the comparison set
REF: 6.3, 6.11, 6.12, 6.15

A true adjectival superlative uses the definite article with más, menos, mejor, or peor. The noun normally carries only one article.
TL: Es la propuesta más viable de las tres.
EN: It is the most viable proposal of the three.
[PAUSE:800]
Equality uses tan before an adjective or adverb, and tanto with nouns or as a stand-alone quantity.
TL: El segundo informe es tan claro como el primero y contiene tantas pruebas como aquel.
EN: The second report is as clear as the first and contains as much evidence as that one.
[PAUSE:900]
For changing scales, use cada vez más or link two comparisons with cuanto.
TL: Cuanto más preciso es el diagnóstico, menos costosa resulta la solución.
EN: The more precise the diagnosis is, the less costly the solution becomes.
SHOW: el más viable · tan claro como · tantas pruebas como · cuanto más, menos
Eighteen full-sentence agreement and adjective-choice items close the unit.
