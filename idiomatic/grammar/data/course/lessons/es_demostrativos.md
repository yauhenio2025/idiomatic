## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Three demonstrative series share one agreement system
REF: 7.1

Spanish divides the pointing words into three series. Este presents something as close to the speaker or current focus, ese marks a less immediate referent, and aquel makes remoteness explicit.
TL: Este informe, esa propuesta y aquellos anexos pertenecen al mismo expediente.
EN: This report, that proposal and those annexes belong to the same file.
[PAUSE:1000]
Each gendered demonstrative agrees with the noun in gender and number. The masculine singular forms are este, ese and aquel, never forms ending in -o.
SHOW: este/esta/estos/estas · ese/esa/esos/esas · aquel/aquella/aquellos/aquellas
Flip for two agreement traps.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Feminine agreement survives a stressed initial a
REF: 7.1

A feminine noun beginning with a stressed a sound still takes a feminine demonstrative, even though its ordinary singular definite article may be el.
TL: Esta agua fría procede de aquella fuente.
EN: This cold water comes from that spring over there.
[PAUSE:900]
Repeat the demonstrative before coordinated nouns when they identify different things. One form can serve two descriptions only when both nouns name the same referent.
TL: Esta arquitecta y este ingeniero presentaron el proyecto.
EN: This architect and this engineer presented the project.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Este investigador y profesor dirigirá el seminario.
EN: This researcher and professor will lead the seminar.
SHOW: grammatical gender controls the form · distinct referents repeat the determiner
Thirteen agreement decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Distance is measured from the conversation
REF: 7.4.1, 7.4.2

Use este for what belongs to the speaker's immediate place, time or topic. Ese is the broad, dependable form for that and can point near the listener or back to an accessible topic.
TL: Conserva este recibo y firma esa copia que tienes delante.
EN: Keep this receipt and sign that copy in front of you.
[PAUSE:1000]
Aquel adds an over-there perspective. It is useful when two distances are contrasted or when remoteness matters to the scene.
TL: No me refiero a esa puerta, sino a aquella del fondo.
EN: I do not mean that door, but the one at the back.
SHOW: este = here/current · ese = there or discourse-accessible · aquel = explicitly remote
Flip for time.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Time creates another near and far axis
REF: 7.4.1, 7.4.2

Este frames the present period. Ese can recall an already mentioned occasion or point toward a future one. Aquel is especially natural for a remote past.
TL: Este trimestre corregiremos los errores detectados ese día.
EN: This quarter we will correct the errors detected that day.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: En aquel verano todavía trabajábamos en el edificio antiguo.
EN: That summer long ago we were still working in the old building.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not use aquel to project remoteness into the future. Ese futuro lejano is coherent; aquel normally looks backward. With a historical present, keep the event close with ese or move the verb into a past tense with aquel.
SHOW: este año · ese día mencionado or futuro · aquel tiempo remoto
Eleven translation decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Before the noun is the neutral position
REF: 7.2

A demonstrative normally comes before its noun and supplies the determiner for the whole noun phrase.
TL: Esa consultora revisó aquellos contratos.
EN: That consultant reviewed those contracts over there.
[PAUSE:900]
In speech, a demonstrative can follow a noun that already has the definite article. This order makes the referent sound familiar, pointed, impatient or sometimes sarcastic.
TL: ¿Has hablado ya con el proveedor ese?
EN: Have you spoken to that supplier we know about yet?
[PAUSE:1000]
Because the effect is marked, use the postposed pattern only when its attitude belongs in the message.
SHOW: neutral: ese proveedor · marked: el proveedor ese
Flip for independent forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A demonstrative can replace an understood noun
REF: 7.1, 7.3, 7.4

When the noun is recoverable, the matching gendered form can stand alone. Agreement keeps the missing noun visible.
TL: La versión final es más clara que esta, pero menos precisa que aquella.
EN: The final version is clearer than this one, but less precise than that one over there.
[PAUSE:1000]
Modern standard spelling does not require an accent to distinguish pronouns from determiners. Write este documento and prefiero este. The neuter forms esto, eso and aquello never take an accent.
SHOW: same written form before a noun or alone · context and agreement reveal the role
The next card separates gendered reference from neuter reference.

[CARD]
TITLE: Gendered forms retrieve a noun; neuter forms package an idea
REF: 7, 8.1, 8.5

Use este, ese or aquel—and their feminine and plural forms—when a specific noun is stated or understood. Use esto, eso or aquello for an unidentified thing, a fact, a remark or a whole situation.
TL: Este es el informe correcto; esto es un simple borrador.
EN: This one is the correct report; this is only a draft.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first demonstrative retrieves the masculine noun informe. The second evaluates whatever is present as a situation or kind of thing.
TL: No esperaba eso de la nueva directora.
EN: I did not expect that from the new director.
SHOW: noun in view → gendered form · proposition or unidentified matter → neuter form
Flip for the human-reference boundary.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Neuter demonstratives are invariable and nonhuman
REF: 8.5

Esto, eso and aquello have no plural and do not agree with a following predicate noun. They refer to a matter first; the predicate then classifies it.
TL: Todo aquello fue una coincidencia extraordinaria.
EN: All that was an extraordinary coincidence.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not introduce an identified person or animal with a neuter form. Choose a gendered demonstrative that retrieves the living referent.
TL: Esta es la médica responsable y ese es su ayudante.
EN: This is the doctor in charge and that is her assistant.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: ¿Qué es esto? · ¿Quién es este?
EN: What is this? · Who is this man?
SHOW: esto/eso/aquello = matter or situation · este/esta = identified living referent
Flip for article-based noun recovery.

[CARD]
TITLE: The article can recover a noun without pointing
REF: 7.5

Spanish often uses el, la, los or las with an adjective or relative clause when the missing noun is already recoverable. This identifies a member of a known class without adding a new distance contrast.
TL: De las dos rutas, elegimos la que evita el centro.
EN: Of the two routes, we chose the one that avoids the city center.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Los que ya hayan firmado pueden salir.
EN: Those who have already signed may leave.
[PAUSE:1000]
English those who therefore does not automatically require aquellos que. Start with the article unless the act of pointing or contrasting is important.
SHOW: recoverable class member → article + modifier · active deixis → demonstrative
Flip for the contexts that favor aquel.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Aquel makes separation or contrast explicit
REF: 7.4.3, 7.5

Use aquel when the intended member is set at a distance from another one, especially before a preposition plus relative clause where repeating the noun would be heavy.
TL: Esta oficina es más luminosa que aquella en la que trabajábamos antes.
EN: This office is brighter than the one in which we used to work.
[PAUSE:1000]
In a two-item written contrast, este can retrieve the latter item and aquel the former because the second mention is textually nearer.
TL: Compararon el tren y el avión: este era más rápido; aquel, más cómodo.
EN: They compared the train and the plane: the latter was faster, the former more comfortable.
[PAUSE:1000]
This device is formal and works best when two clear antecedents make the direction unmistakable.
SHOW: neutral recovery = el/la/los/las · explicit remoteness or paired contrast = aquel
Six article-versus-demonstrative decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Lo turns a quality into an abstract subject
REF: 8.2.1

Lo plus a masculine singular adjective or participle refers to a quality, fact or aspect without naming a masculine noun.
TL: Lo importante es documentar lo sucedido.
EN: The important thing is to document what happened.
[PAUSE:900]
Lo de plus a noun or time expression packages a matter already identifiable in the conversation.
TL: Hablaremos mañana de lo del presupuesto.
EN: Tomorrow we will talk about the budget matter.
[PAUSE:900]
With an adverb, lo can mark the practical limit of an action.
TL: Envíame la versión corregida lo antes posible.
EN: Send me the corrected version as soon as possible.
SHOW: lo + quality/event/matter = a neuter nominal idea
Flip for a different lo construction.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Lo plus adjective plus que measures intensity
REF: 8.2.2

After verbs of noticing, knowing or evaluating, lo can introduce how strongly a quality applies. Here the adjective agrees with the person or thing being described.
TL: No imaginas lo útiles que resultaron esas notas.
EN: You cannot imagine how useful those notes proved.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Todos comentaron lo bien que respondió el equipo.
EN: Everyone commented on how well the team responded.
[PAUSE:1000]
Keep lo invariable, but let an adjective after it reveal the gender and number of its own subject.
SHOW: abstract quality: lo útil · intensity: lo útiles que resultaron
Flip for neuter object lo.

[CARD]
TITLE: Neuter lo resumes an action, fact or predicate
REF: 8.4.1, 8.4.2

As a direct-object pronoun, lo can stand for a whole clause, action or situation rather than for a masculine noun.
TL: Dijeron que cancelarían la reunión, pero nadie lo creyó.
EN: They said they would cancel the meeting, but nobody believed it.
[PAUSE:900]
Lo also resumes the predicate of ser, estar or parecer, where English may leave the complement unspoken.
TL: Parecía imposible, pero no lo era.
EN: It seemed impossible, but it was not.
[PAUSE:1000]
The pronoun preserves the missing proposition or property and prevents the verb from being stranded.
SHOW: clause as direct object → lo · omitted predicate after ser/estar/parecer → lo
Flip for ello.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ello follows a preposition or carries a formal subject
REF: 8.3

Ello is an invariable neuter pronoun for a preceding situation. It is most useful after a preposition and in formal prose as the explicit subject of a new sentence.
TL: Cambiaron las condiciones; por ello revisamos el contrato.
EN: The conditions changed; for that reason we reviewed the contract.
[PAUSE:900]
It is not the direct object form. Use lo for I knew it, and use le when the situation functions as an indirect object.
TL: Todo fue muy confuso y prefiero no hablar de ello.
EN: Everything was very confusing and I prefer not to talk about it.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: direct object = lo · after a preposition or formal subject = ello
Flip for possessive forms.

[CARD]
TITLE: Short possessives agree with the possessed thing
REF: 9.1, 9.2, 9.3.1

Mi, tu and su come before a noun and mark only its number: mi informe, mis informes. Nuestro and vuestro also reveal the noun's gender.
TL: Nuestra propuesta coincide con sus conclusiones.
EN: Our proposal agrees with his, her, your or their conclusions.
[PAUSE:1000]
The form does not copy the gender or number of the owner. Tu and mi carry no written accent in possessive use.
SHOW: mi/mis · tu/tus · su/sus · nuestro/a/os/as · vuestro/a/os/as
Flip for ambiguity and repetition.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Su needs context more than morphology
REF: 9.3.1, 9.3.2, 9.5

Su can mean his, her, its, your or their. When the context cannot identify the owner, replace it or clarify it with de plus a pronoun.
TL: ¿Este despacho es de ella o de ustedes?
EN: Is this office hers or yours?
[PAUSE:900]
Repeat a short possessive before separate people or objects. Use one only when the coordinated nouns describe the same person or closely unified aspects.
TL: Mi hermana y mi hermano llegaron con su amiga y colega.
EN: My sister and my brother arrived with their friend and colleague.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: ambiguous su → de él/ella/usted/ellos/ustedes · separate things repeat the possessive
Flip for long possessives.

[CARD]
TITLE: Long possessives follow a noun or stand alone
REF: 9.2.2, 9.4.1, 9.4.2

Mío, tuyo, suyo, nuestro and vuestro agree fully with the thing possessed. They can follow an indefinite noun or serve as a predicate.
TL: Una colega mía presentó una propuesta suya.
EN: A colleague of mine presented a proposal of his, hers, yours or theirs.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Estas llaves son nuestras; ese paraguas es tuyo.
EN: These keys are ours; that umbrella is yours.
[PAUSE:1000]
When the possessive is the subject or object, or follows a preposition, use the definite article.
TL: No encuentro mis notas; ¿puedo consultar las tuyas?
EN: I cannot find my notes; may I consult yours?
SHOW: noun + mío · ser + mío · take or refer to el mío/la mía/los míos/las mías
Flip for neuter and prepositional choices.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Lo mío names a domain, experience or concern
REF: 9.4.3, 9.7

Lo plus a long possessive packages an abstract matter: one's business, characteristic experience, relationship or proper field.
TL: La programación es lo suyo; la gestión financiera es lo mío.
EN: Programming is her specialty; financial management is mine.
[PAUSE:900]
After spatial adverbs, the broad formal standard uses de plus a pronoun. Regional speech also permits forms such as detrás mío, but their acceptance varies.
TL: El archivo está delante de mí, no detrás de ella.
EN: The file is in front of me, not behind her.
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose the uncontroversial de mí, de ti or de él form when no regional voice is intended.
SHOW: possessed noun → el mío · abstract sphere → lo mío · spatial relation → delante de mí
Flip for article-based possession.

[CARD]
TITLE: Obvious possession usually takes the definite article
REF: 9.3.4

With body parts, clothing and familiar personal objects, Spanish normally uses el, la, los or las when a verb, reflexive pronoun or indirect-object pronoun already identifies the owner.
TL: Me puse la chaqueta y guardé el teléfono en el bolsillo.
EN: I put on my jacket and put my phone in my pocket.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: A Julia le duele la muñeca.
EN: Julia's wrist hurts.
[PAUSE:900]
The article avoids repeating ownership that the sentence has already encoded.
SHOW: owner visible in verb or pronoun → definite article + possessed noun
Flip for contrast and ambiguity.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Restore the possessive when ownership is the point
REF: 9.3.4, 9.5

Use a possessive when no other element identifies the owner, or when the possession is contrasted, corrected or emotionally foregrounded.
TL: Tus gafas están en la mesa; las mías siguen en el coche.
EN: Your glasses are on the table; mine are still in the car.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ponte tu abrigo, no el mío.
EN: Put on your coat, not mine.
[PAUSE:900]
The choice is informational: the article treats ownership as settled background; the possessive makes it part of the message.
SHOW: background owner → article · unknown or contrasted owner → possessive
Review the unit by asking what the form retrieves: a noun, an idea, or an owner.
