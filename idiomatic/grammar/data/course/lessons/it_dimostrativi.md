## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Questo agrees; a singular vowel can trigger elision
REF: 5.1

The basic forms of questo are questo and questa in the singular, questi and queste in the plural. They agree with the noun in gender and number whether they accompany it or replace it.
TL: Questo libro è nuovo, ma queste pagine sono già rovinate.
EN: This book is new, but these pages are already damaged.
[PAUSE:900]
Before a vowel, singular questo or questa may elide to quest apostrophe. Elision is optional, but is especially common when identical vowels would otherwise meet.
TL: Quest'uomo conosce quest'anima inquieta.
EN: This man knows this restless soul.
SHOW: M singular questo or quest' · F singular questa or quest' · M plural questi · F plural queste
Flip for the more elaborate forms of quello.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quello follows the definite-article sound map
REF: 5.1, 4.1

Before a masculine noun, the adjective quello changes like the definite article. Use quel before an ordinary consonant, quello before a special onset such as s plus consonant or z, and quell apostrophe before a vowel.
TL: Quel libro, quello zaino e quell'albergo sono costosi.
EN: That book, that backpack, and that hotel are expensive.
[PAUSE:900]
The plurals are quei before ordinary consonants and quegli before vowels or special onsets. Feminine forms are quella or quell apostrophe in the singular, and quelle in the plural.
TL: Quei libri sono accanto a quegli zaini e a quelle valigie.
EN: Those books are next to those backpacks and those suitcases.
SHOW: quel/quei like il/i · quello/quegli like lo/gli · quell'/quegli like l'/gli · quella or quell'/quelle

[CARD]
TITLE: Questo presents closeness; quello presents distance
REF: 5.2

Questo is oriented toward the speaker or writer. It can mark something close in space or time, something closely associated with the speaker, or the item most recently mentioned in a text.
TL: Prendi questa scala qui; io aspetto su questo pianerottolo.
EN: Take these stairs here; I will wait on this landing.
[PAUSE:900]
Quello is not oriented toward the speaker and commonly points to what is farther away, earlier, or less immediately involved.
TL: Poi continua fino a quei negozi laggiù.
EN: Then continue as far as those shops down there.
SHOW: speaker-close or most recent -> questo · farther or less immediate -> quello
Flip to reinforce the contrast and recognize marked alternatives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Qui and lì reinforce distance; codesto marks the addressee
REF: 5.2, 5.3

A noun phrase can add qui or qua to reinforce questo, and lì or là to reinforce quello.
TL: Vuoi questo tovagliolo qui o quello lì?
EN: Do you want this napkin here or that one there?
[PAUSE:900]
Codesto or cotesto centers the reference on the addressee. It remains current for many Tuscan speakers, but elsewhere sounds archaic or bureaucratic. Informal sto, sta, sti, and ste instead shorten questo and are avoided in formal writing.
TL: In una lettera formale: rispondo a codesta richiesta.
EN: In a formal letter: I am replying to that request of yours.
SHOW: qui/qua reinforces questo · lì/là reinforces quello · codesto = addressee-oriented and region/register marked · 'sto = informal

[CARD]
TITLE: A demonstrative pronoun carries the missing noun's features
REF: 5.4

Without a following noun, questo and quello still agree with the thing or person they replace. They are especially common for non-human referents in ordinary speech.
TL: Quale cornice compri? Questa qui, non quella grande.
EN: Which frame are you buying? This one here, not the large one.
[PAUSE:900]
They can refer to people, but a bare demonstrative may sound detached, dismissive, or insulting. Lui, lei, and loro are safer when the intended reference is a person, especially a named person.
TL: Dopo che Carlo ci ha visti, lui è scoppiato a ridere.
EN: After Carlo saw us, he burst out laughing.
SHOW: thing -> demonstrative pronoun is ordinary · person -> check attitude and register
Flip when the referent is an entire statement rather than a noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A whole proposition takes masculine singular reference
REF: 5.7

Masculine singular questo or quello can point back to an assertion, question, or event. Italian often chooses questo where English says that.
TL: Non vogliono rimborsarci. Questo non lo accetto.
EN: They do not want to refund us. I will not accept that.
[PAUSE:900]
Ciò can perform the same job in a more formal or literary register. Per questo and perciò mean for that reason or therefore. In an unstressed question meaning what does that mean, Italian normally omits the pronoun.
TL: Mi hanno richiamato a Roma; per questo non ho telefonato.
EN: They called me back to Rome; that is why I did not phone.
SHOW: previous proposition -> questo/quello M singular · formal alternative -> ciò · consequence -> per questo/perciò · meaning question -> che vuol dire?

[CARD]
TITLE: Quello can replace a class, group, or repeated noun
REF: 5.6, 5.9

Colloquial quello or quelli followed by di identifies the person or people associated with a place, service, or group.
TL: Sono venuti quelli del gas.
EN: The gas-company people have come.
[PAUSE:900]
Before an adjective, a demonstrative pronoun can replace a repeated noun. Keep the full pronoun form: quello francese means the French one, whereas quel francese means that Frenchman or that French item expressed as a noun.
TL: Preferisco i mobili antichi a quelli moderni.
EN: I prefer the antique furniture to the modern furniture.
SHOW: quello/quelli di + group -> associated person/people · quello + adjective -> the adjective one
Flip for two ways in which quello can point to an unchanged identity.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Essere quello can mean unchanged or no longer oneself
REF: 5.8, 5.9

With essere or sembrare, quello can mean what someone or something has always been. The construction focuses on persistence or a failure to change.
TL: Purtroppo la situazione è sempre quella.
EN: Unfortunately, the situation is still the same.
[PAUSE:900]
In a negative comparison with an earlier self, non essere or non sembrare più quello means no longer to be what one used to be.
TL: Dopo la malattia non sembra più quello.
EN: After the illness, he no longer seems himself.
SHOW: essere sempre quello -> remain unchanged · non sembrare più quello -> no longer be one's former self

[CARD]
TITLE: Questi and quegli are rare singular human subjects
REF: 5.5

In formal prose, singular questi can refer to a previously mentioned male person, often the most recently mentioned one. It is a subject form, not the ordinary plural these.
TL: Marco chiamò Paolo; questi rispose subito.
EN: Marco called Paolo; the latter replied at once.
[PAUSE:900]
Singular quegli can similarly mean the former or he, but is now rare. In ordinary communication, rewrite with a name, lui, or a clearer noun phrase rather than imitating this literary contrast.
TL: Nel linguaggio corrente: Paolo rispose subito.
EN: In current language: Paolo replied at once.
SHOW: questi singular -> latter male person, formal · quegli singular -> former male person, rare
Flip for compact discourse replies and idioms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Questo can form a complete reply or a fixed exclamation
REF: 5.10, 5.11

Questo sì and questo no answer whether a previously mentioned action or fact is true, without repeating a verb.
TL: Ti hanno pagato? Questo sì, ma non abbastanza.
EN: Did they pay you? Yes, they did, but not enough.
[PAUSE:900]
Feminine questa appears in fixed ironic or surprised exclamations. These are lexical expressions, so learn their form rather than deriving the gender from a visible noun.
TL: Questa è bella! Non me l'aspettavo.
EN: Well, that is a surprise! I was not expecting it.
SHOW: questo sì/no -> compact reply · questa è bella/nuova -> fixed reaction

[CARD]
TITLE: Costui points to a human in formal or marked language
REF: 5.12

Costui and costei are singular human pronouns; costoro is plural for either gender. They are restricted to formal or written language and can sound pejorative when used to point at a present person.
TL: Se non ho parlato di costei, è perché non la conosco bene.
EN: If I have not spoken about that woman, it is because I do not know her well.
[PAUSE:900]
Colui, colei, and coloro are very rare as independent demonstratives today, although colui remains useful in relative constructions taught separately.
TL: Ma chi si crede di essere costui?
EN: Just who does that fellow think he is?
SHOW: costui/costei/costoro -> human, formal, sometimes pejorative · colui as a bare demonstrative -> old-fashioned
Flip from pointing at a person to describing a kind or degree.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Così expresses manner, kind, or degree
REF: 5.13

Before an adjective or adverb, così means so or to such a degree. A result can follow with che.
TL: Era così lontano che non si vedeva.
EN: It was so far away that it could not be seen.
[PAUSE:900]
After a noun or adjective, così can mean like that or of that kind. With a noun plus adjective, Italian places così after the adjective: un film così triste.
TL: Non parteciperei mai a un progetto così.
EN: I would never take part in a project like that.
SHOW: così + adjective + che -> so ... that · noun/adjective + così -> like that, of that degree

[CARD]
TITLE: Tale presents such a kind; position changes with its complement
REF: 5.13

Tale normally precedes the noun. Un tale problema means such a problem, and plural tali can refer back to a previously mentioned class.
TL: Non avrei mai creduto a una tale storia.
EN: I would never have believed such a story.
[PAUSE:900]
When che or da expands the degree, tale follows the noun or adjective phrase. Talmente is the adverbial form and can introduce the same kind of result.
TL: Era un rumore tale da svegliare tutto il quartiere.
EN: It was such a noise that it could wake the whole neighborhood.
SHOW: un tale + noun -> such a ... · noun + tale + che/da -> such ... that/to · talmente -> so, to such a degree
Flip for alternatives and the special vocabulary of official documents.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Simile and del genere are current; suddetto belongs to documents
REF: 5.13, 5.14

Simile or del genere can replace tale when the meaning is of that kind. Siffatto is formal and uncommon; cotale is archaic.
TL: Non farei mai una cosa del genere.
EN: I would never do anything like that.
[PAUSE:900]
Written legal or bureaucratic texts use suddetto, summenzionato, anzidetto, or suesposto for the above-mentioned. They are text-internal labels, not neutral conversational demonstratives.
TL: Per i motivi suddetti non posso accettare.
EN: For the above-mentioned reasons, I cannot accept.
SHOW: simile/del genere -> current kind comparison · siffatto/cotale -> marked · suddetto family -> legal/bureaucratic reference

[CARD]
TITLE: Qui and lì point; qua and là indicate an area or direction
REF: 5.15

Qui and qua are speaker-oriented here forms; lì and là are non-speaker-oriented there forms. The i forms tend to identify a focused point, while the a forms suit a broader area or direction.
TL: Metti la firma qui e poi aspetta là.
EN: Put your signature here and then wait over there.
[PAUSE:900]
That contrast explains common directional expressions such as qua e là, di qua, di là, più in qua, and più in là. The addressee-oriented costì and costà remain mainly Tuscan or old-fashioned elsewhere.
TL: Non passare di là; il sentiero è chiuso.
EN: Do not go that way; the path is closed.
SHOW: qui/lì -> focused point · qua/là -> broader area or direction · costì/costà -> addressee-oriented and marked
Flip to combine these words with another locative term.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Place demonstratives precede locative terms
REF: 5.16

Qui, qua, lì, and là can come before accanto, davanti, fuori, vicino, dentro, dietro, sotto, sopra, and similar terms.
TL: C'è un bar qui vicino? La valigia è là dentro.
EN: Is there a bar nearby? The suitcase is in there.
[PAUSE:900]
Qua and là also combine into quassù, lassù, quaggiù, and laggiù. These forms join direction with the speaker-centered here or the more distant there.
TL: Portate le scatole quassù e lasciate le biciclette laggiù.
EN: Bring the boxes up here and leave the bicycles down there.
SHOW: qui vicino · lì dietro · qua sotto · là dentro · quassù/lassù · quaggiù/laggiù

[CARD]
TITLE: Stesso marks the very same identity or an emphatic participant
REF: 5.17

With a definite article before a noun, stesso can identify the selfsame entity. It usually precedes the noun in this reading.
TL: Abbiamo letto lo stesso articolo.
EN: We read the same article.
[PAUSE:900]
Placed after the noun, stesso can instead mean the noun itself. With a person it emphasizes that participant and no other.
TL: Abbiamo discusso l'articolo stesso con l'autrice stessa.
EN: We discussed the article itself with the author herself.
SHOW: article + stesso + noun -> the same entity · noun + stesso -> the noun itself
Flip to attach the emphasis to pronouns and possessives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Stesso agrees with the noun or pronoun it emphasizes
REF: 5.17

After a personal pronoun, stesso agrees with its referent: io stesso or io stessa, voi stessi or voi stesse, loro stessi or loro stesse.
TL: Me l'avete detto voi stessi.
EN: You yourselves told me.
[PAUSE:900]
Stesso can also reinforce a possessive and mean very own. Agreement is with the possessed noun, not the possessor.
TL: Aveva dimenticato il suo stesso nome e la sua stessa età.
EN: He had forgotten his own name and his own age.
SHOW: pronoun + stesso -> emphatic person · possessive + stesso + noun -> very own · agreement follows the modified word

[CARD]
TITLE: Reflexive sé and emphatic stesso do different jobs
REF: 5.18

A stressed reflexive pronoun points back to the subject as the participant affected by the action. Stesso instead emphasizes identity; it does not create reflexive reference.
TL: Anna parla di sé; Anna stessa parla.
EN: Anna talks about herself; Anna herself speaks.
[PAUSE:900]
The two can combine when the reflexive referent is also emphatic. In se stesso, se stessa, se stessi, and se stesse, se normally loses its written accent.
TL: I giudici criticano se stessi.
EN: The judges criticize themselves, their very selves.
SHOW: action returns to subject -> sé · participant and no other -> stesso · emphatic reflexive -> se stesso
Flip for the cases where a full stressed form is required.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use a full form when identity is predicated or shifts objects
REF: 5.18

After essere, sembrare, or diventare, use a full stressed form plus stesso when the meaning is be or become oneself.
TL: Dopo il riposo potrai ridiventare te stessa.
EN: After the rest, you will be able to become yourself again.
[PAUSE:900]
A full se stesso form also avoids ambiguity when the reflexive refers to the object of the main clause rather than its subject.
TL: Giovanni lo costringeva a parlare di se stesso.
EN: Giovanni forced him to talk about himself.
SHOW: predicate identity -> me/te/se stesso · embedded reference to main-clause object -> full se stesso

[CARD]
TITLE: English self can mean personally or without help
REF: 5.19

When English myself means as for me or personally, Italian usually focuses the viewpoint with personalmente, quanto a me, or per quanto mi riguarda, not with stesso.
TL: Personalmente, preferirei restare a casa.
EN: As for myself, I would prefer to stay home.
[PAUSE:900]
When self means alone or unassisted, use da plus a stressed pronoun or da solo. Again, stesso is not the automatic translation.
TL: Ho dovuto fare tutto da me, completamente da sola.
EN: I had to do everything by myself, completely alone.
SHOW: viewpoint self -> personalmente/quanto a · unassisted self -> da me/te/sé or da solo · identity emphasis -> stesso
Flip to separate sameness of identity from similarity.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Stesso can identify one thing; uguale means an equal counterpart
REF: 5.20

Stesso can mean the very same item, though context can sometimes allow a same-design reading. Uguale expresses equivalence or similarity, not shared identity.
TL: Io e lei usavamo lo stesso foulard.
EN: She and I used the same scarf.
[PAUSE:900]
With uguale, the ordinary pattern uses an indefinite article and presents a separate but matching item.
TL: Io avevo un foulard uguale al suo.
EN: I had a scarf identical to hers.
SHOW: article + stesso -> same identity or same specified type · indefinite + noun + uguale -> a matching counterpart

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose the meaning before choosing the form
REF: 5.17, 5.18, 5.19, 5.20

Start by asking which relationship matters. Shared identity selects stesso; reflexive reference selects sé; acting without help selects da solo or da plus a stressed pronoun; mere resemblance selects uguale.
TL: Marta stessa ha riparato la stessa bicicletta da sola.
EN: Marta herself repaired the same bicycle by herself.
[PAUSE:900]
Each expression answers a different question: who in particular, which exact entity, and with whose help. Keeping those questions separate prevents the broad English word self from collapsing distinct Italian constructions.
SHOW: emphatic participant -> stesso · exact entity -> lo stesso · reflexive -> sé · unassisted -> da solo · similar counterpart -> uguale
Flip for a final register check.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Current Italian keeps the core small and marks the alternatives
REF: 5.1, 5.3, 5.5, 5.12, 5.14

For neutral current usage, rely on questo and quello, qui or qua, lì or là, stesso, and uguale. Their agreement and word order carry most everyday contrasts.
TL: Questa proposta è migliore di quella, ma entrambe sono simili.
EN: This proposal is better than that one, but both are similar.
[PAUSE:900]
Treat codesto, singular questi or quegli, costui, colui, cotale, and the suddetto family as register signals. Use them when region, genre, or tone calls for them, not merely as decorative synonyms.
TL: Nel parlato neutro: quella persona; nel burocratico: il suddetto.
EN: In neutral speech: that person; in bureaucratic language: the above-mentioned person.
SHOW: neutral core -> questo/quello, place forms, stesso/uguale · marked forms -> identify region, formality, or genre first
