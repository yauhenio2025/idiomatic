## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Mood tells you how a clause enters the conversation
REF: 20.1

The indicative normally presents a clause as information the speaker is putting forward. The subjunctive places the clause inside another stance: a wish, command, reaction, denial, purpose, or open possibility.
TL: Sé que Laura llega hoy.
EN: I know that Laura is arriving today.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Quiero que Laura llegue hoy.
EN: I want Laura to arrive today.
[PAUSE:1000]
The arrival can be equally possible in both sentences. What changes is its conversational status: sé que asserts it, while quiero que presents it as the content of a desire.
SHOW: indicative = proposition asserted · subjunctive = proposition held inside a stance
Flip for a better diagnostic than memorizing trigger words.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Read the relationship, not one magic word
REF: 20.1.1, 20.1.4

A nearby word may be a useful signal, but the whole construction decides the mood. Ask what the speaker does with the subordinate proposition.
TL: Aunque está cansada, termina el informe.
EN: Although she is tired, she finishes the report.
[PAUSE:900]
The indicative treats her tiredness as a fact in the conversation.
TL: Aunque esté cansada, terminará el informe.
EN: Even if she is tired, she will finish the report.
[PAUSE:1000]
The subjunctive refuses to make tiredness part of the assertion; the conclusion holds either way.
SHOW: assertion · reaction · influence · denial · purpose · pending event
Flip for possibility and probability.

[CARD]
TITLE: Possibility can be asserted or left open
REF: 20.2.1, 20.3.5

An impersonal expression of possibility normally introduces a subjunctive que clause.
TL: Es posible que cambien la fecha.
EN: It is possible that they will change the date.
[PAUSE:900]
With quizá, tal vez, posiblemente, and probablemente, both moods can occur. The indicative leans toward the speaker's acceptance of the possibility; the subjunctive leaves more distance.
TL: Quizá Marta está en casa.
EN: Perhaps Marta is at home.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Quizá Marta esté en casa.
EN: Marta may be at home.
[PAUSE:1000]
By contrast, a lo mejor, igual, and lo mismo normally take the indicative.
SHOW: es posible que + subjunctive · quizá + stance choice · a lo mejor + indicative
Flip to locate the possible event in time.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mood and tense answer different questions
REF: 20.2.1, 20.3.2

Mood marks stance; tense locates the event relative to the viewpoint. A present subjunctive can describe a present state or a future event.
TL: Puede que Elena esté ocupada ahora.
EN: Elena may be busy now.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Puede que Elena llegue mañana.
EN: Elena may arrive tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Use the perfect subjunctive when the uncertain event is viewed as already complete.
TL: Puede que Elena haya salido ya.
EN: Elena may have left already.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not choose a subjunctive tense by copying the tense name in English. Establish the time relation after you identify the clause's status.
SHOW: stance first · then present, prior, or remote time
Three hygiene-clean probability responses follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Influence points from one participant to another
REF: 20.3.7

Verbs of wanting, requesting, ordering, allowing, and preventing commonly take que plus subjunctive when one participant tries to shape another participant's action.
TL: La directora quiere que revisemos las cifras.
EN: The director wants us to review the figures.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Les pidieron que apagaran los teléfonos.
EN: They asked them to turn off their phones.
[PAUSE:1000]
The subordinate event is not reported as a fact. It is the desired or commanded outcome of the main verb.
SHOW: person A influences person B → que + subjunctive
Flip for direct and reported commands.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A reported command remains a command
REF: 20.3.7, 20.3.8

When a direct imperative becomes indirect speech, Spanish does not turn it into a factual statement. It becomes a que clause in the subjunctive.
TL: «Envíame el borrador hoy».
EN: Send me the draft today.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Me pidió que le enviara el borrador aquel día.
EN: She asked me to send her the draft that day.
[PAUSE:1000]
Permission and prohibition can use the same architecture.
TL: No permiten que los visitantes entren sin identificación.
EN: They do not allow visitors to enter without identification.
[PAUSE:900]
Person, time, and viewpoint may shift in the report, but the directive force survives.
SHOW: imperative → pedir, ordenar, permitir, impedir que + subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Shared subjects prefer the infinitive
REF: 20.3.7, 20.3.10

When one participant controls both actions, Spanish usually avoids a finite subordinate clause and uses an infinitive.
TL: Quiero revisar el contrato.
EN: I want to review the contract.
[PAUSE:900]
Introduce a different performer and the full que clause becomes necessary.
TL: Quiero que revises el contrato.
EN: I want you to review the contract.
[PAUSE:1000]
The choice is not simply shorter versus longer. It records whether the two predicates share a grammatical subject.
SHOW: same subject → infinitive · different subject → que + subjunctive
Flip for reactions with the same contrast.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reactions also alternate by subject
REF: 20.3.10

An emotion or evaluation can target the speaker's own action, in which case an infinitive is natural.
TL: Me alegra volver a verte.
EN: I am glad to see you again.
[PAUSE:800]
When the reaction targets a separately expressed proposition, use que and the subjunctive.
TL: Me alegra que hayas venido.
EN: I am glad that you came.
[PAUSE:1000]
Impersonal evaluations follow the same finite pattern.
TL: Es importante que todos conozcan el procedimiento.
EN: It is important that everyone know the procedure.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: reaction to own action → infinitive · reaction to a clause → subjunctive
Fifteen infinitive-versus-clause choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Reaction does not mean disbelief
REF: 20.3.10

The subjunctive after an emotional reaction or value judgement does not imply that the event is false. The speaker can take it for granted while choosing to comment on it instead of asserting it.
TL: Lamento que hayan cancelado la reunión.
EN: I am sorry that they cancelled the meeting.
[PAUSE:900]
The cancellation is presupposed. The new contribution is the regret.
TL: Es extraño que nadie haya respondido.
EN: It is strange that nobody has replied.
[PAUSE:1000]
Again, the speaker evaluates a situation rather than presenting it as neutral news.
SHOW: known or presupposed event + reaction → subjunctive
Flip to contrast evaluation with an assertion.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Factual evaluation and factual assertion are different acts
REF: 20.3.10

An impersonal phrase that asserts certainty normally takes the indicative.
TL: Es evidente que el plazo termina mañana.
EN: It is obvious that the deadline ends tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Change the main phrase into a judgement and the subordinate mood changes.
TL: Es absurdo que el plazo termine mañana.
EN: It is absurd that the deadline ends tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Both speakers may believe exactly the same calendar fact. Evidente puts it forward as true; absurdo reacts to it.
TL: Es una suerte que todavía tengamos tiempo.
EN: It is fortunate that we still have time.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: certainty asserted → indicative · evaluation of proposition → subjunctive
Twelve reaction and value-judgement transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Belief verbs reveal whether a claim is upheld
REF: 20.3.21, 20.3.22

An affirmative belief or report usually presents its complement as part of the current assertion.
TL: Creo que Ana tiene la llave.
EN: I think Ana has the key.
[PAUSE:900]
Negating or seriously challenging that belief normally withdraws the assertion and licenses the subjunctive.
TL: No creo que Ana tenga la llave.
EN: I do not think Ana has the key.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: ¿Crees que Ana tenga la llave?
EN: Do you really think Ana has the key?
[PAUSE:1000]
In questions, mood can convey the questioner's stance; the indicative remains natural when the question neutrally seeks information.
SHOW: claim upheld → indicative · claim rejected or challenged → subjunctive
Flip for negative questions and directives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Negation is not an automatic switch
REF: 20.3.16, 20.3.21

Read what the negation scopes over. A negative question may invite confirmation of an expected fact and keep the indicative.
TL: ¿No ves que estamos ocupados?
EN: Can't you see that we are busy?
[PAUSE:900]
A negative directive can instead place the subordinate action outside what is asserted or permitted.
TL: No creas que el plan sea imposible sin estudiarlo.
EN: Do not think that the plan is impossible without studying it.
[PAUSE:1000]
Some expressions change interpretation with mood, and regional preferences vary. The reliable question is whether the subordinate proposition is being endorsed in this context.
SHOW: locate the negation · identify assertion · then choose mood

[CARD]
TITLE: A fact phrase can still frame an evaluation
REF: 20.3.19

El hecho de que does not mechanically demand either mood. The indicative can foreground the clause as information; the subjunctive often packages it as an accepted basis for a reaction or argument.
TL: El hecho es que faltan dos documentos.
EN: The fact is that two documents are missing.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El hecho de que falten dos documentos no invalida la solicitud.
EN: The fact that two documents are missing does not invalidate the application.
[PAUSE:1000]
In the second sentence, the missing documents are backgrounded inside the subject phrase. The main assertion concerns their consequence.
SHOW: fact asserted · fact packaged as background
Flip for nouns followed by de que.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Nouns can carry the same stance as verbs
REF: 20.3.20

A noun expressing possibility, fear, hope, or need can select a de que clause just as a related verb or adjective would.
TL: Existe la posibilidad de que cambien las condiciones.
EN: There is a possibility that the conditions will change.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nos preocupa el riesgo de que el río se desborde.
EN: We are worried about the risk that the river may overflow.
[PAUSE:1000]
A noun that simply reports established content can instead introduce an indicative clause.
TL: Recibimos la noticia de que el puente estaba cerrado.
EN: We received the news that the bridge was closed.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: stance noun → subjunctive · informational content → indicative
Fifteen factive and noun-clause completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Purpose points toward an unrealized result
REF: 20.4.2, 20.4.3

A purpose clause describes an intended result, not an accomplished fact. With a different subject, use para que and the subjunctive.
TL: Te envío un esquema para que veas la estructura.
EN: I am sending you an outline so that you can see the structure.
[PAUSE:900]
When the subject is shared, use para plus infinitive.
TL: Leo el informe otra vez para comprobar las cifras.
EN: I am reading the report again to check the figures.
[PAUSE:1000]
The same subject distinction appears after antes de and antes de que, sin and sin que, and other subordinating expressions.
SHOW: same subject → subordinator + infinitive · different subject → que + subjunctive
Flip for causes, manner, and comparison.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A denied cause is not presented as the cause
REF: 20.4.4, 20.4.5

Porque with the indicative supplies a cause the speaker endorses.
TL: Cerraron la carretera porque nevaba.
EN: They closed the road because it was snowing.
[PAUSE:800]
No porque with the subjunctive rejects a proposed explanation.
TL: Cerraron la carretera, no porque nevara, sino porque había un accidente.
EN: They closed the road not because it was snowing, but because there had been an accident.
[PAUSE:1000]
Como si always frames a comparison as unreal or hypothetical and uses an imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive.
TL: Habla como si conociera todos los detalles.
EN: She talks as if she knew all the details.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: accepted cause → indicative · rejected cause or unreal comparison → subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Time clauses look backward or forward
REF: 20.4.7

With cuando and similar subordinators, use the indicative for a completed event or a habitual relation.
TL: Cuando terminó la reunión, llamé a Marta.
EN: When the meeting ended, I called Marta.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Cuando termina la reunión, siempre llama a Marta.
EN: When the meeting ends, she always calls Marta.
[PAUSE:900]
Use the subjunctive when the subordinate event is pending and serves as a future checkpoint.
TL: Cuando termine la reunión, llamaré a Marta.
EN: When the meeting ends, I will call Marta.
[PAUSE:1000]
The future meaning comes from the whole sentence; Spanish does not put a future indicative after cuando in this pattern.
SHOW: completed or habitual → indicative · pending checkpoint → subjunctive
Flip for sequence within the subjunctive system.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Choose tense from the viewpoint, not a rigid sequence
REF: 20.3.2, 20.8

A present viewpoint commonly pairs with present or perfect subjunctive.
TL: Espero que llegues a tiempo.
EN: I hope you arrive on time.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Me alegra que hayas llegado a tiempo.
EN: I am glad you arrived on time.
[PAUSE:900]
A past or conditional viewpoint often attracts imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive.
TL: Esperaba que llegaras a tiempo.
EN: I hoped you would arrive on time.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Me habría alegrado que hubieras llegado a tiempo.
EN: I would have been glad if you had arrived on time.
[PAUSE:1000]
Meaning can override a mechanical sequence: preserve the actual temporal relationship.
SHOW: simultaneous/later · already complete · present viewpoint · past viewpoint
Nine clean time-clause transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Conditions and exceptions keep outcomes open
REF: 20.4.8

Expressions such as a menos que, con tal de que, siempre que, and en caso de que introduce a condition or exception not yet realized, so they normally take the subjunctive.
TL: Publicaremos el informe mañana, a menos que surja un problema.
EN: We will publish the report tomorrow unless a problem arises.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Te acompaño con tal de que volvamos antes de las diez.
EN: I will come with you provided that we return before ten.
[PAUSE:1000]
The main clause remains dependent on an open circumstance.
SHOW: pending condition or exception → subjunctive
Flip for simultaneity and concession.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Known facts and hypothetical cases divide the moods
REF: 20.4.9, 20.4.10

Mientras can describe an established simultaneous event with the indicative or impose a future condition with the subjunctive.
TL: Mientras trabajaba, escuchaba la radio.
EN: While she was working, she listened to the radio.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Puedes quedarte mientras no hagas ruido.
EN: You may stay as long as you do not make noise.
[PAUSE:900]
Aunque makes the same stance contrast.
TL: Aunque llueve, salimos.
EN: Although it is raining, we are going out.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Aunque llueva, saldremos.
EN: Even if it rains, we will go out.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first concedes a fact; the second says the result holds under either possibility.
SHOW: known circumstance → indicative · open or irrelevant circumstance → subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Relative-clause mood frames the referent
REF: 20.6

Use the indicative when the speaker identifies or describes an established referent.
TL: Conozco a una traductora que habla islandés.
EN: I know a translator who speaks Icelandic.
[PAUSE:900]
Use the subjunctive when the referent is sought, hypothetical, denied, or not yet established.
TL: Busco una traductora que hable islandés.
EN: I am looking for a translator who speaks Icelandic.
[PAUSE:1000]
The noun phrase does not have to be objectively nonexistent. The subjunctive shows that the speaker has not selected a particular person in this discourse.
SHOW: identified referent → indicative · open description → subjunctive
Flip for negative and future referents.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Existence is a discourse choice
REF: 20.6

Negation often removes any established referent and therefore favors the subjunctive.
TL: No hay nadie que pueda autorizar el pago.
EN: There is nobody who can authorize the payment.
[PAUSE:900]
A required future object is likewise defined by a property rather than identified by name.
TL: Necesitamos un sistema que funcione sin conexión.
EN: We need a system that works offline.
[PAUSE:900]
But a definite noun can still take the subjunctive if the requested object has not been identified.
TL: Busco el informe que explique esta diferencia.
EN: I am looking for the report that explains this difference.
[PAUSE:1000]
Articles are clues, not mood switches. Ask whether the speaker commits to a particular referent.
SHOW: specific and established · versus required, denied, or still to be found
Fifteen relative-clause choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Free-choice patterns refuse to select one case
REF: 20.5

Spanish often repeats a subjunctive form around a relative word to mean that the result is unchanged across every possible case.
TL: Pase lo que pase, terminaremos el proyecto.
EN: Whatever happens, we will finish the project.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Vayas donde vayas, lleva una copia del documento.
EN: Wherever you go, take a copy of the document.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Sea quien sea, no abras la puerta.
EN: Whoever it is, do not open the door.
[PAUSE:1000]
The construction deliberately leaves the person, place, or event unidentified.
SHOW: pase lo que pase · vayas donde vayas · sea quien sea
Flip for wishes and a final decision path.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Wishes build the desired world directly
REF: 20.2.5, Ch. 20

Ojalá and independent que clauses can express a wish without a main verb.
TL: Ojalá llegue el paquete hoy.
EN: I hope the package arrives today.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: ¡Que tengas buen viaje!
EN: Have a good trip!
[PAUSE:800]
Remote past forms can mark a wish as contrary to fact or now impossible.
TL: Ojalá hubiéramos salido antes.
EN: If only we had left earlier.
[PAUSE:1000]
For every new clause, use the same path: identify assertion status, identify the relation between participants or events, then place the event in time.
SHOW: What is asserted? · What stance contains the clause? · When does it happen?
