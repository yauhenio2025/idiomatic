## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A noun carries a lexical profile
REF: 1, 1.1

A French noun can head a subject, an object or a prepositional phrase. Its meaning may be concrete or abstract, countable or mass, common or proper, but every use still needs a lexical profile.
TL: Cette patience remarquable a rassuré toute l'équipe.
EN: This remarkable patience reassured the whole team.
[PAUSE:900]
The head noun is abstract, feminine and singular. The determiner and adjective expose those grammatical features.
TL: Plusieurs solutions concrètes restent possibles.
EN: Several concrete solutions remain possible.
[PAUSE:900]
The second head is countable and plural, so its surrounding words change with it.
SHOW: meaning class + gender + number = the noun's working profile
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Store a noun with the evidence it needs
REF: 1.1.1, 1.1.2, 1.1.3, 1.1.4

A bare translation conceals gender and often conceals countability. Learn the singular with a determiner, then record the plural when the noun has one.
TL: une décision, des décisions
EN: a decision, decisions
[PAUSE:700]
TL: le courage
EN: courage
[PAUSE:700]
TL: les environs
EN: the surrounding area
[PAUSE:900]
The last noun is lexically plural. Proper names behave differently again: most identify a unique referent and normally appear without an indefinite article.
TL: Marseille attire de nouveaux chercheurs.
EN: Marseille attracts new researchers.
SHOW: learn determiner + singular + plural, not a translation alone

[CARD]
TITLE: General meaning often calls for the definite article
REF: 1.1.1, 2.2, 2.3

French commonly uses the definite article when a noun names a whole class, a unique phenomenon or an abstract quality in general.
TL: La patience facilite les négociations difficiles.
EN: Patience makes difficult negotiations easier.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Les abeilles jouent un rôle essentiel dans cet écosystème.
EN: Bees play an essential role in this ecosystem.
[PAUSE:1000]
English may use no article in either sentence. Translate the meaning first, then choose the French noun phrase.
SHOW: general class or quality → often le, la or les
Flip for a particular instance.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A particular instance changes the packaging
REF: 1.1.1, 1.1.2, 2.3, 2.4

When an abstract or mass idea is bounded, described as one instance or measured as a quantity, an indefinite or partitive form may appear.
TL: Elle a montré une patience étonnante pendant l'entretien.
EN: She showed remarkable patience during the interview.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Il nous faut du temps et une méthode précise.
EN: We need time and a precise method.
[PAUSE:1000]
Countability is a choice in context, not simply a property copied from English. Identify whether the speaker presents a class, an instance or an unbounded substance.
SHOW: class · particular instance · unbounded quantity
Nine article-in-context decisions come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Endings give probabilities, not verdicts
REF: 1.2, 1.2.1

French has two noun genders. Written form can narrow the choice: many consonant-final nouns are masculine, while many nouns ending in an unaccented e are feminine.
TL: Le rapport complet arrivera demain.
EN: The complete report will arrive tomorrow.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La réponse définitive dépend du comité.
EN: The final answer depends on the committee.
[PAUSE:1000]
These are broad tendencies. Frequent exceptions make the article the safest evidence.
SHOW: ending suggests · determiner and agreement confirm
Flip for stronger families.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Derivational families compress the lexicon
REF: 1.2.1

Some endings carry stronger gender tendencies. Nouns in -tion, -sion and -aison are usually feminine; those in -isme and -age are usually masculine.
TL: La modernisation exige un financement durable.
EN: Modernization requires sustainable funding.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Le tourisme transforme parfois le paysage local.
EN: Tourism sometimes transforms the local landscape.
[PAUSE:1000]
Exceptions remain lexical: une image and une plage do not follow the usual -age tendency. Store a familiar exception with its article instead of weakening the productive pattern.
SHOW: productive family + short exception list
Six clean gender-and-determiner sentences follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Human nouns use more than one pattern
REF: 1.2.2, 1.2.3, 1.2.9

Some human-denoting nouns keep one spoken and written form while the determiner changes. Others build a distinct feminine form.
TL: Un architecte et une architecte ont présenté le projet.
EN: A male architect and a female architect presented the project.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Le boulanger travaille avec une boulangère expérimentée.
EN: The baker works with an experienced female baker.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn which pattern belongs to each noun. A final e does not by itself tell you whether the noun is feminine or common in gender.
SHOW: determiner-only contrast · form-changing pair
Flip for grammatical gender without natural-gender matching.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reference does not rewrite agreement
REF: 1.2.2, 1.2.3, 1.2.9

A few nouns keep one grammatical gender even when they identify people of different sexes. Agreement stays with the lexical noun.
TL: La victime était consciente quand les secours sont arrivés.
EN: The victim was conscious when the emergency services arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ce témoin est arrivé tôt; cette témoin est arrivée plus tard.
EN: This male witness arrived early; this female witness arrived later.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first noun is always feminine. The second can be masculine or feminine through its determiner and agreement without changing form.
SHOW: identify the noun's pattern before propagating agreement
The fully flagged human-noun set is conservatively omitted.

[CARD]
TITLE: Gender can select a different lexical entry
REF: 1.2.4, 1.2.5

Some identical spellings name different things when their gender changes. The article is then part of the meaning, not decorative agreement.
TL: Le livre est resté sur la table.
EN: The book remained on the table.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Une livre correspond à une ancienne unité de poids.
EN: A pound corresponds to an old unit of weight.
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat these as two vocabulary records. Each record has its own meaning and distribution.
SHOW: same spelling + new gender = new lexical entry
Flip for a contrast inside one scene.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Context and agreement identify the intended noun
REF: 1.2.4

The surrounding sentence normally confirms the choice made by the article.
TL: Elle a posé la poêle neuve sur le vieux poêle.
EN: She put the new frying pan on the old stove.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Il a déchiré la manche de sa veste en saisissant le manche de l'outil.
EN: He tore his jacket sleeve while grabbing the tool handle.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not memorize a vague translation pair. Store article, precise meaning and a short context together.
SHOW: article selects · context confirms · agreement propagates

[CARD]
TITLE: Place names have their own gender map
REF: 1.2.6

Country names ending in e are generally feminine; most others are masculine. The gender determines the prepositional pattern.
TL: Elle travaille en Norvège mais voyage souvent au Japon.
EN: She works in Norway but often travels to Japan.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ils reviennent des États-Unis après un séjour au Canada.
EN: They are returning from the United States after a stay in Canada.
[PAUSE:1000]
Plural names use aux or des, while a masculine singular country normally uses au or du.
SHOW: en + feminine · au + masculine · aux + plural
Flip for regions, towns and exceptions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The ending rule is a default with local exceptions
REF: 1.2.6

Regions and rivers show a similar tendency, but familiar exceptions must be learned. Town names are normally treated as masculine in ordinary use, unless an overt article supplies another gender.
TL: La Seine traverse Paris avant de rejoindre la Manche.
EN: The Seine flows through Paris before reaching the English Channel.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Le Rhône traverse plusieurs régions avant la Méditerranée.
EN: The Rhône crosses several regions before the Mediterranean.
[PAUSE:1000]
The final e is useful evidence, but a stored article remains authoritative.
SHOW: use the pattern · retain named exceptions

[CARD]
TITLE: Frequent nouns deserve fixed-gender retrieval
REF: 1.2.10

English provides no dependable gender cue. High-frequency French nouns should therefore be retrieved directly with their article.
TL: Le choix final dépend du mérite de chaque proposition.
EN: The final choice depends on the merit of each proposal.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Cette menace représente un manque de confiance inquiétant.
EN: This threat represents a worrying lack of confidence.
[PAUSE:1000]
If an ending pattern and a memorized article conflict, trust the lexical record.
SHOW: high frequency → article stored from first exposure
Flip for compound nouns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Find the head inside a compound
REF: 1.2.11

In many adjective-plus-noun and noun-plus-noun compounds, the semantic head supplies the gender. In a noun followed by a prepositional phrase, the first noun normally controls it.
TL: Une porte-fenêtre donne sur le jardin.
EN: A French window opens onto the garden.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Un chef-d'œuvre peut transformer une carrière.
EN: A masterpiece can transform a career.
[PAUSE:1000]
Verb-plus-noun compounds are usually masculine, but lexical exceptions and established usage still matter.
SHOW: identify compound type · locate head · retrieve exceptions

[CARD]
TITLE: Spoken number often lives outside the noun
REF: 1.3, 1.3.1, 1.3.2

The regular written plural adds s, but that s is normally silent. Determiners and agreement frequently carry the audible contrast.
TL: La remarque précise aide le lecteur.
EN: The precise comment helps the reader.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Les remarques précises aident les lecteurs.
EN: The precise comments help the readers.
[PAUSE:1000]
Nouns already ending in s, x or z do not change their spelling in the plural.
TL: Le prix augmente; les prix augmentent.
EN: The price rises; the prices rise.
SHOW: written noun ending + spoken determiner + phrase agreement
Flip for the main irregular families.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build the plural by final pattern
REF: 1.3.3, 1.3.4, 1.3.5, 1.3.6, 1.3.8

Many nouns in -eau, -au and -eu take x. Most nouns in -al change to -aux, while most nouns in -ou simply take s.
TL: un réseau, des réseaux; un journal, des journaux
EN: a network, networks; a newspaper, newspapers
[PAUSE:900]
Each family has exceptions, and a few frequent nouns replace more of the stem.
TL: un œil, des yeux; monsieur, messieurs
EN: an eye, eyes; gentleman, gentlemen
[PAUSE:1000]
Use the productive rule first, then retrieve the compact exception list.
SHOW: default family → spelling change → lexical exception

[CARD]
TITLE: Compound plurals follow their internal structure
REF: 1.2.11, 1.3.9

In adjective-plus-noun compounds, both meaningful parts usually pluralize. In noun-plus-prepositional-phrase compounds, the first noun normally changes.
TL: un coffre-fort, des coffres-forts
EN: a safe, safes
[PAUSE:700]
TL: une pomme de terre, des pommes de terre
EN: a potato, potatoes
[PAUSE:1000]
Verb-plus-noun compounds are often invariable, though established exceptions exist. Analyse the structure before adding a final letter.
SHOW: pluralize the variable lexical parts, not every visible word
Flip for cross-language number.

[SIDE]
TITLE: English and French count concepts differently
REF: 1.3.7, 1.3.10

Translation does not preserve grammatical number. French may use a plural where English presents one mass concept, or a singular where English uses a plural garment name.
TL: Nous avons fait des recherches avant de prendre une décision.
EN: We did research before making a decision.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Son pantalon noir est encore humide.
EN: His black trousers are still damp.
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose the French lexical number first, then propagate it through the phrase and verb.
SHOW: translate concept → retrieve French number → enforce agreement

[CARD]
TITLE: Time nouns distinguish units from lived duration
REF: 1.1.5

French pairs an with année, jour with journée, matin with matinée, and soir with soirée. The shorter form commonly counts or locates a unit; the longer form highlights duration or what fills the period.
TL: Il a travaillé trois ans dans cette entreprise.
EN: He worked for three years in this company.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Après trois années difficiles, l'équipe a retrouvé sa stabilité.
EN: After three difficult years, the team regained its stability.
[PAUSE:1000]
The contrast is semantic, not a mechanical substitution.
SHOW: counted or located unit · experienced or characterized span

[SIDE]
TITLE: Diagnose the phrase from the head outward
REF: 1.1.5, 1.2, 1.3

First identify the head and intended meaning. Then retrieve its gender and lexical number, form any required plural, and propagate agreement through determiners, adjectives and the verb.
TL: Ces longues journées d'hiver semblent interminables.
EN: These long winter days seem endless.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Le programme de recherches internationales paraît ambitieux.
EN: The international research programme seems ambitious.
[PAUSE:1000]
The nearby plural complement in the second sentence does not control the singular verb. The subject head does.
SHOW: head → meaning → gender and number → agreement chain
