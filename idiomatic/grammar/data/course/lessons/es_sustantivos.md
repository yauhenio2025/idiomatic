## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Gender is a grammatical property
REF: 1, 1.1

Every Spanish noun belongs to a grammatical gender. The ending often gives a useful clue, but agreement gives the decisive evidence: articles, adjectives and pronouns reveal how the noun behaves.
TL: La propuesta revisada parece razonable.
EN: The revised proposal seems reasonable.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El informe revisado parece razonable.
EN: The revised report seems reasonable.
[PAUSE:1000]
The two phrases express the same evaluation. Their agreement patterns differ because the head nouns belong to different grammatical classes.
SHOW: noun gender controls agreement across the noun phrase
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Learn the noun as a compact record
REF: 1.1

For every new noun, store an article, the singular form and the plural. A bare translation hides the information that later sentences require.
TL: la red, las redes
EN: the network, the networks
[PAUSE:700]
TL: el análisis, los análisis
EN: the analysis, the analyses
[PAUSE:900]
This habit also exposes invariant forms: the second noun changes number in its article but not in its spelling.
SHOW: article + singular + plural = one lexical record
Ten article-and-noun decisions follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Human nouns follow several patterns
REF: 1.2

Many labels for people alternate a masculine and feminine ending. Others use one form for both and let the article or surrounding agreement identify the referent.
TL: El director y la directora presentaron el proyecto.
EN: The male director and the female director presented the project.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El testigo declaró primero; la testigo habló después.
EN: The male witness testified first; the female witness spoke afterwards.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not force every occupational noun into one mechanical ending swap. Usage and the lexical pattern of the noun decide the form.
SHOW: alternating form or common form · agreement identifies the referent
Flip for reference without a sex distinction.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Grammatical gender and natural reference diverge
REF: 1.2

Some nouns keep one grammatical gender regardless of the person or animal they denote. The agreement stays with the noun, not with the individual's sex.
TL: La víctima estaba consciente cuando llegaron los médicos.
EN: The victim was conscious when the doctors arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El personaje principal, una mujer de edad avanzada, fue descrito con enorme sutileza.
EN: The main character, an elderly woman, was described with enormous subtlety.
[PAUSE:1100]
Context can identify the person, but it does not automatically rewrite the noun's grammatical agreement.
SHOW: natural reference identifies the individual · grammar follows the noun
Ten masculine-to-feminine form decisions come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Productive endings are high-value clues
REF: 1.3

Several derivational endings strongly predict feminine gender, especially endings used for qualities, states and actions.
TL: La investigación reveló una contradicción importante.
EN: The investigation revealed an important contradiction.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La estabilidad financiera sigue siendo frágil.
EN: Financial stability remains fragile.
[PAUSE:1000]
Masculine clues include many learned nouns in Greek-derived endings, though the visible final vowel may tempt an incorrect guess.
TL: El problema metodológico exige otro enfoque.
EN: The methodological problem requires another approach.
SHOW: derivational family is stronger evidence than the final letter alone
Flip for the exception strategy.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use rules to compress, not to gamble
REF: 1.3

A productive pattern reduces memory load, but frequent exceptions still need lexical storage. Treat the rule as a default and attach exceptions to their articles.
TL: El día fue largo, pero la mano de obra respondió bien.
EN: The day was long, but the workforce responded well.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: La foto publicada ayer provocó un debate intenso.
EN: The photo published yesterday caused an intense debate.
[PAUSE:1000]
Shortened nouns normally preserve the gender of the longer expression from which they come.
SHOW: productive default + stored exception · never guess from one letter
Twenty ending-based decisions follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: A stressed initial a changes the article's shape
REF: 1.3

A feminine singular noun beginning with a stressed a sound normally takes el rather than la immediately before it. The noun does not become masculine.
TL: El agua fría cubría el suelo.
EN: The cold water covered the floor.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Un águila blanca sobrevoló el valle.
EN: A white eagle flew over the valley.
[PAUSE:1100]
The adjective remains feminine, and the ordinary feminine article returns when another word intervenes.
TL: La misma agua parecía más clara por la mañana.
EN: The same water seemed clearer in the morning.
SHOW: el agua fría · la misma agua · feminine agreement throughout
Flip for plural and unstressed beginnings.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The substitution is narrow, not a new paradigm
REF: 1.3

In the plural, the regular feminine article appears. An unstressed initial a does not trigger the singular substitution either.
TL: Las aguas tranquilas reflejaban las montañas.
EN: The calm waters reflected the mountains.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La arena húmeda conservaba las huellas.
EN: The wet sand preserved the footprints.
[PAUSE:1000]
Keep the diagnostic precise: feminine noun, singular, and a stressed initial a sound immediately after the article.
SHOW: singular stressed a → el/un · plural or unstressed a → la/las
Ten article-and-agreement sentences follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Gender can select a different meaning
REF: 1.5

Some spellings correspond to two lexical entries distinguished by gender. The article then carries meaning, not merely concord.
TL: El capital extranjero aumentó durante el trimestre.
EN: Foreign capital increased during the quarter.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La capital regional duplicó su población.
EN: The regional capital doubled its population.
[PAUSE:1000]
The masculine noun concerns wealth; the feminine noun names a principal city. Store them as separate vocabulary entries.
SHOW: same spelling + different gender = different lexical entry
Flip for a second contrast.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Context and agreement disambiguate together
REF: 1.5

Another common pair distinguishes an editorial text from an opinion or governing body.
TL: El editorial criticó la falta de transparencia.
EN: The editorial criticized the lack of transparency.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La editorial publicará la novela en septiembre.
EN: The publishing house will publish the novel in September.
[PAUSE:1000]
The article points to the entry, and the rest of the sentence confirms the intended reading.
SHOW: learn article, meaning and plural together for each entry
Twenty meaning-sensitive gender choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The plural starts with the final sound
REF: 2, 2.1

Most vowel-final nouns add s. Most consonant-final nouns add es. A final z changes to c before the plural ending.
TL: la clave, las claves
EN: the key, the keys
[PAUSE:600]
TL: el sector, los sectores
EN: the sector, the sectors
[PAUSE:600]
TL: la raíz, las raíces
EN: the root, the roots
[PAUSE:1000]
These are phonological rules, so spelling adjustments preserve the sound and the stress pattern.
SHOW: vowel + s · consonant + es · z → ces
Flip for accent marks.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Plural spelling preserves lexical stress
REF: 2.1

Adding a syllable can make a written accent unnecessary, while removing an accent could misplace the original stress. Compare forms as a pair rather than copying the singular mechanically.
TL: la canción, las canciones
EN: the song, the songs
[PAUSE:700]
TL: el joven, los jóvenes
EN: the young person, the young people
[PAUSE:900]
Some learned or recent forms show accepted variation. In formal writing, verify the established plural when usage is divided.
SHOW: build the plural, then reapply the normal stress rules
Twenty plural forms follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Some nouns mark number outside the noun
REF: 2.2

Nouns ending in unstressed s after a vowel often keep the same written form in singular and plural. Determiners and adjectives do the visible work.
TL: La crisis energética fue breve.
EN: The energy crisis was brief.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Las crisis energéticas fueron breves.
EN: The energy crises were brief.
[PAUSE:1000]
The contrast belongs to the whole noun phrase, not necessarily to the final letters of the head noun.
SHOW: invariant noun · number exposed by determiners and agreement
Flip for lexical number.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Translation does not preserve countability
REF: 2.2

Spanish and English do not always package the same concept with the same number. Some frequent Spanish expressions are plural where an English equivalent is singular or uncountable, and the reverse also occurs.
TL: La gente de la oficina ya ha llegado.
EN: The people from the office have already arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Necesitamos información más precisa antes de decidir.
EN: We need more precise information before deciding.
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose number from the Spanish lexical pattern, then make the sentence agree with that choice.
SHOW: translate the concept · recover Spanish countability · enforce agreement
Eleven cross-language number decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Agreement follows the grammatical subject
REF: 2.3

The verb normally agrees with the grammatical number of its subject, even when an English translation suggests a different count.
TL: La mayoría de los asistentes aceptó la propuesta.
EN: The majority of those attending accepted the proposal.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Los alrededores del edificio están cerrados al tráfico.
EN: The area around the building is closed to traffic.
[PAUSE:1100]
Collective meaning and grammatical number are separate dimensions. Identify the head of the subject before choosing the verb.
SHOW: locate the subject head · read its grammatical number · choose the verb
Flip for postverbal subjects.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Word order can hide the agreement controller
REF: 2.3

A subject may follow the verb, especially in presentational or information-focused sentences. Do not mistake the first noun phrase for the controller.
TL: Al final del informe aparecen dos recomendaciones urgentes.
EN: Two urgent recommendations appear at the end of the report.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: En la reunión surgió una duda inesperada.
EN: An unexpected doubt arose during the meeting.
[PAUSE:1000]
Recover the predicate's subject first; position alone does not determine agreement.
SHOW: subject after the verb still controls person and number
Seven agreement choices follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Run the full noun-phrase check
REF: 1, 2

An advanced noun phrase can combine a lexical gender decision, plural formation and agreement across several words. Solve those layers in order.
TL: Aquellas jóvenes intérpretes parecían muy seguras.
EN: Those young female interpreters seemed very confident.
[PAUSE:1100]
The head noun is common in gender and overtly plural. The demonstrative, adjective and verb together identify the intended feminine plural reading.
SHOW: lexical entry → gender and number → phrase agreement → verb agreement
Flip for a reliable production routine.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Diagnose from the head outward
REF: 1.1, 2.3

First locate the head noun. Then retrieve its lexical gender and plural. Finally propagate those features through determiners, modifiers, pronouns and the agreeing verb.
TL: El análisis detallado de las cifras reveló varias anomalías.
EN: The detailed analysis of the figures revealed several anomalies.
[PAUSE:1000]
The nearby plural phrase does not control the singular verb; the subject head does.
TL: Las conclusiones del análisis resultaron convincentes.
EN: The conclusions of the analysis proved convincing.
[PAUSE:900]
When the head changes, the agreement chain changes with it.
SHOW: find the head · retrieve its record · propagate agreement
Use the exercises to make each step automatic.
