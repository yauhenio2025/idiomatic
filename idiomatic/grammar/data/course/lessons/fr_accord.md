## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Start agreement from the grammatical subject
REF: 9.1

A finite French verb agrees with its subject in person and number. Find that subject before looking at the noun closest to the verb.
TL: Je voulais partir, mais les voisins voulaient rester.
EN: I wanted to leave, but the neighbours wanted to stay.
[PAUSE:900]
The verb form follows je in the first clause and the plural noun les voisins in the second. Extra complements between subject and verb do not change the controller.
TL: La liste des invités se trouve sur la table.
EN: The guest list is on the table.
SHOW: identify the finite verb → find its subject → copy person and number
Flip for a subject made from several coordinated people.

[SIDE]
TITLE: With et, the highest grammatical person wins
REF: 9.1.1

Subjects joined by et form a plural group. If that group contains a first-person pronoun, use nous agreement; otherwise a second-person pronoun gives vous agreement; an all-third-person group takes ils or elles agreement.
TL: Léa et moi sommes prêtes; toi et Marc êtes prêts; Léa et Marc sont prêts.
EN: Léa and I are ready; you and Marc are ready; Léa and Marc are ready.
[PAUSE:900]
Think of the hierarchy as first person before second person before third person. The written subject may contain names and stressed pronouns, but the verb still selects one plural paradigm.
SHOW: moi + anyone → nous · toi/vous + third person → vous · only third persons → ils/elles

[CARD]
TITLE: Ni, soit and ou can present alternatives or a combined group
REF: 9.1.2

Coordination with ni, soit, or ou does not force one number in every context. Singular agreement presents the members as alternatives; plural agreement treats them additively as a group.
TL: Soit Lina soit son frère viendra; ni Lina ni son frère ne viendront ensemble.
EN: Either Lina or her brother will come; neither Lina nor her brother will come together.
[PAUSE:900]
The intended meaning controls the choice. Ask whether the predicate applies to one alternative at a time or to the coordinated members together.
SHOW: exclusive alternatives → singular is natural · additive group → plural
Flip for agreement inside a cleft sentence.

[SIDE]
TITLE: In c'est X qui, the verb after qui agrees with X
REF: 9.1.6

The relative verb in a c'est or ce sont cleft agrees with the focused expression before qui. Do not let the fixed introductory ce decide the later verb form.
TL: C'est moi qui suis responsable; c'est vous qui avez choisi.
EN: I am the one who is responsible; you are the ones who chose.
[PAUSE:900]
Replace qui mentally with the focused person: moi becomes je, vous stays vous, and eux becomes ils. That reconstructed subject reveals the required form.
TL: Ce sont elles qui partiront les premières.
EN: They are the ones who will leave first.
SHOW: c'est/ce sont + X + qui → later verb agrees with X

[CARD]
TITLE: A singular collective noun normally takes a singular verb
REF: 9.1.3

Words such as gouvernement, comité, famille, and équipe are grammatically singular even when they denote several people. In the ordinary pattern, the finite verb is singular.
TL: Le comité a proposé une nouvelle date; tout le monde est prêt.
EN: The committee proposed a new date; everyone is ready.
[PAUSE:900]
Tout le monde is always singular. A collective followed by a plural de-complement can sometimes shift toward plural agreement when the members are emphasized, but singular remains the safe structural baseline.
TL: L'équipe de bénévoles a terminé le travail.
EN: The volunteer team finished the work.
SHOW: singular collective head → singular verb · tout le monde → always singular
Flip for fractions and quantifiers whose complement controls agreement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Fractions and quantifiers often agree with their complement
REF: 9.1.4, 9.1.5

A fraction with a plural complement normally takes plural agreement, while a fraction with a singular complement agrees in the singular. Most quantifiers, including la plupart de and beaucoup de, follow the number of their complement.
TL: La moitié des candidats sont arrivés; la moitié du bâtiment a été rénovée.
EN: Half the candidates arrived; half the building was renovated.
[PAUSE:900]
Plural les deux tiers and percentages usually take a plural verb. Numeral nouns such as une dizaine or une vingtaine can instead agree with the numeral head or its complement according to the intended emphasis.
TL: La plupart du travail est terminé; soixante pour cent ont répondu.
EN: Most of the work is finished; sixty percent replied.
SHOW: inspect the quantity phrase → locate its complement → choose singular or plural meaning
Fifteen clean subject-agreement drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: A participle with être agrees with the subject
REF: 9.2.1, 9.2.2

In compound forms whose auxiliary is être, the past participle matches the subject in gender and number. The same agreement appears in passive constructions built with être.
TL: Nora est partie; ses frères sont partis; les portes ont été fermées.
EN: Nora left; her brothers left; the doors were closed.
[PAUSE:900]
First identify the grammatical subject, then add e for feminine, s for plural, or es for feminine plural when the participle's spelling shows those distinctions.
SHOW: être + participle → agreement with subject: ∅ / e / s / es
Ten clean subject-controlled participle drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Compound tenses and passives can stack auxiliaries
REF: 9.2.1, 9.2.2

The agreement controller stays the subject even when another auxiliary appears before été. In a compound passive, avoir carries the tense, été builds the passive, and the final lexical participle agrees with the subject.
TL: Les commandes avaient été préparées avant midi.
EN: The orders had been prepared before noon.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not make préparées agree with the nearest word or with the person performing the action. Les commandes is the passive subject and therefore controls feminine-plural agreement.
TL: La route aura été bloquée pendant deux heures.
EN: The road will have been blocked for two hours.
SHOW: tense auxiliary + été + lexical participle → lexical participle agrees with passive subject

[CARD]
TITLE: A participle with avoir is normally invariable
REF: 9.3

With avoir, the ordinary order is subject, auxiliary, participle, then direct object. In that order the past participle does not agree with either the subject or the following object.
TL: Elles ont acheté une voiture; elles ont acheté deux voitures.
EN: They bought one car; they bought two cars.
[PAUSE:900]
This default is the anchor for the exceptional rule. Agreement becomes possible only when a direct object has moved before the participle.
SHOW: subject + avoir + participle + object → no agreement
Flip for the three positions that can place a direct object first.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A preceding direct object triggers agreement with avoir
REF: 9.3.1, 9.3.4, 9.3.7

When a direct object precedes an avoir participle, the participle agrees with that object. The object may be an unstressed pronoun, the head of a que relative, or a fronted interrogative phrase.
TL: Cette voiture, je l'ai achetée; la voiture que j'ai achetée; quelle voiture as-tu achetée?
EN: This car, I bought it; the car that I bought; which car did you buy?
[PAUSE:900]
All three examples reduce to the same relationship: j'ai acheté cette voiture. Position plus direct-object status, not the surface construction's name, licenses agreement.
SHOW: preceding + direct object → participle matches its gender and number

[CARD]
TITLE: Rebuild the noun frame to classify me, te, nous or vous
REF: 9.3.1, 9.3.2

The pronouns me, te, nous, and vous can be direct or indirect. Replace the pronoun with a noun and recover the verb's French complement frame before deciding agreement.
TL: Léa nous a appelées, mais elle nous a téléphoné plus tard.
EN: Léa called us, but she phoned us later.
[PAUSE:900]
Appeler quelqu'un takes a direct object, so appelées agrees with a feminine plural nous. Téléphoner à quelqu'un takes an indirect object, so téléphoné remains unchanged.
SHOW: verb + quelqu'un → direct · verb + à quelqu'un → indirect
Flip for pronouns that never trigger this agreement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Indirect pronouns, en and clausal le do not trigger agreement
REF: 9.3.1, 9.3.2

The participle does not agree with lui, leur, y, or en, nor with me, te, nous, or vous when they represent an indirect object. The invariable le that stands for a whole idea also causes no agreement.
TL: Elle leur a parlé; des erreurs, elle en a trouvé; il l'a souvent répété.
EN: She spoke to them; she found some errors; he often repeated it.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not decide from position alone. A word before the participle must still be the direct object of that verb and must carry the gender and number being matched.
SHOW: preceding indirect object / en / clausal le → no participle agreement
Thirty-five clean classification and pronoun-agreement drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Que can carry a direct object from a relative clause
REF: 9.3.4

When que represents the direct object inside a relative clause, its antecedent precedes the participle and controls agreement.
TL: Voici la lettre que Nadia a écrite et les photos qu'elle a choisies.
EN: Here is the letter Nadia wrote and the photos she chose.
[PAUSE:900]
Rebuild the clauses as Nadia a écrit la lettre and elle a choisi les photos. The restored noun phrases are direct objects, so écrite and choisies match their antecedents.
SHOW: antecedent + que + subject + avoir + participle → test the restored direct object
Flip for relative phrases that resemble objects but do not license agreement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Qui, dont, measures and impersonal il y a do not create a preceding direct object
REF: 9.3.4, 9.3.5

An antecedent linked by qui is the relative subject, and one linked by dont belongs to a de-complement. Neither is a preceding direct object. Expressions of duration, distance, cost, weight, or value can also be adverbial measures rather than objects.
TL: La ville dont elle a parlé; les heures qu'elle a dormi; les difficultés qu'il y a eu.
EN: The town she spoke about; the hours she slept; the difficulties there were.
[PAUSE:900]
Try the passive test: a genuine direct object can usually become a passive subject, but a duration or cost cannot. Impersonal il y a keeps eu invariable even when a complement appears before it.
TL: La valise que nous avons pesée, mais les vingt kilos qu'elle a pesé.
EN: The suitcase we weighed, but the twenty kilos it weighed.
SHOW: restore the clause → test its complement role → agree only with a direct object
Twenty clean relative-clause agreement drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: A questioned direct object follows the same preceding-object rule
REF: 9.3.7

When quel, lequel, or combien de introduces a direct object before an avoir participle, the participle agrees with the noun or pronoun inside that question phrase.
TL: Quelle solution avez-vous retenue? Lesquelles ont-ils acceptées?
EN: Which solution did you retain? Which ones did they accept?
[PAUSE:900]
Reconstruct the neutral statement: vous avez retenu cette solution; ils ont accepté celles-ci. The questioned elements are direct objects and their gender and number remain recoverable.
TL: Combien de lettres avez-vous envoyées?
EN: How many letters did you send?
SHOW: fronted question phrase + direct-object role → agreement
Flip for questions about measures and impersonal events.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A fronted phrase still needs to be a true direct object
REF: 9.3.7, 9.3.8

Questions about duration, distance, cost, weight, or value may front an adverbial measure, so the participle remains unchanged. The impersonal construction il y a likewise keeps eu invariable.
TL: Combien d'heures a-t-elle dormi? Quels incidents y a-t-il eu?
EN: How many hours did she sleep? What incidents were there?
[PAUSE:900]
Contrast personal avoir, where the question phrase can be a direct object and agreement returns.
TL: Quels problèmes a-t-il eus pendant le voyage?
EN: What problems did he have during the trip?
SHOW: fronting alone is insufficient → verify direct-object status
Nineteen clean interrogative agreement drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Before an infinitive, ask who performs the infinitive
REF: 9.3.3, 9.3.6, 9.3.9

A preceding direct object can trigger agreement when it is also understood as the subject of a following infinitive. If it receives the infinitive's action instead, the first participle remains unchanged.
TL: Les musiciennes, je les ai entendues jouer; les chansons, je les ai entendu jouer.
EN: I heard the musicians play; I heard the songs being played.
[PAUSE:900]
In the first sentence, the musicians do the playing, so entendues agrees. In the second, someone plays the songs; les is the object of jouer, so entendu does not agree.
SHOW: preceding object performs infinitive → agreement · receives infinitive action → none
Flip for movement verbs, laisser, and the faire exception.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Movement verbs and laisser use the performer test; faire stays invariable
REF: 9.3.3, 9.3.6, 9.3.9

With movement verbs and the traditional laisser pattern, a preceding object that performs the infinitive licenses agreement. Faire followed by an infinitive is an exception and its participle fait never agrees in this construction.
TL: Nous les avons envoyées chercher le dossier; nous les avons fait revenir.
EN: We sent them to fetch the file; we made them come back.
[PAUSE:900]
The same analysis applies whether the preceding object is a pronoun, a relative head, or a questioned phrase. Identify its role in both verb phrases before adding an ending.
TL: Quelles collègues avez-vous entendues répondre?
EN: Which colleagues did you hear answer?
SHOW: performer of infinitive → possible agreement · fait + infinitive → always fait
Nineteen clean infinitive-construction drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Pronominal agreement depends on the underlying object role
REF: 9.4

Pronominal verbs use être in compound tenses, but their participles do not automatically agree with the subject. Rebuild the non-pronominal frame and decide whether the reflexive pronoun is direct or indirect.
TL: Elles se sont regardées; elles se sont parlé.
EN: They looked at each other; they spoke to each other.
[PAUSE:900]
Regarder quelqu'un makes se direct and triggers agreement. Parler à quelqu'un makes se indirect, so parlé stays unchanged. An intrinsic pronominal verb normally agrees with its subject.
TL: Elles se sont tues; ils se sont abstenus.
EN: They fell silent; they abstained.
SHOW: direct se → agreement · indirect se → none · intrinsic pronominal verb → normally agreement
Flip for explicit objects and pronominal infinitive constructions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Locate any explicit object before agreeing a pronominal participle
REF: 9.4

When a body part or another direct object follows the participle, se is indirect and does not trigger agreement. A direct object placed before the participle can instead control agreement.
TL: Elle s'est coupé le doigt; les lettres qu'elles se sont écrites.
EN: She cut her finger; the letters they wrote to each other.
[PAUSE:900]
In the first clause, le doigt is the following direct object. In the second, se still represents à l'une à l'autre, but the preceding direct object les lettres licenses écrites.
TL: Elles se sont fait aider; elle s'est laissé convaincre; elle s'est vu offrir un poste.
EN: They had themselves helped; she let herself be persuaded; she was offered a position.
SHOW: following direct object → no agreement with se · preceding direct object → agreement · se faire/laisser/voir + infinitive → participle invariable
Twenty-five clean pronominal-agreement drills follow.
