## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: An adverb describes a relation, not a noun
REF: 35.1, 35.2, 35.3

An adverb can modify an action, an adjective, another expression, or the speaker's assessment of a whole statement. It does not agree with a noun.
TL: La investigadora explicó el problema claramente.
EN: The researcher explained the problem clearly.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El problema es políticamente delicado.
EN: The problem is politically sensitive.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Probablemente llegarán mañana.
EN: They will probably arrive tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
First identify the scope: how, to what degree, where, when, or with what attitude?
SHOW: action · degree · place · time · speaker attitude
Flip to form adverbs in -mente.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build -mente from the feminine singular base
REF: 35.2.1, 35.2.2

If an adjective has distinct masculine and feminine forms, add -mente to the feminine singular. Otherwise add it to the single singular form.
TL: cuidadoso → cuidadosa → cuidadosamente
EN: careful → carefully
[PAUSE:900]
TL: evidente → evidentemente
EN: evident → evidently
[PAUSE:900]
The original adjective keeps any written accent. The suffix does not erase it.
TL: rápido → rápidamente · fácil → fácilmente · cortés → cortésmente
EN: quickly · easily · courteously
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: feminine singular or invariant singular + mente · keep the accent
Ten formation prompts follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Mark -mente once in a coordinated series
REF: 35.2.3

When two or more -mente adverbs are joined by a conjunction, only the final member keeps the suffix. Earlier members take the feminine adjective form.
TL: Respondió clara y sinceramente.
EN: She answered clearly and sincerely.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Actuaron rápida pero cuidadosamente.
EN: They acted quickly but carefully.
[PAUSE:1000]
The same pattern works with paired structures.
TL: El texto no es ni lógica ni estéticamente coherente.
EN: The text is coherent neither logically nor aesthetically.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: clara y sinceramente · rápida pero cuidadosamente
Flip when the adverbs are not coordinated.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Separate adverbs keep their full form
REF: 35.2.3, 35.2.7

Dropping the suffix is a coordination rule, not a ban on repeating independent adverbs. Without a joining conjunction, each can retain -mente.
TL: Habló pausadamente, deliberadamente.
EN: She spoke slowly, deliberately.
[PAUSE:900]
Even when grammar permits repetition, a chain of -mente forms can sound heavy. Recast one idea as a noun phrase, clause, or established adverbial expression.
TL: Es evidente que el sistema cambia constantemente.
EN: It is clear that the system changes constantly.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not stack two -mente forms so that one directly modifies the other.
SHOW: coordination → suffix on the last · separate comments → full forms
Ten coordinated pairs follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Prefer an established manner expression when Spanish has one
REF: 35.2.6, 35.3.2

Spanish has many fixed phrases that do the work of English -ly adverbs. They are often more idiomatic than a mechanically possible -mente form.
TL: Lo hizo a mano y lo explicó de memoria.
EN: She made it by hand and explained it from memory.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Contestó de mala gana, pero llegó a tiempo.
EN: He answered reluctantly, but arrived on time.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn the whole phrase with its preposition: a ciegas, con cuidado, de improviso, en serio.
SHOW: a + noun · con + noun · de + noun · en + noun
Flip for flat adjectives and subject-oriented adjectives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A bare adjective has a narrower license
REF: 35.3.3, 35.3.4, 35.3.8

Some masculine singular adjective forms work as fixed adverbs with particular verbs. They remain invariable.
TL: Habla claro y trabaja duro.
EN: She speaks clearly and works hard.
[PAUSE:900]
A subject-oriented adjective is different: it agrees with the subject because it describes the participant and the action together.
TL: Las niñas salieron contentas.
EN: The girls left happily, feeling pleased.
[PAUSE:900]
Place a manner expression where its scope is clear, normally close to the verb or phrase it modifies.
SHOW: habla claro · salieron contentas · respondió con calma
Seventeen hygiene-gated recasts and manner phrases follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Aquí, ahí, and allí form a deictic map
REF: 35.6.1

Aquí points near the speaker. Ahí commonly points near the hearer or to a nearby place already indicated. Allí points to a more distant place.
TL: Aquí tengo las llaves; ahí tienes tu abrigo; allí está la estación.
EN: Here I have the keys; there you have your coat; over there is the station.
[PAUSE:1000]
On the telephone, the hearer's location is naturally ahí.
TL: ¿Qué tiempo hace ahí?
EN: What is the weather like there?
[PAUSE:900]
Distance is interpreted from the conversational situation, not from an English here-versus-there pair.
SHOW: aquí = speaker · ahí = hearer or indicated place · allí = distant
Flip for acá and allá.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Acá and allá add regional and directional nuance
REF: 35.6.2

Across much of Latin America, acá and allá are ordinary alternatives to aquí and allí. In Spain they more often suggest a broad area, vagueness, or movement.
TL: Ven para acá; luego iremos para allá.
EN: Come over here; later we will go over there.
[PAUSE:900]
Allá also appears in conventional expressions that are not simple coordinates.
TL: Allá ellos si no quieren escuchar.
EN: That is their problem if they do not want to listen.
[PAUSE:900]
It can make a remote time deliberately vague.
TL: Allá por 1990 empezó el proyecto.
EN: The project began sometime around 1990.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: precise point · broad area · motion · conventional expression
Nine unflagged deictic choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Separate location, relation, and manner on the vertical axis
REF: 35.6.6, 38.4

Abajo is an adverb meaning down, downstairs, or in a lower place. Debajo de is a relational phrase meaning under something.
TL: Mis vecinos viven abajo. El paquete está debajo de la mesa.
EN: My neighbors live downstairs. The parcel is under the table.
[PAUSE:1000]
Latin American usage often permits abajo de where Spain prefers debajo de.
TL: El garaje está abajo del edificio.
EN: The garage is underneath the building.
[PAUSE:900]
First ask whether the sentence names a lower region or a relation to a landmark.
SHOW: lower place → abajo · under X → debajo de X
Flip for bajo and the upper axis.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Bajo can describe degree, pitch, or manner
REF: 35.6.6

Used adverbially with certain verbs, bajo means low rather than downstairs or underneath.
TL: Habla más bajo; el bebé está durmiendo.
EN: Speak more quietly; the baby is sleeping.
[PAUSE:900]
On the upper axis, arriba is up or upstairs, while encima de gives a relation on or above something.
TL: Sube arriba. Las llaves están encima del armario.
EN: Go upstairs. The keys are on top of the cupboard.
[PAUSE:1000]
These forms also support idioms, so interpret the whole phrase.
TL: Más abajo veremos otro ejemplo.
EN: Further below we will see another example.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: bajo = low · abajo = down · debajo de = under
Eight hygiene-gated vertical choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Detrás locates; atrás points backward
REF: 35.6.7

Detrás and detrás de describe a static position behind a reference point. Atrás points backward, to a rear area, or to something left behind.
TL: El jardín está detrás de la casa.
EN: The garden is behind the house.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Dio dos pasos atrás.
EN: She took two steps backward.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Los niños viajan atrás.
EN: The children are riding in the back.
[PAUSE:1000]
In much of Latin America, atrás de also occurs for detrás de; the regional option does not erase the basic diagnostic.
SHOW: static behind X → detrás de · direction or rearward area → atrás
Flip for delante, adelante, and time.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The same contrast runs forward—and atrás reaches into time
REF: 35.6.7, 35.6.8, 36.4

Delante de locates something in front of a landmark. Adelante points or moves forward.
TL: Espera delante del edificio. Sigue adelante.
EN: Wait in front of the building. Keep going forward.
[PAUSE:900]
Atrás also follows a measured period to mean ago or earlier.
TL: Nos conocimos muchos años atrás.
EN: We met many years ago.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not confuse that time use with the ordinary hace construction, which places the duration before a completed event.
TL: Nos conocimos hace diez años.
EN: We met ten years ago.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: delante de / detrás de = position · adelante / atrás = direction
Eight hygiene-gated rearward and elapsed-time choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Aún with an accent means still or yet
REF: 35.7.3

Aún normally patterns with todavía: it says that a state continues or an expected event has not happened.
TL: El museo aún está abierto. Aún no han contestado.
EN: The museum is still open. They have not answered yet.
[PAUSE:900]
Before a comparative, it increases the degree and translates as even.
TL: La segunda propuesta es aún más cara.
EN: The second proposal is even more expensive.
[PAUSE:1000]
If todavía can replace the word without changing the basic meaning, the accented form is usually the right choice.
SHOW: aún ≈ todavía · still · yet · even more
Flip for unaccented aun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Aun without an accent means even or including
REF: 35.8

Unaccented aun often means incluso or hasta: it brings an unexpected case into the statement.
TL: Todos aceptaron, aun los más escépticos.
EN: Everyone agreed, even the most skeptical people.
[PAUSE:900]
In concessive phrases such as aun si and aun cuando, it means even if or even though.
TL: Aun si llueve, iremos.
EN: Even if it rains, we will go.
[PAUSE:900]
Use substitution as a diagnostic rather than trusting the English word even, which can correspond to either form.
SHOW: todavía → aún · incluso → aun
Ten accent choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ya marks a boundary in the discourse
REF: 35.7.1

Ya often signals that a relevant change or threshold has been reached. With an affirmative verb it may mean already or now.
TL: Ya han llegado. Marta ya es médica.
EN: They have already arrived. Marta is a doctor now.
[PAUSE:900]
With no, it says that a former state has ended.
TL: Ya no viven aquí.
EN: They no longer live here.
[PAUSE:900]
With a future or promise, it can reassure the listener that the expected result will come.
TL: Ya te llamaré.
EN: I will call you, do not worry.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: change reached · expectation fulfilled · former state ended
Flip for expressive ya.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Tense and intonation supply ya's translation
REF: 35.7.1

In commands, ya can add urgency. As a response, it can abbreviate ya entiendo or ya lo sé.
TL: ¡Dímelo ya! —Ya, ya entiendo.
EN: Tell me now! —Right, I understand.
[PAUSE:900]
It can also mark resignation, impatience, disbelief, or a speaker's stance without a neat one-word English equivalent.
TL: Bueno, ya veremos.
EN: Well, we will see.
[PAUSE:900]
Read the tense, polarity, and conversational purpose together before translating.
SHOW: already · now · no longer · right away · I understand · stance
Ten contextual translations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Entonces anchors a situation; luego advances the sequence
REF: 35.7.4

As time words, entonces points to the relevant moment or state of affairs, while stressed luego usually means afterwards or later.
TL: En 2010 vivía en Lima; entonces trabajaba en un banco.
EN: In 2010 she lived in Lima; at that time she worked in a bank.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Primero revisamos los datos y luego decidimos.
EN: First we review the data and then decide.
[PAUSE:1000]
Hasta entonces and desde entonces define an endpoint or starting point around that anchored situation.
SHOW: entonces = at that time · luego = afterwards
Flip when the relation is logical rather than temporal.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Both words can introduce a consequence
REF: 35.7.4

Entonces can mean in that case, presenting a conclusion from the situation. Unstressed luego can also introduce an inference, especially in formal reasoning.
TL: Si no hay entradas, entonces veremos la película mañana.
EN: If there are no tickets, then we will see the film tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Pienso, luego existo.
EN: I think, therefore I am.
[PAUSE:900]
When the meaning is the next event, después is a useful check for luego. When the meaning is at that point or in that case, entonces is the safer choice.
SHOW: next event → luego · anchored point or case → entonces
Two hygiene-gated contextual passages follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ongoing duration needs an ongoing construction
REF: 36.3.1, 36.3.2, 36.3.7

For an activity that began earlier and continues now, llevar plus a duration and gerund is one of the most natural spoken patterns.
TL: Llevo tres años estudiando español.
EN: I have been studying Spanish for three years.
[PAUSE:900]
Hace plus a duration, que, and the present gives the same continuing viewpoint.
TL: Hace tres años que estudio español.
EN: I have studied Spanish for three years.
[PAUSE:900]
Desde names the starting point; desde hace introduces a measured span.
TL: Estudio español desde 2023, desde hace tres años.
EN: I have studied Spanish since 2023, for three years.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: llevar + span · hace + span + que · desde + starting point
Flip to place the same duration in the past.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Match the construction to whether the event still holds
REF: 36.3.1, 36.3.2, 36.3.3, 36.3.4

At a past reference point, ongoing duration uses llevaba or hacía with an imperfect viewpoint.
TL: Llevaba dos meses trabajando allí cuando cerró la oficina.
EN: She had been working there for two months when the office closed.
[PAUSE:900]
If the event is finished, a past tense plus a bare duration or durante presents the completed span.
TL: Trabajó allí dos años. Fue directora durante una década.
EN: She worked there for two years. She was director for a decade.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not use present-tense estar plus a duration by itself for an ongoing English perfect.
SHOW: still ongoing → llevar / hace que · completed span → past + duration
Flip for ago and future distance.

[CARD]
TITLE: Hace looks backward; dentro de looks forward
REF: 36.4, 36.5

To locate a completed event a measured time ago, put hace plus the span before or after the past-tense event.
TL: Llegaron hace dos horas. Hace dos horas llegaron.
EN: They arrived two hours ago.
[PAUSE:900]
Dentro de locates an event after a future interval.
TL: Volveré dentro de dos horas.
EN: I will return in two hours.
[PAUSE:900]
En plus a duration normally describes the time needed to complete something; it also commonly replaces dentro de for future time in many regions.
TL: Terminé el informe en dos horas.
EN: I finished the report in two hours.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: ago → hace · future interval → dentro de · completion time → en
Flip for choosing among durante, por, and para.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Duration prepositions encode viewpoint
REF: 36.3.4, 36.3.5, 36.3.6

Durante places an event throughout a stated period. Por can emphasize a limited or provisional period, with regional variation. Para can reserve something for a future span or imply that it is enough for that span.
TL: Vivió allí durante tres años.
EN: She lived there for three years.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Alquilamos el coche por dos días.
EN: We rented the car for two days.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Hay comida para una semana.
EN: There is enough food for a week.
[PAUSE:1000]
The English preposition for is not the decision rule; ongoing status, completed extent, provisional duration, and intended sufficiency are.
SHOW: durante = throughout · por = limited span · para = intended span
Flip for the final diagnostic.

[CARD]
TITLE: Put every adverb on an axis
REF: 35, 36

Before choosing a form, name the relation it contributes. Manner may call for -mente, a flat adverb, an agreeing adjective, or a fixed phrase. Place needs a speaker-centered coordinate or a landmark relation.
TL: Contestó cortésmente, con calma, desde allí.
EN: She answered courteously, calmly, from there.
[PAUSE:1000]
Time needs a point, a span, a starting boundary, an endpoint, or a discourse transition.
TL: Ya llevaba una hora allí cuando por fin llamaron.
EN: She had already been there for an hour when they finally called.
[PAUSE:1000]
Multiple adverbs are safe when each one's scope is clear.
SHOW: manner · place · point in time · duration · discourse relation
Flip for a compact production check.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Run a four-question production check
REF: 35.2, 35.6, 35.7, 36.3

First, what does the adverb modify? Second, is the expression a coordinate, a relation to a landmark, or a direction? Third, is the time event ongoing, completed, or future? Fourth, is the form standard across the target region?
TL: Aún vivimos aquí, pero dentro de un mes trabajaremos allá.
EN: We still live here, but in a month we will work over there.
[PAUSE:1000]
Then check the small contrasts: aún or aun, detrás or atrás, entonces or luego, hace or desde hace.
TL: Hace años llegué aquí; desde entonces trabajo tranquilamente.
EN: I arrived here years ago; since then I have worked calmly.
[PAUSE:1000]
Translate the relation and viewpoint, not the nearest English adverb.
SHOW: scope · coordinate · time contour · regional register
