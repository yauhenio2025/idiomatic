## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose a pronoun from its job, not from one English form
REF: 7, 7.1

Portuguese distinguishes a subject, an unstressed object, and a form used after a preposition. For the first person, the core contrasts are eu, me, mim and nós, nos, nós.
TL: Eu trouxe o relatório; a gerente me agradeceu e falou comigo.
EN: I brought the report; the manager thanked me and spoke with me.
[PAUSE:900]
Me and nos can be direct or indirect objects. Mim follows an ordinary preposition and cannot serve as the subject of a finite verb.
TL: O técnico nos mostrou o painel e deixou uma cópia para nós.
EN: The technician showed us the panel and left a copy for us.
[PAUSE:950]
First identify subject, verb object, or prepositional complement; then retrieve the corresponding form.
SHOW: subject: eu/nós · verb object: me/nos · after preposition: mim/nós · com + mim -> comigo
Flip for the everyday alternative to nós.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A gente means we or us but controls singular grammar
REF: 7.1.1

Everyday Brazilian speech very often uses a gente instead of nós. As a subject, a gente takes a third-person singular verb.
TL: A gente chega cedo e depois conversa com a diretora.
EN: We arrive early and then talk with the director.
[PAUSE:900]
After a verb or preposition, the whole phrase remains a gente. An adjective is normally masculine singular, or feminine singular when the group is entirely female.
TL: O motorista espera a gente; hoje a gente está cansada.
EN: The driver is waiting for us; today we are tired. Said by an all-female group.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not combine the meaning of a gente with plural verb agreement.
SHOW: a gente + 3SG verb · verb/preposition + a gente · adjective: SG, gender by group
The informal dialogue source block follows.

[CARD]
TITLE: Você and vocês use third-person verb forms
REF: 7.2.1, 7.2.2, 7.2.3

Você addresses one person and vocês more than one, but both pattern grammatically with third-person verbs.
TL: Você precisa assinar aqui; vocês podem esperar naquela sala.
EN: You need to sign here; you can wait in that room.
[PAUSE:900]
For respectful address, use o senhor or a senhora. These are noun phrases, so they also select third-person agreement and can follow a preposition.
TL: A senhora deseja falar com o diretor? Posso enviar o contrato ao senhor.
EN: Would you like to speak with the director, ma'am? May I send the contract to you, sir?
[PAUSE:950]
Plural os senhores and as senhoras belong to especially formal settings; omission or vocês is more usual elsewhere.
SHOW: você + 3SG · vocês + 3PL · o senhor/a senhora + 3SG · respect is part of form choice
Flip for object forms that address the listener.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Spoken te and formal o, a, lhe belong to different registers
REF: 7.2.4, 7.2.5

In familiar speech, te commonly serves as the direct or indirect object corresponding to você and stands immediately before its verb.
TL: Eu te aviso quando chegar e te mostro as fotos amanhã.
EN: I will let you know when I arrive and show you the photos tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Formal language can address a man as o, a woman as a, and either one indirectly as lhe. Plurals are os, as, and lhes.
TL: É um prazer recebê-la; em seguida lhe apresentaremos a equipe.
EN: It is a pleasure to welcome you; next we will introduce the team to you. Addressing a woman.
[PAUSE:950]
The formal object pronoun does not simply replace every occurrence of everyday você; choose it with the social register.
SHOW: familiar object -> te · formal direct -> o/a/os/as · formal indirect -> lhe/lhes
The formal-dialogue source block follows.

[CARD]
TITLE: Third-person objects expose the speech-writing divide
REF: 7.3.1, 7.3.2

Ele, ela, eles, and elas are subjects and post-preposition forms in every register. In ordinary speech, they also appear after the verb as direct objects.
TL: Encontrei a Marina cedo e vi ela de novo no almoço.
EN: I met Marina early and saw her again at lunch.
[PAUSE:900]
Neutral or formal writing instead uses the unstressed direct objects o, a, os, and as. Indirect objects use lhe or lhes, without a gender contrast.
TL: Encontrei a Marina cedo e a vi de novo; depois lhe enviei a pauta.
EN: I met Marina early and saw her again; later I sent her the agenda.
[PAUSE:950]
Register decides the construction before gender and number decide the particular form.
SHOW: speech DO -> ele/ela/eles/elas after verb · writing DO -> o/a/os/as · writing IO -> lhe/lhes
Flip for the spoken indirect-object pattern.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Spoken indirect objects favor para plus a stressed form
REF: 7.3.2, 7.6.2

Everyday speech normally expresses a third-person recipient with para ele, para ela, para eles, or para elas.
TL: Eu mandei o endereço para ela e contei a novidade para eles.
EN: I sent her the address and told them the news.
[PAUSE:900]
When the direct object is already understood, speech often leaves it unexpressed and retains only the recipient.
TL: Você trouxe os documentos? Mostrei para ele ontem.
EN: Did you bring the documents? I showed them to him yesterday.
[PAUSE:950]
Written lhe and spoken para ele may express the same recipient, but they occupy different grammatical frames.
SHOW: speech IO -> para + ele/ela/eles/elas · understood inanimate DO -> often zero
The spoken-versus-written third-person source block follows.

[CARD]
TITLE: Portuguese often leaves an inanimate it or them unspoken
REF: 7.3.3

When a subject pronoun refers back to a thing, Portuguese can omit it if the reference stays clear. Informal speech may include ele or ela to prevent ambiguity.
TL: Onde está a impressora? Está na outra sala.
EN: Where is the printer? It is in the other room.
[PAUSE:900]
As an object in speech, a previously named thing is very often left out. Careful writing uses o, a, os, or as instead.
TL: Separei as cópias e depois entreguei. No relatório, escrevi que as entreguei.
EN: I sorted the copies and then handed them in. In the report, I wrote that I handed them in.
[PAUSE:950]
Impersonal English it has no Portuguese pronoun at all.
SHOW: recoverable thing-subject -> often zero · spoken thing-object -> often zero · impersonal it -> always zero
Flip to the third-person reflexive.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Se points back to a third-person subject
REF: 7.3.4

The unstressed reflexive se refers back to ele, ela, eles, elas, você, vocês, or a gente when subject and object are the same.
TL: A pesquisadora se apresentou; os colegas se cumprimentaram.
EN: The researcher introduced herself; the colleagues greeted one another.
[PAUSE:900]
With a plural subject, context distinguishes a reflexive reading from a reciprocal one. A gente still uses se because its grammar is third-person singular.
TL: A gente se encontrou perto da estação.
EN: We met one another near the station.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not substitute first-person nos merely because a gente means we.
SHOW: ele/ela/você/a gente -> se · eles/elas/vocês -> se · plural se: selves or each other

[CARD]
TITLE: State the subject when emphasis or clarity requires it
REF: 7.4.1, 7.4.2

Portuguese permits subject pronouns to be omitted whenever the verb form and context identify the subject. Include one for emphasis, contrast, or an otherwise ambiguous third-person form.
TL: Eu reviso os dados; ela prepara os gráficos.
EN: I review the data; she prepares the charts.
[PAUSE:900]
Once a subject has been established, repeated pronouns can sound heavy, especially in writing.
TL: Meu nome é Caio. Trabalho em Recife e estudo à noite.
EN: My name is Caio. I work in Recife and study at night.
[PAUSE:950]
In verbless replies and comparisons, Portuguese uses subject forms where conversational English may use an object form.
SHOW: clear subject -> pronoun optional · emphasis/contrast/ambiguity -> include · reply/comparison -> eu/nós etc.
Flip for the true zero subjects.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Weather, time, and impersonal judgments have no it
REF: 7.3.3, 7.4.2

English inserts it into weather, time, and many impersonal evaluations. Portuguese begins directly with the predicate.
TL: Está frio, são cinco horas e é difícil prever o resultado.
EN: It is cold, it is five o'clock, and it is difficult to predict the result.
[PAUSE:900]
A referential ele or ela is different: use it only when an actual masculine or feminine noun is being resumed and clarity benefits.
TL: O aplicativo é novo, mas ainda apresenta erros.
EN: The app is new, but it still has errors.
[PAUSE:950]
Ask whether English it points to a concrete antecedent before considering a Portuguese pronoun.
SHOW: weather/time/impersonal evaluation -> zero · specific antecedent -> optional ele/ela for clarity

[CARD]
TITLE: An unstressed object pronoun stays beside its verb
REF: 7.5.1, 7.5.2, 7.5.3

Me, te, se, nos, o, a, os, as, lhe, and lhes stand immediately before a verb or attach immediately after it with a hyphen.
TL: Ela me chamou. O diretor cumprimentou-a.
EN: She called me. The director greeted her.
[PAUSE:900]
Everyday speech freely begins a clause with me, te, se, or nos before the verb. Standard writing does not begin a sentence or clause with an unstressed pronoun.
TL: Me avisa amanhã. Avise-me amanhã.
EN: Let me know tomorrow. Spoken, then standard written.
[PAUSE:950]
At a written clause opening with no trigger or explicit subject, attach the pronoun to the finite verb.
SHOW: speech: me avisa · written clause start: avise-me · clitic must touch its verb
Flip for contexts that force the written pronoun before the verb.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A trigger before the verb attracts the clitic
REF: 7.5.4

In standard writing, a negative, relative word, or subordinating conjunction directly before the verb requires the object pronoun to precede that verb.
TL: Não o encontrei. A colega que me ajudou chegou cedo.
EN: I did not find him. The colleague who helped me arrived early.
[PAUSE:900]
An unbroken pre-verbal adverb, an indefinite or demonstrative pronoun, or an interrogative or exclamatory word has the same effect.
TL: Já lhe respondi. Alguém os chamou? Quando a conheceram?
EN: I have already replied to her. Did someone call them? When did they meet her?
[PAUSE:950]
A comma after an initial adverb breaks the attracting unit, so analyze the actual clause boundary.
SHOW: negative/relative/subordinator/adverb/quantifier/question -> clitic + finite verb
The source placement block follows.

[CARD]
TITLE: With an explicit subject, register chooses before or after
REF: 7.5.5, 7.5.6

When a noun or pronoun subject precedes the verb and no attracting trigger applies, neutral writing favors the clitic before the verb.
TL: A polícia o encontrou perto da ponte.
EN: The police found him near the bridge.
[PAUSE:900]
Formal or literary writing can attach the clitic after the verb. Writers may state a subject precisely to permit the more neutral pre-verbal order.
TL: A polícia encontrou-o perto da ponte. Eu me preparei e saí.
EN: The police found him near the bridge. I got ready and left.
[PAUSE:950]
This is a register contrast, not a change in who receives the action.
SHOW: subject + clitic + verb -> neutral writing · subject + verb-clitic -> formal/literary
Flip for the spelling changes after certain verb endings.

[SIDE]
TITLE: O, a, os, and as change shape after a verb
REF: 7.5.7

After an infinitive, drop final r and attach -lo, -la, -los, or -las. Add an accent to preserve stressed a or e; an ordinary final i needs no added accent.
TL: revisar + o -> revisá-lo · escrever + as -> escrevê-las · dividir + os -> dividi-los
EN: to review it · to write them · to divide them
[PAUSE:900]
After a nasal ending, use -no, -na, -nos, or -nas. After a first-person plural form in -mos, drop final s before -lo forms.
TL: põem + as -> põem-nas · entregamos + o -> entregamo-lo
EN: they put them · we delivered it
[PAUSE:950]
Apply the sound-conditioned form only when the direct-object pronoun is attached after the verb.
SHOW: -r + o/a -> -lo/-la · nasal + o/a -> -no/-na · -mos + o/a -> -mo-lo/-mo-la

[CARD]
TITLE: Infinitives preserve both register and pronoun class
REF: 7.5.8

Me, te, se, nos, and lhe can precede an infinitive in speech and neutral writing or attach after it in formal writing.
TL: O curso pode nos ajudar. O curso pode ajudar-nos.
EN: The course can help us. Neutral, then formal.
[PAUSE:900]
Third-person direct objects normally attach as -lo, -la, -los, or -las. Formal writing can put them before an infinitive after an attracting word.
TL: Decidimos comprá-las, mas lamentamos não as ter testado.
EN: We decided to buy them, but regret not having tested them.
[PAUSE:950]
An infinitive used as the subject begins with a clitic in speech but attaches it in standard writing.
SHOW: neutral: pode nos ajudar · formal: pode ajudar-nos · DO: comprá-lo · written subject: adaptar-se
Flip to compound tenses.

[SIDE]
TITLE: In speech, many clitics sit between auxiliary and main verb
REF: 7.5.9, 7.5.10

With a perfect tense, speech and neutral writing place me, te, se, nos, and often lhe between the auxiliary and participle, without a hyphen.
TL: Ela tinha me contado a verdade; eles têm se preparado bem.
EN: She had told me the truth; they have been preparing themselves well.
[PAUSE:900]
With estar plus a gerund, the same class normally sits between estar and the gerund. Third-person direct objects follow the broader written placement rules.
TL: Estamos nos organizando. A equipe o está acompanhando.
EN: We are getting organized. The team is monitoring him.
[PAUSE:950]
An adverbial gerund outside a continuous construction takes an attached pronoun.
SHOW: auxiliary + me/te/se/nos/lhe + participle/gerund · adverbial gerund -> gerund-clitic

[CARD]
TITLE: Use a prepositional recipient when lhe is not enough
REF: 7.6.1, 7.6.3, 7.6.4

In writing, a ele or a ela can replace lhe. The explicit phrase is required for contrast, to resolve ambiguity, or when direct and indirect objects are both pronouns.
TL: Eu queria que você o entregasse a ela.
EN: I wanted you to deliver it to her.
[PAUSE:900]
Brazilian Portuguese does not build Spanish-style clusters of unstressed direct and indirect third-person objects. Keep one object as a prepositional phrase.
TL: O gerente a apresentou a eles.
EN: The manager introduced her to them.
[PAUSE:950]
When an action affects a person physically, colloquial usage often chooses em; a beneficiary can also appear as an indirect clitic with a definite article on the possessed item.
SHOW: stressed/clear recipient -> a + pronoun · two pronoun objects -> DO clitic + a + recipient · physical effect -> em
Flip to the forms that follow ordinary prepositions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: After a preposition, use a stressed pronoun
REF: 7.7.1, 7.7.2, 7.7.3

After most prepositions, use mim for first-person singular and the ordinary subject-shaped forms for the others.
TL: O acordo depende de mim, dela e de vocês.
EN: The agreement depends on me, her, and you.
[PAUSE:900]
Com combines as comigo and conosco; with other pronouns it remains separate. Third-person de and em contract to dele, dela, deles, delas and nele, nela, neles, nelas.
TL: Venha comigo; depois falaremos com eles sobre isso.
EN: Come with me; afterward we will speak with them about it.
[PAUSE:950]
Formal reflexive si and consigo are restricted; ordinary speech usually uses ele mesmo or ela mesma.
SHOW: mim · comigo/conosco · com você/eles · de+ela -> dela · em+eles -> neles

[CARD]
TITLE: Emphasis can replace a clitic with a stressed phrase
REF: 7.8

Formal writing can emphasize a direct object with a plus a stressed pronoun. If a coordinated noun joins it, that noun also receives a.
TL: Convidaram a mim e ao novo diretor.
EN: They invited me and the new director.
[PAUSE:900]
Speech instead places the stressed subject form in ordinary noun-object position. Words such as só, todos, and mesmo can make the emphasis explicit.
TL: Chamaram eu primeiro; depois decidiram ajudar só ela.
EN: They called me first; then they decided to help only her.
[PAUSE:950]
Keep the formal and colloquial constructions separate rather than mixing their case and preposition patterns.
SHOW: formal emphasis -> a mim/a ela · speech -> eu/ela after verb · mesmo adds self-emphasis
Flip for regional forms and a final register check.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Treat regional forms as register choices, not universal defaults
REF: 7.9, 7.10, 7.11

Some Brazilian regions use tu with a third-person verb in highly colloquial speech, and some use lhe as a direct as well as indirect object of você. Vós is largely limited to biblical language.
TL: Tu vai hoje? This combination is regional and colloquial.
EN: Are you going today?
[PAUSE:900]
Institutional honorifics such as Vossa Excelência behave as third person. Their agreement follows the person addressed, while reflexive and possessive forms remain third-person.
TL: Vossa Excelência está preparada para apresentar sua decisão?
EN: Is Your Excellency prepared to present your decision? Addressing a woman.
[PAUSE:950]
For every production, decide register, identify grammatical role, select person and number, and only then place or attach the form.
SHOW: register -> role -> person/number/gender -> placement -> spelling · speech and writing are parallel systems
