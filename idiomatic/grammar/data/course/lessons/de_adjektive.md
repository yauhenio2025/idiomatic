## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: The adjective has three lives
SVG: de_adjektive_c01f.svg
REF: 6, 6.1.1

An adjective can sit inside a noun phrase, complete a verb, or describe how an event unfolds. Only the first position forces it to agree with a noun. Hear the three jobs in sequence:
TL: Eine gründliche Analyse verhindert teure Fehler.
EN: A thorough analysis prevents costly mistakes.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Analyse war gründlich.
EN: The analysis was thorough.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Das Team prüfte den Vertrag gründlich.
EN: The team examined the contract thoroughly.
Before the noun, the adjective has an ending. After the linking verb, or while describing the examination, it stays bare.
SHOW: before a noun → agreement · after a verb → no ending
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Complements stay bare too
REF: 6, 6.1.1

An endingless adjective can describe the subject, but it can also describe the object as the result of an action:
TL: Die Restauratorin strich die Wand ockerfarben.
EN: The restorer painted the wall ochre.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Ausschuss erklärte den Vorschlag für untauglich.
EN: The committee declared the proposal unsuitable.
[PAUSE:1000]
And an adjective separated from its noun behaves the same way:
TL: Die Gäste verließen das Gebäude unverletzt.
EN: The guests left the building unharmed.
Do not ask whether the adjective is descriptive. They all are. Ask whether it stands before a noun inside that noun phrase. That position, and only that position, triggers declension.
SHOW: attributive = declined · predicative and adverbial = bare
Next, the logic behind the endings.

[CARD]
TITLE: One clear signal is enough
SVG: de_adjektive_c02f.svg
REF: 6.1.2, 6.1.3

German wants case, gender and number to be visible somewhere near the front of a noun phrase. If no determiner carries that information, the adjective does the heavy work with a strong ending:
TL: Kalter Wind zog durch die Straßen.
EN: Cold wind swept through the streets.
[PAUSE:1000]
If a determiner already carries the signal, the adjective takes a weak ending:
TL: Der kalte Wind ließ am Abend nach.
EN: The cold wind died down in the evening.
[PAUSE:1000]
With an endingless determiner, the adjective supplies what is missing:
TL: Ein kalter Wind kam vom Meer.
EN: A cold wind came from the sea.
SHOW: no signal before adjective → strong · clear signal → weak
Flip the card for the ending map.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Strong points outward, weak recedes
REF: 6.1.2, 6.1.3a

Strong endings mostly resemble the endings of the definite article: masculine nominative ends in -er, neuter in -es, feminine and plural in -e. The other conspicuous forms are -en in masculine accusative, -em in dative masculine and neuter, and -er in dative feminine.
TL: Mit frischem Brot schmeckt die Suppe besser.
EN: The soup tastes better with fresh bread.
[PAUSE:1000]
The genitive singular masculine and neuter are the useful exception: the adjective normally takes -en because the noun itself carries the genitive marker.
TL: Wegen heftigen Regens blieb der Platz leer.
EN: Because of heavy rain, the square remained empty.
Weak declension is deliberately small: -e in the five nominative-or-accusative singular slots, and -en everywhere else.
SHOW: strong endings identify · weak endings mostly reduce to -e / -en
The first exercise block tests this signal-sharing system.

[CARD]
TITLE: Let the determiner choose
SVG: de_adjektive_c03f.svg
REF: 6.1.3a, 6.1.3b, 6.1.3c

You do not need three unrelated adjective tables. Read the word before the adjective. No determiner means strong agreement:
TL: Frischer Spargel schmeckt im Mai am besten.
EN: Fresh asparagus tastes best in May.
[PAUSE:1000]
An endingless possessive or indefinite article also leaves a gap, so the adjective takes the strong signal:
TL: Ihr kluger Einwand änderte den Verlauf der Debatte.
EN: Her clever objection changed the course of the debate.
[PAUSE:1000]
Once the determiner has a visible ending, the adjective turns weak:
TL: Wir folgten ihrem klugen Einwand.
EN: We followed her clever objection.
SHOW: inspect the determiner first — the adjective fills only missing information
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mixed is a route, not a third system
REF: 6.1.3b, 6.1.3c

The so-called mixed declension simply alternates between those two routes. An endingless determiner calls for a strong adjective; an inflected form of the same determiner calls for a weak one.
TL: Kein neues Verfahren ist völlig risikofrei.
EN: No new procedure is completely free of risk.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Mit keinem neuen Verfahren lässt sich das Risiko ganz beseitigen.
EN: The risk cannot be eliminated entirely with any new procedure.
[PAUSE:1000]
The contrast is structural, not lexical: the first determiner reveals too little, while the second already marks the dative.
SHOW: endingless determiner + strong · inflected determiner + weak
A broad declension drill follows this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Plural gatekeepers
REF: 6.1.4a, 6.1.4b, 6.1.4c

Plural quantifiers are where the tidy principle meets real usage. A fully inflected universal determiner strongly favors a weak adjective:
TL: Alle offenen Fragen kamen erneut auf den Tisch.
EN: All unresolved questions came up for discussion again.
[PAUSE:1000]
The same predominant pattern follows the word for both:
TL: Beide plausiblen Lösungen hätten denselben Nachteil.
EN: Both plausible solutions would have the same disadvantage.
[PAUSE:1000]
If another determiner comes first, the quantifier itself behaves like an adjective, and every following adjective follows the ordinary agreement logic.
TL: Diese vielen kleinen Verzögerungen summieren sich.
EN: These numerous small delays add up.
SHOW: fully marked gatekeeper → weak adjective
Flip for the variable group.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quantifiers preserve some variation
REF: 6.1.4a, 6.1.4c

After the common words meaning several, many, few and some, modern German usually gives the following adjective a strong ending. The contrast is clearest in the genitive plural:
TL: Die Kritik vieler erfahrener Fachleute war eindeutig.
EN: The criticism from many experienced specialists was unequivocal.
[PAUSE:1000]
Compare the weak adjective after the universal determiner:
TL: Die Kritik aller erfahrenen Fachleute wurde berücksichtigt.
EN: The criticism from all the experienced specialists was taken into account.
[PAUSE:1000]
Some less settled quantifiers allow both patterns, with preferences changing by case and register. Learn the stable high-frequency contrasts first, then accept the documented variation instead of forcing a false universal rule.
SHOW: many / few / several / some → usually strong · all → weak
The next block concentrates on plural determiners and quantifiers.

[CARD]
TITLE: Agreement reaches every adjective
REF: 6.1.3d, 6.1.3f

Two coordinate adjectives describing the same noun normally take the same ending:
TL: Ein ruhiger, präziser Ton überzeugte das Publikum.
EN: A calm, precise tone convinced the audience.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Mit einem ruhigen, präzisen Ton gewann sie Vertrauen.
EN: With a calm, precise tone, she gained trust.
[PAUSE:1000]
But one shared adjective cannot silently cover nouns of different gender or number. German repeats the determiner and adjective so that each noun phrase can agree for itself:
TL: Der neue Tisch und die neuen Stühle wurden gestern geliefert.
EN: The new table and the new chairs were delivered yesterday.
SHOW: same noun → same endings · different nouns → repeat and agree
Flip the card for forms that resist the grid.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Irregular stems and long attributes
REF: 6.1.5, 6.1.6

Some stems change when an ending arrives. The high-frequency patterns deserve automatic recall:
TL: Der hohe Turm war schon aus der Ferne sichtbar.
EN: The tall tower was already visible from a distance.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Sie bestellte einen teuren, dunkelblauen Mantel.
EN: She ordered an expensive dark-blue coat.
[PAUSE:1000]
Formal writing can place an entire complement before an attributive adjective. The agreement ending still sits at the far edge of the adjective:
TL: Die für den Notfall vorgesehenen Mittel reichen aus.
EN: The funds earmarked for an emergency are sufficient.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Eine dem Vorstand bislang unbekannte Klausel änderte die Lage.
EN: A clause previously unknown to the board changed the situation.
SHOW: long modifier + adjective + agreement ending + noun
With the declension system consolidated, the next card turns adjectives into nouns.

[CARD]
TITLE: Any adjective can become a noun
SVG: de_adjektive_c06f.svg
REF: 6.2.1, 6.2.3

German can promote an adjective into a noun without adding a new suffix. Capitalization announces the new job, while the adjective ending still marks gender, case and number:
TL: Ein Unbekannter hinterließ die Nachricht.
EN: An unknown man left the message.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Verantwortliche antwortete noch am selben Abend.
EN: The woman responsible replied that same evening.
[PAUSE:1000]
The article and ending can identify a man, a woman, or a group even though the lexical core stays unchanged.
SHOW: adjective → capital letter + adjective ending = noun
Flip for neuter abstractions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Neuter means a thing, fact or idea
REF: 6.2.1, 6.2.4a, 6.2.4b

Neuter adjectival nouns compress what English often expands into a clause or a phrase with thing:
TL: Etwas Unerwartetes verzögerte die Abfahrt.
EN: Something unexpected delayed the departure.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Sie hat nichts Entscheidendes verschwiegen.
EN: She concealed nothing decisive.
[PAUSE:1000]
With the definite article, the same mechanism can name an abstract feature or everything covered by a prior description:
TL: Das Unangenehme daran war die Ungewissheit.
EN: The unpleasant thing about it was the uncertainty.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Das bereits Vereinbarte gilt weiterhin.
EN: What has already been agreed remains in force.
SHOW: neuter adjectival noun = the abstract, the collective, the unsaid thing
Next, three forms that look deceptively similar.

[CARD]
TITLE: Capital letter or hidden noun?
SVG: de_adjektive_c07f.svg
REF: 6.1.3e, 6.2.1, 6.2.2a

When a previously named noun is merely omitted, the adjective stays lowercase. Its ending still agrees with that understood noun:
TL: Welchen Mantel nimmst du? – Den dunklen.
EN: Which coat are you taking? The dark one.
[PAUSE:1000]
When the adjective itself names a person or thing, it is a noun and takes a capital:
TL: Wen hast du angerufen? – Den Kranken.
EN: Whom did you call? The sick man.
[PAUSE:1000]
Sound does not reveal the difference. Meaning and capitalization do.
SHOW: hidden named noun → lowercase · adjective is the noun → capital
Flip for the weak-noun look-alike.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Adjectival noun is not weak noun
REF: 6.2.2a, 6.2.3, 6.2.4c

A masculine adjectival noun keeps adjective declension, while a weak masculine noun has its own noun paradigm:
TL: Ein Deutscher sprach mit einem Franzosen.
EN: A German man spoke with a Frenchman.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first nominative exposes the strong adjective ending. The second noun already has its ordinary nominative form and adds its weak-noun ending outside the nominative.
TL: Wir baten die Angestellte um eine Stellungnahme.
EN: We asked the female employee for a statement.
[PAUSE:1000]
Language names add another contrast: bare language names are endingless, while the substantivized form carries an adjective ending.
TL: Sie verhandeln auf Deutsch und übersetzen den Vertrag aus dem Englischen.
EN: They negotiate in German and translate the contract from English.
SHOW: adjectival noun declines as adjective · weak noun declines as noun
Six clean adjectival-noun items follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Adjectives can assign case
SVG: de_adjektive_c08f.svg
REF: 6.3, 6.3.1

Some adjectives open a grammatical slot for a dependent noun phrase. The individual adjective determines the case. Dative is the largest group, often marking the person for whom a quality matters:
TL: Der Ausgang ist dem Team noch unklar.
EN: The outcome is still unclear to the team.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Diese Abkürzung ist mir nicht geläufig.
EN: This abbreviation is not familiar to me.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Ihre Unterstützung war dem Projekt äußerst nützlich.
EN: Her support was extremely useful to the project.
The dependent normally precedes the adjective, although position is not the source of the case. Lexical government is.
SHOW: adjective + licensed dependent · dative is the largest class
Flip for accusative and genitive.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Accusative is compact, genitive formal
REF: 6.3.2, 6.3.3

A small group of predicative adjectives takes an accusative:
TL: Sie ist den ständigen Lärm inzwischen gewohnt.
EN: She is now used to the constant noise.
[PAUSE:1000]
One adjective can even govern a dative person and an accusative thing:
TL: Wir sind dem Auftraggeber noch eine Antwort schuldig.
EN: We still owe the client an answer.
[PAUSE:1000]
Genitive complements belong mainly to formal registers. Two common patterns also require a reflexive pronoun:
TL: Er war sich der Folgen durchaus bewusst.
EN: He was fully aware of the consequences.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Zeugin war sich ihrer Aussage sicher.
EN: The witness was sure of her statement.
SHOW: accustomed → accusative · aware / sure → reflexive + genitive
That is the whole case system for adjectives. Next, their prepositional partners.

[CARD]
TITLE: Prepositions are lexical partners
SVG: de_adjektive_c09f.svg
REF: 6.4, 6.4.1a

Many adjectives select a particular preposition. Treat the pair as one lexical frame, because translation alone will not predict it:
TL: Der Erfolg ist von verlässlichen Daten abhängig.
EN: Success depends on reliable data.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Juristin ist auf schwierige Verhandlungen vorbereitet.
EN: The lawyer is prepared for difficult negotiations.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir sind mit der überarbeiteten Fassung einverstanden.
EN: We agree with the revised version.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Er ist für die Sicherheit zuständig.
EN: He is responsible for safety.
SHOW: learn adjective + preposition + case as one frame
Flip for pronouns and clauses.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Things become da-compounds
REF: 6.4.1a, 6.4.1c

With a person, use the ordinary preposition and pronoun:
TL: Die Direktorin war wütend auf ihn.
EN: The director was furious with him.
[PAUSE:1000]
For a thing, an idea or an anticipated clause, German typically uses a da-compound:
TL: Ich bin darauf gespannt, wie der Ausschuss entscheidet.
EN: I am curious to see how the committee decides.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wir sind daran gewöhnt, unter Zeitdruck zu arbeiten.
EN: We are used to working under time pressure.
[PAUSE:1000]
The anticipatory da-compound can sometimes be omitted, but it is common, especially in writing, and it focuses attention on the clause that follows.
SHOW: person → preposition + pronoun · thing or clause → da-compound
Fourteen preposition-and-case decisions come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Complements travel inside attributes
REF: 6.1.6, 6.3.1, 6.4.1

An adjective keeps its case or prepositional complement when the whole structure moves before a noun. Start with a predicate:
TL: Die Regel ist den meisten Bewerbern unbekannt.
EN: The rule is unknown to most applicants.
[PAUSE:900]
Now pack the same relationship into the noun phrase:
TL: Die den meisten Bewerbern unbekannte Regel wurde geändert.
EN: The rule unknown to most applicants was changed.
[PAUSE:1000]
The dative complement moves before the adjective, and the adjective acquires the ending required by the noun phrase.
SHOW: complement + adjective + agreement ending + noun
Flip for the prepositional version.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Formal German packs information leftward
REF: 6.1.6, 6.4.1a

The same transformation works with a governed preposition:
TL: Die Abteilung ist für die Prüfung zuständig.
EN: The department is responsible for the examination.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die für die Prüfung zuständige Abteilung meldet sich morgen.
EN: The department responsible for the examination will get in touch tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Predicatively, a personal pronoun almost always follows its adjective and preposition:
TL: Die Forscherin ist auf ihn angewiesen.
EN: The researcher depends on him.
[PAUSE:1000]
Extended attributes are dense and mainly written. Parse them from the right: find the noun, then the adjective and its ending, then attach everything the adjective governs.
SHOW: read extended attributes from the noun leftward
Seven clean translation items consolidate adjective complements.

[CARD]
TITLE: Comparison adds a layer
REF: 6.5.1a, 6.5.1b, 6.5.1c, 6.5.1d

German normally builds the comparative with -er and the superlative with -st or -est, regardless of adjective length. Frequent short adjectives may add an umlaut, and four common stems are irregular:
TL: präzise – präziser – am präzisesten
EN: precise – more precise – most precise
[PAUSE:700]
TL: klug – klüger – am klügsten
EN: clever – cleverer – cleverest
[PAUSE:700]
TL: gut – besser – am besten
EN: good – better – best
[PAUSE:700]
TL: hoch – höher – am höchsten
EN: high – higher – highest
SHOW: base + -er · base + -st / -est · learn common umlauts and irregulars
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Comparative plus agreement
REF: 6.5.1a, 6.5.1e, 6.5.2a, 6.5.2b, 6.5.2h

Before a noun, comparison and agreement stack. First build the comparative stem, then add the ordinary adjective ending:
TL: Wir brauchen eine präzisere Messung.
EN: We need a more precise measurement.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use the standard particle for unequal comparison, and the equality frame with the word for like:
TL: Die zweite Fassung ist klarer als die erste.
EN: The second version is clearer than the first.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die dritte ist genauso klar wie die zweite.
EN: The third is just as clear as the second.
[PAUSE:1000]
A measured difference can stand in an accusative phrase or follow the preposition meaning by:
TL: Der neue Sensor reagiert um zwei Sekunden schneller als der alte.
EN: The new sensor responds two seconds faster than the old one.
SHOW: comparative stem + agreement · unequal: als · equal: so … wie

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose the right superlative
SVG: de_adjektive_c12f.svg
REF: 6.5.3a, 6.5.3b, 6.5.3c

Use the declined superlative with a definite article when it modifies an expressed or understood noun:
TL: Sie wählte den kürzesten Weg.
EN: She chose the shortest route.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Dieser Weg ist der kürzeste.
EN: This route is the shortest one.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use the form with the preposition and -en when there is no noun to recover, or when one thing is compared across times, places or conditions:
TL: Im Winter ist dieser Weg am gefährlichsten.
EN: In winter, this route is at its most dangerous.
SHOW: noun expressed or understood → definite article · no noun / self-comparison → am
Flip for scales that keep moving.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Moving scales and paired clauses
REF: 6.5.2f, 6.5.2g, 6.5.3b

The comparative can show an increasing trend with the word meaning always:
TL: Die Verhandlungen wurden immer schwieriger.
EN: The negotiations became more and more difficult.
[PAUSE:1000]
For two linked scales, put the first comparative in a subordinate clause and the second at the front of the main clause:
TL: Je länger sie dauerten, desto ungeduldiger wurde der Ausschuss.
EN: The longer they lasted, the more impatient the committee became.
[PAUSE:1200]
A superlative can also be absolute: it intensifies without comparing a real set.
TL: Der Bericht wurde mit größter Sorgfalt geprüft.
EN: The report was examined with the utmost care.
SHOW: immer + comparative = progression · je … desto = linked scales
