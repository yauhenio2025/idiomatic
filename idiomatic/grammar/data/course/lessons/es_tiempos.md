## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A tense name is not a translation
REF: 17.2

Spanish tense choice combines three questions: where the speaker places the viewpoint, whether an event is seen from within or as a whole, and what stance the speaker adds. The English tense label is only a clue.
TL: Cuando llegó la directora, revisábamos el informe que habíamos preparado.
EN: When the director arrived, we were reviewing the report that we had prepared.
[PAUSE:1000]
Llegó advances the story, revisábamos opens the scene around that arrival, and habíamos preparado places preparation before both.
SHOW: viewpoint · boundaries · relation to another time
Flip for forms that reach beyond their names.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Present and future forms travel in time
REF: 17.2, 17.3, 17.6

The present can express a current event, a habit, a future arrangement, a historic scene, or a directive. The future can locate a later event, but it can also mark a present guess.
TL: Mañana salimos a las ocho; Marta estará ya en la oficina.
EN: Tomorrow we leave at eight; Marta is probably already at the office.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first verb is present with explicit future time. The second is morphologically future but makes a conjecture about now.
TL: En 1812 se aprueba la Constitución de Cádiz.
EN: In 1812 the Constitution of Cádiz is adopted.
SHOW: choose the meaning before the form
Flip for the simple and continuous present.

[CARD]
TITLE: The simple present often covers now
REF: 17.3.1, 17.3.2, 19.1.2

Unlike English, Spanish does not need a continuous form merely because an action is happening at this moment. The simple present can report a current event as well as a habit or state.
TL: Busco a la responsable del proyecto. ¿Sabes dónde está?
EN: I am looking for the project lead. Do you know where she is?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Normalmente busco los expedientes por número.
EN: I normally look up the files by number.
[PAUSE:1000]
Context distinguishes the current search from the normal procedure.
SHOW: simple present = current, habitual, or stative
Flip for what the continuous adds.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The continuous foregrounds activity
REF: 19.1, 19.2.1, 19.2.2

Estar plus gerund presents an event as visibly under way, temporary, developing, or unusually persistent. It is an aspectual choice, not an automatic equivalent of English be plus ing.
TL: Estamos revisando las cifras; te llamo cuando terminemos.
EN: We are reviewing the figures; I will call you when we finish.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Últimamente el equipo está trabajando hasta muy tarde.
EN: Lately the team has been working until very late.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first sentence focuses on the activity in progress. The second frames a temporary pattern rather than a neutral habit.
SHOW: estar + gerund = activity foregrounded
Ten simple-versus-continuous responses follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The preterite closes a boundary
REF: 17.4.1, 17.4.2, 17.4.4

The preterite presents an event or state as a bounded whole. Its duration can be long or short; what matters is that the speaker views the interval from outside and treats it as complete.
TL: La comisión estudió el caso durante seis meses y publicó el informe en junio.
EN: The committee studied the case for six months and published the report in June.
[PAUSE:1000]
Durante seis meses does not require the imperfect. The interval is measured and closed, and publicó adds the next completed event.
TL: Vivieron en Quito hasta 2019.
EN: They lived in Quito until 2019.
SHOW: bounded interval or completed event → preterite
Flip to enter the past scene.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The imperfect opens the scene
REF: 17.5.1, 17.5.2

The imperfect views a past state or event from within, without asserting its beginning or end. It supplies descriptions, circumstances, habits, and actions already in progress around another event.
TL: La oficina estaba vacía y llovía cuando sonó el teléfono.
EN: The office was empty and it was raining when the phone rang.
[PAUSE:1000]
Estaba and llovía form the open background. Sonó is the bounded event that advances the timeline.
TL: De niña, Clara leía junto a la ventana todas las tardes.
EN: As a child, Clara used to read by the window every afternoon.
SHOW: scene, state, habit, or ongoing frame → imperfect
Flip for a reliable contrast procedure.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ask what the sentence asserts
REF: 17.4, 17.5

Do not reduce the contrast to short versus long. Ask whether the sentence asserts a complete occurrence or opens a past situation. Repetition can take either tense according to that viewpoint.
TL: Durante aquel verano fuimos al mercado cada sábado.
EN: During that summer we went to the market every Saturday.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Cuando vivíamos allí, íbamos al mercado cada sábado.
EN: When we lived there, we used to go to the market every Saturday.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first counts a habit inside a closed summer. The second makes the habit part of an unbounded past frame.
SHOW: same duration or repetition · different viewpoint
Flip for changes of state and lexical effects.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A boundary can change a verb's reading
REF: 17.4.7, 17.4.9, 17.4.10

With some verbs, the preterite naturally highlights entry into a state or a successful outcome, while the imperfect describes the state already holding.
TL: Sabía la dirección, pero ayer supe por qué habían cerrado la oficina.
EN: I knew the address, but yesterday I found out why they had closed the office.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Conocíamos a la autora; la conocimos en un congreso en Lima.
EN: We knew the author; we met her at a conference in Lima.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not memorize an English word swap without the viewpoint: supe and conocimos mark the onset of knowing.
SHOW: state in progress → imperfect · entry or outcome → preterite
Fifteen preterite-versus-imperfect corrections and completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The perfect keeps a link to now
REF: 18.2.1, 18.2.2, 18.2.3

The present perfect combines present haber with an invariant past participle. It is natural when the time frame includes now, the result matters now, or the event is presented as recent.
TL: Este mes hemos recibido tres propuestas y todavía no hemos tomado una decisión.
EN: This month we have received three proposals and still have not made a decision.
[PAUSE:1000]
The open phrase este mes and the current lack of a decision keep the events connected to the present.
TL: He perdido la llave; no puedo abrir el archivo.
EN: I have lost the key; I cannot open the file.
SHOW: past event viewed from the present → perfect
Flip for the regional fault line.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Region helps set the perfect boundary
REF: 18.2, 18.2.8

Peninsular usage often contrasts an open current period with a closed past period. Much of Latin America uses the preterite in many contexts where Spain prefers the present perfect, especially for a recent completed event.
TL: Esta mañana he hablado con Lucía. Ayer hablé con Andrés.
EN: This morning I spoke with Lucía. Yesterday I spoke with Andrés.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ya comí, gracias.
EN: I have already eaten, thank you.
[PAUSE:1000]
The second example is a normal Latin American preterite with present relevance. Treat variation as part of the system, not as an error.
SHOW: time frame + present relevance + regional norm
Flip for the earlier past.

[CARD]
TITLE: The pluperfect looks back from the past
REF: 18.3.1

The pluperfect uses imperfect haber plus the past participle. It locates an event before a past reference point and prevents the two layers from collapsing.
TL: Cuando empezó la reunión, la asesora ya había enviado las correcciones.
EN: When the meeting began, the adviser had already sent the corrections.
[PAUSE:1000]
Empezó supplies the past checkpoint. Había enviado looks backward from it.
TL: No pudimos entrar porque alguien había cambiado la cerradura.
EN: We could not enter because someone had changed the lock.
SHOW: earlier past → había + participle
Flip for compound-tense word order.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Haber and its participle stay together
REF: 18.1.1, 18.1.3

Object pronouns and ordinary adverbs normally stand before haber or after the complete compound form; they do not split the auxiliary from its participle. With haber, the participle does not agree with a preceding object.
TL: Nunca la habíamos visto tan preocupada.
EN: We had never seen her so worried.
[PAUSE:900]
Tener plus a participle is a different construction and can describe a resulting state; that participle agrees with its object.
TL: Ya tenemos revisadas todas las solicitudes.
EN: We already have all the applications reviewed.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: la habíamos visto · tenemos revisadas las solicitudes
Ten perfect-or-pluperfect completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Spanish has three common routes to the future
REF: 17.3.8, 17.6.1, 17.6.3, 17.6.4

A future event can appear in the present, with ir a plus infinitive, or in the morphological future. The present is natural for a scheduled or contextually secure arrangement.
TL: El tren sale mañana a las siete.
EN: The train leaves tomorrow at seven.
[PAUSE:900]
Ir a often presents a plan, intention, or development grounded in current evidence.
TL: Vamos a revisar el contrato esta tarde.
EN: We are going to review the contract this afternoon.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: schedule/context → present · plan/evidence → ir a + infinitive
Flip for what the future form contributes.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The future form makes projection explicit
REF: 17.6.1, 17.6.2

The morphological future is especially useful for prediction, promise, firm projection, and emphatic instruction. It can coexist with the other future routes, but the speaker's stance is different.
TL: El nuevo sistema reducirá los errores y ahorrará tiempo.
EN: The new system will reduce errors and save time.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: No revelarás esta información a nadie.
EN: You will not disclose this information to anyone.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first predicts. The second is formally future but functions as a forceful command.
SHOW: explicit projection, promise, prediction, or command
Seventeen future-form completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Future morphology can describe the present
REF: 17.6.5

The future can mark a conjecture, estimate, or probability about now. English often translates it with probably, must, or I wonder rather than will.
TL: ¿Dónde está Elena? Estará todavía en el laboratorio.
EN: Where is Elena? She is probably still in the laboratory.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: ¿Cuántas personas caben aquí? Cabrá un centenar.
EN: How many people fit here? Probably about a hundred.
[PAUSE:1000]
Time reference and verb morphology point in different directions: the situation is present, while the future ending marks the speaker's uncertainty.
SHOW: future + present context = conjecture about now
Flip to conjecture about a completed event.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The future perfect guesses about completion
REF: 17.6.5, 18.6

For a conjecture about an event already complete, use the future perfect. Haber carries the future ending and the participle carries the lexical event.
TL: No contestan; se habrán quedado sin batería.
EN: They are not answering; their battery has probably died.
[PAUSE:900]
The same form can locate genuine completion before a future checkpoint.
TL: Para el viernes habremos terminado la auditoría.
EN: By Friday we will have finished the audit.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: habrá ocurrido = probably happened · or will have happened
Ten suppositional-future transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The conditional is a future viewed from the past
REF: 17.7.3

In reported thought or speech, the conditional locates a later event relative to a past viewpoint. It is the future shifted one step back.
TL: Marta dijo que enviaría el informe al día siguiente.
EN: Marta said that she would send the report the following day.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sabíamos que la reparación costaría más de lo previsto.
EN: We knew that the repair would cost more than expected.
[PAUSE:1000]
The sending and the cost lie after dijo and sabíamos, even if all the events are now past.
SHOW: past viewpoint + later event → conditional
Flip for its hypothetical and courteous work.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Distance softens a claim or request
REF: 17.7.1, 17.7.4

The conditional presents a consequence as dependent, a wish as remote, or a request as less direct. The shared idea is distance from a plain assertion.
TL: Con más tiempo, revisaríamos también los anexos.
EN: With more time, we would also review the appendices.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: ¿Podría enviarme una copia, por favor?
EN: Could you send me a copy, please?
[PAUSE:1000]
The form does not by itself build the if clause; the surrounding syntax supplies the condition or social frame.
SHOW: later-from-past · hypothetical consequence · courteous distance
Thirty conditional completions and transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The conditional also estimates the past
REF: 17.7.2

Just as the future can conjecture about now, the conditional can conjecture or approximate from a past viewpoint.
TL: Cuando llamó, serían cerca de las once.
EN: When she called, it was probably around eleven.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: No abrió la puerta; estaría durmiendo.
EN: He did not open the door; he was probably sleeping.
[PAUSE:1000]
The event is past, but the speaker presents the explanation or quantity as an estimate rather than a fact.
SHOW: conditional + past context = conjecture about then
Flip for the compound version.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The conditional perfect keeps both distances
REF: 18.6

The conditional perfect places a completed event after a past viewpoint, or presents a completed past event as unreal, reported, or inferred.
TL: Prometió que habría terminado antes del viernes.
EN: She promised that she would have finished before Friday.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sin el aviso, habríamos perdido el tren.
EN: Without the warning, we would have missed the train.
[PAUSE:1000]
Habría terminado is completion viewed from a past promise. Habríamos perdido is a completed consequence that did not occur.
SHOW: habría + participle = completed event at conditional distance
Flip for the progressive system.

[CARD]
TITLE: Continuous forms exist across the timeline
REF: 19.1.1, 19.2

Estar can carry the tense while the gerund keeps the event in progress. This produces forms such as está trabajando, estaba trabajando, estuvo trabajando, ha estado trabajando, and estará trabajando.
TL: Ayer a las cinco estábamos esperando los resultados.
EN: Yesterday at five we were waiting for the results.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Mañana a esta hora estaremos cruzando los Andes.
EN: Tomorrow at this time we will be crossing the Andes.
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose the temporal viewpoint on estar, then ask whether foregrounding the unfolding activity is useful.
SHOW: tense on estar + gerund = event viewed in progress
Flip for the limits on this construction.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Do not force every English progressive
REF: 19.1.2, 19.3

Spanish often uses a simple tense for states, position, scheduled events, and actions whose progress does not need emphasis. Several verbs strongly resist the continuous in their ordinary stative use.
TL: Hoy lleva una chaqueta roja y parece cansado.
EN: Today he is wearing a red jacket and looks tired.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: La lámpara colgaba del techo; Ana estaba sentada junto a la puerta.
EN: The lamp was hanging from the ceiling; Ana was sitting by the door.
[PAUSE:1000]
Estaba sentándose would describe the act of sitting down, not the resulting seated position.
SHOW: state or position → usually simple tense or estar + participle
Flip for a final selection procedure.

[CARD]
TITLE: Build the timeline before choosing the tense
REF: 17.4, 17.5, 18.2, 18.3

For past narration, mark the reference point first. Use the imperfect for the open scene, the preterite for bounded events that move it forward, the perfect for a past linked to the present, and the pluperfect for an earlier past.
TL: Esta semana he hablado con Julia. Ayer me contó que había dejado el trabajo porque necesitaba un cambio.
EN: This week I spoke with Julia. Yesterday she told me that she had left her job because she needed a change.
[PAUSE:1000]
Each form has a distinct job: current frame, narrative event, earlier event, and background motivation.
SHOW: now-link · story event · open scene · earlier past
Flip for the complete decision loop.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use four questions, in order
REF: 17.2, 17.6, 17.7, 19.1

First, locate the viewpoint: present, past, or future. Second, decide whether the event is bounded, ongoing, or anterior to another point. Third, ask whether the speaker is asserting, predicting, or conjecturing. Finally, use a continuous form only when the activity itself deserves focus.
TL: A estas horas estarán cenando; cuando lleguemos, ya habrán terminado.
EN: At this time they are probably having dinner; when we arrive, they will already have finished.
[PAUSE:1000]
Estarán cenando is a present conjecture with activity foregrounded. Habrán terminado is genuine future completion before a later checkpoint.
SHOW: viewpoint → boundary → stance → progressive focus
Twelve mixed past-tense choices close the unit.
