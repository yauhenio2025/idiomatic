## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A comparison needs a scale, a target, and a benchmark
REF: 16.1, 16.7, 16.18

Before choosing a form, identify what is being measured, which term is the target, and which term supplies the benchmark. Italian then marks greater degree, lower degree, or equality.
TL: Il treno è più rapido dell'autobus; questa strada è meno rumorosa di quella.
EN: The train is faster than the bus; this road is less noisy than that one.
[PAUSE:900]
Più and meno can modify an adjective, an adverb, a noun quantity, or the degree of a verb. The grammar after them depends on what the second term is doing.
SHOW: target + più/meno + scale + benchmark
Flip to distinguish comparison from superlative and intensity.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Relative superlative and high intensity answer different questions
REF: 16.1, 16.13, 16.15

A relative superlative identifies an extreme member inside a stated or understood set. An elative simply presents a quality as very intense.
TL: È la via più breve del quartiere; è una via strettissima.
EN: It is the shortest street in the neighborhood; it is an extremely narrow street.
[PAUSE:900]
The same più adjective string can be comparative or superlative; the article, noun phrase, and comparison set reveal the reading. The ending -issimo never by itself means the most within a set.
SHOW: extreme within a set -> relative superlative · very high degree -> elative

[CARD]
TITLE: Più and meno build the regular degree forms
REF: 16.1

Put più or meno directly before an adjective or adverb. In a noun phrase, the ordinary definite article belongs to the noun phrase, not to a separate superlative ending.
TL: Questo ufficio è meno luminoso; Marta ha scelto la soluzione più semplice.
EN: This office is less bright; Marta chose the simplest solution.
[PAUSE:900]
With an adverb, Italian commonly leaves the superlative reading to context and the comparison set.
TL: Leo risponde più rapidamente di tutti noi.
EN: Leo answers most rapidly of all of us.
SHOW: più/meno + adjective or adverb · article + noun phrase + più/meno + adjective

[SIDE]
TITLE: Migliore and peggiore are adjectives; meglio and peggio are adverbs
REF: 16.2

Use migliore or peggiore with a noun or as an adjective. Use meglio or peggio to modify a verb or to evaluate a whole situation.
TL: Abbiamo trovato un metodo migliore; oggi il motore funziona meglio.
EN: We found a better method; today the engine works better.
[PAUSE:900]
Maggiore and minore often concern age, rank, importance, or degree. Più grande and più piccolo normally keep the concrete size reading clearer.
TL: Giulia è la sorella maggiore, ma ha la stanza più piccola.
EN: Giulia is the older sister, but she has the smaller room.
SHOW: noun quality -> migliore/peggiore · manner or situation -> meglio/peggio · rank/degree -> maggiore/minore

[CARD]
TITLE: Di introduces a distinct benchmark or limit
REF: 16.7

Use di when the second term is a distinct comparison point rather than a parallel alternative under the same predicate. It combines with an article in the usual way.
TL: Nina è più alta di Paolo; il viaggio è più lungo del previsto.
EN: Nina is taller than Paolo; the journey is longer than expected.
[PAUSE:900]
Di is also required before a number or other limit, and before an oblique pronoun when the two subjects are compared.
TL: Erano più di trenta; lei è più paziente di me.
EN: There were more than thirty; she is more patient than I am.
SHOW: distinct term / pronoun / limit -> di · di + article -> del, della, dei, delle

[SIDE]
TITLE: Che aligns alternatives under a virtually repeated predicate
REF: 16.7, 16.8, 16.9, 16.10, 16.11

Che is favored when the same subject and predicate can be mentally repeated after the comparator: between two qualities, objects, prepositional phrases, or infinitives in one frame.
TL: Il compito è più lungo che difficile; viaggio più spesso in treno che in autobus.
EN: The assignment is more long than difficult; I travel more often by train than by bus.
[PAUSE:900]
Before a finite clause, a common frame is di quanto plus indicative or subjunctive. Che plus subjunctive often includes a non that does not negate the clause.
TL: Il test era più facile di quanto pensassi; è più utile che non crediate.
EN: The test was easier than I thought; it is more useful than you think.
SHOW: parallel alternatives -> che · finite benchmark -> di quanto ... / che non + subjunctive · comparative non != negation

[CARD]
TITLE: A relative superlative locates the extreme member of a set
REF: 16.1, 16.13, 16.14

The noun phrase supplies its normal definite article, while più or meno marks the extreme degree. Italian normally uses di where English uses in or of to name the set.
TL: È il ponte più antico della città; Marta è la meno esperta del gruppo.
EN: It is the oldest bridge in the city; Marta is the least experienced in the group.
[PAUSE:900]
Tra or fra can introduce a plural set, and dei/delle più or fra i/le più can give an indefinite noun an emphatic most reading.
TL: È tra i candidati più preparati; hanno scelto una proposta delle più ambiziose.
EN: He is among the best-prepared candidates; they chose a most ambitious proposal.
SHOW: article + noun + più/meno + adjective + di set · tra/fra + plural set

[SIDE]
TITLE: The ending -issimo expresses intensity, with normal agreement
REF: 16.15, 16.16

Remove the adjective's final vowel and add an agreeing form of -issimo. Preserve c or g stem spelling: ricco gives ricchissimo, while simpatico gives simpaticissimo.
TL: una risposta chiarissima; due corridoi lunghissimi
EN: an extremely clear answer; two extremely long corridors
[PAUSE:900]
Eligible simple adverbs also take the suffix, but adverbs in -mente normally avoid it. The lexical elatives ottimo, pessimo, minimo, and massimo carry their own quality or degree meanings.
TL: Parla benissimo; il rischio è minimo.
EN: She speaks extremely well; the risk is minimal.
SHOW: adjective stem + agreeing -issimo · simple adverb + -issimo · not a relative superlative

[CARD]
TITLE: Equality pairs two matching correlatives
REF: 16.18

Use tanto ... quanto, altrettanto ... quanto, or così ... come to compare equal degree. With adjectives and adverbs, the correlatives do not agree.
TL: Questo tavolo è tanto lungo quanto largo; Ada lavora così rapidamente come Leo.
EN: This table is as long as it is wide; Ada works as quickly as Leo.
[PAUSE:900]
After quanto or come, use the oblique pronoun forms me, te, lui, lei, noi, voi, or loro.
TL: Pietro è preciso quanto te.
EN: Pietro is as precise as you are.
SHOW: tanto ... quanto · altrettanto ... quanto · così ... come · after quanto/come -> oblique pronoun
Ten source equality transformations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Correlatives agree when they quantify nouns
REF: 16.18, 16.19

When tanto and quanto mean as many or as much before nouns, each agrees with its own noun. Così ... come does not serve this noun-quantifying job.
TL: Abbiamo tante domande quante risposte.
EN: We have as many questions as answers.
[PAUSE:900]
Altrettanto can mean an equal amount or number and can stand alone. Lo stesso di or che expresses identity rather than merely equal degree.
TL: Marta ha due ore libere e io ne ho altrettante; uso lo stesso programma di Luca.
EN: Marta has two free hours and I have the same number; I use the same program as Luca.
SHOW: noun quantity -> agreeing tanto/quanto · equal amount -> altrettanto · identity -> lo stesso di/che

[CARD]
TITLE: Special comparison meanings use whole constructions
REF: 16.4, 16.5, 16.6, 16.12

Put a measured extra or shortfall after the noun with in più or in meno. Use cambiare in meglio or in peggio for a change in quality.
TL: Ci servono tre sedie in più; la situazione è cambiata in meglio.
EN: We need three extra chairs; the situation has changed for the better.
[PAUSE:900]
English had better has no single mechanical equivalent: Italian can use convenire, the conditional of dovere, or bisogna che. Present or past conditional forms of preferire express would rather.
TL: Ti conviene partire subito; avrei preferito restare a casa.
EN: You had better leave at once; I would rather have stayed home.
SHOW: extra/short -> quantity + noun + in più/in meno · had better -> convenire/dovere/bisogna · would rather -> preferire

[SIDE]
TITLE: Paired scales express progression; repetition adds informal intensity
REF: 16.20, 16.21, 16.22

Italian uses più ... più or meno ... meno without definite articles for the more ... the more pattern. Sempre più or sempre meno presents a continuing trend.
TL: Più ascolti, più capisci; il traffico diventa sempre meno intenso.
EN: The more you listen, the more you understand; the traffic is becoming less and less heavy.
[PAUSE:900]
Repeating a short adjective or adverb can add playful, informal emphasis. It is a register choice, not the neutral default.
TL: Il corridoio era stretto stretto; rispose piano piano.
EN: The corridor was ever so narrow; she answered very softly.
SHOW: paired change -> più/meno ... più/meno · continuing trend -> sempre più/meno · informal intensity -> short form repeated

[CARD]
TITLE: Più and meno can quantify nouns or the degree of an action
REF: 16.3

Before a noun, invariable più means more and invariable meno means less or fewer. The noun keeps its own number and gender without making the quantifier agree.
TL: Abbiamo più tempo ma meno risorse; servono meno riunioni e più decisioni.
EN: We have more time but fewer resources; fewer meetings and more decisions are needed.
[PAUSE:900]
Un po' più and un po' meno soften the difference; before a following noun, di is optional in this frame.
TL: Aggiungi un po' più di acqua e un po' meno sale.
EN: Add a little more water and a little less salt.
SHOW: noun quantity -> invariable più/meno + noun · small adjustment -> un po' più/meno (di) + noun

[SIDE]
TITLE: Verb degree commonly uses di più or di meno
REF: 16.2, 16.3

When più or meno describes how much an action happens, Italian commonly uses di più or di meno, especially at the end of the clause. Degree modifiers can also strengthen a lexical comparative.
TL: Quest'anno leggiamo di più; il nuovo sistema è molto migliore.
EN: This year we read more; the new system is much better.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not confuse this adverbial di with the comparator that introduces a benchmark. One measures the action; the other links the action or quality to a second term.
TL: Ora dormo di più, ma sempre meno di mia sorella.
EN: Now I sleep more, but still less than my sister.
SHOW: degree of action -> di più/meno · benchmark -> di + comparison term

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose the construction from meaning before translating the words
REF: 16.2, 16.7, 16.15, 16.18, 16.20

First decide whether the sentence compares unequal degree, marks an extreme within a set, claims equality, or merely intensifies a quality. Then identify the grammatical function of each term.
TL: Questo piano è migliore; funziona meglio; è il migliore dei tre; è ottimo.
EN: This plan is better; it works better; it is the best of the three; it is excellent.
[PAUSE:900]
Only after those decisions choose di or che, adjective or adverb, relative superlative or elative, and neutral or informal register. This prevents a plausible form from expressing the wrong relationship.
SHOW: relationship -> scope -> grammatical function -> comparator -> register
Flip for a compact final check.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Keep the benchmark, scope, and register visible
REF: 16.7, 16.13, 16.15, 16.22

Ask four questions: Is there a real benchmark? Is the second term distinct or parallel? Does an article identify a set? Is the expression neutral, emphatic, or playful?
TL: È più serio di Marco; è più serio che severo; è il più serio del gruppo; è serissimo.
EN: He is more serious than Marco; he is more serious than strict; he is the most serious in the group; he is extremely serious.
[PAUSE:900]
The forms are close, but their scopes differ. Preserve that scope when producing a sentence and when interpreting a source answer.
SHOW: di -> benchmark · che -> aligned alternative · article + set -> relative superlative · -issimo -> elative intensity
