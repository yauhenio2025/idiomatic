## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Three signals before a noun
SVG: de_artikel_c01f.svg
REF: 4

An article is not decoration. It tells the listener how to locate a noun in the conversation. The definite article points to something recoverable. The indefinite article introduces one member of a class. No article presents a substance, quality, activity or indefinite plural without packaging it as one countable item. Listen to all three:
TL: Der Bericht liegt schon auf deinem Schreibtisch.
EN: The report is already on your desk.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Eine Kollegin hat ihn gestern geschickt.
EN: A colleague sent it yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Geduld braucht man trotzdem.
EN: You still need patience.
SHOW: definite = locate it · indefinite = introduce one · zero = leave it unbounded
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The ending carries coordinates
REF: 4, 4.1

Once you choose an article type, its ending gives the noun phrase grammatical coordinates: case, gender and number. The noun itself often reveals little; the small word in front does the visible work:
TL: Ich gebe dem neuen Kollegen eine kurze Zusammenfassung.
EN: I am giving the new colleague a brief summary.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first article marks masculine dative; the second marks feminine accusative. Across the definite paradigm, masculine changes visibly through all four cases, while the indefinite paradigm carries parallel endings where they are present:
TL: Der Plan überzeugt mich; ich prüfe den Plan; ich folge dem Plan; die Kosten des Plans steigen.
EN: The plan convinces me; I examine the plan; I follow the plan; the plan's costs are rising.
[PAUSE:1100]
TL: Ein Plan entsteht; sie prüft einen Plan; sie folgt einem Plan; die Folgen eines Plans zeigen sich später.
EN: A plan emerges; she examines a plan; she follows a plan; the consequences of a plan become apparent later.
[PAUSE:1100]
The indefinite article has no plural. A bare plural supplies the positive form, while the negative counterpart does have plural endings:
TL: Hier arbeiten erfahrene Übersetzer, aber keine Dolmetscher.
EN: Experienced translators work here, but no interpreters do.
SHOW: reference choice first · endings encode case · no indefinite plural
Next, compress those forms the way German normally does.

[CARD]
TITLE: Contractions are the neutral form
SVG: de_artikel_c02f.svg
REF: 4.1.1c

Several prepositions fuse with a following definite article. In a neutral, familiar phrase, the contraction is not merely shorter; it is the normal form:
TL: Wir besprechen die Einzelheiten beim Mittagessen.
EN: We will discuss the details over lunch.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Am Freitag wird der Vertrag unterzeichnet.
EN: The contract will be signed on Friday.
[PAUSE:900]
Set phrases and place expressions strongly favor the fused form. Treat the contraction as one smooth pointer to an already established setting.
SHOW: neutral familiar setting → fused preposition + article
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Full forms point harder
REF: 4.1.1c

Keep the preposition and article separate when the noun is being singled out or heavily specified. A following relative clause is a strong clue:
TL: Wir fuhren zu dem Dorf, das in der Akte erwähnt worden war.
EN: We drove to the village that had been mentioned in the file.
[PAUSE:1100]
Now compare the neutral destination:
TL: Wir fuhren zum Dorf, bevor es dunkel wurde.
EN: We drove to the village before it got dark.
[PAUSE:1000]
The full form says that particular one. The contraction says the context already supplies the place.
SHOW: singled out or contrastive → full form · neutral → contraction

[CARD]
TITLE: Definite means recoverable
REF: 4

Definiteness is a listener test. Can your listener identify the intended referent from the room, the previous sentence, shared knowledge or a modifier? If yes, use the definite article:
TL: Die Lampe im Flur flackert.
EN: The lamp in the hallway is flickering.
[PAUSE:900]
If you are introducing one previously unidentified member of a class, use the indefinite article:
TL: Im Flur fehlt noch eine Lampe.
EN: The hallway still needs a lamp.
[PAUSE:900]
And if you mean the unbounded phenomenon rather than a countable unit, leave the article out:
TL: Strom spart hier niemand.
EN: Nobody here conserves electricity.
SHOW: identifiable referent · new class member · unbounded concept
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Discourse changes the article
REF: 4

The noun does not come with a permanent article setting. Discourse can move it from new to known in one sentence:
TL: Auf dem Bahnsteig stand eine Frau mit einem roten Koffer.
EN: A woman with a red suitcase was standing on the platform.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Frau telefonierte, während der Koffer unbeaufsichtigt blieb.
EN: The woman was on the phone while the suitcase remained unattended.
[PAUSE:1100]
First mention opens a file; the next mention retrieves it. That new-to-given movement is the core system beneath many article rules.
SHOW: first mention opens a file · later mention retrieves it
Now apply the same logic to abstract ideas and substances.

[CARD]
TITLE: Whole concept or some instance?
SVG: de_artikel_c04f.svg
REF: 4.2.1

Abstract nouns often take a definite article where English has none. German is treating the idea as a familiar whole or a bounded concept:
TL: Die Geduld des Ausschusses ist erschöpft.
EN: The committee's patience has run out.
[PAUSE:1000]
But when the quality is vague, partial or newly required, the noun stays bare:
TL: Für diese Verhandlung braucht man Geduld.
EN: This negotiation requires patience.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not memorize that an abstract noun takes an article. Ask whether this context presents the whole concept or merely an unspecified amount of it.
SHOW: familiar whole → definite · vague or partial instance → zero
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Substances, meals and illnesses follow the scale
REF: 4.2.2

The same bounded-versus-partial contrast reaches concrete domains. A particular supply can be definite; an unspecified amount is bare:
TL: Der Kaffee aus dieser Rösterei ist erstaunlich mild.
EN: The coffee from this roastery is surprisingly mild.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Für die Nachtschicht brauchen wir Kaffee.
EN: We need coffee for the night shift.
[PAUSE:900]
Symptoms after the verb meaning have are commonly bare, while a specified bout can be packaged as one event:
TL: Er hat seit gestern Fieber.
EN: He has had a fever since yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Nach der Reise hatte sie eine hartnäckige Erkältung.
EN: After the trip she had a persistent cold.
SHOW: known supply = definite · unspecified amount = zero · bounded bout = indefinite
Next, lift the choice from individuals to whole classes.

[CARD]
TITLE: Generalize three ways
SVG: de_artikel_c05f.svg
REF: 4.3

German can generalize with a definite singular, an indefinite singular or a bare plural. The first treats the species as one conceptual type and is especially at home in writing:
TL: Der Oktopus löst erstaunlich komplexe Aufgaben.
EN: The octopus solves surprisingly complex tasks.
[PAUSE:1000]
The bare plural makes the class sound like a population:
TL: Oktopusse reagieren empfindlich auf veränderte Wasserwerte.
EN: Octopuses react sensitively to changes in water quality.
[PAUSE:1000]
The indefinite singular presents any representative member:
TL: Ein Oktopus braucht eine abwechslungsreiche Umgebung.
EN: An octopus needs a varied environment.
SHOW: type as one whole · population · any representative
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Names and domains have grammar too
REF: 4.2.2d, 4.2.2f, 4.4

Most city and neuter country names are bare. Feminine and plural country names take the definite article. Any proper name modified by an adjective takes one too:
TL: Die Delegation reist von Österreich aus weiter in die Slowakei.
EN: The delegation is travelling on from Austria to Slovakia.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Das heutige Wien verbindet kaiserliche Fassaden mit radikaler Gegenwartskunst.
EN: Present-day Vienna combines imperial façades with radical contemporary art.
[PAUSE:1000]
Domains also split by viewpoint. A language as a system is inflected with the definite article; a language being learned or used is normally bare:
TL: Sie übersetzt aus dem Finnischen ins Deutsche.
EN: She translates from Finnish into German.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Bericht wurde auf Finnisch verfasst.
EN: The report was written in Finnish.
[PAUSE:900]
And academic fields take the definite article as general domains but stay bare as subjects of study:
TL: Ihre Forschung verbindet die Linguistik mit der Informatik.
EN: Her research connects linguistics with computer science.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ihr Sohn studiert Informatik.
EN: Her son studies computer science.
SHOW: bare name by default · modified or gender-marked name takes the article

[CARD]
TITLE: Time has its own article map
SVG: de_artikel_c06f.svg
REF: 4.5

Months and seasons take the definite article when they are treated as time periods, especially after the prepositions meaning on, until and in:
TL: Im Februar beginnt die neue Spielzeit.
EN: The new season begins in February.
[PAUSE:900]
But after beginning, middle or end, the month is bare:
TL: Ende Februar veröffentlicht das Institut seinen Bericht.
EN: The institute will publish its report at the end of February.
[PAUSE:900]
Modifiers meaning next, last, previous or past also remove the article:
TL: Nächsten Herbst ziehen wir nach Graz.
EN: We are moving to Graz next autumn.
SHOW: in a period → definite · boundary word or next/last → zero
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Calendar names and rates
REF: 4.5, 4.7

The major festival names are normally bare:
TL: Zu Ostern besuchen uns meine Schwiegereltern.
EN: My parents-in-law are visiting us at Easter.
[PAUSE:900]
German also uses the definite article distributively where English says per or uses an indefinite article:
TL: Der Seminarraum kostet sechzig Euro die Stunde.
EN: The seminar room costs sixty euros an hour.
[PAUSE:900]
And the superlative meaning most always carries the definite article:
TL: Die meisten Teilnehmer reisten noch am selben Abend ab.
EN: Most participants left that same evening.
SHOW: festivals = zero · rates = definite · most = always definite
Next comes the sharpest German-English possession contrast.

[CARD]
TITLE: Put the owner in the dative
SVG: de_artikel_c07f.svg
REF: 4.6.1

With body parts and clothing, German usually avoids a possessive determiner. The definite article marks the affected item, and a dative pronoun marks its owner:
TL: Nach der Wanderung tun mir die Füße weh.
EN: My feet hurt after the hike.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Ich ziehe mir die nassen Socken aus.
EN: I am taking off my wet socks.
[PAUSE:1100]
This distributes the information efficiently: the dative answers whose sphere is affected; the definite article points to the obvious body part or garment within that sphere.
SHOW: dative = owner · definite noun phrase = body part or clothing
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use a possessive when ownership is the point
REF: 4.6.1

Keep the possessive when the owned item leads the sentence, when the verb cannot accommodate an owner dative, or when you need contrast or disambiguation:
TL: Meine linke Hand ist noch immer geschwollen.
EN: My left hand is still swollen.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Auf ihrem Ärmel saß ein winziger Käfer.
EN: A tiny beetle was sitting on her sleeve.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Zieh bitte deinen Mantel an, nicht meinen.
EN: Please put on your coat, not mine.
[PAUSE:1000]
The definite-article pattern is the neutral choice only when the owner is structurally clear.
SHOW: clear affected owner → dative + article · contrast or ambiguity → possessive

[CARD]
TITLE: Identity can be article-free
SVG: de_artikel_c08f.svg
REF: 4.8.2

After the verbs meaning be, become and remain, professions, nationalities, origins and broad social classes are normally bare. The noun classifies the subject rather than identifying one individual:
TL: Meine Schwester ist Architektin.
EN: My sister is an architect.
[PAUSE:1000]
Add a description that individualizes or evaluates the person, and the indefinite article returns:
TL: Sie ist eine außergewöhnlich geduldige Architektin.
EN: She is an exceptionally patient architect.
[PAUSE:1000]
So this is not simply profession means no article. Classification is bare; an individualized portrait is packaged as one member of the class.
SHOW: class membership → zero · individualized description → indefinite
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Roles, some and any also go bare
REF: 4.8.3, 4.8.4a

A role introduced by the word meaning as is normally bare:
TL: Als Ärztin widersprach sie dem Vorschlag.
EN: Speaking as a doctor, she objected to the proposal.
[PAUSE:900]
And the ordinary German equivalent of some or any is often no article at all, with both mass nouns and indefinite plurals:
TL: Haben wir noch Batterien?
EN: Do we have any batteries left?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Für die Suppe brauchen wir Butter.
EN: We need some butter for the soup.
[PAUSE:1000]
English inserts a small determiner; German can let number and context do the work.
SHOW: role after as · indefinite plural · unspecified mass → zero

[CARD]
TITLE: Formal German compresses noun phrases
REF: 4.8.1, 4.8.2b, 4.8.4

Paired nouns and enumerations often drop articles, especially in fixed or balanced expressions:
TL: Tag und Nacht liefen die Pumpen.
EN: The pumps ran day and night.
[PAUSE:900]
Formal prose also omits the article in compact predicate formulas:
TL: Ursache des Ausfalls war ein beschädigtes Kabel.
EN: The cause of the outage was a damaged cable.
[PAUSE:900]
Official language can even use a role noun without an article:
TL: Antragsteller muss einen Identitätsnachweis vorlegen.
EN: The applicant must present proof of identity.
[PAUSE:1000]
These are register signals. In ordinary prose, expand them unless the compact official tone is intended.
SHOW: pairs · formal predicate formulas · official role labels → zero
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Structure can license the omission
REF: 4.8.4a, 4.8.4b

Formal apposition may omit the article because the preceding name already anchors the referent:
TL: Wir erreichten Weimar, Zentrum der deutschen Klassik.
EN: We reached Weimar, a centre of German classicism.
[PAUSE:1000]
A bare role phrase works for the same reason: it classifies an already identified person.
TL: Er arbeitet als freier Übersetzer.
EN: He works as a freelance translator.
[PAUSE:1000]
The omission is not random deletion. Another structure has already supplied reference, while the bare noun contributes a label.
SHOW: anchored referent + classifying label → article may disappear
The broad translation drill comes after one final family of lexical patterns.

[CARD]
TITLE: Some constructions are lexical islands
REF: 4.9

Phrasal verbs often fix the presence or absence of an article. Meaning alone will not predict every phrase, but several tendencies help. Nominalized infinitives after a preposition take the definite article; many abstract nouns in verb phrases stay bare:
TL: Beim Überarbeiten bemerkte sie drei widersprüchliche Zahlen.
EN: While revising, she noticed three contradictory figures.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Nach zwei Stunden kam die Debatte in Gang.
EN: After two hours the debate got under way.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Nachricht setzte die Behörde unter Druck.
EN: The news put the authority under pressure.
SHOW: nominalized action → article · many fixed abstract phrases → zero
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Prepositions create local rules
REF: 4.10

Several prepositions conventionally take a bare noun, particularly in formal writing or where English would use an indefinite article:
TL: Er öffnete die Kiste ohne Werkzeug.
EN: He opened the crate without a tool.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Behörde handelte gemäß geltendem Recht.
EN: The authority acted in accordance with applicable law.
[PAUSE:900]
An adjective can also extend a formal prepositional phrase without forcing an indefinite article:
TL: Der Vertrag wurde in klarer Sprache abgefasst.
EN: The contract was drafted in clear language.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn frequent phrase frames as units, then notice what modification does inside them.
SHOW: preposition + noun is often a stored frame · check register and modifiers

[CARD]
TITLE: Decide in layers
SVG: de_artikel_c11f.svg
REF: 4.2, 4.8, 4.9, 4.10

For a difficult noun phrase, decide in layers. First ask whether a fixed construction controls the article. If not, ask whether the listener can recover one particular referent. If not, ask whether you are introducing one singular countable member. What remains is often an unbounded mass, quality, activity or indefinite plural:
TL: An der Rezeption wartet ein Kurier.
EN: A courier is waiting at reception.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Kurier braucht noch eine Unterschrift.
EN: The courier still needs a signature.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ohne Unterschrift darf er die Sendung nicht abgeben.
EN: He may not hand over the parcel without a signature.
SHOW: fixed frame? · recoverable? · one countable member? · otherwise test zero
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Edit for the meaning, not the noun
REF: 4.5, 4.8.2

On a final reread, challenge every article. Ask what changes if it disappears or changes type. Classification can turn into characterization:
TL: Sie ist Professorin.
EN: She is a professor.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Sie ist eine Professorin, die keinen Widerspruch duldet.
EN: She is a professor who tolerates no disagreement.
[PAUSE:1000]
Time can move from a recurring season to one specifically selected season:
TL: Im Sommer arbeiten wir in Portugal.
EN: In summer we work in Portugal.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Diesen Sommer arbeiten wir in Portugal.
EN: This summer we are working in Portugal.
[PAUSE:1000]
The expert habit is not choosing an article mechanically. It is hearing the reference structure the article creates.
SHOW: every article commits you to a reference structure
