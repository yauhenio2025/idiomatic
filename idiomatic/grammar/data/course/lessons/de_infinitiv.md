## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Three forms without a finite anchor
SVG: de_infinitiv_c01f.svg
REF: 11, 11.1.1

German has three non-finite verb forms: the infinitive, the present participle and the past participle. None of them carries agreement, tense or mood by itself. What changes is the job each form can do. Compare the same act of examining:
TL: den Vorschlag prüfen
EN: to examine the proposal
[PAUSE:800]
TL: die den Vorschlag prüfende Kommission
EN: the committee examining the proposal
[PAUSE:800]
TL: der von der Kommission geprüfte Vorschlag
EN: the proposal examined by the committee
[PAUSE:1200]
The infinitive builds clauses and compound verbs. The present participle packages an ongoing, active relation. The past participle packages a completed or passive relation. These are architectural parts, waiting for a finite verb or a noun phrase to anchor them.
SHOW: infinitive = clause · Partizip I = active · Partizip II = completed/passive

[SIDE]
TITLE: Where zu docks
REF: 11.1.2

The particle has a precise docking point. With a simple or inseparable verb, it stands before the infinitive as a separate word:
TL: Die Redaktion hofft, den Bericht heute zu prüfen.
EN: The editorial team hopes to examine the report today.
[PAUSE:1000]
With a separable verb, it goes between prefix and stem, and the result is one word:
TL: Die Redaktion hofft, den Bericht heute abzuschließen.
EN: The editorial team hopes to finalize the report today.
[PAUSE:1000]
With a compound infinitive, it stands before the final auxiliary:
TL: Die Redaktion behauptet, den Bericht bereits geprüft zu haben.
EN: The editorial team claims to have examined the report already.
[PAUSE:1200]
So do not attach the particle by sound alone. First identify the infinitive's internal structure; then place it at the seam.
SHOW: zu prüfen · abzuschließen · geprüft zu haben
Next, put that seam inside a whole clause.

[CARD]
TITLE: The infinitive clause owns the right edge
SVG: de_infinitiv_c02f.svg
REF: 11.2, 11.2.1

An infinitive with its objects and adverbials forms a reduced clause. Inside that clause, the infinitive sits at the right edge, exactly where a finite verb would sit in a subordinate clause:
TL: Der Ausschuss hat beschlossen, die Anhörung in den September zu verschieben.
EN: The committee has decided to postpone the hearing until September.
[PAUSE:1200]
The whole reduced clause normally follows the clause it depends on. It does not usually squeeze inside the finite verbal frame:
TL: Der Ausschuss bleibt länger, um die offenen Fragen gründlich zu erörtern.
EN: The committee is staying longer in order to discuss the unresolved questions thoroughly.
[PAUSE:1200]
Mentally draw a boundary before the reduced clause. Everything governed by its infinitive goes inside; the infinitive closes it.
SHOW: matrix clause | objects + adverbials + zu-infinitive
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The silent subject has a controller
REF: 11.2.3

An infinitive has no agreement ending, but its clause still has a subject. That subject is supplied by a controller in the matrix clause. With a promise, the matrix subject normally controls the infinitive:
TL: Mara versprach Jonas, ihn am Bahnhof abzuholen.
EN: Mara promised Jonas that she would pick him up at the station.
[PAUSE:1200]
With a request, the matrix object normally controls it:
TL: Mara bat Jonas, sie am Bahnhof abzuholen.
EN: Mara asked Jonas to pick her up at the station.
[PAUSE:1200]
Some directives leave the controller generic — somebody is to carry out the order:
TL: Die Leitung ordnete an, sämtliche Zugänge zu sperren.
EN: Management ordered all entrances to be closed.
[PAUSE:1200]
The endings do not reveal the controller. The governing verb and the context do.
SHOW: promise: subject controls · request: object controls · order: generic agent
Two construction sets now train clause shape and compound infinitives.

[CARD]
TITLE: One clause, three syntactic jobs
SVG: de_infinitiv_c03f.svg
REF: 11.2.2a, 11.2.2c, 11.2.2d

An infinitive clause can fill the subject slot:
TL: Den Vertrag jetzt zu kündigen wäre riskant.
EN: Cancelling the contract now would be risky.
[PAUSE:1000]
The finite verb is third-person singular because the entire clause counts as one subject. The same content can move right, with anticipatory es holding its place:
TL: Es wäre riskant, den Vertrag jetzt zu kündigen.
EN: It would be risky to cancel the contract now.
[PAUSE:1000]
Or the infinitive clause can be the object selected by a verb:
TL: Wir hoffen, die Verhandlungen morgen abzuschließen.
EN: We hope to conclude the negotiations tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1200]
The reduced clause is not an afterthought. It occupies a real argument slot.
SHOW: subject clause · es + postponed subject · object clause

[SIDE]
TITLE: Es or darauf: the valency survives
REF: 11.2.2e, 11.2.2f, 16.5.14

Some verbs can announce a following object clause with es:
TL: Sie bedauert es, den Termin versäumt zu haben.
EN: She regrets having missed the appointment.
[PAUSE:1000]
If the governing verb selects a preposition, reduction does not erase that preposition. A da-compound carries it forward:
TL: Sie besteht darauf, den Termin persönlich zu bestätigen.
EN: She insists on confirming the appointment in person.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wir rechnen damit, die Genehmigung im Mai zu erhalten.
EN: We expect to receive the permit in May.
[PAUSE:1200]
The choice is lexical, not stylistic. Learn the matrix verb together with its argument pattern; the infinitive clause inherits the slot.
SHOW: object slot → es · prepositional slot → da(r) + preposition

[CARD]
TITLE: Semi-auxiliaries reshape modality
REF: 11.2.4

Some full verbs bind so tightly to a following infinitive that they behave like semi-auxiliaries. They add necessity, possibility, appearance, habit or impending danger while the infinitive carries the event:
TL: Die Akten sind bis Freitag einzureichen.
EN: The files must be submitted by Friday.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Schaden scheint geringer zu sein als befürchtet.
EN: The damage appears to be less severe than feared.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Das Problem droht außer Kontrolle zu geraten.
EN: The problem threatens to get out of control.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Sie braucht den Antrag nur online einzureichen.
EN: She only needs to submit the application online.
[PAUSE:1200]
Notice the passive force in the first pattern: the files are not doing the submitting. The finite semi-auxiliary supplies the modal lens; the infinitive supplies the process.
SHOW: sein + zu = necessity/possibility · scheinen · drohen · brauchen
Flip for places where English tempts you into the wrong structure.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Modal force can hide passive voice
REF: 11.2.4

The tightest semi-auxiliary patterns assign modal force to a passive relation. With sein, context decides between possibility and obligation:
TL: Der Einwand ist ernst zu nehmen.
EN: The objection must be taken seriously.
[PAUSE:1000]
Colloquial gehen can express possibility, usually under negation:
TL: Die Maschine geht nicht mehr zu reparieren.
EN: The machine can no longer be repaired.
[PAUSE:1000]
Bleiben presents a task as still unresolved:
TL: Die Finanzierung bleibt noch zu klären.
EN: The financing still remains to be clarified.
[PAUSE:1000]
And es gibt presents something available or pending:
TL: Es gibt noch mehrere Einwände zu prüfen.
EN: There are still several objections to examine.
[PAUSE:1200]
Read these as one predicate. The surface subject is often the patient of the infinitive, not its agent.
SHOW: sein / gehen / bleiben + zu-infinitive → modal meaning, often passive

[CARD]
TITLE: English does not license the German frame
REF: 11.2.3b, 11.2.7

English allows an overt noun before many infinitives. German often requires a finite subordinate clause instead:
TL: Ich möchte, dass du den Entwurf noch einmal prüfst.
EN: I want you to check the draft once more.
[PAUSE:1000]
After a noun, English may use an infinitive where German uses a relative clause:
TL: Hier sind die Unterlagen, die Sie noch unterschreiben müssen.
EN: Here are the documents for you to sign.
[PAUSE:1000]
And an English with-phrase can encode a reason rather than a German infinitive:
TL: Da sie noch zwei Gutachten verfassen musste, blieb sie länger im Institut.
EN: With two reports still to write, she stayed longer at the institute.
[PAUSE:1200]
The English marker to does not license a matching German construction. First identify subject and relation; then choose the clause type.
SHOW: overt new subject → finite clause · noun attachment → relative clause
Flip for two more traps.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Questions and adjectives have narrower frames
REF: 11.2.5, 11.2.7c, 11.2.7d

An indirect question stays finite in German, usually with a modal:
TL: Wir wissen nicht, welchen Schalter wir betätigen sollen.
EN: We do not know which switch to operate.
[PAUSE:1000]
Only a small group of adjectives allows the subject of sein to be understood as the object of the infinitive:
TL: Der Datensatz ist leicht zu durchsuchen.
EN: The dataset is easy to search.
[PAUSE:1000]
The construction requires an accusative object. A dative verb needs an impersonal frame instead:
TL: Es war schwierig, dem Zeugen zu helfen.
EN: It was difficult to help the witness.
[PAUSE:1000]
English also attaches infinitives to superlatives and similar nouns where German normally uses a relative clause:
TL: Sie war die Einzige, die den Rechenfehler bemerkte.
EN: She was the only person to notice the calculation error.
[PAUSE:1200]
Translate the dependency, not the English surface form.
SHOW: indirect question → modal clause · easy + accusative object only · noun → relative clause

[CARD]
TITLE: Purpose requires shared control
SVG: de_infinitiv_c06f.svg
REF: 11.2.6a

The purpose construction with um works only when one participant controls both actions:
TL: Nora schloss das Fenster, um den Straßenlärm auszublenden.
EN: Nora closed the window in order to block out the street noise.
[PAUSE:1200]
Nora closes and Nora blocks out. If the purpose clause needs a new subject, switch to a finite clause with damit:
TL: Nora schloss das Fenster, damit das Kind schlafen konnte.
EN: Nora closed the window so that the child could sleep.
[PAUSE:1200]
The same control rule applies after too and enough:
TL: Der Text ist klar genug, um ohne Kommentar verstanden zu werden.
EN: The text is clear enough to be understood without commentary.
[PAUSE:1200]
Here the passive infinitive keeps the text as its understood subject.
SHOW: same subject → um … zu · new subject → damit + finite verb
Flip for three more prepositions that open reduced clauses.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ohne, statt, außer: same subject, new relation
REF: 11.2.6b, 11.2.6c, 11.2.6d

These prepositions state the relation to the matrix event while the silent subject remains controlled by the same participant:
TL: Er verließ die Sitzung, ohne sich zu verabschieden.
EN: He left the meeting without saying goodbye.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sie überprüfte die Quelle, statt das Gerücht weiterzugeben.
EN: She checked the source instead of passing on the rumour.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Uns blieb nichts übrig, außer auf die Entscheidung zu warten.
EN: We had no option except to wait for the decision.
[PAUSE:1200]
With a different subject, use a finite clause:
TL: Er verließ die Sitzung, ohne dass die Vorsitzende es bemerkte.
EN: He left the meeting without the chair noticing.
[PAUSE:1200]
The comma is mandatory before all four prepositional infinitive clauses.
SHOW: um · ohne · (an)statt · außer + zu-clause — comma required
Five surviving purpose transformations reinforce the control test.

[CARD]
TITLE: The bare infinitive is licensed, not optional
SVG: de_infinitiv_c07f.svg
REF: 11.3.1a, 11.3.1b, 11.3.1c, 11.3.1e, 11.3.1f

A small set of governing verbs selects an infinitive without zu. Modal auxiliaries form the largest group:
TL: Wir müssen den Kurs ändern.
EN: We must change course.
[PAUSE:800]
Perception verbs let you witness a complete event:
TL: Ich hörte die Tür zufallen.
EN: I heard the door slam shut.
[PAUSE:800]
Lassen introduces permission or causation:
TL: Sie ließ den Motor überprüfen.
EN: She had the engine inspected.
[PAUSE:800]
Motion verbs can add a purpose activity, and position verbs can hold a state:
TL: Wir fahren morgen Möbel kaufen.
EN: We are going to buy furniture tomorrow.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Er blieb im Flur stehen.
EN: He remained standing in the corridor.
[PAUSE:1200]
These are licensed families, not a free option to drop the particle.
SHOW: modal · perception · lassen · motion · position → bare infinitive
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Bare still means clause
REF: 11.3.1b, 11.3.1c

Removing the particle does not remove clause structure. After a perception verb, the matrix object controls the witnessed event:
TL: Ich sah die Technikerin den Sensor austauschen.
EN: I saw the technician replace the sensor.
[PAUSE:1200]
After causative lassen, the object may instead be understood as what receives the action:
TL: Die Technikerin ließ den Sensor austauschen.
EN: The technician had the sensor replaced.
[PAUSE:1200]
A passive witnessed event cannot use that bare-infinitive frame. German expands it into a finite wie-clause:
TL: Wir sahen, wie der Sensor ausgetauscht wurde.
EN: We saw the sensor being replaced.
[PAUSE:1200]
The surface forms are short; their controller and voice relations are not.
SHOW: perception: object acts · causative: object can be affected · passive: wie-clause

[CARD]
TITLE: The governing head chooses zu or zero
REF: 11.1.2, 11.3.1g

When you hesitate over zu, look left for the governing head. A lexical verb such as hope selects a zu-clause:
TL: Sie hofft, den Fehler rechtzeitig zu finden.
EN: She hopes to find the error in time.
[PAUSE:900]
A modal selects a bare infinitive:
TL: Sie kann den Fehler rechtzeitig finden.
EN: She can find the error in time.
[PAUSE:900]
A few verbs, including help, teach and learn, allow both patterns; longer clauses tend to favour zu:
TL: Die Mentorin half uns, die widersprüchlichen Angaben sorgfältig zu prüfen.
EN: The mentor helped us examine the contradictory information carefully.
[PAUSE:1200]
This is lexical government. Memorize the selector with the governing verb, not as a property of the infinitive itself.
SHOW: lexical selector → zu · modal/perception/lassen → zero · a small variable class
Flip for the perfect-tense consequence.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ersatzinfinitiv rewires the perfect
SVG: de_infinitiv_c08b.svg
REF: 11.3.2

When a modal governs another infinitive in the perfect, it normally appears as an infinitive too. This replacement infinitive creates a final double infinitive:
TL: Sie hat die Frist verlängern müssen.
EN: She had to extend the deadline.
[PAUSE:1200]
Lassen behaves the same way:
TL: Er hat den Vertrag von einer Fachanwältin prüfen lassen.
EN: He had the contract reviewed by a specialist lawyer.
[PAUSE:1200]
Several infinitives can stack:
TL: Wir hätten den Termin absagen lassen können.
EN: We could have had the appointment cancelled.
[PAUSE:1200]
In a subordinate clause, the finite auxiliary moves before that infinitive cluster:
TL: Sie erklärte, dass sie die Frist habe verlängern müssen.
EN: She explained that she had had to extend the deadline.
[PAUSE:1200]
Think cluster, not isolated participle: the construction determines the perfect form and the order.
SHOW: hat + lexical infinitive + modal infinitive · subordinate: auxiliary before cluster

[CARD]
TITLE: Turn an event into a neuter noun
REF: 11.4.1

Almost any infinitive can become a noun. Capitalize it, give it neuter gender, and let articles and adjectives show its case:
TL: Das sorgfältige Prüfen der Belege verhindert Fehler.
EN: Carefully checking the documents prevents errors.
[PAUSE:1200]
Its verbal arguments become nominal dependents, often genitives or prepositional phrases:
TL: Das wiederholte Verschieben des Termins verärgerte alle Beteiligten.
EN: Repeatedly postponing the appointment annoyed everyone involved.
[PAUSE:1200]
Reflexive material normally disappears when the noun is established, but it can remain inside a novel compound when the meaning needs it:
TL: Sein ständiges Sichrechtfertigen machte jedes Gespräch mühsam.
EN: His constant self-justification made every conversation exhausting.
[PAUSE:1200]
Nominalization trades verbal clause structure for noun-phrase structure.
SHOW: infinitive → capitalized neuter noun · arguments → genitive/prepositional dependents
Flip for the prepositions that make these nouns productive.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Beim, zum, ins, am encode viewpoint
REF: 11.4.2

Fused preposition and article tell you how to view the nominalized event. Beim locates another event during it:
TL: Beim Überqueren der Straße bemerkte sie den Radfahrer.
EN: While crossing the street, she noticed the cyclist.
[PAUSE:1000]
Zum presents a purpose or intended use:
TL: Der Beamer ist zum Vorführen kurzer Videos gedacht.
EN: The projector is intended for showing short videos.
[PAUSE:1000]
Ins with a motion verb presents an onset:
TL: Nach der ersten Wortmeldung kam die Debatte ins Rollen.
EN: After the first contribution, the debate got under way.
[PAUSE:1000]
Am presents an activity as ongoing, especially in speech:
TL: Die Nachfrage ist seit Wochen am Steigen.
EN: Demand has been rising for weeks.
[PAUSE:1200]
The last pattern is widespread in conversation but still less neutral in formal writing.
SHOW: beim = during · zum = purpose · ins = onset · am = ongoing

[CARD]
TITLE: Participles compress relative clauses
SVG: de_infinitiv_c10f.svg
REF: 11.5.1a, 11.5.1e, 11.5.1f

As an adjective, a present participle normally presents its noun as active in an unfolding relation:
TL: die schweigenden Abgeordneten
EN: the members of parliament who are remaining silent
[PAUSE:900]
A past participle normally presents the noun as completed or affected:
TL: der gestern veröffentlichte Bericht
EN: the report published yesterday
[PAUSE:900]
A present participle with zu adds passive necessity or possibility:
TL: eine bis Ende Juni zu erfüllende Auflage
EN: a condition that must be fulfilled by the end of June
[PAUSE:1200]
Objects and adverbials expand leftward before the participle; then the participle takes the adjective ending immediately before its noun.
SHOW: Partizip I = active relation · Partizip II = completed/passive · zu + Partizip I = to be done
Flip for the register choice.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Extended phrases belong mainly to writing
REF: 11.5.1f, 11.5.2

German can compress a full relative clause into the space between article and noun:
TL: Die seit Monaten auf eine Entscheidung wartenden Anwohner reichten Klage ein.
EN: The residents who had spent months waiting for a decision filed a lawsuit.
[PAUSE:1200]
With a past participle, the same architecture packages a passive relation:
TL: Der von mehreren Fachleuten scharf kritisierte Entwurf wurde zurückgezogen.
EN: The draft that had been sharply criticized by several experts was withdrawn.
[PAUSE:1200]
The internal order resembles a subordinate clause: complements first, participle last. But register matters. In speech, unpack the phrase:
TL: Die Anwohner, die seit Monaten auf eine Entscheidung warteten, reichten Klage ein.
EN: The residents who had spent months waiting for a decision filed a lawsuit.
[PAUSE:1200]
Compression is a written-register resource, not a general mark of sophistication.
SHOW: article | complements + participle-ending | noun · speech prefers a relative clause
Twelve rewrites now practise both participles inside the noun phrase.

[CARD]
TITLE: English -ing is a question, not an answer
SVG: de_infinitiv_c11f.svg
REF: 11.6.1, 11.6.2

English uses one -ing shape for many relations. German makes you classify the job before choosing a form. If the event is functioning as a noun, nominalize:
TL: Aufmerksames Zuhören verhindert Missverständnisse.
EN: Listening attentively prevents misunderstandings.
[PAUSE:1000]
If a matrix expression selects an event and the subject is shared, use a zu-clause:
TL: Es lohnt sich, die Zahlen noch einmal zu prüfen.
EN: Checking the figures once more is worthwhile.
[PAUSE:1000]
If the embedded event has its own subject, use a finite clause:
TL: Ich kann mir nicht vorstellen, dass Leo den Posten ablehnt.
EN: I cannot imagine Leo turning down the position.
[PAUSE:1000]
Prepositions express still other relations:
TL: Sie löste das Problem, indem sie den Cache leerte.
EN: She solved the problem by clearing the cache.
[PAUSE:1200]
The English suffix does not survive translation; its syntactic and semantic job does.
SHOW: noun → infinitival noun · shared subject → zu · new subject → finite clause
Flip for simultaneous action and verb-specific equivalents.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Translate the relation, not the suffix
REF: 11.6.3, 11.6.4, 11.6.5

For consecutive actions, ordinary coordinated verbs are often the cleanest German:
TL: Sie öffnete das Fenster und atmete tief durch.
EN: Opening the window, she took a deep breath.
[PAUSE:1000]
For a reason, use a causal clause:
TL: Da er den Ausgang nicht kannte, folgte er der Menge.
EN: Not knowing the way out, he followed the crowd.
[PAUSE:1000]
After perception, a bare infinitive is idiomatic:
TL: Ich hörte die Nachbarn im Treppenhaus diskutieren.
EN: I heard the neighbours arguing in the stairwell.
[PAUSE:1000]
And English keep plus -ing may reduce to a simple verb with weiter:
TL: Er hustete während des ganzen Vortrags weiter.
EN: He kept coughing throughout the talk.
[PAUSE:1200]
German has participial clauses, but they are formal and far rarer. Your default should be the finite relation the sentence actually means.
SHOW: sequence → und · reason → da/weil · perception → bare infinitive · continuation → weiter
