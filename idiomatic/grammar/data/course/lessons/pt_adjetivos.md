## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: An adjective normally follows the noun and agrees with it
REF: 5, 5.1

The default place for an attributive adjective in Portuguese is after the noun. The adjective agrees with the noun in gender and number, even when English puts its adjective first.
TL: A biblioteca comprou revistas científicas brasileiras.
EN: The library bought Brazilian scientific journals.
[PAUSE:900]
Classifying words and names used adjectivally follow the same order.
TL: Eles anunciaram uma promoção relâmpago para o modelo elétrico.
EN: They announced a flash sale for the electric model.
[PAUSE:950]
Choose the noun first, propagate its features, and then place the adjective unless a recognized pre-nominal pattern applies.
SHOW: noun identity -> gender+number -> agreeing adjective · default order: noun + adjective
Flip for the adjective classes that normally come first.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ordinals, quantities, and a small lexical class precede the noun
REF: 5.2

Ordinal numbers, último, indefinite adjectives, and quantity words normally precede the noun. The same is true of chamado, futuro in its ordinary sense, and mero.
TL: Vários alunos leram os primeiros capítulos.
EN: Several students read the first chapters.
[PAUSE:900]
Agreement still operates in front of the noun.
TL: Minha futura colega apresentou outras duas propostas.
EN: My future colleague presented two other proposals.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not treat pre-nominal position as a license to leave a variable adjective uninflected.
SHOW: ordinal/último · quantity/indefinite · chamado/futuro/mero -> before noun · agreement remains

[CARD]
TITLE: Pre-nominal position can frame a quality as inherent or evaluative
REF: 5.1

A descriptive adjective may precede the noun when it presents an inherent quality or a writer's evaluation instead of distinguishing one referent from another. This pattern is especially common in literary and journalistic style.
TL: A equipe atravessou o vasto deserto em três dias.
EN: The team crossed the vast desert in three days.
[PAUSE:900]
An opinion adjective can also come first when a following adjective or complement supplies the classification.
TL: Provamos um excelente café brasileiro.
EN: We tried an excellent Brazilian coffee.
[PAUSE:950]
This is a choice of information structure and style, not a reversal to English word order.
SHOW: inherent/evaluative framing -> adjective + noun · identifying class -> noun + adjective
Flip for a firm post-nominal condition.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A qualified adjective stays after the noun
REF: 5.3

When an adjective has its own degree word or complement, it follows the noun. The extra material forms one adjective phrase that should remain intact.
TL: Eles alugaram uma sala muito pequena.
EN: They rented a very small room.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Conhecemos uma pesquisadora boa de conversa.
EN: We met a researcher who was a good conversationalist.
[PAUSE:950]
Post-nominal position can also sharpen a contrast: um apartamento pequeno sets small against a larger alternative more directly than um pequeno apartamento.
SHOW: noun + [degree/complement + adjective phrase] · contrastive size/quality -> usually post-nominal

[CARD]
TITLE: Position can select a different meaning of the same adjective
REF: 5.3

With several frequent adjectives, the position is part of the lexical choice. Before the noun, antigo means former; after it, antigo means old-fashioned or antique.
TL: Encontrei uma antiga professora numa loja de móveis antigos.
EN: I met a former teacher in an antique-furniture shop.
[PAUSE:900]
Before a noun, grande can mean great, novo another or new to the situation, and certo a certain. After it, the usual readings are large, brand-new, and correct.
TL: É uma grande ideia, mas precisamos da medida certa.
EN: It is a great idea, but we need the correct measurement.
[PAUSE:950]
Learn these as position-meaning pairs rather than translating the adjective in isolation.
SHOW: antigo N -> former · N antigo -> antique · grande N -> great · N grande -> large · certo N -> certain · N certo -> correct
Eight source meaning-and-position choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Portuguese keeps the full adjective where Spanish may shorten it
REF: 5.3, 5.14

Portuguese does not use the Spanish-style short forms of adjectives before a noun. Grande, primeiro, and terceiro remain complete.
TL: Foi um grande erro no primeiro dia do terceiro mês.
EN: It was a big mistake on the first day of the third month.
[PAUSE:900]
Nearby pairs still need direct learning: próximo means next before a noun and nearby after it; único means only before and unique after it.
TL: A próxima estação fica numa região única.
EN: The next station is in a unique region.
[PAUSE:950]
Resemblance across the two languages does not override Portuguese form or position.
SHOW: no apocope: grande/primeiro/terceiro · próximo N -> next · N próximo -> nearby · único N -> only

[CARD]
TITLE: Some adjective forms function as invariant adverbs
REF: 5.5

A frequent set of adjectives can modify a verb in the masculine singular base form. In this use they do not agree with the subject or object.
TL: As crianças falam baixo e os músicos tocam alto.
EN: The children speak quietly, and the musicians play loudly.
[PAUSE:900]
The inventory includes alto, baixo, caro, barato, direito, direto, duro, fácil, forte, junto, rápido, and others, each with learned collocations.
TL: Elas trabalharam duro, mas chegaram junto ao destino.
EN: They worked hard, but they arrived together at the destination.
[PAUSE:950]
Confirm that the word describes the event before leaving it in the base form.
SHOW: adjective describes noun -> agreement · base form describes event -> invariant adverbial use
Seven hygiene-gated source adverb choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An adjective can stand for a person, thing, or understood noun
REF: 5.4

An adjective describing a person can be used as a noun with an article. The masculine plural can name a mixed or generic group.
TL: Os desempregados receberam apoio; uma idosa pediu informação.
EN: The unemployed received support; an elderly woman asked for information.
[PAUSE:900]
An agreeing adjective can also stand alone when its noun is recoverable from context. English often adds one or ones.
TL: Estas caixas são pesadas; precisamos de mais leves.
EN: These boxes are heavy; we need lighter ones.
[PAUSE:950]
The neuter-looking o plus masculine singular adjective can name an abstract quality: o importante, o difícil, o possível.
SHOW: article + adjective -> person/group/abstract quality · understood noun -> adjective agrees with omitted referent

[CARD]
TITLE: Form a manner adverb from the feminine adjective plus -mente
REF: 5.6

Use the feminine singular form as the base and add -mente. Adjectives that do not vary for gender simply take the suffix.
TL: lenta -> lentamente · cuidadosa -> cuidadosamente · feliz -> felizmente
EN: slowly · carefully · happily
[PAUSE:900]
A written accent on the adjective normally disappears in the derived adverb, and comum gives the spelling comumente.
TL: fácil -> facilmente · séria -> seriamente · comum -> comumente
EN: easily · seriously · commonly
[PAUSE:950]
Build the feminine base first so the derivation remains predictable.
SHOW: adjective -> feminine singular base -> remove written accent if required -> +mente
Four conservatively capped source formation items follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: In a coordinated pair, only the final adverb carries -mente
REF: 5.6, 5.7

When two manner adverbs are coordinated, the first appears in its feminine base and only the final member takes -mente.
TL: A técnica explicou o processo clara e pacientemente.
EN: The technician explained the process clearly and patiently.
[PAUSE:900]
As a neutral order, place a manner adverb after the verb and before a direct object. Moving it after the object can give it extra emphasis.
TL: Revisamos cuidadosamente o contrato; revisamos o contrato cuidadosamente.
EN: We carefully reviewed the contract; we reviewed the contract carefully.
[PAUSE:950]
For a more formal alternative, de forma plus a feminine adjective can replace the -mente form.
SHOW: adv1-F + e + adv2-mente · verb + manner + object · de forma + F adjective

[CARD]
TITLE: Frequency and viewpoint adverbs usually precede the verb
REF: 5.7

Words such as sempre, geralmente, logo, só, apenas, talvez, and provavelmente normally appear between the subject and a finite verb.
TL: A diretora provavelmente chega amanhã.
EN: The director will probably arrive tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
With ser and estar, the adverb normally follows the verb instead.
TL: O escritório está sempre silencioso de manhã.
EN: The office is always quiet in the morning.
[PAUSE:950]
Adverb placement helps reveal its scope, so locate the verb and decide exactly what the adverb modifies.
SHOW: subject + frequency/viewpoint adverb + verb · ser/estar + adverb
Flip to separate adjective agreement from adverb invariability.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ask what the modifier describes before choosing its form
REF: 5, 5.5, 5.6

An adjective assigns a property to a noun or pronoun and agrees with it. An adverb describes an event, quality, or another modifier and remains invariable.
TL: As respostas foram rápidas; elas responderam rapidamente.
EN: The answers were quick; they answered quickly.
[PAUSE:900]
A lexical base-form adverb can express the same function without -mente, but it belongs to a restricted learned set.
TL: A música está baixa; eles falam baixo.
EN: The music is low; they speak quietly.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not infer function from the word's shape alone; use the relationship inside the sentence.
SHOW: modifies noun -> agreeing adjective · modifies event/quality -> invariant adverb · base-form adverb -> lexical pattern

[CARD]
TITLE: Mais and menos form regular comparisons and relative superlatives
REF: 5.8

Portuguese has no productive equivalent of English -er or -est. Put mais or menos before an adjective or adverb to compare degrees.
TL: Este caminho é mais curto; Ana chegou menos cedo hoje.
EN: This route is shorter; Ana arrived less early today.
[PAUSE:900]
A definite article or possessive normally marks the relative-superlative reading inside a noun phrase.
TL: Esta é a rota mais segura; é minha decisão mais difícil.
EN: This is the safest route; it is my most difficult decision.
[PAUSE:950]
With adverbs, context may be the only signal that the degree is extreme within a set.
SHOW: mais/menos + adjective/adverb -> comparison · article/possessive + noun phrase -> relative superlative
Ten source comparative-and-superlative completions follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Melhor, pior, maior, and menor are lexical comparative forms
REF: 5.9

Bom or bem normally compares as melhor; mau, ruim, or mal compares as pior. Grande and pequeno compare as maior and menor.
TL: O segundo plano é melhor e funciona melhor.
EN: The second plan is better and works better.
[PAUSE:900]
These forms vary only for number: melhores, piores, maiores, menores. Mais and menos remain invariable.
TL: Precisamos de salas maiores e de menos móveis.
EN: We need larger rooms and less furniture.
[PAUSE:950]
Brazilian Portuguese uses mais novo and mais velho for age; maior and menor express size, degree, or rank here.
SHOW: bom/bem -> melhor · mau/ruim/mal -> pior · grande -> maior · pequeno -> menor · age -> mais novo/velho

[CARD]
TITLE: The absolute superlative presents a quality as extremely intense
REF: 5.11

The absolute superlative does not rank a member inside a set. It intensifies an adjective, commonly with an agreeing form of -íssimo.
TL: O curso foi longuíssimo; as últimas aulas foram curtíssimas.
EN: The course was extremely long; the last classes were extremely short.
[PAUSE:900]
Replace final o or e and preserve the needed stem change: feliz becomes felicíssimo and agradável becomes agradabilíssimo.
TL: Eles ficaram felicíssimos com a notícia agradabilíssima.
EN: They were delighted with the extremely pleasant news.
[PAUSE:950]
The suffix carries intensity; agreement still carries the referent's gender and number.
SHOW: adjective stem + agreeing -íssimo · -z -> -císsimo · -vel -> -bilíssimo
Eight source absolute-superlative choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Several absolute superlatives are lexical, and playful forms mark register
REF: 5.11, 5.12

Frequent lexical forms include ótimo, péssimo, facílimo, dificílimo, mínimo, antiquíssimo, and paupérrimo. Learn their meaning with their form.
TL: O exercício foi facílimo, mas o acesso ao prédio era péssimo.
EN: The exercise was extremely easy, but access to the building was terrible.
[PAUSE:900]
Colloquial speech also uses super- and intentionally playful formations for emphasis. They can sound humorous or strongly informal.
TL: O hotel é superconfortável, mas caríssimo.
EN: The hotel is really comfortable but extremely expensive.
[PAUSE:950]
Choose intensity and register together; a grammatically possible form may be wrong for the setting.
SHOW: lexical elative -> memorize · super- -> colloquial emphasis · playful innovation -> marked register

[CARD]
TITLE: Comparators depend on the shape of the second term
REF: 5.10

Use do que, or less often que, before an ordinary comparison term. Before a numerical threshold after mais or menos, use de.
TL: O relatório é mais longo do que o resumo; tem mais de cem páginas.
EN: The report is longer than the summary; it has more than one hundred pages.
[PAUSE:900]
Equality uses tão with an adjective or adverb and quanto or como before the second term.
TL: O novo sistema é tão rápido quanto o antigo.
EN: The new system is as fast as the old one.
[PAUSE:950]
A relative-superlative domain is introduced by de, normally contracted with its article.
SHOW: ordinary benchmark -> do que/que · numeric threshold -> de · equality -> tão...quanto/como · superlative domain -> de
Flip for changing scales.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quanto mais links one changing scale to another
REF: 5.10

Use quanto mais or quanto menos in the dependent scale, followed by mais or menos in the result. No English-style definite articles are added.
TL: Quanto mais praticamos, mais natural fica a pronúncia.
EN: The more we practice, the more natural the pronunciation becomes.
[PAUSE:900]
Cada vez mais presents an ongoing trend, while ainda mais raises an already established degree.
TL: As tarefas ficam cada vez mais complexas e exigem ainda mais atenção.
EN: The tasks are becoming more and more complex and require even more attention.
[PAUSE:950]
Keep each degree word attached to the scale it controls.
SHOW: quanto mais/menos X, mais/menos Y · ongoing trend -> cada vez mais · added degree -> ainda mais

[CARD]
TITLE: One adjective after multiple nouns normally takes plural agreement
REF: 5.13, 5.14

An adjective that follows two singular nouns, or follows a linking verb whose subject contains them, normally takes the plural. Mixed genders select masculine plural.
TL: A praça e a avenida estavam vazias; o parque e a praça estavam vazios.
EN: The square and avenue were empty; the park and square were empty.
[PAUSE:900]
Before coordinated nouns, the adjective normally agrees with the first noun.
TL: A reforma causou muita demora e transtorno.
EN: The renovation caused a great deal of delay and disruption.
[PAUSE:950]
When singular adjectives classify different instances of a plural noun, singular can be deliberate rather than an error.
SHOW: N + N + adjective -> plural · mixed gender -> masculine plural · adjective + N + N -> agreement with first N
Flip for the final production sequence.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Decide function, scope, and meaning before producing the form
REF: 5.1, 5.3, 5.5, 5.8, 5.11, 5.13

First ask whether the word modifies a noun, an event, or another quality. Then decide whether position changes its meaning and whether degree compares, ranks, or merely intensifies.
TL: É uma antiga fábrica; a fábrica é antiga; funciona melhor; é a melhor da região; é moderníssima.
EN: It is a former factory; the factory is old; it works better; it is the best in the region; it is extremely modern.
[PAUSE:1000]
Finally apply gender and number wherever the form is adjectival, and keep adverbs and mais or menos invariable.
SHOW: function -> position meaning -> degree relationship -> gender+number -> register
