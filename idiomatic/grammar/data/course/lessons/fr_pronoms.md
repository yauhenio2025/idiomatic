## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A French pronoun packages reference and grammatical role
REF: 3, 3.1, 3.2

A pronoun does not merely replace an English noun phrase. Its form records the role assigned by the French construction: subject, direct object, indirect object, prepositional complement, or neutral reference to a whole idea.
TL: La proposition convainc Léa; elle la défendra demain.
EN: The proposal convinces Léa; she will defend it tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Elle retrieves Léa as subject. La retrieves la proposition as direct object. The two forms are similar in meaning but occupy different grammatical slots.
SHOW: identify referent → identify French role → choose form
Flip for the position of the unstressed forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Unstressed pronouns stay close to the finite verb
REF: 3.1.1, 3.2.2

An unstressed subject normally stands immediately before the finite verb, with negation and object pronouns packed between them. Ordinary adverbs do not split the subject from that verbal group.
TL: Nous ne leur en parlerons probablement pas.
EN: We probably will not talk to them about it.
[PAUSE:900]
With inversion, the subject pronoun moves immediately after the finite verb.
TL: Viendront-ils malgré la grève?
EN: Will they come despite the strike?
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat the clitic group as syntax, not as a freely ordered string of English equivalents.
SHOW: subject · negation · object cluster · finite verb

[CARD]
TITLE: Tu and vous encode the relationship in force now
REF: 3.1.2, 3.1.3

Vous addresses any plural group and also one person at social distance. Tu addresses one person within a relationship where familiarity, solidarity, or an accepted local norm licenses it.
TL: Si vous le souhaitez, nous pourrons nous tutoyer.
EN: If you wish, we can use tu with each other.
[PAUSE:900]
The cautious default with an unfamiliar adult is vous. A switch to tu is negotiated by the relationship; an unexpected tu can deliberately signal intimacy, dominance, or contempt.
TL: Tu peux me tutoyer; ici, tout le monde le fait.
EN: You can use tu with me; everyone does so here.
SHOW: plural → vous · distant singular → vous · licensed familiar singular → tu
Flip for third-person agreement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Third-person pronouns follow the intended referent
REF: 3.1.4, 3.1.5, 3.1.6, 3.1.7, 3.1.9

Il, elle, ils and elles normally inherit gender and number from the noun phrase they retrieve. A collective noun remains grammatically singular even when English prefers they.
TL: Le comité a rendu son avis; il reste prudent.
EN: The committee has delivered its opinion; it remains cautious.
[PAUSE:800]
For a person, real sex can override the lexical gender of a role noun. A mixed group takes ils.
TL: Paul est notre nouvelle recrue; il dirigera le projet.
EN: Paul is our new recruit; he will lead the project.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Maya et Thomas sont prêts; ils nous rejoindront demain.
EN: Maya and Thomas are ready; they will join us tomorrow.
SHOW: retrieve the noun phrase · preserve its number · resolve human reference
Ten clean address and agreement decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: On leaves the human agent open
REF: 3.1.10, 3.1.11, 3.1.12

On can mean someone, people in general, or an English generic you. It also lets French describe an event without naming an agent, where English may choose a passive.
TL: On a reporté la réunion sans prévenir les participants.
EN: The meeting was postponed without warning the participants.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ici, on ne paie qu'après le service.
EN: Here, you pay only after the service.
[PAUSE:1000]
Whatever its interpretation, on takes a third-person singular verb.
SHOW: unknown agent · general people · generic you · singular verb
Flip for on meaning we.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Spoken on often carries the meaning of nous
REF: 3.1.13, 3.1.14

Informal speech frequently uses on for a group that includes the speaker. The verb remains singular even when the meaning is plural.
TL: Avec Inès, on a déjeuné près du canal.
EN: Inès and I had lunch near the canal.
[PAUSE:900]
Written French may use l'on after a word ending in a vowel sound. The l' is stylistic and optional; it does not change the reference.
TL: C'est une décision que l'on regrettera longtemps.
EN: It is a decision that people will regret for a long time.
SHOW: spoken we → on + singular verb · written smoothing → optional l'on
Eight on-versus-l'on choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Personal pronouns retrieve entities; neutral forms retrieve situations
REF: 3.1.15, 3.1.16

Il and elle point back to a specific noun phrase, whether concrete or abstract. Ce, cela and ça can instead package an event, action, state, or general class as a single neutral idea.
TL: Cette méthode est lente, mais elle reste fiable.
EN: This method is slow, but it remains reliable.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Refaire toute l'analyse? Cela prendra deux jours.
EN: Redo the entire analysis? That will take two days.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first pronoun retrieves a feminine noun. The second retrieves the proposed action.
SHOW: named entity → il or elle · event or state → ce, cela or ça
Flip for register and verb choice.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ce favors être; cela and ça travel more widely
REF: 3.1.15, 3.1.16

Ce is the usual neutral subject with être. Cela is at home in careful writing, while ça is the ordinary unstressed choice in conversation and may also present a whole class as a topic.
TL: Ce retard est regrettable; cela compromet le calendrier.
EN: This delay is regrettable; it jeopardizes the schedule.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Les réunions à sept heures, ça épuise tout le monde.
EN: Seven-o'clock meetings exhaust everyone.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not let conversational ça drift unexamined into formal prose when a personal il or elle is retrieving a specific noun.
SHOW: ce + être · careful neutral subject → cela · conversational topic → ça
Ten clean personal-versus-neutral choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Some impersonal frames require il
REF: 3.1.17, 3.1.18

An impersonal pronoun fills the subject position without retrieving a prior noun. Clock time and fixed frames such as il y a, il s'agit de, il faut and il reste require il.
TL: Il est minuit, et il reste encore deux dossiers à examiner.
EN: It is midnight, and there are still two files to examine.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Il s'agit de vérifier les chiffres, pas de réécrire le rapport.
EN: The task is to check the figures, not to rewrite the report.
[PAUSE:1000]
Il s'agit is always impersonal: the topic never becomes its grammatical subject.
SHOW: time · existence · necessity · remaining amount · il s'agit de → il
Flip for predicates that admit another neutral subject.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The experiencer's syntax selects il or cela
REF: 3.1.19, 3.1.20

Some impersonal predicates take il in careful style and allow ça in informal speech. With an experiencer introduced by à, il is possible.
TL: Il convient à Nora de répondre elle-même.
EN: It is appropriate for Nora to answer herself.
[PAUSE:800]
When the experiencer is a direct object, use cela in careful prose or ça in conversation, not il.
TL: Cela agace Nora de devoir tout recommencer.
EN: It annoys Nora to have to start everything again.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ça m'inquiète qu'il n'ait toujours pas appelé.
EN: It worries me that he still has not called.
SHOW: à + experiencer → il possible · direct object experiencer → cela or ça
Eight clean impersonal-subject decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Il est describes; c'est identifies or classifies
REF: 3.1.22

With a bare profession, nationality, or status after être, use a personal subject. Once a determiner creates a full noun phrase, use ce.
TL: Elle est architecte, mais c'est une architecte spécialisée dans le patrimoine.
EN: She is an architect, but she is an architect who specializes in heritage buildings.
[PAUSE:1000]
Identity and non-adjectival complements also call for ce.
TL: C'est Amine qui présentera le projet.
EN: Amine is the one who will present the project.
SHOW: bare role → il or elle est · noun phrase or identity → c'est
Flip for adjective predicates.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An adjective can describe a referent or judge a situation
REF: 3.1.22, 3.1.23

Il or elle plus an adjective normally describes a person or specific thing. C'est plus naturally evaluates an action or situation.
TL: Ce dossier? Il est complexe, mais c'est passionnant de l'analyser.
EN: This file? It is complex, but analyzing it is fascinating.
[PAUSE:900]
With an impersonal adjective followed by de plus an infinitive, both il est and c'est are possible. Compound forms of ce remain available too.
TL: Ce serait imprudent de signer aujourd'hui.
EN: It would be unwise to sign today.
SHOW: specific referent + adjective → il or elle · situation + adjective → ce
Eight clean il-est-versus-c'est choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Let the French verb assign direct or indirect object form
REF: 3.2.1, 3.2.2

An English direct object does not guarantee a French direct object. Learn a verb with its French complement pattern, then choose le, la or les for a direct object and lui or leur for an indirect one.
TL: J'ai téléphoné à Salomé; je lui ai conseillé d'attendre.
EN: I phoned Salomé; I advised her to wait.
[PAUSE:900]
Conversely, aider takes a direct human object before its infinitive complement.
TL: J'ai aidé Salomé à déménager; je l'ai aidée toute la journée.
EN: I helped Salomé move; I helped her all day.
SHOW: learn verb + complement · direct → le, la, les · indirect → lui, leur
Flip for placement around infinitives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Attach an object pronoun to the verb that governs it
REF: 3.2.2, 3.2.3, 3.2.4, 3.2.5

Before a finite verb or auxiliary, the object cluster is preverbal. With an ordinary infinitive, it normally sits directly before that infinitive.
TL: Je voudrais lui en parler avant qu'elle ne parte.
EN: I would like to talk to her about it before she leaves.
[PAUSE:900]
With faire, laisser and perception verbs, the understood subject of the infinitive normally moves before the first verb.
TL: Je l'ai vue traverser la cour.
EN: I saw her cross the courtyard.
[PAUSE:900]
Negative imperatives also keep object pronouns before the verb.
TL: Ne me le rappelle pas.
EN: Do not remind me of it.
SHOW: find governing verb → place its pronoun immediately beside it
Ten clean direct-and-indirect-object transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Neutral le retrieves a property or proposition
REF: 3.2.8, 3.2.9, 3.2.10, 3.2.11

Neutral le can stand for a state, adjective, identity, or whole proposition. French sometimes requires it where English leaves the complement unspoken.
TL: Ils sont prêts, et je crois qu'elle le sera bientôt.
EN: They are ready, and I think she will be soon.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Cette solution est coûteuse; nous le savons.
EN: This solution is expensive; we know it.
[PAUSE:1000]
But French omits le in impersonal frames after verbs such as trouver or juger.
TL: Je trouve difficile de contester ces résultats.
EN: I find it difficult to dispute these results.
SHOW: retrieved property or claim → le · impersonal evaluation → no le
Flip for se that belongs to the verb.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Se can describe a state or be lexically inseparable
REF: 3.2.15, 3.2.16

Some pronominal constructions present a general state of affairs in a way English often renders as a passive. Others simply contain se as part of the lexical verb, without a recoverable reflexive meaning.
TL: Ce tissu se lave à basse température.
EN: This fabric should be washed at a low temperature.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Nadia s'est souvenue de l'adresse au dernier moment.
EN: Nadia remembered the address at the last moment.
[PAUSE:1000]
Store the second type as a whole verb. Do not force every se to mean self or each other.
SHOW: state-of-affairs se · lexical pronominal verb · not always reflexive
Ten clean neutral-le and pronominal-verb drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Not every à phrase is an indirect object
REF: 3.2.19, 3.2.21, 3.2.23

With verbs such as penser à, tenir à and faire attention à, à behaves as an ordinary preposition. A human complement therefore becomes à plus a stressed pronoun, not lui or leur.
TL: Je pense souvent à mes anciens collègues; je pense encore à eux.
EN: I often think of my former colleagues; I still think of them.
[PAUSE:900]
A non-human complement in the same structural family normally becomes y.
TL: Je tiens à cette règle; j'y tiens malgré les critiques.
EN: I care about this rule; I stand by it despite the criticism.
SHOW: ordinary à + person → à lui, à elle, à eux · non-human → y
Flip for the body-part construction.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An affected person can appear as an indirect object
REF: 3.2.20, 2.2.8, 8.7.2

With an action affecting someone's body, French normally marks the possessor with an indirect or reflexive pronoun and uses the definite article on the body part.
TL: Le kinésithérapeute lui a massé l'épaule.
EN: The physiotherapist massaged her shoulder.
[PAUSE:900]
The construction is not available when the verb merely describes a state or attitude.
TL: J'admire son sourire.
EN: I admire his smile.
[PAUSE:1000]
Ask whether the verb depicts an action upon the body, not merely whether a body part is mentioned.
SHOW: affected possessor + action → lui or se + definite body part
Ten clean à-complement and body-part decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Y replaces a non-human prepositional complement
REF: 3.2.21, 3.2.22, 3.2.23, 3.2.28

Y commonly retrieves a non-human complement introduced by à, en, dans, sur, or another place-like preposition. Its use follows the French construction, even when English shows no preposition.
TL: Nous entrerons dans ce débat demain; nous y entrerons avec prudence.
EN: We will enter this debate tomorrow; we will enter it cautiously.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Elle renonce à cette option; elle y renonce à contrecœur.
EN: She is giving up this option; she is giving it up reluctantly.
SHOW: French à, en, dans, sur plus non-human complement → y
Flip for de and quantities.

[SIDE]
TITLE: En carries de and preserves an omitted quantity
REF: 3.2.24, 3.2.25, 3.2.26, 3.2.27, 3.2.28

En normally retrieves a complement introduced by de. It is also required when a counted noun disappears but its numeral or quantifier remains.
TL: Ils parlent souvent de cette réforme; ils en parlent sans détour.
EN: They often discuss this reform; they discuss it frankly.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il nous faut quatre exemplaires; j'en ai déjà imprimé deux.
EN: We need four copies; I have already printed two.
[PAUSE:1000]
Because en, not the quantified noun, precedes the compound verb, the past participle stays unchanged here.
SHOW: de-complement → en · omitted counted noun → en + retained quantity
Eight clean y-and-en transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Preverbal object pronouns occupy fixed slots
REF: 3.2.29, 3.2.30, 3.2.32, 3.2.33

Before a verb, me, te, se, nous and vous come first; then le, la or les; then lui or leur; then y; then en. Use only combinations licensed by the underlying complements.
TL: Le rapport? Je le leur enverrai demain.
EN: The report? I will send it to them tomorrow.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Des exemples? Nous leur en montrerons trois.
EN: Examples? We will show them three.
[PAUSE:1000]
When two required forms occupy an impossible combination, keep a stressed prepositional complement instead of inventing a clitic cluster.
SHOW: me/te/se/nous/vous → le/la/les → lui/leur → y → en
Flip for affirmative commands.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An affirmative imperative changes the local order
REF: 3.2.31

In an affirmative command, object pronouns follow the verb with hyphens. Le, la and les precede moi, toi, lui, nous, vous and leur. Before y or en, moi and toi reduce to m' and t'.
TL: Montrez-le-moi maintenant, mais ne me le laissez pas.
EN: Show it to me now, but do not leave it with me.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Parlez-lui-en avant de décider.
EN: Talk to him about it before deciding.
[PAUSE:1000]
A negative command returns to the ordinary preverbal order.
SHOW: affirmative → verb-le-moi, verb-lui-en · negative → me-le-verb
Ten clean multiple-pronoun transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Stressed pronouns carry emphasis and prepositions
REF: 3.3, 3.3.1, 3.3.2, 3.3.3

Moi, toi, lui, elle, soi, nous, vous, eux and elles can stand alone, follow prepositions, or double an unstressed pronoun for contrast. First- and second-person subjects still retain their ordinary subject pronoun.
TL: Moi, je resterai; eux, ils partiront avant l'aube.
EN: I will stay; they will leave before dawn.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sans elle, nous n'aurions jamais terminé à temps.
EN: Without her, we would never have finished on time.
SHOW: standalone · after preposition · emphatic double → stressed form
Flip for coordination and non-specific soi.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Coordination reveals the person hierarchy
REF: 3.3.4, 3.3.5, 3.3.6, 3.3.7

Only stressed pronouns coordinate with another pronoun or noun. A coordinated subject is first person if it includes moi or nous, otherwise second person if it includes toi or vous.
TL: Toi et moi devons trancher avant midi.
EN: You and I must decide before noon.
[PAUSE:900]
Soi refers back to a non-specific subject such as on or chacun. With seul, même and similar forms, stressed pronouns also build compact contrasts.
TL: Chacun devrait garder une copie pour soi.
EN: Everyone should keep a copy for themselves.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Lui seul saura convaincre le comité.
EN: He alone will know how to convince the committee.
SHOW: coordination · preposition · non-specific reflexive · focused contrast
Eight clean stressed-pronoun choices follow.
