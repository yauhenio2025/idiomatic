## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: The governing expression chooses the mood
REF: 11.1

The indicative presents an event as an asserted fact. The subjunctive presents it through a governing expression of uncertainty, desire, feeling, evaluation, condition, or hypothesis. Start with that governing expression, not with que alone.
TL: Léa affirme que le train est parti; Léa doute que le train soit parti.
EN: Léa states that the train has left; Léa doubts that the train has left.
[PAUSE:900]
The event can be identical while the speaker's framing changes. Affirmer treats the departure as information; douter withholds that commitment.
SHOW: asserted event → indicative · personally interpreted or non-asserted event → subjunctive
Flip for the forms that carry this contrast.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build the present and past subjunctive
REF: 11.1.1, 11.1.2

For most verbs, take the ils form of the present indicative, remove -ent, and add -e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent. The nous and vous stems may follow the nous form instead. Frequent irregulars such as être, avoir, aller, faire, pouvoir, savoir, vouloir, and falloir must be learned.
TL: qu'elle parle · que nous finissions · qu'ils viennent · que vous soyez
EN: that she speak · that we finish · that they come · that you be
[PAUSE:800]
To place the subordinate event earlier, use the present subjunctive of avoir or être plus a past participle.
TL: Je regrette qu'il soit parti; je doute qu'ils aient compris.
EN: I regret that he left; I doubt that they understood.
SHOW: present subjunctive → same-time or later event · auxiliary in subjunctive + participle → earlier event

[CARD]
TITLE: Desire, emotion, and evaluation select the subjunctive
REF: 11.1.3

Expressions of wishing, ordering, permission, fear, regret, surprise, satisfaction, and expectation normally require a subjunctive clause. They project the subject's response onto the event even when the event itself is real.
TL: Nous regrettons que Léa soit partie; le directeur exige que chacun réponde.
EN: We regret that Léa left; the director requires everyone to answer.
[PAUSE:900]
The subjunctive after regret does not make the departure unreal. It marks the attitude through which the departure is presented.
SHOW: vouloir / exiger / permettre / craindre / regretter / être surpris + que → subjunctive
Flip when the two clauses share a subject.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A shared subject often replaces que with an infinitive
REF: 11.1.3

When one person both feels or intends and performs the action, French commonly uses an infinitive. Use que plus subjunctive when the subjects differ.
TL: Je souhaite partir; je souhaite que vous partiez avec moi.
EN: I wish to leave; I want you to leave with me.
[PAUSE:800]
Some governing expressions take a preposition before que, especially s'attendre à ce que. Keep the whole trigger intact.
TL: Nous nous attendons à ce qu'elle arrive demain.
EN: We expect her to arrive tomorrow.
SHOW: same subject → infinitive · different subject → que + subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Thinking and saying normally assert the indicative
REF: 11.1.4

Affirmer, croire, déclarer, imaginer, penser, and similar verbs normally present their dependent event as information, so an affirmative use takes the indicative.
TL: Je pense que Nora connaît la réponse.
EN: I think Nora knows the answer.
[PAUSE:800]
Negation or a genuine question can weaken that commitment enough to select the subjunctive.
TL: Je ne pense pas que Nora connaisse la réponse.
EN: I do not think Nora knows the answer.
SHOW: affirmative belief or report → indicative · denied or questioned belief → often subjunctive
Flip for two important safeguards.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Do not let negation or si trigger the mood mechanically
REF: 11.1.4

The meaning still governs. A negated verb can remain inside an asserted statement, while a hypothetical command such as supposons que selects the subjunctive. Espérer keeps the indicative even when negated or questioned.
TL: J'espère qu'il viendra; n'espérez-vous pas qu'il viendra?
EN: I hope he will come; don't you hope he will come?
[PAUSE:800]
Interrogative si means whether and does not itself select the subjunctive.
TL: Je ne sais pas s'il a compris.
EN: I do not know whether he understood.
SHOW: espérer + indicative · si = whether + ordinary tense sequence · hypothetical supposition + subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Impersonal expressions divide by probability and stance
REF: 11.1.5

Expressions that assert evidence or probability take the indicative: il est certain, évident, probable, or vrai que, and il me semble que.
TL: Il est probable que le musée ouvrira demain.
EN: The museum will probably open tomorrow.
[PAUSE:800]
Expressions of doubt, mere possibility, or impossibility take the subjunctive: il est douteux, possible, peu probable, or impossible que.
TL: Il est peu probable que le musée ouvre demain.
EN: The museum is unlikely to open tomorrow.
SHOW: asserted probability → indicative · doubt or mere possibility → subjunctive
Flip for necessity and the subtle sembler contrast.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Necessity and evaluation also select the subjunctive
REF: 11.1.5

Il faut, il est important, il est nécessaire, il est regrettable, il est temps, and il vaut mieux all evaluate or impose the event, so their que clause takes the subjunctive.
TL: Il faut que vous soyez prêts; il vaut mieux que Léa conduise.
EN: You must be ready; it is better for Léa to drive.
[PAUSE:800]
Il me semble que normally states the speaker's view and takes the indicative; impersonal il semble que presents a less committed appearance and takes the subjunctive.
TL: Il me semble qu'il est prêt; il semble qu'il soit prêt.
EN: I think he is ready; it seems that he is ready.
SHOW: il me semble + indicative · il semble + subjunctive
Eight clean impersonal-expression choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Purpose, prevention, and concession select the subjunctive
REF: 11.1.6

Conjunctions that make an event intended, prevented, conceded, or conditional normally take the subjunctive. Learn each conjunction as a complete mood-selecting unit.
TL: Je répète afin que chacun comprenne; bien qu'il soit tard, nous continuons.
EN: I am repeating it so everyone understands; although it is late, we are continuing.
[PAUSE:900]
Common triggers include afin que, pour que, à moins que, sans que, bien que, quoique, en attendant que, and pourvu que.
SHOW: intended / prevented / conceded / conditional event → conjunction + subjunctive
Flip when the conjunction describes a result as a fact.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Result can switch the mood by changing the meaning
REF: 11.1.6

De façon que, de manière que, and de sorte que take the subjunctive when they express a purpose, but the indicative when they report an achieved result.
TL: Parlez de façon que tous vous entendent.
EN: Speak so that everyone can hear you.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Il a parlé très clairement, de sorte que tous l'ont entendu.
EN: He spoke very clearly, so everyone heard him.
SHOW: intended outcome → subjunctive · observed result → indicative

[CARD]
TITLE: Before and until remain unrealized at the reference point
REF: 11.1.7

Avant que and jusqu'à ce que take the subjunctive because the dependent event is viewed as pending relative to the main event.
TL: Vérifiez le dossier avant que le client ne le signe.
EN: Check the file before the client signs it.
[PAUSE:800]
The ne in careful French after avant que is often non-negative; it does not mean not. If the subject is shared, avant de plus infinitive is usually simpler.
TL: Vérifiez le dossier avant de le signer.
EN: Check the file before signing it.
SHOW: avant que / jusqu'à ce que + subjunctive · shared subject → avant de + infinitive
Flip for the completed event after après que.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Après que normally reports a completed fact
REF: 11.1.7

Après que traditionally takes the indicative because its event has already occurred. The subjunctive is widespread in modern usage by analogy with avant que, but the indicative remains the controlled baseline.
TL: Nous avons commencé après qu'ils étaient arrivés.
EN: We began after they had arrived.
[PAUSE:800]
Other factual time conjunctions such as dès que and maintenant que likewise use an indicative tense appropriate to the timeline.
SHOW: event still pending → subjunctive · event presented as completed fact → indicative
Nine clean conjunction choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: A peerless claim invites the subjunctive
REF: 11.1.8

A superlative or expressions such as le seul, l'unique, le premier, le dernier, personne, and rien take the subjunctive when they claim that someone or something is unmatched.
TL: C'est la solution la plus sûre que nous ayons trouvée.
EN: It is the safest solution we have found.
[PAUSE:800]
The claim reaches beyond a mere list: no competing solution is treated as equally safe.
SHOW: unique or unmatched status → relative clause in subjunctive
Flip for an ordinary fact within a series.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A numbered place in a series stays indicative
REF: 11.1.8

If first, last, or only merely identifies one factual event in a limited series, use the indicative. Ask whether the sentence claims unmatched status or simply reports what happened.
TL: C'est le premier film que j'ai vu cette année.
EN: It is the first film I saw this year.
[PAUSE:800]
Compare a genuine record.
TL: C'est le premier film qui ait remporté les trois prix.
EN: It is the first film ever to have won all three prizes.
SHOW: ordinary series fact → indicative · record or peerless evaluation → subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Hypothetical and indefinite clauses resist assertion
REF: 11.1.10, 11.1.11, 11.1.12

When a si condition is extended with et que, the coordinated verb is normally subjunctive. A whole clause used as the subject of a sentence also takes the subjunctive.
TL: S'il appelle et qu'il veuille parler au directeur, prenez un message.
EN: If he calls and wants to speak to the director, take a message.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Qu'elle ait refusé nous étonne.
EN: That she refused surprises us.
SHOW: si ... et que + subjunctive · Que-clause as subject + subjunctive
Flip for open-ended people, things, places, and qualities.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Whoever and whatever take the subjunctive
REF: 11.1.12

Qui que, quoi que, où que, quel que, quelque plus noun plus que, and similar indefinite expressions take the subjunctive because the identity or circumstances remain open.
TL: Où que vous alliez, gardez ce numéro.
EN: Wherever you go, keep this number.
[PAUSE:800]
A hypothetical relative clause works the same way; an identified real referent stays indicative.
TL: Elle cherche un logement qui ait un balcon; elle visite un logement qui a un balcon.
EN: She is looking for any flat with a balcony; she is viewing a flat that has a balcony.
SHOW: hypothetical or indefinite referent → subjunctive · identified real referent → indicative

[CARD]
TITLE: Devoir and falloir express different kinds of necessity
REF: 11.2.1, 11.2.4, 11.3.2, 11.3.6, 11.3.7

Devoir can express logical probability, obligation, a plan, or a necessary outcome. Tense and context select the reading.
TL: Elle doit être au bureau; vous devez répondre avant midi.
EN: She must be at the office; you must answer before noon.
[PAUSE:800]
The conditional softens obligation into advice, while the conditional perfect marks an unfulfilled past duty.
TL: Vous devriez vérifier; vous auriez dû vérifier.
EN: You should check; you should have checked.
SHOW: doit → inference or obligation · devrait → advice or probability · aurait dû → unfulfilled past duty
Flip for impersonal necessity.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Falloir removes the personal subject
REF: 11.2.4, 11.3.2, 11.3.6, 11.3.7

Falloir is impersonal. Use il faut plus an infinitive for a general instruction, or il faut que plus subjunctive when the person responsible appears as a clause subject.
TL: Il faut partir; il faut que vous partiez.
EN: We must leave; you must leave.
[PAUSE:800]
For past duty that was not met, il aurait fallu parallels aurait dû.
TL: Il aurait fallu prévenir le directeur.
EN: The director should have been warned.
SHOW: general necessity → il faut + infinitive · named clause subject → il faut que + subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Translate the modal meaning, not the English word
REF: 11.2.2, 11.2.3, 11.3

Pouvoir covers ability, permission, possibility, and polite requests. Savoir plus infinitive means possessing a learned skill; pouvoir means being able in the present circumstances.
TL: Je sais réparer ce moteur, mais je ne peux pas le faire sans outils.
EN: I know how to repair this engine, but I cannot do it without tools.
[PAUSE:900]
The conditional of pouvoir often makes a possibility or request more tentative.
TL: Cela pourrait fonctionner; pourriez-vous m'aider?
EN: That could work; could you help me?
SHOW: learned skill → savoir · practical ability / permission / possibility → pouvoir
Flip for the tense clues hidden in English modals.

[SIDE]
TITLE: English would, could, may, and must split several ways
REF: 11.3.1, 11.3.3, 11.3.4, 11.3.5, 11.3.7

Would may require a conditional, vouloir, or an imperfect habit. Could may be past ability, conditional possibility, or permission. May and might can become pouvoir, peut-être plus indicative, or il se peut que plus subjunctive. Logical must is usually devoir.
TL: Chaque été, nous venions ici; il se peut qu'elle vienne; il a dû oublier.
EN: Every summer, we would come here; she may come; he must have forgotten.
[PAUSE:800]
Identify the intended function first: time, ability, permission, probability, obligation, willingness, or politeness.
SHOW: English modal → identify meaning → choose French verb, mood, and tense

[CARD]
TITLE: Exclamations keep their own small set of patterns
REF: 11.4, 11.5.7

Comme or que can turn a full indicative clause into an exclamation without changing its word order. Quel agrees with the noun it highlights, and que de means what a lot of.
TL: Comme elle parle vite! Quelle surprise! Que de monde!
EN: How quickly she speaks! What a surprise! What a crowd!
[PAUSE:800]
Use the pattern that matches the focus: a whole proposition, a noun, or a large quantity.
SHOW: whole clause → comme / que + indicative · noun → quel · quantity → que de
Flip for commands about a third person.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Que plus subjunctive creates a third-person imperative
REF: 11.4.4, 11.5.7

French has no ordinary third-person imperative form. Use que plus subjunctive for let him, let her, or let them, and for wishes and formulaic exclamations.
TL: Qu'il attende ici; qu'ils entrent; puissiez-vous réussir!
EN: Let him wait here; let them come in; may you succeed!
[PAUSE:800]
This is one of the few places where an independent main clause can contain a subjunctive.
SHOW: third-person command or wish → que + subjunctive
