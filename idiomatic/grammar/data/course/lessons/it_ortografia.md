## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Italian spelling gives you a strong first pronunciation
REF: 2.1, 2.16

Italian has a close relationship between letters and sounds. Once you know the main letter groups, an unfamiliar written word usually gives you a useful first pronunciation.
TL: La radio trasmette un programma italiano.
EN: The radio is broadcasting an Italian program.
[PAUSE:900]
That reliability has limits. Stress is not always written, and the open or closed values of stressed e and o are often lexical. Pronunciations also vary naturally by region.
TL: Come si scrive il suo cognome?
EN: How is your surname spelled?
[PAUSE:1000]
Use spelling as a map, not as a claim that every educated speaker sounds Tuscan.
SHOW: regular letter groups -> strong first guess · stress and regional detail -> verify
Flip for the hard and soft consonant spellings.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The alphabet includes names you need for spelling aloud
REF: 2.16

The core alphabet runs from a to z. The letters j, k, w, x, and y occur mainly in names and borrowed words, but their Italian names matter whenever you spell something aloud.
TL: La mia iniziale è acca, non acca doppia.
EN: My initial is h, not double h.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Si scrive con la zeta: Z-A-I-N-O.
EN: It is spelled with z: Z-A-I-N-O.
[PAUSE:1000]
Useful letter names include acca for h, cu for q, erre for r, esse for s, vu or vi for v, and zeta for z.
SHOW: spelling aloud -> Italian letter names · borrowed letters remain recognizable

[CARD]
TITLE: C and g change before e and i
REF: 2.2

Before a, o, or u, c and g normally have their hard values. Immediately before e or i, they take the soft values heard in cena and giro.
TL: Carlo cerca una camera economica.
EN: Carlo is looking for an inexpensive room.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Giulia paga il conto prima di partire.
EN: Giulia pays the bill before leaving.
[PAUSE:1000]
The sequence sc follows the same front-vowel pattern: scena has the sh-like sound, while scala begins with sk.
SHOW: ca/co/cu = hard c · ce/ci = soft c · ga/go/gu = hard g · ge/gi = soft g
Flip to keep a hard consonant before e or i.

[SIDE]
TITLE: H preserves hardness; i can mark softness
REF: 2.2

Add silent h after c or g to keep the hard sound before e or i. That is why the plurals of bianco and lago are bianchi and laghi.
TL: Chiara ordina gli spaghetti ai funghi.
EN: Chiara orders spaghetti with mushrooms.
[PAUSE:900]
Before a, o, or u, an i after c, g, or sc may act only as a spelling signal for the soft sound.
TL: Gianni lascia la valigia in albergo.
EN: Gianni leaves the suitcase at the hotel.
[PAUSE:1000]
Silent h also distinguishes forms of avere from common short words: ho from o, hai from ai, ha from a, and hanno from anno.
SHOW: che/chi and ghe/ghi -> hard · cia/gia/scia -> i may be diacritic

[CARD]
TITLE: Qu, gn, gli, and vowel-like i or u are spelling units
REF: 2.2

The sound at the start of quando is normally written qu. Its long counterpart is generally written cqu, as in acqua. The groups gn and gli represent single consonant patterns in words such as bagno and figlio.
TL: Quel ragazzo sceglie un albergo tranquillo.
EN: That boy chooses a quiet hotel.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Beviamo acqua ogni giorno.
EN: We drink water every day.
[PAUSE:900]
An unstressed i or u before another vowel often behaves like a glide rather than a separate full vowel.
TL: Siamo pronti per il viaggio.
EN: We are ready for the trip.
[PAUSE:1000]
Prefixes, stressed vowels, and some learned words can preserve a full vowel, so lexical evidence still matters.
SHOW: qu/cqu · gn · gli · unstressed i/u may join the following vowel
Flip for the vowels whose quality spelling does not settle.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Stressed e and o have open and closed values
REF: 2.3

Unstressed e and o are relatively stable. Under stress, each letter can represent an open or a closed vowel, and ordinary spelling usually does not tell you which one.
TL: La pesca è matura.
EN: The peach is ripe.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Domani andremo a pesca.
EN: Tomorrow we will go fishing.
[PAUSE:1000]
Regional standards distribute these vowels differently, and an unfamiliar choice rarely blocks understanding. Learn important contrasts from reliable audio or a dictionary rather than inventing certainty from the letters.
SHOW: same spelling can hide vowel quality · regional variation is normal

[CARD]
TITLE: S and z contain lexical and regional choices
REF: 2.4

S is unvoiced at the start of many words and before an unvoiced consonant. Before a voiced consonant it becomes voiced, as in sbaglio and smettere.
TL: Sara smette di lavorare alle sei.
EN: Sara stops working at six.
[PAUSE:900]
Between vowels, s may be voiced or unvoiced depending on the word and regional model. Z likewise represents either of two affricates, and internal z is often lexical.
TL: Lo zio legge il giornale durante il pranzo.
EN: The uncle reads the newspaper during lunch.
[PAUSE:1000]
A dictionary with pronunciation is more dependable than a rigid rule for intervocalic s and z.
SHOW: s + voiced consonant -> voiced · intervocalic s and internal z -> learn with the word
Flip for contrastive consonant length.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Written doubles must be held longer
REF: 2.5

Italian consonant length can distinguish words. A double consonant closes the preceding timing interval and is held longer than its single counterpart.
TL: Il nono treno arriva alle nove.
EN: The ninth train arrives at nine.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: La pala è accanto alla palla.
EN: The shovel is next to the ball.
[PAUSE:1000]
Between vowels, gn, gli, soft sc, and z also have long pronunciations even when their conventional spelling does not show an ordinary doubled letter.
SHOW: consonant length carries meaning · pronounce the written contrast

[CARD]
TITLE: A word boundary can lengthen the next consonant
REF: 2.6

Rafforzamento fonosintattico is consonant lengthening at the start of a word after certain triggers inside the same phrase. It is systematic in much central and southern speech but is almost never written.
TL: La città vecchia è molto bella.
EN: The old city is very beautiful.
[PAUSE:900]
Many triggers end in a stressed vowel, including short forms such as è, già, più, and tre. Several common function words, including a, e, ma, se, che, and qualche, can also trigger it.
TL: Ho fame, ma Carlo ha già mangiato.
EN: I am hungry, but Carlo has already eaten.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not add a letter in ordinary separate spelling. First learn to hear the longer onset; then imitate the regional standard you follow.
SHOW: phrase trigger + consonant -> possible long onset · spelling usually unchanged
Flip when the boundary becomes one written word.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Fusion can make the length visible in spelling
REF: 2.6, 2.7

When a trigger and its following word fuse, the long consonant is commonly written double.
TL: Dammi un minuto e fallo con calma.
EN: Give me a minute and do it calmly.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Soprattutto, non contraddire il direttore.
EN: Above all, do not contradict the director.
[PAUSE:1000]
Older Italian sometimes inserted i before initial s plus a consonant. Forms such as in Isvizzera survive as archaic or fixed-sounding variants; modern neutral usage normally has in Svizzera.
SHOW: separate words -> sound only · fused form -> double letter · prothetic i -> archaic edge

[CARD]
TITLE: Final-vowel loss is licensed, optional, and register-sensitive
REF: 2.8

Inside a phrase, an unstressed final vowel may disappear after certain sonorant consonants, especially r. It cannot simply be removed from any word, and a pause or sentence boundary resists the process.
TL: Dopo aver controllato i dati, Marta ha firmato.
EN: After checking the data, Marta signed.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Il professor Bianchi parlerà per primo.
EN: Professor Bianchi will speak first.
[PAUSE:1000]
Common environments include auxiliary infinitives before a participle, fixed verb phrases, and titles before names. Many other reductions sound elevated, old-fashioned, or poetic.
SHOW: phrase-internal licensed form -> possible loss · pause or free invention -> keep the vowel

[SIDE]
TITLE: Spoken reductions do not all carry the same register
REF: 2.8

Short han and son can occur in informal speech, while reductions such as maggior and amor often belong to elevated or established phrases.
TL: Han detto che son pronti.
EN: They said that they are ready.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Non si può far finta di niente.
EN: One cannot pretend that nothing is happening.
[PAUSE:1000]
The full form remains available in many of these environments. Treat the shorter form as a choice supported by phrase, rhythm, region, and register.
SHOW: aver/far before a complement · signor before a name · han/son informal · literary reductions marked

[CARD]
TITLE: Stress is usually near the end, but it is not fully predictable
REF: 2.9, 2.10

Most Italian words carry primary stress on one of their final three syllables. Penultimate stress is very common, but antepenultimate stress also occurs and often must be learned with the word.
TL: ra-GAZ-zo · TE-le-fo-no · cit-TÀ
EN: boy · telephone · city
[PAUSE:900]
Stress can distinguish otherwise identical spellings.
TL: L'àncora è ancora sul ponte.
EN: The anchor is still on the deck.
[PAUSE:1000]
Suffixes and verb endings provide useful tendencies, not an infallible algorithm. Some words even admit competing stress patterns among native speakers.
SHOW: final three syllables dominate · morphology gives clues · lexical stress still matters
Flip for written accents.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A final stressed vowel requires a written accent
REF: 2.9, 2.12

In a multisyllabic word ending in a stressed vowel, Italian writes the accent.
TL: caffè · città · perché · partirò
EN: coffee · city · because · I will leave
[PAUSE:900]
Accents also distinguish a small group of short forms, such as è from e, sì from si, dà from da, and né from ne.
TL: Sì, è già lì.
EN: Yes, it is already there.
[PAUSE:1000]
The grave accent is broadly safe; conventional closed stressed e often takes an acute accent, as in perché. Capital letters do not cancel the accent.
SHOW: final stressed vowel -> written accent · short homographs -> meaning contrast

[CARD]
TITLE: Secondary stress preserves rhythm and compound structure
REF: 2.11

Long words normally acquire one or more weaker stresses so that prominent syllables do not crowd together or drift too far apart.
TL: caratterizzabile
EN: characterizable
[PAUSE:900]
In a transparent compound, an earlier element keeps a weaker version of the stress it has as an independent word, while the final element carries the primary stress.
TL: MACIna + cafFÈ -> MAcinacafFÈ
EN: grind + coffee -> coffee grinder
[PAUSE:1000]
This rhythm is heard, not normally marked in writing. Build it by listening to complete words rather than stressing every written component equally.
SHOW: one primary peak · weaker rhythmic peaks · compound parts retain structure
Flip from word rhythm to legal line breaks.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Syllable boundaries control hyphenation
REF: 2.14

Every syllable contains a vowel. Between vowel, consonant, vowel, the consonant normally begins the following syllable.
TL: a-mo-re · pa-ta-ta
EN: love · potato
[PAUSE:900]
An initial-looking consonant cluster stays together, while a cluster that cannot begin an Italian word is split.
TL: a-stro · a-pri-le · ter-ra · pal-la
EN: star · April · earth · ball
[PAUSE:1000]
Unstressed glide-like i and u normally remain in their vowel group, as in pie-de and uo-va. Use a hyphen at a line break only on one of these syllable boundaries.
SHOW: V-CV · legal initial cluster stays together · illegal onset splits

[CARD]
TITLE: Punctuation organizes relations, not breath alone
REF: 2.13

Italian uses the full stop, comma, colon, semicolon, and quotation marks broadly as English does. A comma can frame an opening phrase or an insertion, but it should not split an ordinary subject from its verb.
TL: Dopo la riunione, Giulia ha inviato il verbale.
EN: After the meeting, Giulia sent the minutes.
[PAUSE:900]
Commas in a simple list can be lighter than English readers expect, and punctuation choices depend on the structure of the sentence.
TL: Ho comprato pane, latte, frutta e caffè.
EN: I bought bread, milk, fruit, and coffee.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use punctuation to show which words form a unit and how one clause explains, contrasts with, or follows another.
SHOW: comma -> local structure · colon -> announced expansion · semicolon -> related units
Flip for dialogue layout.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A long dash can mark a turn in dialogue
REF: 2.13

Italian publishing often introduces direct speech with a long dash. A further dash may separate the spoken words from a reporting clause.
TL: —Posso entrare?— chiese Luca.
EN: “May I come in?” Luca asked.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: —Certo,— rispose Anna, —la porta è aperta.
EN: “Of course,” Anna replied, “the door is open.”
[PAUSE:1000]
Quotation conventions vary by publisher, so read the punctuation as a layout system rather than as part of the spoken sentence.
SHOW: — speech turn · — reporting clause · publisher style may vary

[CARD]
TITLE: Italian capitalization is more selective than English
REF: 2.15

Proper names take capitals, but days, months, compass points, nationality words, and ordinary language names normally remain lower-case.
TL: Partiremo lunedì 3 marzo verso nord.
EN: We will leave on Monday, March 3, heading north.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Marta è italiana e studia il tedesco.
EN: Marta is Italian and studies German.
[PAUSE:1000]
Classify the word's function before copying English capitalization.
SHOW: proper name -> capital · calendar/direction/nationality/language -> lower-case

[SIDE]
TITLE: Titles usually capitalize only their opening and true names
REF: 2.15

Books, plays, and organization names usually capitalize the first word and any internal proper names, not every important-looking word.
TL: Ho riletto I promessi sposi.
EN: I reread The Betrothed.
[PAUSE:900]
Newspaper and magazine mastheads may preserve additional capitals as part of their established name.
TL: L'articolo è uscito sul Corriere della Sera.
EN: The article appeared in the Corriere della Sera.
[PAUSE:1000]
Formal address may capitalize pronouns such as Lei and La. That courtesy convention is distinct from the ordinary lower-case rules.
TL: Dottoressa Rossi, La ringrazio per la risposta.
EN: Doctor Rossi, thank you for the reply.
SHOW: work title -> first word + names · masthead -> established styling · formal address -> courtesy capital
