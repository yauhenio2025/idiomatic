## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Build the frame first
SVG: de_wortstellung_c01f.svg
REF: 19, 19.1.2

German word order is mobile inside a rigid frame. Case endings identify the roles, while the finite verb fixes the architecture. What moves is usually not a single word but a whole constituent — a complete phrase that does one job in the clause:
TL: Nach einer ungewöhnlich langen Debatte hat der Ausschuss den Entwurf doch noch gebilligt.
EN: After an unusually long debate, the committee approved the draft after all.
[PAUSE:1200]
The opening phrase is one constituent. The finite verb opens a bracket; the participle closes it. Most of the clause lives between those two points.
SHOW: opening field · left bracket · middle field · right bracket · final field
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The middle keeps its logic
REF: 19.1.2, 19.3

Statements, questions and subordinate clauses place the finite verb differently, but the relative order inside the middle field stays broadly the same. Listen to one set of elements survive three frames:
TL: Die Redaktion hat den Absatz gestern sorgfältig geprüft.
EN: The editorial team carefully checked the paragraph yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Hat die Redaktion den Absatz gestern sorgfältig geprüft?
EN: Did the editorial team carefully check the paragraph yesterday?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir wissen, dass die Redaktion den Absatz gestern sorgfältig geprüft hat.
EN: We know that the editorial team carefully checked the paragraph yesterday.
[PAUSE:1200]
Learn the frame first. Then learn what pulls an element left or right inside it.
SHOW: the verb frame changes · the middle-field logic persists

[CARD]
TITLE: Three clause types, three verb positions
SVG: de_wortstellung_c02f.svg
REF: 19.1.1a, 19.1.1b

In a main-clause statement, the finite verb is the second constituent. In a yes-or-no question or a command, it comes first:
TL: Morgen legt der Ausschuss seinen Bericht vor.
EN: Tomorrow the committee will present its report.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Legt der Ausschuss seinen Bericht morgen vor?
EN: Is the committee presenting its report tomorrow?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Legen Sie den Bericht bitte morgen vor!
EN: Please present the report tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1200]
The separable prefix waits at the right edge in all three. The finite part alone determines the clause type.
SHOW: statement = verb second · yes/no question and command = verb first
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Subordinate clauses close with the finite verb
REF: 19.1.1c, 19.1.3b

In a subordinate clause, the conjunction opens the frame and the finite verb closes it:
TL: Die Sitzung wurde vertagt, weil der Ausschuss den Bericht noch nicht vorgelegt hat.
EN: The meeting was adjourned because the committee has not yet presented the report.
[PAUSE:1100]
With a simple verb cluster, the main verb comes before its auxiliary. A double infinitive triggers the advanced exception: the finite auxiliary normally steps in front of both infinitives.
TL: Die Frist wurde verlängert, weil die Fachleute den Entwurf hätten überarbeiten müssen.
EN: The deadline was extended because the specialists would have had to revise the draft.
[PAUSE:1200]
Do not count from the left. Find the finite verb and identify the frame it builds.
SHOW: subordinate clause: conjunction … verb cluster · double infinitive: finite auxiliary first
Ten clause-combining drills reinforce the closing bracket next.

[CARD]
TITLE: One constituent before the finite verb
SVG: de_wortstellung_c03f.svg
REF: 19.2.1a, 19.2.1b

Verb second means second constituent, not second written word. The opening field can hold a subject, an object, an adverbial, a complement, or even a whole clause — but normally only one of them:
TL: Nach der überraschend langen Sitzung hat der Ausschuss den Vorschlag angenommen.
EN: After the surprisingly long meeting, the committee accepted the proposal.
[PAUSE:1000]
That entire opening phrase counts as one unit. An object can occupy the same slot:
TL: Den endgültigen Wortlaut hat die Ministerin erst nach Mitternacht bestätigt.
EN: The minister did not confirm the final wording until after midnight.
[PAUSE:1100]
Everything else moves behind the finite verb.
SHOW: one constituent — however long — fills the opening field
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Topic first, new information later
REF: 19.2.2

The first constituent is a starting point, not automatically the strongest emphasis. German commonly starts with information already active in the conversation and lets the important new material arrive later:
TL: Im Innenhof steht seit heute eine ungewöhnliche Skulptur.
EN: As of today, there is an unusual sculpture in the courtyard.
[PAUSE:1000]
The courtyard is our point of departure; the sculpture is the news. If the sculpture is already our topic, the order changes naturally:
TL: Die ungewöhnliche Skulptur steht seit heute im Innenhof.
EN: The unusual sculpture has been in the courtyard since today.
[PAUSE:1200]
Movement is not free decoration. It tells the listener what is already on the table and what deserves the final stress.
SHOW: opening field = topic · later position = stronger news value
Eight drills now make you rebuild the same message from different starting points.

[CARD]
TITLE: German usually skips the cleft
REF: 19.2.3a, 19.2.3b

English often needs a cleft or a passive to move the desired topic to the front. German can usually move that constituent directly and keep a single main clause:
TL: Erst nach der zweiten Prüfung fiel der Fehler auf.
EN: It was only after the second review that the error was noticed.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Diesen Einwand hat niemand ernst genommen.
EN: It was this objection that nobody took seriously.
[PAUSE:1100]
The German sentences are simpler because case marking preserves the roles and verb second preserves the frame.
SHOW: English reshapes the clause · German moves the topic
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Some openings stand outside the clause
REF: 19.2.1c

An opening comment can sit outside the clause proper. A comma marks the boundary, and the following main clause starts normally:
TL: Mit anderen Worten, wir brauchen einen neuen Zeitplan.
EN: In other words, we need a new timetable.
[PAUSE:900]
An integrated phrase, by contrast, occupies the opening field and is followed immediately by the finite verb:
TL: Meiner Einschätzung nach brauchen wir einen neuen Zeitplan.
EN: In my assessment, we need a new timetable.
[PAUSE:900]
Concessive clauses of the whatever type also stand outside:
TL: Wie sorgfältig sie auch rechnet, das Budget reicht nicht aus.
EN: However carefully she calculates, the budget is not sufficient.
[PAUSE:1200]
The comma is structural: outside comment, then a complete verb-second clause.
SHOW: outside element, topic + finite verb · integrated topic + finite verb
The next block turns English clefts into direct German topics.

[CARD]
TITLE: The middle field has two magnets
REF: 19.3

Inside the bracket, two forces shape the neutral order. Known, light material drifts left; important new material drifts right. At the same time, elements tightly selected by the verb stay near it. A reliable neutral sentence therefore grows from light context toward heavier, verb-bound information:
TL: Die Gutachterin hat dem Ausschuss gestern den überarbeiteten Bericht ausführlich erläutert.
EN: Yesterday the expert explained the revised report to the committee in detail.
[PAUSE:1200]
The noun subject and recipient are early; the manner of explaining is late. This is a default path, not an iron law.
SHOW: left: known and unstressed · right: new and verb-bound
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Context can reverse the default
REF: 19.3, 19.5.1b

Later position creates prominence, so the right order depends on the question being answered. If the time is new, let it arrive late:
TL: Den Bericht hat sie im letzten Moment korrigiert.
EN: She corrected the report at the very last moment.
[PAUSE:900]
If the object is the new information, keep that object later instead:
TL: Im letzten Moment hat sie den entscheidenden Zahlenfehler korrigiert.
EN: At the very last moment, she corrected the crucial numerical error.
[PAUSE:1200]
Both are grammatical. Only the context tells you which information contour is honest.
SHOW: later element = greater prominence · neutral order bends to context
Two broad ordering sets now train the neutral template and its controlled variations.

[CARD]
TITLE: Pronouns sprint to the left
SVG: de_wortstellung_c06f.svg
REF: 19.4.1a, 19.4.1b, 19.4.1c

Personal pronouns normally carry old, unstressed information, so they cluster immediately after the finite verb or the subordinating conjunction. Among them, the neutral order is nominative, accusative, dative:
TL: Gestern hat sie ihn mir endlich vorgestellt.
EN: Yesterday she finally introduced him to me.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Ich wusste nicht, dass sie es ihm längst erklärt hatte.
EN: I did not know that she had explained it to him long ago.
[PAUSE:1100]
With a noun subject and two pronoun objects, the noun subject commonly precedes the pair.
TL: Gestern hat die Kollegin es ihm ausführlich erklärt.
EN: Yesterday the colleague explained it to him in detail.
SHOW: personal pronouns go early · pronoun order: nominative, accusative, dative
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reflexives and demonstratives join the queue
REF: 19.4.1d, 19.4.1e

A reflexive pronoun occupies the same early zone as other personal pronouns:
TL: Gestern hat sich die neue Kollegin beim Vorstand vorgestellt.
EN: Yesterday the new colleague introduced herself to the executive board.
[PAUSE:900]
When two pronouns occur, their cases still control the sequence:
TL: Sie hat es sich ganz anders vorgestellt.
EN: She had imagined it quite differently.
[PAUSE:900]
Personal pronouns also precede demonstrative pronouns, irrespective of case:
TL: Warum hat er mir das verschwiegen?
EN: Why did he conceal that from me?
[PAUSE:1100]
Do not carry the noun order over to pronouns. They form their own compact queue.
SHOW: reflexive = early pronoun · personal pronoun before demonstrative
Fourteen pronoun-order drills follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Full noun phrases prefer nominative, dative, accusative
REF: 19.4.2a, 19.4.2b

When subject and objects are full noun phrases, the neutral sequence is nominative, dative, accusative:
TL: Der Verlag hat der Autorin einen neuen Vertrag angeboten.
EN: The publishing house offered the author a new contract.
[PAUSE:1000]
But information weight can override that default. A familiar accusative object may precede a surprising, indefinite recipient:
TL: Der Verlag hat den bereits erwähnten Vertrag einer bislang unbekannten Autorin angeboten.
EN: The publishing house offered the contract already mentioned to a previously unknown author.
[PAUSE:1200]
The later recipient is the news. The marked order earns its place from the context.
SHOW: noun default: nominative, dative, accusative · newsworthy dative may move right
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A new subject can arrive late
REF: 19.4.2c, 19.2.2d

With events, appearances and passives, an indefinite subject often carries the main news and therefore comes late:
TL: Im Labor ist gestern ein bislang unbekannter Fehler aufgetreten.
EN: An error previously unknown occurred in the laboratory yesterday.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Vor dem Rathaus versammelten sich am Abend mehrere hundert Demonstrierende.
EN: Several hundred demonstrators gathered outside the town hall in the evening.
[PAUSE:1200]
English may need there or a passive to delay the subject. German simply gives another constituent the opening field and lets the subject take the stressed position.
SHOW: event verb + new indefinite subject → subject often late
Ten object-replacement drills now expose the switch from pronoun order to noun order.

[CARD]
TITLE: Adverbials form a flexible hierarchy
SVG: de_wortstellung_c08f.svg
REF: 19.5.1, 19.5.2a, 19.5.2f

For equally weighted adverbials, the full neutral hierarchy is attitude, time, reason, viewpoint, place, manner. Real sentences rarely fill every slot, but the sequence explains more than the familiar three-word mnemonic:
TL: Das Unternehmen hat sich vermutlich im vergangenen Jahr wegen der Energiekrise wirtschaftlich in mehreren Regionen nur langsam erholt.
EN: The company probably recovered only slowly in economic terms in several regions last year because of the energy crisis.
[PAUSE:1300]
Short, unstressed adverbials can move even farther left. A later adverbial receives more stress.
SHOW: attitude · time · reason · viewpoint · place · manner
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Place is not always a place adverbial
REF: 19.5.2g, 19.5.2h

Neutral adverbials usually put place before manner:
TL: Die Pianistin hat gestern im kleinen Saal außergewöhnlich konzentriert gespielt.
EN: Yesterday the pianist played with exceptional concentration in the small hall.
[PAUSE:1000]
But a destination or required location is a verb complement, not a free place adverbial. Its close bond with the verb pulls it behind manner:
TL: Der Kurier ist gestern trotz des Sturms vorsichtig zum Konsulat gefahren.
EN: Yesterday, despite the storm, the courier drove carefully to the consulate.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Akten lagen seit Montag ungeordnet im Archiv.
EN: The files had been lying in disorder in the archive since Monday.
[PAUSE:1200]
Ask whether the phrase merely adds scenery or completes the verb's idea.
SHOW: place adverbial before manner · locative complement after manner

[CARD]
TITLE: Global negation finds the right edge
SVG: de_wortstellung_c09f.svg
REF: 19.6a

For whole-clause negation, the negative normally comes late. It follows noun objects and most adverbials, but precedes manner and the complements most tightly attached to the verb:
TL: Die Sachverständige hat den Bericht gestern nicht gründlich geprüft.
EN: The expert did not examine the report thoroughly yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sie hat dem Ausschuss den Bericht nicht geschickt.
EN: She did not send the report to the committee.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir sind wegen des Unwetters nicht nach Innsbruck gefahren.
EN: We did not travel to Innsbruck because of the storm.
[PAUSE:1200]
Think of a boundary: background and ordinary objects before it; manner, complements and the closing verb after it.
SHOW: objects and background · not · manner and complements · closing verb
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Partial negation stands before its target
REF: 19.6b

When the negation rejects one constituent rather than the entire event, it moves directly in front of that constituent. The contrast tells you its scope:
TL: Nicht die Sachverständige hat den Bericht zurückgezogen, sondern der Minister.
EN: It was not the expert who withdrew the report, but the minister.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Sachverständige hat nicht den Bericht zurückgezogen, sondern nur den Anhang.
EN: The expert did not withdraw the report, but only the appendix.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Sachverständige hat den Bericht nicht gestern zurückgezogen, sondern bereits am Montag.
EN: The expert did not withdraw the report yesterday, but as early as Monday.
[PAUSE:1200]
Position is scope. Put the negative immediately before exactly what you deny.
SHOW: whole event: negative near the right edge · one contrast: negative before its target
Twelve negation items now make you locate that boundary.

[CARD]
TITLE: Complements stay close to the verb
SVG: de_wortstellung_c10f.svg
REF: 19.7.1

Prepositional objects, genitive objects, predicate complements and required place or direction phrases belong at the right edge of the middle field. Their position is comparatively stable because the verb selects them:
TL: Die Kommission hat gestern ausführlich über den Vorschlag beraten.
EN: Yesterday the commission discussed the proposal in detail.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Verhandlungen sind trotz der Einwände überraschend schwierig geblieben.
EN: Despite the objections, the negotiations remained surprisingly difficult.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Fahrer hat den Lastwagen äußerst vorsichtig in die Halle gelenkt.
EN: The driver steered the lorry into the hall with extreme care.
[PAUSE:1200]
The complement completes the verb, so it follows even a manner phrase.
SHOW: manner · selected complement · closing verb
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Fixed noun phrases behave like verb parts
REF: 19.7.2

In a fixed verb expression, the noun portion clings to the verb almost like a separable prefix. It therefore stays at the far right of the middle field:
TL: Die neue Regelung ist nach monatelangen Verhandlungen endlich in Kraft getreten.
EN: After months of negotiations, the new regulation finally came into force.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Sprecherin hat die Öffentlichkeit gestern über den Beschluss in Kenntnis gesetzt.
EN: Yesterday the spokesperson informed the public about the decision.
[PAUSE:1000]
The same rightward pull explains why a prepositional object follows freer scene-setting material:
TL: Die Delegation hat im Foyer zwei Stunden lang auf die Ministerin gewartet.
EN: The delegation waited for the minister in the foyer for two hours.
[PAUSE:1200]
Treat the verb and its selected complement as a semantic unit, even when other words stand between them.
SHOW: free context first · verb-bound complement last
Twelve prepositional-object drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The sentence can continue after the bracket
SVG: de_wortstellung_c11f.svg
REF: 19.8, 19.8.1

The closing verb is normally the end of the clause, but a final field can follow it. This is especially useful for a subordinate or infinitive clause that would make the middle field cumbersome:
TL: Wir haben den Verdacht nicht ausräumen können, dass interne Daten weitergegeben wurden.
EN: We have not been able to dispel the suspicion that internal data were passed on.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Behörde hat beschlossen, den Bescheid vollständig zu überarbeiten.
EN: The authority decided to revise the decision completely.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Gestern wurde ein Gutachten veröffentlicht, das sämtliche Annahmen infrage stellt.
EN: Yesterday a report was published that calls every assumption into question.
[PAUSE:1200]
Close one frame before opening the next. That is how German avoids a nest of clauses inside clauses.
SHOW: complete the bracket · then add the heavy clause
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Extraposition has register limits
REF: 19.8.2, 19.8.3

Comparative phrases commonly stand after the closing bracket:
TL: Die Sitzung hat deutlich länger gedauert als die gestrige Besprechung.
EN: The meeting lasted considerably longer than yesterday's discussion.
[PAUSE:1000]
Long prepositional phrases and some prepositional objects can also move out, especially in speech or when they feel like an afterthought:
TL: Sie hat sich lange geärgert über die völlig unnötige Verzögerung.
EN: She was annoyed for a long time about the completely unnecessary delay.
[PAUSE:1000]
Formal prose is more selective. Keep ordinary subjects, direct and indirect objects, and required locations inside the bracket unless exceptional length forces a different design.
TL: In der schriftlichen Fassung hat sie sich lange über die völlig unnötige Verzögerung geärgert.
EN: In the written version, she was annoyed for a long time about the completely unnecessary delay.
[PAUSE:1200]
Use the final field to release structural pressure, not as a general dumping ground.
SHOW: clauses and comparisons: natural outside · ordinary objects: keep inside
