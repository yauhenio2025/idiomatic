## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Mood shows how a proposition enters the sentence
REF: 20.1.1, 20.1.3

The indicative normally presents an event as information. The subjunctive places that event inside a stance such as desire, doubt, evaluation, emotion, purpose, or an outcome that remains open.
TL: Sei que Ana está em casa. · Espero que Ana esteja em casa.
EN: I know Ana is at home. · I hope Ana is at home.
[PAUSE:900]
The second sentence does not claim that Ana is absent or that her presence is impossible. It simply presents her being at home as the content of a hope rather than as an asserted fact.
TL: É certo que ele vem. · É possível que ele venha.
EN: It is certain that he is coming. · It is possible that he may come.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: asserted information -> indicative · event inside a stance or open outcome -> subjunctive
Flip for the tense system that operates inside this mood.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Portuguese has three simple subjunctive tenses
REF: 20.1.2

The present subjunctive covers events viewed from a present or forward-looking stance. The imperfect handles a past viewpoint and remote or unreal conditions. The future subjunctive marks an event still pending in a future time or condition.
TL: que ela fale · se ela falasse · quando ela falar
EN: that she speak · if she spoke · when she speaks
[PAUSE:900]
Compound forms add ter in the relevant subjunctive tense plus an invariable participle. They place the subordinate event before its reference point.
TL: que ela tenha falado · se ela tivesse falado · quando ela tiver falado
EN: that she has spoken · if she had spoken · when she has spoken
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: fale = present · falasse = imperfect · falar = future · ter + participle = earlier event

[CARD]
TITLE: Build the present subjunctive from the eu present
REF: 15.6, 20.1.2

Start from the first-person singular present indicative, remove final -o, and add the opposite vowel: -e endings for -ar verbs and -a endings for -er and -ir verbs.
TL: falo -> fale · como -> coma · decido -> decida
EN: I speak -> that I speak · I eat -> that I eat · I decide -> that I decide
[PAUSE:900]
This route carries stem changes and irregular first-person stems into the whole paradigm. Learn the high-frequency irregular forms as working anchors.
TL: faço -> faça · digo -> diga · tenho -> tenha · sou -> seja · vou -> vá
EN: that I do · that I say · that I have · that I be · that I go
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: eu present minus -o + opposite-vowel endings · irregular eu stem often predicts the subjunctive
Flip to distribute the endings across persons.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Present-subjunctive endings repeat a compact pattern
REF: 15.6, 20.1.2

For an -ar verb, use -e, -e, -emos, -em with the Brazilian person set eu, você/ele/ela, nós, vocês/eles/elas. For -er and -ir verbs, use -a, -a, -amos, -am.
TL: que eu fale · que você fale · que nós falemos · que eles falem
EN: that I speak · that you speak · that we speak · that they speak
[PAUSE:850]
TL: que eu coma · que nós comamos · que eles comam · que eu decida · que eles decidam
EN: that I eat · that we eat · that they eat · that I decide · that they decide
[PAUSE:950]
Keep que when it introduces the subordinate clause. English can omit that, but ordinary Portuguese cannot omit que here.
SHOW: -ar -> e series · -er/-ir -> a series · que remains visible

[CARD]
TITLE: Form the imperfect subjunctive from the preterite eles form
REF: 15.7, 20.1.2

Take the third-person plural preterite, remove -ram, and add -sse, -sse, -ssemos, or -ssem for the common Brazilian persons. This rule preserves irregular preterite stems.
TL: falaram -> falasse · comeram -> comesse · decidiram -> decidisse
EN: if I spoke · if I ate · if I decided
[PAUSE:900]
TL: fizeram -> fizesse · tiveram -> tivesse · foram -> fosse · puderam -> pudesse
EN: if I did · if I had · if I went or were · if I could
[PAUSE:950]
The nós form bears an accent before -ssemos. Do not build this tense from the infinitive when the preterite stem is irregular.
SHOW: preterite eles minus -ram + sse endings · fizeram -> fizéssemos
Flip to connect this form with a past viewpoint.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A past main verb normally calls for the imperfect subjunctive
REF: 20.2.1

When a present, future, or present-perfect main verb governs a subjunctive clause, Portuguese normally uses the present subjunctive for a simultaneous or later event.
TL: Ela quer que eu ajude. · Ela vai pedir que eu ajude.
EN: She wants me to help. · She will ask me to help.
[PAUSE:850]
Move the governing viewpoint into the imperfect, preterite, conditional, or pluperfect, and the subordinate verb normally moves to the imperfect subjunctive.
TL: Ela queria que eu ajudasse. · Ela pediu que eu ajudasse.
EN: She wanted me to help. · She asked me to help.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: present/future viewpoint -> present subjunctive · past/conditional viewpoint -> imperfect subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Compound subjunctives mark an event as already complete
REF: 20.1.2, 20.2.2

Use tenha plus a participle when an earlier subordinate event is viewed from the present. Use tivesse plus a participle when that earlier event is viewed from a past or remote standpoint.
TL: Espero que ela tenha recebido a mensagem. · Eu esperava que ela tivesse recebido a mensagem.
EN: I hope she received the message. · I hoped she had received the message.
[PAUSE:900]
The participle remains unchanged after ter. Tense sequence is a strong default, but actual time meaning wins when the ordinary pairing would misrepresent the event.
TL: Duvido que eles tivessem vencido sem ajuda.
EN: I doubt they would have won without help.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: present viewpoint + earlier event -> tenha feito · past/remote viewpoint + earlier event -> tivesse feito
Flip for the main families of present and imperfect triggers.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Evaluation, influence, emotion, and doubt license the subjunctive
REF: 20.3.1, 20.3.2, 20.3.3, 20.3.4

Impersonal judgements and expressions of possibility frame a proposition instead of simply asserting it. Verbs of wanting, requesting, permitting, preventing, and recommending do the same when another participant performs the subordinate action.
TL: É importante que todos participem. · Recomendo que você espere.
EN: It is important that everyone participate. · I recommend that you wait.
[PAUSE:850]
Emotional reactions, doubt, denial, and a genuinely negated belief also take the subjunctive. An affirmative belief normally keeps the indicative because it puts the proposition forward.
TL: Lamento que ela tenha saído. · Não acredito que seja tarde. · Acho que é tarde.
EN: I am sorry she left. · I do not believe it is late. · I think it is late.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: value / influence / reaction / rejected belief -> subjunctive · affirmed belief -> indicative

[CARD]
TITLE: Form the future subjunctive from the preterite eles form
REF: 15.8, 20.2.4

Again start with the third-person plural preterite and remove -ram. The remaining form serves eu and você/ele/ela; add -mos for nós and -em for vocês/eles/elas.
TL: falaram -> quando eu falar · quando nós falarmos · quando eles falarem
EN: when I speak · when we speak · when they speak
[PAUSE:900]
Irregular preterites produce irregular future-subjunctive stems. This is what distinguishes many forms from the personal infinitive.
TL: fizeram -> quando fizer · tiveram -> se tiver · foram -> quando for · souberam -> quando souber
EN: when someone does · if someone has · when someone goes or is · when someone knows
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: preterite eles minus -ram -> future-subjunctive base · fizeram -> fizer, not fazer
Flip to see where this future form belongs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use the future subjunctive for a pending future event
REF: 20.4.1, 20.4.2, 20.4.3

After quando, assim que, logo que, enquanto, depois que, and sempre que, choose the future subjunctive when the subordinate event has not happened yet and the main clause points forward.
TL: Eu te aviso quando souber. · Vamos sair assim que a chuva parar.
EN: I will let you know when I find out. · We will leave as soon as the rain stops.
[PAUSE:850]
Future and open-ended relatives use it too, including quem, o que, and onde with an ever-like meaning.
TL: Pegue o ônibus que chegar primeiro. · Pode sentar onde quiser.
EN: Take whichever bus arrives first. · You may sit wherever you like.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: future reference + event still pending/open -> future subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Time clauses contrast pending events with facts
REF: 20.4.1, 20.4.5

The conjunction alone does not choose the mood. A habitual, present, or completed event is presented with an indicative tense.
TL: Sempre que posso, visito minha avó. · Ela ligou assim que chegou.
EN: Whenever I can, I visit my grandmother. · She called as soon as she arrived.
[PAUSE:850]
The same conjunction takes the future subjunctive for an event pending from the present. Viewed from a past narrative, a then-pending event normally takes the imperfect subjunctive.
TL: Ela ligará assim que chegar. · Disse que ligaria assim que chegasse.
EN: She will call as soon as she arrives. · She said she would call as soon as she arrived.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: fact/habit -> indicative · pending from now -> future subjunctive · pending from past -> imperfect subjunctive
Flip for open and identified referents.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Relative clauses reveal whether a referent is established
REF: 20.3.6, 20.4.2

Use an indicative relative clause for a specific person or thing whose relevant property is asserted. Use the present or imperfect subjunctive when the sought or described referent may not exist.
TL: Conheço uma tradutora que fala japonês. · Procuro uma tradutora que fale japonês.
EN: I know a translator who speaks Japanese. · I am looking for a translator who speaks Japanese.
[PAUSE:850]
For an open future choice or outcome, use the future subjunctive. Time viewpoint, existence, and specificity work together.
TL: Contrataremos a pessoa que tiver mais experiência.
EN: We will hire whoever has the most experience.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: identified fact -> indicative · indefinite or nonexistent target -> present/imperfect subjunctive · open future -> future subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Purpose, concession, and safeguards select the subjunctive
REF: 20.3.7, 20.3.8

Learn a fim de que, a menos que, a não ser que, antes que, caso, contanto que, embora, mesmo que, para que, and sem que as complete subjunctive-selecting links.
TL: Repita para que todos entendam. · Embora seja tarde, vamos continuar.
EN: Repeat it so that everyone understands. · Although it is late, we will continue.
[PAUSE:850]
Até que and de modo que take the subjunctive when their event is still unfulfilled or intended. They take the indicative when they report an actual event or achieved result.
TL: Espere até que eu volte. · Falou baixo, de modo que ninguém ouviu.
EN: Wait until I return. · He spoke quietly, so nobody heard.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: purpose/concession/condition/unfulfilled limit -> subjunctive · achieved result -> indicative
Flip for phrases that intensify the open-ended meaning.

[SIDE]
TITLE: However and wherever keep the outcome noncommittal
REF: 20.3.9, 20.3.10, 20.7

Por mais plus an adjective, adverb, or clause uses the subjunctive to mean however or no matter how. Interrogatives followed by quer que create whoever, whatever, or wherever expressions.
TL: Por mais difícil que seja, vou tentar. · Onde quer que esteja, mande notícias.
EN: However difficult it may be, I will try. · Wherever you are, send news.
[PAUSE:850]
Portuguese also pairs present and future subjunctive forms in compact fixed expressions. Treat the whole expression as a unit.
TL: Seja como for. · Venha o que vier. · Custe o que custar.
EN: Be that as it may. · Come what may. · Whatever the cost.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: por mais ... que · quem/o que/onde quer que · seja como for

[CARD]
TITLE: Se distinguishes open, unreal, and factual conditions
REF: 20.5.1, 20.5.2, 20.5.5, 20.5.6

Use the future subjunctive after conditional se for a possible future event. Use the imperfect subjunctive for a remote or unreal present condition, normally paired with the conditional or a conversational imperfect.
TL: Se tiver tempo, eu ligo. · Se tivesse tempo, eu ligaria.
EN: If I have time, I will call. · If I had time, I would call.
[PAUSE:850]
Use an indicative tense when se introduces a condition treated as a fact. Se meaning whether introduces an indirect question and also takes the ordinary indicative sequence.
TL: Se você está cansado, descanse. · Não sei se ele vem.
EN: If you are tired, rest. · I do not know whether he is coming.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: possible future if -> future subjunctive · unreal if -> imperfect subjunctive · factual if / whether -> indicative
Flip to look backward at an impossible condition.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Tivesse feito marks an unreal earlier condition
REF: 20.5.3, 20.5.4

Use the pluperfect subjunctive in a se clause when the condition did not happen in the past. The result commonly uses the conditional perfect.
TL: Se eles tivessem avisado, teríamos esperado.
EN: If they had warned us, we would have waited.
[PAUSE:850]
Como se always sets up an unreal comparison and therefore takes the imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive, regardless of the tense in the main clause.
TL: Ele fala como se soubesse tudo. · Ela reagiu como se tivesse visto um fantasma.
EN: He talks as if he knew everything. · She reacted as if she had seen a ghost.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: unreal past condition -> se + tivesse feito · unreal comparison -> como se + subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: The subjunctive can stand in wishes and main-clause expressions
REF: 20.6.1, 20.6.2, 20.6.4, 20.6.5

Formal imperatives use present-subjunctive forms. Third-person wishes and tomara que also place the desired event in the present subjunctive.
TL: Entre, por favor. · Que tudo dê certo! · Tomara que não chova!
EN: Come in, please. · May everything work out! · Let us hope it does not rain!
[PAUSE:850]
Talvez takes a subjunctive form, with the tense chosen by meaning. A present form covers a current or future possibility; an imperfect form can look into the past or present a remote possibility.
TL: Talvez ela esteja ocupada. · Talvez fosse diferente naquela época.
EN: Perhaps she is busy. · Perhaps it was different at that time.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: formal command / wish / tomara que / talvez -> subjunctive
Flip for the spoken alternatives that keep the system natural.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Speech often chooses a personal infinitive or a factual indicative
REF: 20.8, 20.9

When a subordinate actor can be expressed clearly, spoken Brazilian Portuguese often prefers a personal infinitive to a more formal subjunctive clause.
TL: É importante que vocês participem. -> É importante vocês participarem.
EN: It is important that you participate. -> It is important for you to participate.
[PAUSE:850]
With reactions to an unquestioned past or present fact, colloquial speech may also use an indicative where formal grammar favors a subjunctive. Recognize the register contrast without treating the formal form as doubt.
TL: Que bom que você está aqui! · Que bom que você esteja aqui!
EN: It is great that you are here!
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: formal framing -> subjunctive · clear spoken subject -> personal infinitive · colloquial factual reaction -> often indicative

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose mood and tense in five passes
REF: 20.2, 20.3, 20.4, 20.5

First ask whether the clause is asserted information or sits inside a stance. Second identify the construction: que clause, time link, relative clause, condition, wish, or fixed expression.
[PAUSE:850]
Third locate the viewpoint in present, future, or past time. Fourth decide whether the subordinate event is simultaneous, already complete, or still pending. Fifth check whether an infinitive or an indicative is the natural register choice.
TL: Quero que venha. · Queria que viesse. · Avisarei quando vier. · Duvido que tenha vindo.
EN: I want you to come. · I wanted you to come. · I will let you know when they come. · I doubt they came.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: assertion/stance -> construction -> viewpoint -> event time -> register
Flip for the compact decision map.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Let the construction narrow the tense choice
REF: 20.2.1, 20.2.2, 20.4.5, 20.5

After a present desire, evaluation, emotion, or doubt, start with the present subjunctive; use tenha feito for an earlier event. After a past governing verb, start with the imperfect; use tivesse feito for an earlier event.
[PAUSE:850]
For a future time clause, open relative, or possible future se condition, use the future subjunctive. For an unreal se condition or como se comparison, use the imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive.
[PAUSE:850]
Finally test the alternatives: an asserted fact calls for the indicative, and a same-subject or conversationally compact construction may call for the infinitive.
TL: É certo que vem. · É possível que venha. · Quando vier, conversamos. · Se viesse, conversaríamos.
EN: It is certain they are coming. · They may come. · When they come, we will talk. · If they came, we would talk.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: present stance -> fale/tenha falado · past/remote -> falasse/tivesse falado · open future -> falar
