## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A French verb form has an architecture
REF: 7.1

A simple form packages a stem and an ending into one word. The ending locates the form in a person, number, tense, and mood; the stem identifies the verb and may also signal a tense family.
TL: Nous donnions une réponse précise.
EN: We were giving a precise answer.
[PAUSE:900]
In donnions, donn is the stem and ions is the ending. Learn to split a form this way before trying to name or produce it.
TL: Ils finiront le travail demain.
EN: They will finish the work tomorrow.
SHOW: donn-ions · finir-ont → stem + ending
Flip for compound forms built from several words.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Compound forms add an auxiliary before a past participle
REF: 7.1, 7.6.1, 7.6.2

A compound tense combines a form of avoir or être with a past participle. The auxiliary carries person, number, tense, and mood; the participle supplies the lexical verb.
TL: Elle a répondu; ils étaient partis.
EN: She answered; they had left.
[PAUSE:900]
To name a compound form, identify the auxiliary first. Present auxiliary plus participle gives the compound past; imperfect auxiliary plus participle gives the pluperfect; future auxiliary plus participle gives the compound future.
TL: Nous avions compris; vous aurez terminé.
EN: We had understood; you will have finished.
SHOW: auxiliary form + past participle → compound tense

[CARD]
TITLE: Four infinitive families organize the regular paradigms
REF: 7.2, 7.2.1

The largest family ends in er. The ir family splits in two: finir-type verbs add iss in parts of the paradigm, while dormir-type verbs do not. Other regular patterns are organized around re and oir infinitives.
TL: Nous parlons, nous finissons, nous dormons, nous vendons, nous recevons.
EN: We speak, finish, sleep, sell, and receive.
[PAUSE:900]
The present nous form is a useful diagnostic: finissons reveals the iss family, while dormons shows the shorter dormir stem. Truly irregular verbs must be learned from their own principal forms.
SHOW: -er · -ir with -iss- · -ir without -iss- · -re · -oir
Flip for the difference between a paradigm and a productive rule.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use a generator only inside the pattern it describes
REF: 7.2.1, 7.3

Many regular forms can be generated from a reliable anchor such as the infinitive, the present nous form, or a past participle. That is more efficient than memorizing every cell separately.
TL: finissons → finiss-; recevons → recev-; vendu → vend-.
EN: The anchor reveals the stem used by a particular form family.
[PAUSE:900]
An irregular paradigm can change stems at several points. For those verbs, retrieve a small set of anchors and notice which later forms reuse each one.
TL: je viens, nous venons, ils viennent, je viendrai.
EN: I come, we come, they come, I will come.
SHOW: identify family → choose anchor → attach endings → verify irregulars

[CARD]
TITLE: Present endings depend on the conjugation family
REF: 7.3.1, 7.6.3, 7.6.4, 7.6.6

Regular er verbs use e, es, e, ons, ez, ent. Most finir-type ir verbs use is, is, it, issons, issez, issent. Regular re verbs use s, s, no written ending, ons, ez, ent.
TL: je parle, tu finis, elle vend, nous parlons, vous finissez, ils vendent.
EN: I speak, you finish, she sells, we speak, you finish, they sell.
[PAUSE:900]
Several written endings are silent, so sound alone does not identify the person. Read the subject and the spelling as one retrieval unit.
SHOW: -er: e/es/e/ons/ez/ent · finir: is/is/it/issons/issez/issent · -re: s/s/∅/ons/ez/ent
Flip for the shorter stems of dormir-type verbs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The plural anchors expose many present-tense stem changes
REF: 7.6.4, 7.6.5, 7.6.7

Finir-type verbs retain iss in the plural and in ils: nous finissons, vous finissez, ils finissent. Dormir-type verbs use a shorter singular stem but restore a consonant in the plural.
TL: je dors, tu dors, il dort; nous dormons, vous dormez, ils dorment.
EN: I sleep, you sleep, he sleeps; we sleep, you sleep, they sleep.
[PAUSE:900]
Oir verbs often alternate too. Store the present as a set of anchors instead of forcing it through the er pattern.
TL: je reçois, nous recevons, ils reçoivent.
EN: I receive, we receive, they receive.
SHOW: singular anchor · nous/vous anchor · ils/elles anchor

[CARD]
TITLE: The imperfect starts from the present nous form
REF: 7.3.2

Take the present nous form, remove ons, and add ais, ais, ait, ions, iez, aient. This generator works across the regular conjugation families and preserves spelling adjustments already visible in the nous stem.
TL: nous finissons → je finissais; nous recevons → ils recevaient.
EN: We finish becomes I was finishing; we receive becomes they were receiving.
[PAUSE:900]
The same method handles verbs such as commencer and partager: nous commençons gives commençais, while nous partageons gives partageais.
TL: Nous partagions le bureau et nous commencions tôt.
EN: We shared the office and started early.
SHOW: present nous − ons + ais/ais/ait/ions/iez/aient
Twelve clean contextual imperfect drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Être is the one essential imperfect exception
REF: 7.3.2, 7.6.2

The present nous form sommes does not generate the imperfect. Être instead uses the stem ét with the normal imperfect endings.
TL: j'étais, tu étais, il était, nous étions, vous étiez, ils étaient.
EN: I was, you were, he was, we were, you were, they were.
[PAUSE:900]
Compound pluperfect forms place this imperfect auxiliary before the participle. Recognizing the auxiliary lets you name the whole form.
TL: Nous avions compris; elles étaient arrivées.
EN: We had understood; they had arrived.
SHOW: être → ét- + imperfect endings · imperfect auxiliary + participle → pluperfect

[CARD]
TITLE: The simple past uses three main ending families
REF: 7.3.3

Most er verbs use ai, as, a, âmes, âtes, èrent. Many ir and re verbs use is, is, it, îmes, îtes, irent. Oir verbs commonly use us, us, ut, ûmes, ûtes, urent.
TL: elle parla, nous finîmes, ils vendirent, vous reçûtes.
EN: She spoke, we finished, they sold, you received.
[PAUSE:900]
For regular er verbs, start from the present nous stem. For many ir, re, and oir verbs, the past participle points to the simple-past stem: fini gives fin, vendu gives vend, and reçu gives reç.
SHOW: -er → a-series · -ir/-re → i-series · -oir → u-series
Twelve clean contextual simple-past drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Accent marks distinguish the plural simple-past forms
REF: 7.3.3, 7.6.1, 7.6.2

The nous and vous endings carry a circumflex in the simple past: âmes and âtes, îmes and îtes, or ûmes and ûtes. Do not transfer that accent to the third-person singular.
TL: nous parlâmes, vous finîtes, nous reçûmes; il parla, elle finit, on reçut.
EN: We spoke, you finished, we received; he spoke, she finished, one received.
[PAUSE:900]
Avoir and être use the u-series with their own stems: eus, eut, eûmes and fus, fut, fûmes. These forms also become auxiliaries in literary compound paradigms.
SHOW: nous/vous → â/î/û · singular il/elle → a/it/ut

[CARD]
TITLE: Future and conditional share one stem
REF: 7.3.4

Regular er and ir verbs use the infinitive as the stem. Re verbs drop the final e. Add ai, as, a, ons, ez, ont for the future, or the imperfect endings for the conditional.
TL: je parlerai, nous finirons, elle vendra; je parlerais, nous finirions, elle vendrait.
EN: I will speak, we will finish, she will sell; I would speak, we would finish, she would sell.
[PAUSE:900]
The endings reveal the mood: future ai contrasts with conditional ais, and future ons contrasts with conditional ions.
SHOW: future stem + ai/as/a/ons/ez/ont · same stem + ais/ais/ait/ions/iez/aient
Flip for high-value irregular future stems.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Irregular future stems still take regular endings
REF: 7.3.4, 7.6.1, 7.6.2, 7.6.8

Once an irregular future stem is known, both the future and conditional become predictable. Avoir uses aur, être uses ser, aller uses ir, faire uses fer, and venir uses viendr.
TL: nous aurons, vous seriez, j'irai, elle ferait, ils viendront.
EN: We will have, you would be, I will go, she would do, they will come.
[PAUSE:900]
Learn the stem with one future form, then switch ending sets when the conditional is required.
TL: il viendra demain; il viendrait si tu l'invitais.
EN: He will come tomorrow; he would come if you invited him.
SHOW: aur-/ser-/ir-/fer-/viendr- + future or conditional endings

[CARD]
TITLE: The present subjunctive starts from a present plural anchor
REF: 7.3.5

For a regular paradigm, start from the present ils form, remove ent, and attach e, es, e, ions, iez, ent. The result is normally introduced by que when the form is named or practised.
TL: ils finissent → que je finisse; ils vendent → qu'elle vende.
EN: They finish becomes that I finish; they sell becomes that she sell.
[PAUSE:900]
Stem-changing verbs may use the present nous stem for subjunctive nous and vous. Compare ils reçoivent and nous recevons.
TL: qu'ils reçoivent, mais que nous recevions.
EN: That they receive, but that we receive.
SHOW: ils − ent → singular/ils stem · present nous stem may govern nous/vous
Flip for the literary imperfect subjunctive.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The imperfect subjunctive grows from the simple past
REF: 7.3.6

Take the je form of the simple past, remove its last letter, and add sse, sses, a circumflexed t, ssions, ssiez, ssent.
TL: je donnai → que je donnasse; je finis → que nous finissions; je reçus → qu'il reçût.
EN: The simple-past anchor generates the corresponding imperfect subjunctive.
[PAUSE:900]
The third-person singular is visually distinctive: qu'il donnât, qu'elle finît, qu'on reçût. Modern speech rarely uses this paradigm, but form identification still matters in literary reading.
SHOW: je simple past − final letter + sse/sses/ˆt/ssions/ssiez/ssent

[CARD]
TITLE: The imperative borrows three present-tense forms
REF: 7.3.7, 11.5.1

Use the present forms for tu, nous, and vous, then omit the subjects. An imperative ending in es or as normally drops its final s.
TL: tu parles, nous parlons, vous parlez → parle, parlons, parlez.
EN: Speak, let's speak, speak.
[PAUSE:900]
The s returns before y or en to keep the pronunciation link. Other present forms keep their final consonant.
TL: Va au bureau, mais vas-y maintenant; parle du problème, mais parles-en calmement.
EN: Go to the office, but go there now; talk about the problem, but talk about it calmly.
SHOW: present tu/nous/vous − subjects · -es/-as loses s · before y/en restore s
Flip for the four irregular imperative anchors.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Être, avoir, savoir and vouloir have special imperatives
REF: 7.3.7

Être gives sois, soyons, soyez. Avoir gives aie, ayons, ayez. Savoir gives sache, sachons, sachez. Vouloir supplies veuille and veuillez in polite requests.
TL: Soyez prudents, ayez confiance et sachez attendre.
EN: Be careful, have confidence, and know how to wait.
[PAUSE:900]
Retrieve these as complete three-form sets. Their present-tense forms do not mechanically generate the imperative.
TL: Veuillez fermer la porte.
EN: Please close the door.
SHOW: sois/soyons/soyez · aie/ayons/ayez · sache/sachons/sachez · veuillez

[CARD]
TITLE: Avoir supplies both lexical forms and auxiliary forms
REF: 7.6.1

Anchor the present as ai, as, a, avons, avez, ont and the imperfect with av. The future and conditional use aur, while the simple past and imperfect subjunctive use the eu/euss family.
TL: j'ai, nous avions, vous aurez, ils auraient, elle eut, que nous eussions.
EN: I have, we had, you will have, they would have, she had, that we had.
[PAUSE:900]
When avoir is followed by eu, name the entire compound form from the first word: ai eu is compound past, avais eu is pluperfect, aurai eu is compound future, and aurais eu is compound conditional.
SHOW: av- → imperfect · aur- → future/conditional · eu-/euss- → literary forms

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mood markers separate aie, eus and eusse
REF: 7.6.1

Present subjunctive forms are aie, aies, ait, ayons, ayez, aient. The simple past uses eus, eus, eut, eûmes, eûtes, eurent; the imperfect subjunctive uses eusse, eusses, eût, eussions, eussiez, eussent.
TL: Bien que tu aies le temps, tu eus peur qu'il eût raison.
EN: Although you have time, you feared that he might be right.
[PAUSE:900]
Accents and surrounding words are evidence: que points toward a subjunctive reading, while a following eu signals a compound form.
SHOW: present subjunctive: ai-/ay- · simple past: eu-/eur- · imperfect subjunctive: euss-/eût

[CARD]
TITLE: Être rotates through four high-value stems
REF: 7.6.2

The present must be learned as suis, es, est, sommes, êtes, sont. The imperfect uses ét, the future and conditional use ser, and the simple past uses fu.
TL: je suis, vous étiez, elle sera, nous serions, ils furent.
EN: I am, you were, she will be, we would be, they were.
[PAUSE:900]
The present subjunctive uses soi for the singular and third-person plural, but soy for nous and vous. The imperfect subjunctive uses fuss, with fût in the third-person singular.
TL: qu'il soit, que nous soyons, qu'elles soient; que vous fussiez.
EN: That he be, that we be, that they be; that you were.
SHOW: present set · ét- · ser- · fu-/fuss- · soi-/soy-
Fourteen clean être form-identification and production drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Être forms its compounds with avoir plus été
REF: 7.6.2

Although être is an auxiliary for other verbs, its own compound forms use avoir. Present avoir plus été gives the compound past; imperfect avoir plus été gives the pluperfect.
TL: ils ont été patients; vous aviez été prévenus.
EN: They were patient; you had been warned.
[PAUSE:900]
The same architecture continues through other tenses and moods: j'aurai été is compound future, j'aurais été compound conditional, and que j'aie été compound past subjunctive.
SHOW: form of avoir + été → compound form of être

[CARD]
TITLE: Stem spelling protects pronunciation
REF: 7.4, 7.5

Many er verbs alter the written stem before endings that begin with a silent e. Appeler and jeter double a consonant; acheter and mener change an unstressed e to è; employer changes y to i.
TL: j'appelle, je jette, j'achète, je mène, j'emploie.
EN: I call, throw, buy, lead, and employ.
[PAUSE:900]
In the spelling convention used by the source, an é-changing verb such as espérer has j'espère in the present but preserves é in j'espérerai. Verbs whose stems already end in a vowel keep both vowels visible.
TL: nous créons, nous riions.
EN: We create; we were laughing.
SHOW: silent-e environment → double consonant, è, or y→i · preserve required vowel sequences
Flip for c and g before pronounced vowel endings.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Cedilla and silent e keep c and g soft
REF: 7.5

A soft c becomes ç before an ending beginning with a, o, or u. A soft g inserts e before an ending beginning with a or o.
TL: nous commençons, je commençais; nous partageons, je partageais.
EN: We begin, I was beginning; we share, I was sharing.
[PAUSE:900]
These are spelling repairs, not new tense endings. Generate the form normally, inspect the stem-ending boundary, and then preserve the intended consonant sound.
TL: nous reçûmes la lettre et nous partageâmes la nouvelle.
EN: We received the letter and shared the news.
SHOW: soft c + a/o/u → ç · soft g + a/o → ge
