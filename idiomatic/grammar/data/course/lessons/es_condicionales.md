## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Read a conditional as two clauses
REF: 29.1

A conditional links a condition to its consequence. Find the clause introduced by si, then find the result clause.
TL: Si termina la reunión pronto, cenaremos juntos.
EN: If the meeting ends early, we will have dinner together.
[PAUSE:900]
The order can reverse without changing the two jobs.
TL: Cenaremos juntos si termina la reunión pronto.
EN: We will have dinner together if the meeting ends early.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: si-clause = condition · other clause = consequence
Flip to classify the speaker's stance.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Time and stance select the pattern
REF: 29.1, 29.2, 29.3, 29.4

An open condition treats the outcome as genuinely possible. A remote condition makes it less likely or contrary to present fact. An unfulfilled condition looks back at a past possibility that did not happen.
TL: Si llega, hablamos. Si llegara, hablaríamos. Si hubiera llegado, habríamos hablado.
EN: If she arrives, we will talk. If she arrived, we would talk. If she had arrived, we would have talked.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not translate one verb at a time. Decide first whether the condition is open, remote, or already unfulfilled.
SHOW: open → indicative · remote → imperfect subjunctive · unfulfilled → pluperfect subjunctive
Flip for open conditions.

[CARD]
TITLE: Open conditions keep si in the indicative
REF: 29.2, 29.8.1

When the condition is a real possibility, use an indicative tense after si. For a future result, Spanish normally puts the present indicative in the si-clause.
TL: Si encuentras las llaves, me llamarás.
EN: If you find the keys, you will call me.
[PAUSE:900]
The result may instead be present or imperative.
TL: Si tienes frío, cierra la ventana.
EN: If you are cold, close the window.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: si + present · result = present, future, or command
Flip for conditions about facts in the past.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A past indicative can still describe an open fact
REF: 29.2

An indicative past after si can ask whether something actually happened or report a condition from an earlier viewpoint.
TL: Si ya enviaron el contrato, mañana tendremos respuesta.
EN: If they already sent the contract, we will have an answer tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
In reported speech, imperfect indicatives can preserve an originally open condition.
TL: Dijo que si llovía, se quedaría en casa.
EN: She said that if it rained, she would stay home.
[PAUSE:1000]
This is not the default pattern for an unreal present hypothesis.
SHOW: uncertain past fact → indicative · reported open condition → imperfect indicative
Eleven clean open-condition completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Remote conditions move the si-clause into the subjunctive
REF: 29.3

For a less likely future or a present situation contrary to fact, use the imperfect subjunctive after si.
TL: Si pagáramos hoy, costaría menos.
EN: If we paid today, it would cost less.
[PAUSE:900]
The usual consequence is the conditional.
TL: Si yo fuera rico, compraría aquella casa.
EN: If I were rich, I would buy that house.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: si + imperfect subjunctive → conditional
Flip to compare the two imperfect-subjunctive endings.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The ra and se forms carry the same conditional job
REF: 29.1, 29.3

In the si-clause, the ra and se forms of the imperfect subjunctive are interchangeable; the ra form is more common.
TL: Si tuvieras tiempo, vendrías. Si tuvieses tiempo, vendrías.
EN: If you had time, you would come. If you had time, you would come.
[PAUSE:900]
Clause order does not alter the mood pattern.
TL: Iríamos al parque si dejara de llover.
EN: We would go to the park if it stopped raining.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: tuviera/tuviese · result stays conditional
Flip for the two most common transfer errors.

[CARD]
TITLE: Keep the conditional out of the si-clause
REF: 29.3, 29.8.1

Standard neutral Spanish does not copy English would into the condition itself.
TL: Si pudiera ayudarte, lo haría.
EN: If I could help you, I would.
[PAUSE:900]
Use pudiera in the si-clause, not podría. Likewise, do not use the imperfect indicative merely because English says if I had.
TL: Si tuviera más espacio, pondría otra mesa.
EN: If I had more space, I would put in another table.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: condition → pudiera/tuviera · result → podría/pondría
Flip to diagnose a remote question.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The question test still follows the same pattern
REF: 29.3

A question mark does not change the clause roles. Put the imagined condition in the imperfect subjunctive and the consequence being asked about in the conditional.
TL: ¿Qué harías si encontraras una cartera?
EN: What would you do if you found a wallet?
[PAUSE:900]
The condition can be merely cautious rather than impossible.
TL: Si necesitaran más tiempo, ¿podrían avisarnos?
EN: If they needed more time, could they let us know?
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: ¿consequence in conditional? + si + imperfect subjunctive
Eighteen clean remote-condition drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Unfulfilled past conditions use a compound subjunctive
REF: 29.4

When the past condition did not happen, build the si-clause with hubiera or hubiese plus a past participle.
TL: Si hubieras reservado, habríamos conseguido mesa.
EN: If you had booked, we would have got a table.
[PAUSE:900]
The usual result is the perfect conditional: habría plus participle.
SHOW: si + hubiera/hubiese + participle → habría + participle
Flip when the consequence belongs to the present.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A past condition can have a present result
REF: 29.4

Choose the tense of each clause from its own time reference. A missed past condition may explain a consequence that is true now.
TL: Si hubiéramos guardado el recibo, podríamos reclamar ahora.
EN: If we had kept the receipt, we could complain now.
[PAUSE:900]
If both condition and result belong to the unreal past, use a perfect form in both clauses.
TL: Si hubiéramos guardado el recibo, habríamos reclamado ayer.
EN: If we had kept the receipt, we would have complained yesterday.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: past condition + present result · past condition + past result
Flip for a careful result-clause distinction.

[CARD]
TITLE: Hubiera has two jobs; hubiese does not always share both
REF: 29.4, 29.6

Inside the si-clause, hubiera and hubiese are both standard forms of the pluperfect subjunctive.
TL: Si hubiera llegado antes... Si hubiese llegado antes...
EN: If she had arrived earlier... If she had arrived earlier...
[PAUSE:900]
In the result clause, careful Spanish can use either habría or the ra form hubiera as the perfect conditional alternative.
TL: Habría entrado. Hubiera entrado.
EN: She would have gone in. She would have gone in.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: condition → hubiera/hubiese · result → habría or hubiera
Flip to keep the result form conservative.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Prefer habría for an unmistakable result
REF: 29.4, 29.6

The se form is not the careful-language substitute for habría in a result clause. When there is any risk of confusing the two clause roles, keep habría in the consequence.
TL: Si hubiese funcionado la alarma, habríamos salido antes.
EN: If the alarm had worked, we would have left earlier.
[PAUSE:900]
This makes the condition-result contrast audible even though the ra alternative is also established.
SHOW: safe audit form → si hubiese/hubiera... habríamos...
Five clean unfulfilled-condition completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Indicative si can explain a fact rather than imagine one
REF: 29.7

Some si clauses mean whenever, given that, or the reason why. These fulfilled conditions describe facts, so both clauses stay in the indicative.
TL: Si salíamos temprano, desayunábamos en la estación.
EN: Whenever we left early, we had breakfast at the station.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Si te llamé fue porque necesitaba tu consejo.
EN: If I called you, it was because I needed your advice.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: repeated fact / known reason → indicative
Flip to separate this from a remote condition.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ask whether the condition is asserted as real
REF: 29.2, 29.3, 29.7

The same time period can support different moods. The indicative presents a fact or open possibility; the imperfect subjunctive steps back from it.
TL: Si tenía tiempo, venía. Si tuviera tiempo, vendría.
EN: Whenever she had time, she came. If she had time, she would come.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not choose a form from the English past tense alone. Decide what the speaker commits to.
SHOW: asserted/repeated → tenía, venía · remote → tuviera, vendría
Flip for conversational result forms.

[CARD]
TITLE: Spoken Spanish sometimes shortens the result pattern
REF: 29.5, 29.6

Relaxed speech may use the imperfect indicative where formal prose prefers a conditional in the result.
TL: Si yo fuera tú, no aceptaba esa oferta.
EN: If I were you, I would not accept that offer.
[PAUSE:900]
For a neutral production target, prefer aceptaría. Keep the imperfect subjunctive in the si-clause either way.
TL: Si yo fuera tú, no aceptaría esa oferta.
EN: If I were you, I would not accept that offer.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: conversational result → aceptaba · neutral result → aceptaría
Flip for the advanced past-result alternative.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Treat register variants as results, not new condition rules
REF: 29.4, 29.5, 29.6

Hubiera in a past result and an imperfect indicative in a relaxed present result are alternatives to conditional forms. Neither licenses a conditional inside the si-clause.
TL: Si nos hubieran avisado, habríamos ido. Si nos hubieran avisado, hubiéramos ido.
EN: If they had told us, we would have gone. If they had told us, we would have gone.
[PAUSE:1000]
First label the clause, then choose a careful default, and only then recognize a register variant.
SHOW: identify clause → choose time → choose mood → check register
Twelve clean remote, past, and mixed-condition rewrites follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Si meaning if and si meaning whether behave differently
REF: 29.8.1

Conditional si does not normally take a future indicative or a present subjunctive. Use the present indicative for a possible future condition.
TL: Si viene mañana, prepararemos otra mesa.
EN: If she comes tomorrow, we will prepare another table.
[PAUSE:900]
When si means whether, a future is possible because the clause reports an unresolved question rather than a condition.
TL: No sé si vendrá mañana.
EN: I do not know whether she will come tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: if → si viene · whether → no sé si vendrá
Flip for two compact alternatives to si.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Como and de package conditions with different constraints
REF: 29.8.2, 29.8.3

In warnings and threats, como plus subjunctive can replace an open si-clause.
TL: Como llegues tarde otra vez, empezaremos sin ti.
EN: If you are late again, we will start without you.
[PAUSE:900]
De plus infinitive is compact for future or unfulfilled conditions, especially when its understood subject matches the other clause.
TL: De haberlo sabido, habría cambiado la reserva.
EN: Had I known, I would have changed the booking.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: warning → como + subjunctive · compact condition → de + infinitive
Flip for a wider menu of conditional connectors.

[CARD]
TITLE: Unless and provided that require the subjunctive
REF: 29.9

A menos que and a no ser que introduce an exception; con tal de que and a condición de que set a requirement. These connectors take the subjunctive.
TL: Iremos a pie a menos que llueva.
EN: We will walk unless it rains.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Te presto el coche con tal de que vuelvas antes de las diez.
EN: I will lend you the car provided that you return before ten.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: a menos que + subjunctive · con tal de que + subjunctive
Flip for compressed conditions and advice.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Preserve meaning when you replace si
REF: 29.9, 29.10

A gerund can express the means that makes a result possible, and por si introduces a precaution rather than an ordinary if-clause.
TL: Practicando cada día, ganarás soltura. Lleva agua por si hace calor.
EN: By practising every day, you will gain fluency. Take water in case it is hot.
[PAUSE:900]
For advice, use si yo fuera tú or yo que tú with a conditional or a conversational imperfect result.
TL: Si yo fuera tú, pediría otra opinión.
EN: If I were you, I would ask for another opinion.
[PAUSE:1000]
Audit every rewrite for the same time, stance, subject, and consequence.
SHOW: time · stance · subject · consequence
Fifteen clean transformations between si and alternative frames follow.
