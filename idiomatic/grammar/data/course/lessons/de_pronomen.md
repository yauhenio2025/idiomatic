## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Pronouns inherit a noun phrase's job
SVG: de_pronomen_c01f.svg
REF: 3, 3.1

A pronoun does not merely replace a noun. It inherits the whole noun phrase's grammatical job: person, number, gender and, above all, case. Watch three full noun phrases collapse into three pronouns:
TL: Der Ausschuss hat der Bewerberin den Vertrag geschickt.
EN: The committee sent the applicant the contract.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Er hat ihn ihr gestern geschickt.
EN: The committee sent it to her yesterday.
[PAUSE:1200]
The committee is masculine and nominative, the contract masculine and accusative, the applicant feminine and dative. The short forms preserve every role. Read a pronoun from the syntax outward, not from an English translation inward.
SHOW: identify the role → choose case → preserve number and gender
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Learn the case rows as a system
REF: 3.1.1, 3.1.2

The nominative row names the subject. The accusative row marks the direct object. The dative row marks the indirect object or follows a dative-governing word. The contrasts become audible in small pairs:
TL: Du verstehst mich, aber ich widerspreche dir.
EN: You understand me, but I disagree with you.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Sie unterstützt uns, und wir danken ihr.
EN: She supports us, and we thank her.
[PAUSE:1000]
The polite second person keeps its capital letter in every case:
TL: Wir schicken Ihnen morgen die Unterlagen.
EN: We will send you the documents tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Because personal pronouns are normally unstressed, speech often reduces them. The common contraction of the neuter form is also routine in written dialogue:
TL: Ich hab's ihm gestern erklärt.
EN: I explained it to him yesterday.
[PAUSE:1000]
Genitive personal pronouns survive mainly in formal constructions and usually refer to people or animals:
TL: Die Kommission bedarf seiner nicht mehr.
EN: The commission no longer requires him.
SHOW: subject: nominative · direct object: accusative · indirect object: dative
The first exercise block makes you recover these forms from their roles.

[CARD]
TITLE: Reflexive case is a slot decision
SVG: de_pronomen_c02f.svg
REF: 3.2.1

A reflexive pronoun points back to the subject. In the first and second persons, its forms look like ordinary personal pronouns. The distinctive third-person form works for singular, plural and polite address:
TL: Ich beeile mich, und die anderen beeilen sich auch.
EN: I am hurrying, and the others are hurrying too.
[PAUSE:1000]
With a reflexive verb whose reflexive element is the only object, that element is commonly accusative:
TL: Nach dem Lauf duscht sie sich.
EN: After the run, she takes a shower.
SHOW: reflexive = a pronoun bound back to the subject
Flip the card for the dative contrast.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Another accusative often pushes the reflexive into dative
REF: 3.2.1

When the clause already contains a direct object, the reflexive participant is often dative. First locate the ordinary accusative noun phrase, then choose the reflexive case:
TL: Sie bindet sich die Haare zusammen.
EN: She ties back her hair.
[PAUSE:1000]
The hair is the direct object; the reflexive pronoun marks the person affected. The same architecture appears with an abstract object:
TL: Wir gönnen uns eine kurze Pause.
EN: We allow ourselves a short break.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Er hat sich den Namen sorgfältig notiert.
EN: He carefully wrote down the name for himself.
SHOW: reflexive alone: often accusative · another direct object: often dative
Two exercise sets now train this accusative–dative decision.

[CARD]
TITLE: Reflexive reference follows structure
SVG: de_pronomen_c03f.svg
REF: 3.2.2a

After a preposition, a reflexive form normally points back to the subject. An ordinary personal pronoun points to someone else. One small form therefore changes the reference:
TL: Lara schloss die Tür hinter sich.
EN: Lara closed the door behind herself.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Lara schloss die Tür hinter ihr.
EN: Lara closed the door behind her.
[PAUSE:1200]
In the first sentence, Lara is on both ends of the relation. In the second, the person behind the door is another woman. Do not choose by English surface form; trace the reference line.
SHOW: reflexive → back to the local subject · personal pronoun → someone else
Flip the card for infinitives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The understood subject controls the infinitive
REF: 3.2.2b, 3.2.2c, 3.2.2d

In an infinitive with its own understood actor, the reflexive points to that actor. The governing verb tells you who it is:
TL: Felix bat Mara, sich zuerst anzumelden.
EN: Felix asked Mara to register first.
[PAUSE:1000]
Here Mara is understood as the one registering. Change the governing verb, and control shifts:
TL: Felix versprach Mara, sich zuerst anzumelden.
EN: Felix promised Mara that he would register first.
[PAUSE:1200]
With an inverted compound subject, German may use the third-person form even when the group includes the speaker:
TL: Letzte Woche trafen sich mein Bruder und ich in Köln.
EN: Last week my brother and I met in Cologne.
SHOW: find the understood actor before choosing the reflexive
The next block tests whether the pronoun really points back or names somebody else.

[CARD]
TITLE: One form can mean self or each other
REF: 3.2.3

With a plural subject, the reflexive form can describe separate self-directed actions or a reciprocal relation. Context may settle the reading; if it does not, German can make the contrast explicit:
TL: Die beiden Zeugen widersprachen sich.
EN: The two witnesses contradicted each other.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die beiden Zeugen widersprachen sich selbst.
EN: The two witnesses contradicted themselves.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die beiden Zeugen widersprachen sich gegenseitig.
EN: The two witnesses contradicted each other.
SHOW: self adds reflexive force · mutually adds reciprocal force
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Prepositions fuse with the reciprocal form
REF: 3.2.3

The independent reciprocal form is at home in careful writing. After a preposition, it joins that preposition into one word, making the reciprocal relation unmistakable:
TL: Die Koalitionspartner verhandelten miteinander.
EN: The coalition partners negotiated with one another.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die beiden Abteilungen arbeiten gegeneinander.
EN: The two departments work against one another.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Geschwister verlassen sich aufeinander.
EN: The siblings rely on one another.
[PAUSE:1000]
A preposition plus the ordinary reflexive keeps the self-directed reading; the fused reciprocal points from each member of the group to the others.
SHOW: preposition + reciprocal → one compound word
One mixed block now separates reflexive from reciprocal reference.

[CARD]
TITLE: Address encodes a relationship
REF: 3.3

German does not have one neutral second-person choice. The familiar singular, familiar plural and distance-marking form each bring their own agreement:
TL: Kommst du morgen früher?
EN: Are you coming earlier tomorrow?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Kommt ihr morgen früher?
EN: Are you all coming earlier tomorrow?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Kommen Sie morgen früher?
EN: Are you coming earlier tomorrow?
[PAUSE:1200]
The first asks one familiar person, the second a familiar group, the third one or more people addressed with social distance. Number and relationship are separate decisions.
SHOW: one familiar person · familiar group · respectful distance
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A change of address is negotiated
REF: 3.3

The familiar form signals solidarity or intimacy; the distance-marking form signals respect and a social boundary. If the relationship changes, speakers usually make that change explicit:
TL: Wollen wir uns duzen?
EN: Shall we address each other informally?
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Gern. Dann nenn mich bitte Anna.
EN: Gladly. Then please call me Anna.
[PAUSE:1000]
Until that switch, the formal paradigm stays internally consistent:
TL: Könnten Sie mir bitte Ihre Unterlagen schicken?
EN: Could you please send me your documents?
[PAUSE:1000]
In letters, familiar address forms, including the possessive, may optionally be capitalized:
TL: Vielen Dank für Deine ausführliche Nachricht.
EN: Thank you very much for your detailed message.
SHOW: choose the relationship once → keep pronouns and agreement consistent
Gender and reference take over on the next card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Third-person pronouns track grammatical gender
SVG: de_pronomen_c06f.svg
REF: 3.4.1

For things, the German pronoun agrees with the noun's grammatical gender, not with English it. The same case mechanism then selects the precise form:
TL: Wo ist der Bescheid? Ich finde ihn nicht.
EN: Where is the official notice? I cannot find it.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Frist endet morgen; sie lässt sich nicht verlängern.
EN: The deadline expires tomorrow; it cannot be extended.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Das Gutachten liegt vor; ich lese es heute.
EN: The report is available; I will read it today.
SHOW: antecedent gender + syntactic case = pronoun form
Flip for agreement beyond the simple noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Meaning, emphasis and comparison refine the choice
REF: 3.4.1, 3.4.2, 3.4.3

With words for people, natural gender can take over once the individual is clearly in focus:
TL: Das neue Mitglied im Forschungsteam heißt Nora. Sie promoviert gerade.
EN: The new member of the research team is named Nora. She is currently doing a doctorate.
[PAUSE:1000]
Colloquial German often uses a demonstrative for an emphatic person reference. It is stronger than a neutral personal pronoun and can sound rude if the person is present:
TL: Lea kennt den neuen Hausmeister. Der wohnt gleich nebenan.
EN: Lea knows the new caretaker. He lives right next door.
[PAUSE:1000]
In comparative clauses, agreement reveals exactly what is being compared:
TL: Das ist ein Fehler, wie ihn selbst erfahrene Redakteure machen.
EN: That is the kind of mistake even experienced editors make.
SHOW: grammatical gender is the default · human reference can follow meaning
The next exercise block drills third-person forms across all cases.

[CARD]
TITLE: Prepositional adverbs divide people from things
SVG: de_pronomen_c07f.svg
REF: 3.5.1a, 3.5.1b, 3.5.1c, 3.5.1e

After a preposition, German normally uses a personal pronoun for an individual person and a prepositional adverb for a thing, an idea or an entire situation:
TL: Die Beraterin ist zuverlässig; wir können uns auf sie verlassen.
EN: The adviser is reliable; we can rely on her.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Zeitplan ist realistisch; wir können uns darauf verlassen.
EN: The schedule is realistic; we can rely on it.
[PAUSE:1000]
Before a preposition beginning with a vowel, the linking consonant appears. Whole clauses take this route without hesitation:
TL: Der Ausschuss hat den Entwurf gebilligt. Darüber bin ich erleichtert.
EN: The committee approved the draft. I am relieved about that.
SHOW: individual person → preposition + pronoun · thing or clause → prepositional adverb
Flip for the boundaries of the system.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Motion and four prepositions resist the pattern
REF: 3.5.1f, 3.5.2b, 3.5.2c

When literal movement is involved, German uses a directional compound rather than a prepositional adverb:
TL: Wir erreichten den Saal und gingen sofort hinein.
EN: We reached the hall and went straight inside.
[PAUSE:1000]
Four common prepositions do not build the usual compound; a pronoun may remain where the meaning allows it:
TL: Außer ihm hat niemand widersprochen.
EN: Nobody apart from him objected.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Am Ende der Straße steht ein Theater; ihm gegenüber liegt ein kleines Café.
EN: At the end of the street is a theater; a small café is opposite it.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ohne es wäre die Messung wertlos.
EN: Without it, the measurement would be worthless.
[PAUSE:1000]
For a thing after the preposition meaning since, a separate adverb takes over:
TL: Seit der Reform gelten neue Regeln; seither sind die Verfahren kürzer.
EN: New rules have applied since the reform; since then, the procedures have been shorter.
[PAUSE:1000]
Speech often splits a prepositional adverb, although edited writing normally keeps it whole:
TL: Da will ich mich nicht drauf verlassen.
EN: I do not want to rely on that.
SHOW: motion: directional form · four lexical exceptions · speech may split the compound
A short but demanding block now makes you choose the correct route.

[CARD]
TITLE: The small word can point to three kinds of antecedent
SVG: de_pronomen_c08f.svg
REF: 3.6.1a, 3.6.1b

The neuter pronoun can resume a neuter noun, but that is only its first job:
TL: Das Protokoll ist fertig. Ich verschicke es gleich.
EN: The minutes are ready. I will send them out shortly.
[PAUSE:1000]
It can also package an entire event as one object:
TL: Der Vorstand vertagt die Abstimmung. Ich hatte es befürchtet.
EN: The board is postponing the vote. I had feared that.
[PAUSE:1000]
And after the verbs be or become, it obligatorily resumes a previous predicate:
TL: Klara gilt als unbestechlich, und sie ist es tatsächlich.
EN: Klara is considered incorruptible, and she really is.
SHOW: neuter noun · whole event · previous predicate
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Identification uses an unstressed placeholder
REF: 3.6, 3.6.2b, 3.6.2c

In identifying constructions, the small pronoun does not inherit the gender or number of the person being identified:
TL: Wer steht vor der Tür? Es ist die neue Nachbarin.
EN: Who is at the door? It is the new neighbor.
[PAUSE:1000]
The person remains the grammatical subject when the order is reversed:
TL: Du bist es! Ich habe deine Stimme erkannt.
EN: It is you; I recognized your voice.
[PAUSE:1000]
This pronoun is never stressed. For emphatic identification or reference, German switches to a demonstrative:
TL: Das war die entscheidende Frage.
EN: That was the decisive question.
SHOW: unstressed identification: the small pronoun · emphasis: a demonstrative
The next block tests noun reference, event reference and identification together.

[CARD]
TITLE: Some subject uses stay; others vanish
SVG: de_pronomen_c09f.svg
REF: 3.6.2a

Start by separating two systems. Weather verbs and other genuinely impersonal predicates require a formal subject, even inside a subordinate clause:
TL: Weil es seit Tagen friert, bleiben die Wege glatt.
EN: Because it has been freezing for days, the paths remain slippery.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Leitung weiß, dass es dem Projekt an Zeit mangelt.
EN: Management knows that the project lacks time.
[PAUSE:1200]
But in an impersonal passive, the initial pronoun merely fills the first position. As soon as another element occupies that position, it disappears:
TL: Es wurde bis Mitternacht verhandelt.
EN: Negotiations continued until midnight.
SHOW: lexical impersonal subject: retained · first-position filler: removable
Flip the card for the removal test.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Move another element forward and test agreement
REF: 3.6.2a, 3.6.2d

Move the time phrase into first position, and the filler is gone:
TL: Bis Mitternacht wurde verhandelt.
EN: Negotiations continued until midnight.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Bericht bestätigt, dass bis Mitternacht verhandelt wurde.
EN: The report confirms that negotiations continued until midnight.
[PAUSE:1000]
The same filler can introduce a real subject placed later. The verb agrees with that real subject, not with the filler:
TL: Es liegt noch ein Antrag auf dem Tisch.
EN: There is still one application on the table.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Es liegen noch drei Anträge auf dem Tisch.
EN: There are still three applications on the table.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Auf dem Tisch liegen noch drei Anträge.
EN: There are still three applications on the table.
SHOW: initial filler disappears · the verb agrees with the real subject
The next block turns main clauses into subordinate clauses and tests that distinction.

[CARD]
TITLE: An anticipatory pronoun points forward
SVG: de_pronomen_c10f.svg
REF: 3.6.2e

A clause can itself be the subject. When that heavy clause comes later, an anticipatory pronoun can occupy its place and point forward to it:
TL: Es überrascht mich, dass niemand widerspricht.
EN: It surprises me that nobody objects.
[PAUSE:1000]
Move the clause to the front, and the anticipatory element disappears:
TL: Dass niemand widerspricht, überrascht mich.
EN: That nobody objects surprises me.
[PAUSE:1000]
In the middle of a main clause, usage varies with the predicate and information structure:
TL: Wichtig ist, alle Zahlen noch einmal zu prüfen.
EN: It is important to check all the figures again.
SHOW: later subject clause → pointer possible · subject clause first → no pointer
Flip for object clauses.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Object clauses have lexical preferences
REF: 3.6.2e, 3.6.2f

When the following clause is an object, the choice depends heavily on the governing expression. With many verbs, the anticipatory element is the normal frame:
TL: Ich schätze es sehr, dass du so offen sprichst.
EN: I greatly appreciate that you speak so openly.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir haben es vermieden, unnötige Details zu veröffentlichen.
EN: We avoided publishing unnecessary details.
[PAUSE:1000]
With verbs of knowing, saying and thinking, it is often optional and is favored when an adverb evokes prior knowledge:
TL: Ich hatte es schon vermutet, dass der Termin verschoben worden war.
EN: I had already suspected that the appointment had been postponed.
[PAUSE:1000]
One boundary is firm: before a conditional subject clause, the anticipatory element remains:
TL: Mir ist es recht, wenn wir morgen beginnen.
EN: I am fine with us starting tomorrow.
SHOW: subject or object? · then learn the governing expression's preference
The next exercise block builds complete clauses with these patterns.

[CARD]
TITLE: In fixed expressions, deletion breaks the phrase
REF: 3.6.2g, 3.6.2h

Not every occurrence points backward or forward. In many verbal expressions, the pronoun is simply a fixed part of the lexical frame:
TL: Wir sollten es bei dieser Warnung belassen.
EN: We should leave it at this warning.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die neue Aufgabe hat es in sich.
EN: The new task is tougher than it looks.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sie nimmt es mit den Fristen sehr genau.
EN: She is very strict about deadlines.
[PAUSE:1000]
There is no noun antecedent to recover. Learn the expression as a unit; deleting the pronoun changes or destroys it.
SHOW: no antecedent + fixed verb frame = lexical element
Flip the card for the final diagnostics.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Diagnose reference, anticipation or fixed frame
REF: 3.6.2g, 3.6.2h, 3.6.2i

Some adjective frames also require the pronoun:
TL: Ich bin es leid, ständig nachfragen zu müssen.
EN: I am tired of constantly having to ask.
[PAUSE:1000]
Some short expressions package a complete situation:
TL: Er hat es heute besonders eilig.
EN: He is in a particular hurry today.
[PAUSE:1000]
After the verbs hope and believe, the pronoun can mean so and is often optional:
TL: Wird die Genehmigung rechtzeitig erteilt? Ich hoffe es.
EN: Will the permit be granted in time? I hope so.
[PAUSE:1200]
For every example in this final block, ask one question: does the pronoun resume something, announce a following clause, fill a syntactic position, or belong to a fixed expression? That classification predicts its behavior better than any single English word.
SHOW: look backward · look forward · test the first position · check the lexicon
The final exercise block mixes obligatory, optional and impossible uses.
