## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Italian adverbs answer more than how
REF: 13.1, 13.8, 13.11, 13.12, 13.16

An adverb can describe manner, degree, time, place, frequency, or the speaker's view of a whole sentence. Its job determines both its form and its position.
TL: Probabilmente Marta arriverà molto tardi.
EN: Marta will probably arrive very late.
[PAUSE:900]
Here probabilmente comments on the clause, molto grades another adverb, and tardi locates the event in time.
SHOW: whole-clause comment -> probabilmente · degree -> molto · time -> tardi
Flip for a reliable way to analyse an adverb.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Identify the target before choosing the position
REF: 13.8, 13.9, 13.11, 13.12

First ask what the adverb modifies: an adjective, another adverb, a verb phrase, or the whole clause. Then decide whether the expression focuses manner, result, frequency, or the speaker's stance.
TL: Giulia ha quasi finito il lavoro sorprendentemente presto.
EN: Giulia has almost finished the work surprisingly early.
[PAUSE:900]
Italian has strong placement tendencies, but meaning comes first: moving an adverb can change its scope rather than merely its style.
SHOW: target -> function -> position · adjective/adverb target -> immediately before · whole clause -> usually at an edge

[CARD]
TITLE: Form -mente adverbs from the feminine adjective
REF: 13.1

For an adjective in -o, use its feminine singular base before -mente. An adjective already ending in -e normally adds the suffix directly.
TL: lento -> lenta -> lentamente · felice -> felicemente
EN: slow -> slowly · happy -> happily
[PAUSE:900]
Adjectives ending in vowel plus -re or -le usually drop their final e before the suffix.
TL: regolare -> regolarmente · naturale -> naturalmente · stabile -> stabilmente
EN: regularly · naturally · stably
SHOW: -o -> -a + mente · ordinary -e -> + mente · vowel + re/le -> drop e + mente

[SIDE]
TITLE: Learn the exceptions as complete forms
REF: 13.1

Pronunciation blocks elision in a few common words, and several high-frequency adverbs have lexicalized stems.
TL: folle -> follemente · violento -> violentemente · leggero -> leggermente
EN: madly · violently · lightly
[PAUSE:900]
Some pairs are not productive spelling rules at all, so retrieve them as vocabulary.
TL: altro -> altrimenti · pari -> parimenti · benevolo -> benevolmente
EN: otherwise · likewise · benevolently
SHOW: preserve pronounceable stem -> follemente · special stem -> violentemente/leggermente · lexicalized -> altrimenti/parimenti

[CARD]
TITLE: A masculine singular adjective can act as an adverb
REF: 13.2

Several common forms stay identical to the masculine singular adjective when they describe how an action happens.
TL: Parla piano, lavora sodo e mira alto.
EN: He speaks softly, works hard, and aims high.
[PAUSE:900]
The unchanged form is not simply a shorter spelling of the -mente form: the two can express different meanings.
TL: L'aereo vola basso, ma il truffatore si è comportato bassamente.
EN: The plane flies low, but the fraudster behaved basely.
SHOW: physical height -> alto/basso · moral manner -> altamente/bassamente · ordinary manner -> fixed adjective-form adverb
Eleven meaning-sensitive choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Contrast the fixed form with -mente
REF: 13.2

Use chiaro for direct, plain expression but chiaramente for clearly visible or evident meaning. Use duro in fixed physical senses but duramente for harsh treatment.
TL: Te lo dico chiaro; il direttore ci ha criticati duramente.
EN: I am telling you plainly; the director criticized us harshly.
[PAUSE:900]
Giusto can mean exactly or just in time, while giustamente means rightly. Lontano is far away, while lontanamente is remotely or vaguely.
TL: Siamo arrivati giusto in tempo; non gli somiglia neppure lontanamente.
EN: We arrived just in time; he does not resemble him even remotely.
SHOW: plain/direct -> chiaro · evident -> chiaramente · exactly -> giusto · rightly -> giustamente · far -> lontano · remotely -> lontanamente

[CARD]
TITLE: Many frequent adverbs are lexical forms
REF: 13.3, 13.4, 13.5

Bene, male, meglio, peggio, and tardi do not follow a productive adjective-to-adverb rule. Bene and male may shorten before an adjective or participle.
TL: Il mobile è ben fatto, ma la porta è mal dipinta.
EN: The piece of furniture is well made, but the door is badly painted.
[PAUSE:900]
Other basic adverbs must also be learned directly: abbastanza, già, quasi, sempre, subito, volentieri, and many more.
TL: Torneremo presto e verremo volentieri.
EN: We will return soon and will gladly come.
SHOW: buono -> bene · cattivo -> male · before adjective/participle -> ben/mal · independent lexical form -> learn whole
Eleven lexical-form choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Multiword expressions behave as single adverbials
REF: 13.4, 13.5, 13.6

Italian often uses a preposition plus a noun or adjective: a fatica, di notte, in fretta, per caso, sul serio, and su per giù.
TL: Ha finito a fatica e se n'è andato in fretta.
EN: He finished with difficulty and left in a hurry.
[PAUSE:900]
Manner can also use in modo plus a masculine adjective, in maniera plus a feminine adjective, or con plus a noun. Posture forms in -oni are lexical items such as carponi and bocconi.
TL: Ha risposto in modo cortese e il bambino avanzava carponi.
EN: She answered politely and the child moved forward on all fours.
SHOW: preposition phrase -> one adverbial · in modo + masculine · in maniera + feminine · posture -> lexical -oni form

[CARD]
TITLE: Put degree adverbs directly before their target
REF: 13.9, 13.10

An adverb modifying an adjective or another adverb normally stands immediately before it.
TL: È un compito piuttosto difficile; Luca parla molto lentamente.
EN: It is a rather difficult task; Luca speaks very slowly.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not normally stack one -mente form directly on another. Replace the first with a degree phrase or recast the construction.
TL: Ha risposto del tutto correttamente.
EN: She answered entirely correctly.
SHOW: degree + adjective/adverb -> immediate order · two -mente forms -> recast · entirely correctly -> del tutto correttamente
Seventeen placement transformations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Verb and sentence adverbs occupy different zones
REF: 13.11, 13.12

Manner adverbs generally follow the verb, either before or after a compact complement. Many focusing adverbs move between an auxiliary or modal and its following verb.
TL: Anna ha letto attentamente il contratto; lo ha quasi firmato.
EN: Anna read the contract carefully; she almost signed it.
[PAUSE:900]
Sentence adverbs normally stand at the beginning or an isolated edge of the clause. Internal placement is possible when commas and intonation mark the comment.
TL: Purtroppo non verranno; verranno domani, probabilmente.
EN: Unfortunately they will not come; they will probably come tomorrow.
SHOW: manner -> after verb or complement · focusing with auxiliary -> between auxiliary and main verb · sentence comment -> clause edge/comma isolation

[CARD]
TITLE: Molto and troppo change only outside the adverb role
REF: 13.8, 13.11

As adverbs of degree, molto and troppo are invariant. They can modify an adjective, an adverb, or a verb.
TL: Elena è molto paziente, guida molto bene e lavora troppo.
EN: Elena is very patient, drives very well, and works too much.
[PAUSE:900]
Before a noun they are adjectives and agree; standing for a quantity of people or things, they are pronouns and still agree.
TL: Ha troppi impegni, ma molte delle sue colleghe la aiutano.
EN: She has too many commitments, but many of her colleagues help her.
SHOW: degree/action -> invariant molto/troppo · before noun -> agreement · quantity standing alone -> agreement
Eleven adjective-pronoun-adverb decisions follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Tutto may be a pronoun, a quantifier, or an intensifier
REF: 13.13

When tutto means the whole thing, it agrees with what it replaces. Before an adjective it can intensify a state and agrees with the subject.
TL: Ho letto tutto il rapporto; Sara era tutta agitata.
EN: I read the whole report; Sara was completely agitated.
[PAUSE:900]
With a plural subject, context distinguishes completely from all, although a fuller quantifier can make the all reading explicit.
TL: Le finestre erano tutte aperte; erano tutte quante danneggiate.
EN: The windows were all open; every one of them was damaged.
SHOW: whole object -> tutto/a/i/e · intensifier before adjective -> agree with subject · explicit every one -> tutti/e quanti/e

[CARD]
TITLE: Italian spatial combinations form a limited phrasal system
REF: 13.15

Common motion, carrying, and placing verbs combine with giù, su, fuori, dentro, avanti, via, and indietro.
TL: Porta giù le valigie e metti via i documenti.
EN: Bring the suitcases down and put the documents away.
[PAUSE:900]
Where a simple verb exists, it may replace the combination without changing the basic direction.
TL: I bambini sono usciti, poi sono saliti in soffitta.
EN: The children went out, then went up to the attic.
SHOW: motion verb + place adverb · andare fuori -> uscire · venire su -> salire · andare giù -> scendere

[SIDE]
TITLE: Translate the event, not the English particle
REF: 13.15

English reuses a small verb with many particles. Italian often selects a distinct verb or an idiomatic phrase instead.
TL: Guardati intorno, abbassa lo sguardo e stai attento.
EN: Look around, look down, and look out.
[PAUSE:900]
The Italian place word belongs to the whole construction, so do not assume that one English particle always maps to one adverb.
TL: Il direttore si è dimesso, ma il suo progetto continua.
EN: The director stood down, but his project carries on.
SHOW: look around -> guardarsi intorno · look down -> abbassare lo sguardo · look out -> stare attento · stand down -> dimettersi

[CARD]
TITLE: Distinguish now, just, and by now
REF: 13.16

Ora and adesso mean now and can also refer to a very recent or imminent moment. Appena commonly marks something that has just happened.
TL: Ho appena chiuso il negozio; adesso torno a casa.
EN: I have just closed the shop; now I am going home.
[PAUSE:900]
Ormai means by now or now that a change is effectively settled. Allora points to a time or consequence, while poi emphasizes what happens next.
TL: Ormai è tardi; allora prendiamo un taxi e poi telefoniamo.
EN: It is late by now; so let us take a taxi and then call.
SHOW: now -> ora/adesso · just happened -> appena · by now -> ormai · at that time/therefore -> allora · subsequently -> poi

[SIDE]
TITLE: Frequency and temporal viewpoint choose the form
REF: 13.16

Ancora means still, and non ancora means not yet. Già means already; mai means ever in questions and comparisons, but with negation it means never.
TL: Non sono ancora pronti, ma hanno già provato il sistema.
EN: They are not ready yet, but they have already tested the system.
[PAUSE:900]
Fa measures backwards from the time of speaking. Prima measures an earlier point relative to another past viewpoint.
TL: L'ho visto due giorni fa; disse che l'aveva visto due giorni prima.
EN: I saw him two days ago; he said that he had seen him two days earlier.
SHOW: still/yet -> ancora/non ancora · already -> già · ever/never -> mai · ago from now -> fa · earlier than past point -> prima

[CARD]
TITLE: Negative adverbs can negate a verb or a whole reply
REF: 13.17

Mai, neanche, nemmeno, and neppure can follow a negated verb or stand before it for emphasis. With a compound verb, they commonly sit between the auxiliary and the participle.
TL: Non l'ho mai incontrata; mai accetterei quelle condizioni.
EN: I have never met her; never would I accept those conditions.
[PAUSE:900]
Neanche, nemmeno, and neppure may also directly negate the following constituent.
TL: Non è venuto neanche Marco; neppure domani avremo una risposta.
EN: Not even Marco came; we will not have an answer even tomorrow.
SHOW: ordinary negation -> non + verb + mai · compound verb -> auxiliary + mai + participle · emphatic fronting -> mai + verb · not even X -> neanche/nemmeno/neppure + X

[SIDE]
TITLE: Choose the negative reply by strength and register
REF: 13.17

Mica is conversational and challenges or corrects an assumption. Affatto, assolutamente, per niente, and per nulla reinforce a negative statement.
TL: Non è mica facile; non sono affatto preoccupato.
EN: It certainly is not easy; I am not worried at all.
[PAUSE:900]
As short replies, assolutamente no and niente affatto mean absolutely not. Neanche per sogno is a strong colloquial refusal.
TL: Vuoi rinunciare? Neanche per sogno!
EN: Do you want to give up? No way!
SHOW: corrective colloquial not -> mica · not at all -> non ... affatto/per niente · absolute refusal -> assolutamente no · no chance -> neanche per sogno
