## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Spanish keeps the relative link visible
REF: 39.1.3

English often hides the object relative or leaves its preposition at the end. Spanish does neither. State the relative form, and put the governing preposition before it.
TL: El informe que pediste está sobre la mesa.
EN: The report you requested is on the table.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Esta es la cuestión de la que hablábamos.
EN: This is the issue we were talking about.
[PAUSE:900]
A compact English modifier may also need a full relative clause in Spanish.
TL: Han retirado los productos que contienen ese aditivo.
EN: They have withdrawn the products containing that additive.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: express the relative · preposition before it · expand English shortcuts
Flip for the attachment rule.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Keep the relative clause beside its antecedent
REF: 39.1.3, 42.2.1

Spanish resists separating a relative clause from the noun it modifies. Move the whole noun phrase or choose a presentational order.
TL: No existe el candidato en quien todos confíen.
EN: The candidate whom everyone trusts does not exist.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Han regresado los investigadores que trabajaban en Chile.
EN: The researchers who were working in Chile have returned.
[PAUSE:900]
The relative introduces a finite clause; it is not an optional ornament copied from English punctuation.
SHOW: antecedent + adjacent relative clause · no stranded preposition · no silent object relative
Three unflagged English-to-Spanish translations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Restrictive clauses identify a subset
REF: 39.1.2

A restrictive relative answers which one or which ones. It narrows the antecedent and is written without framing commas.
TL: Archivaremos las solicitudes que llegaron a tiempo.
EN: We will file the applications that arrived on time.
[PAUSE:900]
The statement concerns only the applications that met the deadline; it says nothing about the rest.
TL: Los vecinos que protestaron recibieron una respuesta.
EN: The residents who protested received a reply.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: no commas · identifies the intended subset · essential to reference
Flip to change the information structure.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Non-restrictive clauses add a parenthesis
REF: 39.1.2, 39.3

A non-restrictive relative adds information about an already identified antecedent. Commas in writing and pauses in speech mark the parenthesis.
TL: Las solicitudes, que llegaron a tiempo, serán examinadas mañana.
EN: The applications, which arrived on time, will be examined tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Here the timing applies to all the applications. With no preposition, que is neutral; quien is limited to people, while an agreeing cual form is more formal.
TL: La directora, quien ya conocía el problema, pidió otro informe.
EN: The director, who already knew about the problem, requested another report.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: commas change scope · que neutral · quien human · cual formal
Three hygiene-gated clause combinations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Que is the default restrictive relative
REF: 39.2

Without a preceding preposition, que normally covers human and non-human antecedents, subjects and objects. It never takes a written accent as a relative.
TL: La técnica que diseñó el protocolo dirigirá el ensayo.
EN: The technician who designed the protocol will lead the trial.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El protocolo que diseñó la técnica ya está aprobado.
EN: The protocol that the technician designed has already been approved.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not replace restrictive que with bare quien merely because the antecedent is a person.
TL: Busco al técnico que reparó este equipo.
EN: I am looking for the technician who repaired this equipment.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: restrictive and no preposition → que · relative forms have no accent
Flip when the clause contains a governed relation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Let syntax choose the larger form
REF: 39.2, 39.4

If the relative is the complement of a preposition, preserve that preposition and choose a form that can follow it.
TL: La colega con quien colaboro vive en Montevideo.
EN: The colleague with whom I work lives in Montevideo.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El método en el que se basa el cálculo tiene varias limitaciones.
EN: The method on which the calculation is based has several limitations.
[PAUSE:900]
For an unknown or irrelevant human gender, quien is especially useful.
TL: Necesitamos a alguien en quien podamos confiar.
EN: We need someone we can trust.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: inspect the verb or noun frame · carry its preposition into the relative
Seven clean relative-form completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: A preposition stays in front of its relative
REF: 39.4.1

For a non-human antecedent, use an agreeing que or cual form after the preposition. For a person, quien is also available.
TL: La norma contra la que protestaron sigue vigente.
EN: The rule against which they protested remains in force.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El asesor a quien consultamos confirmó el diagnóstico.
EN: The adviser whom we consulted confirmed the diagnosis.
[PAUSE:900]
Agreement belongs to the antecedent, not to a nearby noun inside the clause.
TL: Las condiciones bajo las cuales aceptaron el acuerdo eran estrictas.
EN: The conditions under which they accepted the agreement were strict.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: non-human → agreeing que or cual form · human → quien also possible
Flip for article omission after short prepositions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Bare que after a preposition has limits
REF: 39.4.2

After a, con, de, or en, the article may sometimes disappear, especially with a definite abstract antecedent and no precise spatial reading.
TL: La serenidad con que respondió sorprendió al tribunal.
EN: The composure with which she replied surprised the court.
[PAUSE:900]
Keep the article when the relation is concrete or when a marks a human direct object.
TL: La investigadora a la que conociste presentará los resultados.
EN: The researcher whom you met will present the results.
[PAUSE:900]
With place nouns, a donde form may be more natural than treating every en as literal containment.
TL: La casa en la que guardan el archivo necesita una reforma.
EN: The house in which they keep the archive needs renovating.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: abstract short-preposition frame may allow bare que · concrete relation keeps agreement visible
Four unambiguous prepositional-relative completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: El cual signals formality or restores clarity
REF: 39.5

An agreeing cual form is usually more formal than an agreeing que form. It earns its place after a complex preposition or when distance makes the antecedent harder to recover.
TL: Aprobaron un procedimiento mediante el cual se reducen los errores.
EN: They approved a procedure by which errors are reduced.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Habló con la ministra después del pleno, el cual duró tres horas.
EN: He spoke with the minister after the plenary session, which lasted three hours.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not scatter this formal form through ordinary restrictive clauses merely as a synonym for que.
SHOW: complex preposition · distant antecedent · formal register · agreement clarifies
Flip when the antecedent is an entire proposition.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A whole proposition takes a neuter relative
REF: 39.6

When the relative comments on a preceding fact or idea rather than on one gendered noun, use an invariable neuter form.
TL: El comité aplazó la votación, lo cual sorprendió a la prensa.
EN: The committee postponed the vote, which surprised the press.
[PAUSE:900]
Without the comma, ordinary que can instead attach to the nearest noun and change the meaning.
TL: El comité aplazó la votación que había propuesto la secretaria.
EN: The committee postponed the vote that the secretary had proposed.
[PAUSE:900]
Both neuter options can refer back to the whole event; the cual form is especially explicit in formal prose.
SHOW: noun antecedent → agreeing or plain relative · whole fact → neuter lo que or lo cual
Six hygiene-gated choices between gendered and neuter forms follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Cuyo agrees with what is possessed
REF: 39.7

Cuyo is a relative adjective. It links a possessor to a following noun and agrees with that following noun, not with the possessor.
TL: Entrevistaron a una arquitecta cuyos proyectos han recibido varios premios.
EN: They interviewed an architect whose projects have received several awards.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Visitamos un museo cuya colección permanece cerrada al público.
EN: We visited a museum whose collection remains closed to the public.
[PAUSE:900]
If two coordinated possessions follow, agreement is controlled by the first noun.
TL: Es una autora cuya obra y pensamiento siguen despertando debate.
EN: She is an author whose work and thought continue to provoke debate.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: possessor + cuyo + possessed noun · agreement with the possessed noun
Flip for register and fixed extensions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Written cuyo is compact, but speech often recasts it
REF: 39.7

Cuyo is normal in careful written Spanish but less common in spontaneous conversation. A new clause can sound more natural in speech.
TL: Conocí al director; su hija trabaja en Bruselas.
EN: I met the director; his daughter works in Brussels.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not insert a repeated noun after cuyo merely to imitate English in which. A few fixed abstract nouns do license established combinations.
TL: Si rechazan la propuesta, en cuyo caso habrá que empezar de nuevo, perderemos un mes.
EN: If they reject the proposal, in which case we will have to start again, we will lose a month.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: careful prose → cuyo · conversation may recast · fixed: en cuyo caso
Ten clean agreement completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Donde encodes a spatial relation
REF: 39.10

Donde can stand for a place relation that could also be expressed with a preposition plus an agreeing relative.
TL: Volvimos al barrio donde nos conocimos.
EN: We returned to the neighbourhood where we met.
[PAUSE:900]
Add the path preposition when source, route, or direction matters.
TL: Este es el cajón de donde saqué los documentos.
EN: This is the drawer from which I took the documents.
[PAUSE:900]
With a clearly identified destination and a motion verb, adonde is available; interrogative adónde belongs to questions, not to relatives.
TL: Encontraron por fin el refugio adonde se dirigían.
EN: They finally found the shelter they were heading to.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: location → donde · source → de donde · destination → adonde · no accent in relatives
Flip from space to manner and time.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Como and cuando preserve the relation
REF: 39.11, 39.12

After manner or mode, como can introduce the relative, although an en que construction is often more usual.
TL: No comparto la manera como resolvieron el conflicto.
EN: I do not agree with the way they resolved the conflict.
[PAUSE:900]
Cuando readily adds a non-restrictive comment to an identified time. A restrictive time expression more often takes que, with optional en.
TL: Nos veremos en agosto, cuando termine el curso.
EN: We will meet in August, when the course ends.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Solo descanso los días en que no viajo.
EN: I rest only on the days when I do not travel.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: manner → como or en que · identified time aside → cuando · restricted time → en que
Nine clean relative-adverb completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Mood reveals whether the antecedent is identified
REF: 39.15.1, 39.15.3

The indicative presents an antecedent as known or identified. The subjunctive presents the required type without claiming that a matching individual has been found.
TL: Conozco a una traductora que domina el islandés.
EN: I know a translator who is fluent in Icelandic.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Busco a una traductora que domine el islandés.
EN: I am looking for a translator who is fluent in Icelandic.
[PAUSE:900]
Negated existence also selects the subjunctive because no actual bearer of the description is asserted.
TL: No hay ningún programa que detecte todos estos errores.
EN: There is no program that detects all these errors.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: identified antecedent → indicative · sought, hypothetical, or nonexistent → subjunctive
Flip for future and superlative contexts.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Future reference can open the relative set
REF: 39.15.4, 39.15.5

When the main clause places the choice or identification in the future, the relative clause commonly takes the subjunctive.
TL: Será el jurado quien determine el resultado.
EN: It will be the jury that determines the result.
[PAUSE:900]
After superlatives, the subjunctive is possible when the speaker emphasizes an open or evaluative extreme, but the indicative remains ordinary for established experience.
TL: Es la propuesta más audaz que he leído este año.
EN: It is the boldest proposal I have read this year.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Quizá sea la propuesta más audaz que haya leído jamás.
EN: It may be the boldest proposal I have ever read.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: future identification → subjunctive · superlative mood follows viewpoint, not a mechanical trigger

[CARD]
TITLE: A gendered nominalizer replaces a known noun
REF: 40.1.2, 40.1.4

An article plus de means the one or ones belonging to, from, or associated with something. The article preserves the omitted noun's gender and number.
TL: Mi despacho es más luminoso que el de la directora.
EN: My office is brighter than the director's.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Estas cifras no coinciden con las del informe anterior.
EN: These figures do not match those in the previous report.
[PAUSE:900]
Only de can directly follow this bare article pattern. Other relations require a relative clause.
TL: Necesito la carpeta que está en el armario, no la de la mesa.
EN: I need the folder that is in the cupboard, not the one on the table.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: article + de → one of or belonging to · agreement recovers the omitted noun
Flip from a prepositional complement to a clause.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Article plus que builds the one that
REF: 40.1.4, 40.1.6

An agreeing article plus que nominalizes a relative clause and identifies a person or thing without repeating its noun.
TL: Las que llegaron primero ya han pasado a la sala.
EN: The women who arrived first have already gone into the room.
[PAUSE:900]
For human reference, quien can often replace the article form when gender is irrelevant or deliberately left open.
TL: Quien tenga la llave puede abrir el almacén.
EN: Whoever has the key can open the storeroom.
[PAUSE:900]
With a clearly identified and gendered person, the agreeing article can carry information that quien does not.
SHOW: article + que → gendered one or ones · quien → human, gender-neutral

[CARD]
TITLE: Lo de turns a topic into a noun phrase
REF: 40.1.3

The invariable neuter sequence lo de packages an affair, topic, event, or loosely bounded matter. It does not stand for an omitted masculine noun.
TL: Tenemos que hablar de lo del contrato.
EN: We need to talk about the contract business.
[PAUSE:900]
It can also package an activity or something already salient in the conversation.
TL: Lo de trabajar desde casa requiere una organización distinta.
EN: Working from home and everything involved in it requires a different kind of organization.
[PAUSE:900]
Context supplies the breadth: a fact, a problem, some belongings, or the whole situation.
SHOW: lo de + topic → the matter of, the business about, everything involving
Flip to the neuter clause nominalizer.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Lo que means the thing or fact that
REF: 40.1.5

Lo que nominalizes a clause without retrieving a gendered noun. It often translates what or the thing that.
TL: Lo que necesitamos es una decisión clara.
EN: What we need is a clear decision.
[PAUSE:900]
Contrast it with an agreeing form, which selects one member from a known noun class.
TL: Entre todos los diseños, este es el que necesitamos.
EN: Among all the designs, this is the one we need.
[PAUSE:900]
Ask whether the discourse supplies a specific noun whose gender should survive. If not, the neuter form is the safer analysis.
SHOW: no recoverable gendered noun → lo que · known noun class → agreeing article + que

[CARD]
TITLE: A cleft assigns explicit focus
REF: 41.1, 41.2

A cleft uses ser to isolate one element as the answer to an implicit contrast. Spanish joins the focus to the remaining clause with a nominalizer, not with bare que.
TL: Fue Marta quien detectó el error.
EN: It was Marta who detected the error.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Este documento es el que falta.
EN: This document is the one that is missing.
[PAUSE:900]
For a genderless phrase or proposition, use lo que.
TL: La falta de tiempo fue lo que impidió el acuerdo.
EN: Lack of time was what prevented the agreement.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: ser + focus + nominalizer · human → quien or agreeing form · proposition → lo que
Flip for tense and person agreement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Coordinate the two halves of the cleft
REF: 41.1.2, 41.5

The tense of ser normally matches the time frame of the focused event.
TL: Fue allí donde encontramos la carta.
EN: It was there that we found the letter.
[PAUSE:900]
For learners, the safest person rule is to make the relative-clause verb agree with the focused subject, especially in the plural.
TL: Fuimos nosotros los que propusimos el cambio.
EN: We were the ones who proposed the change.
[PAUSE:900]
Third-person agreement also occurs in some singular patterns, but plural person agreement is the dependable production choice.
TL: Eres tú quien tiene que decidir.
EN: You are the one who has to decide.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: align the time frame · plural focus controls person · singular variation exists

[CARD]
TITLE: Repeat the preposition across a cleft
REF: 41.3

When the focused constituent begins with a preposition, standard Spanish normally repeats that relation before the linking nominalizer.
TL: Fue con Elena con quien negociamos el acuerdo.
EN: It was with Elena that we negotiated the agreement.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Es de este riesgo de lo que debemos hablar.
EN: It is this risk that we need to discuss.
[PAUSE:900]
European informal Spanish strongly prefers the repeated structure; informal Latin American usage may use a single linking que, but the repeated pattern travels well in formal language.
SHOW: preposition + focus · same preposition + nominalizer · then the clause
Flip for focused time, place, manner, and reason.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Time, place, and manner choose their own link
REF: 41.3, 41.4

A focused time links with cuando, a place with donde, and a manner with como. Bare que is not the neutral pan-Spanish default here.
TL: Fue entonces cuando comprendimos la causa.
EN: It was then that we understood the cause.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Es desde esta terraza desde donde se ve el puerto.
EN: It is from this terrace that the harbour can be seen.
[PAUSE:900]
For that's why, simple por eso is often best. A full cleft repeats por in the standard European pattern.
TL: Perdimos el último tren; por eso tuvimos que volver en taxi.
EN: We missed the last train; that is why we had to return by taxi.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Fue por eso por lo que cambiamos el horario.
EN: That was why we changed the timetable.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: time → cuando · place → donde · manner → como · reason → por eso or por eso por lo que
