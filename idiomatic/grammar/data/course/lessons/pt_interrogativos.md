## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: O que asks for an unnamed thing, fact, or event
REF: 11.1.1, 11.1.2

Use o que when what stands alone as a pronoun. It can be the subject or object of the question.
TL: O que mudou? O que você decidiu?
EN: What changed? What did you decide?
[PAUSE:900]
If the verb or expression requires a preposition, put it before the whole interrogative. After a preposition, especially in formal style, the o may be omitted.
TL: Do que eles precisam? A que isso se refere?
EN: What do they need? What does that refer to?
[PAUSE:950]
Start from the missing role, recover its governing preposition, and then choose o que or the more formal que.
SHOW: unnamed thing/event -> o que · de + o que -> do que · formal after a preposition -> de que
Flip for what directly before a noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Que goes directly before the noun being identified
REF: 11.2, 11.1.3

Use que when what modifies a following noun. The word does not change for gender or number.
TL: Que documento falta? Que horas são?
EN: What document is missing? What time is it?
[PAUSE:900]
When o que or preposition plus que falls at the end of a sentence, the final word is stressed and written quê.
TL: Você escolheu o quê? Eles viajaram para quê?
EN: What did you choose? What did they travel for?
[PAUSE:950]
The circumflex marks the word's sentence-final stressed form; it does not turn every interrogative que into quê.
SHOW: before noun -> que · standing alone -> o que · at sentence end -> o quê / de quê / para quê

[CARD]
TITLE: Qual selects one answer from a range
REF: 11.3.1, 11.3.2

Qual means which one, and quais means which ones. Use the plural when the possible answers are plural.
TL: Qual você prefere? Quais são os seus arquivos?
EN: Which one do you prefer? Which ones are your files?
[PAUSE:900]
Before a noun, Brazilian Portuguese often prefers a partitive or identifying frame instead of bare qual plus noun.
TL: Qual dos relatórios está pronto? Qual é a proposta que você recomenda?
EN: Which of the reports is ready? Which proposal do you recommend?
[PAUSE:950]
Treat qual as a selector. Que plus a noun asks for the requested category directly.
SHOW: one of a range -> qual · several -> quais · which of the ... -> qual/quais dos/das ...
Flip for the contrast between selecting and defining.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Qual specifies; o que asks for a definition
REF: 11.3.3, 11.3.4

With ser, qual asks the listener to supply a particular value from the possible answers. O que asks what something is or what a term means.
TL: Qual é o seu endereço? O que é computação quântica?
EN: What is your address? What is quantum computing?
[PAUSE:900]
Portuguese also uses qual é to request measurements and personal details where English begins with how.
TL: Qual é a altura do prédio? Qual é a sua idade?
EN: How tall is the building? How old are you?
[PAUSE:950]
Ask whether the answer should identify a value or explain a category before choosing qual or o que.
SHOW: specify a value -> qual é ...? · define a thing -> o que é ...? · measurement -> qual é ...?
The focused source set now contrasts o que, que, and qual.

[CARD]
TITLE: Quem asks about people and keeps its own form
REF: 11.4.1, 11.4.2

Quem can ask for a person as subject or object. A required preposition comes before it.
TL: Quem ligou? Quem você encontrou? Com quem você falou?
EN: Who called? Who did you meet? Who did you speak with?
[PAUSE:900]
As a subject, quem normally takes a singular verb. Ser is the exception: it agrees with the plural complement that follows.
TL: Quem trabalha aqui? Quem são aquelas pessoas?
EN: Who works here? Who are those people?
[PAUSE:950]
Do not make quem plural; number appears on ser only when its complement requires agreement.
SHOW: quem + singular verb · preposition + quem · quem são + plural identification
Flip from people to quantities.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quanto agrees with a counted noun but not as an adverb
REF: 11.5.1, 11.5.2, 11.5.3, 11.5.4

Before a noun, quanto changes to quanta, quantos, or quantas. The same agreement remains when the noun is understood rather than repeated.
TL: Quanto tempo resta? Quantas páginas faltam? Quantos você quer?
EN: How much time remains? How many pages are left? How many do you want?
[PAUSE:900]
When quanto means how much as an adverb, it is invariable. In an embedded question, o quanto can add emphasis.
TL: Quanto custou? Você sabe o quanto isso mudou?
EN: How much did it cost? Do you know just how much that changed?
[PAUSE:950]
Look for a noun to agree with; without one, use adverbial quanto.
SHOW: M SG quanto · F SG quanta · M PL quantos · F PL quantas · adverb -> quanto

[CARD]
TITLE: Como asks about manner, condition, or character
REF: 11.6.1, 11.6.2

Como asks how something happens or is done. With ser or estar, it can ask what a person, place, situation, or experience is like.
TL: Como você chegou? Como está o trânsito? Como é o curso?
EN: How did you arrive? What is the traffic like? What is the course like?
[PAUSE:900]
English uses several frames for these questions, but Portuguese keeps como and lets the verb distinguish manner from condition or character.
SHOW: manner -> como + action · temporary condition -> como está · character/description -> como é
Flip for conversational reactions and embedded degree.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Como can request repetition or introduce a surprising degree
REF: 11.6.3, 11.6.4

On its own, Como? asks someone to repeat or clarify. Como é que é? is stronger and can signal disbelief or impatience.
TL: Como? Não ouvi. Como é que é? Você cancelou tudo?
EN: Sorry? I did not hear. What did you say? You canceled everything?
[PAUSE:900]
After an evaluative expression, como can introduce the degree or manner that caused the reaction.
TL: É impressionante como ela aprende rápido.
EN: It is impressive how quickly she learns.
[PAUSE:950]
Intonation and the surrounding clause separate a neutral information question from a reaction.
SHOW: Como? -> please repeat · Como é que é? -> disbelief · evaluation + como -> striking degree

[CARD]
TITLE: Onde locates; de onde and aonde make direction explicit
REF: 11.7.1, 11.7.2

Onde asks where for location and is also widespread for destination in speech. Add de for origin.
TL: Onde você mora? Onde eles vão? De onde ela veio?
EN: Where do you live? Where are they going? Where did she come from?
[PAUSE:900]
Careful usage reserves aonde for movement toward a destination, although ordinary speech often uses onde in both roles.
TL: Aonde vocês vão depois da reunião?
EN: Where are you going after the meeting?
[PAUSE:950]
Let the verb's direction decide whether the preposition should be visible.
SHOW: location -> onde · origin -> de onde · destination in careful usage -> aonde
Flip for the compact spoken form and the time question.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Cadê already contains the idea of where is or where are
REF: 11.8.1, 11.9.1

Cadê is an everyday spoken way to ask for the temporary location of a person or thing, or for something expected but missing. Put the noun or pronoun directly after it; do not add estar.
TL: Cadê a chave? E a Paula, cadê ela?
EN: Where is the key? And Paula, where is she?
[PAUSE:900]
Quando asks when in direct and embedded questions. A preposition can narrow the time relation.
TL: Quando começa? Você sabe quando termina? Até quando fica aberto?
EN: When does it start? Do you know when it ends? Until when is it open?
[PAUSE:950]
Cadê is conversational and self-contained; quando behaves like the other interrogatives after a preposition.
SHOW: cadê + noun/pronoun · no cadê está · quando · desde/até quando

[CARD]
TITLE: Por que asks for a reason; para que asks for a purpose
REF: 11.10.1, 11.10.2, 11.11.1, 11.11.2

Write interrogative por que as two words when the missing information is a cause or reason. Porque, one word, introduces the answer.
TL: Por que você saiu cedo? Porque eu estava cansado.
EN: Why did you leave early? Because I was tired.
[PAUSE:900]
Use para que when asking what something is for or toward what purpose. At the end of a sentence, write por quê or para quê.
TL: Para que serve este botão? Você desligou o sistema por quê?
EN: What is this button for? Why did you shut down the system?
[PAUSE:950]
First distinguish reason from purpose, then check whether que is sentence-final.
SHOW: reason question -> por que · reason answer -> porque · purpose -> para que · final -> por quê/para quê
Flip for the general preposition rule.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Portuguese places the governing preposition before the question word
REF: 11.14.1

English often leaves a preposition at the end. Portuguese normally puts it before the interrogative expression.
TL: Para quem você enviou o arquivo? De onde veio esta informação?
EN: Who did you send the file to? Where did this information come from?
[PAUSE:900]
The same principle applies when the question word modifies a noun.
TL: Até que horas o atendimento funciona?
EN: What time does customer service operate until?
[PAUSE:950]
Recover the complete relation before forming the question: enviar para, vir de, funcionar até.
SHOW: verb/expression + preposition -> preposition + interrogative · never strand it at the end

[CARD]
TITLE: Que tal proposes an option or asks for an impression
REF: 11.12.1, 11.12.2

Que tal commonly introduces a suggestion. It can be followed by a noun, an infinitive, or an infinitive with its own subject.
TL: Que tal um café? Que tal sair mais cedo? Que tal vocês começarem amanhã?
EN: How about a coffee? How about leaving earlier? How about you starting tomorrow?
[PAUSE:900]
With an existing person, thing, or event, que tal asks for an opinion or impression.
TL: Que tal o novo escritório? Que tal a viagem?
EN: How is the new office? How was the trip?
[PAUSE:950]
Context separates a proposed next step from a request for evaluation.
SHOW: que tal + new option -> suggestion · que tal + known referent -> opinion
Flip for the formal written degree word.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quão expresses degree mainly in writing
REF: 11.13.1, 11.13.2

Quão means how before an adjective or adverb, but it belongs mainly to written Portuguese and is especially common in embedded questions.
TL: O relatório mostra quão urgente é a mudança.
EN: The report shows how urgent the change is.
[PAUSE:900]
O quão adds emphasis in an embedded construction. Ordinary conversation usually chooses a different frame with quanto, como, or an adjective plus intensity expression.
TL: Ainda não sabemos o quão difícil será a tarefa.
EN: We still do not know just how difficult the task will be.
[PAUSE:950]
Portuguese interrogatives do not take the Spanish-style written accent; quão has its own lexical nasal spelling.
SHOW: formal degree -> quão + adjective/adverb · emphatic embedded degree -> o quão

[CARD]
TITLE: É que adds conversational focus without changing the answer sought
REF: 11.14.2, 11.14.3

Spoken questions often place é que after the interrogative, or foi que when the event is in the past. The added frame emphasizes the request but does not change its grammatical role.
TL: Onde é que eles moram? Quando foi que ela ligou?
EN: Where do they live? When did she call?
[PAUSE:900]
Portuguese does not use English-style auxiliary inversion. A pronoun subject normally remains before the finite verb.
TL: O que você quer? Por que ele saiu?
EN: What do you want? Why did he leave?
[PAUSE:950]
Build the ordinary Portuguese clause order around the missing element rather than copying English do, does, or did.
SHOW: interrogative + (é/foi) que + subject + verb · no do-support · pronoun subject before verb
Flip for noun subjects and question words left in place.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Noun subjects may follow some verbs; speech may leave the question word in place
REF: 11.14.4, 11.14.5, 11.14.6

With an intransitive verb, a noun subject may come before or after the verb. Inversion is especially natural with verbs of being, location, or movement and with reflexive constructions.
TL: Onde fica a estação? A que horas o filme acaba?
EN: Where is the station? What time does the film end?
[PAUSE:900]
Conversation can leave the interrogative where the answer would appear in an ordinary statement, usually with strong question intonation.
TL: Você vai voltar quando? Ela resolveu o problema como?
EN: When are you coming back? How did she solve the problem?
[PAUSE:950]
This in-place pattern is conversational emphasis, not the only neutral order.
SHOW: location/being verb -> verb + noun subject is natural · speech may leave interrogative in answer position

[CARD]
TITLE: An interrogative plus an infinitive can frame an open decision
REF: 11.14.7, 11.14.8

Place an interrogative before an infinitive to ask an impersonal question or identify the unresolved action.
TL: Como explicar isso? Que computador comprar?
EN: How can one explain that? Which computer should one buy?
[PAUSE:900]
The same pattern can be embedded after verbs such as saber, decidir, or dizer.
TL: Não sei como responder. Ela vai nos dizer o que fazer.
EN: I do not know how to answer. She will tell us what to do.
[PAUSE:950]
The infinitive carries no explicit subject unless the larger construction supplies one.
SHOW: interrogative + infinitive -> how/what/which to ... · saber/dizer + interrogative + infinitive
Flip for the existential pattern tested by the translation block.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ter or haver plus an interrogative can express an available means or reason
REF: 11.14.9

With ter or haver and an infinitive, interrogatives can name something available: onde for a place, como for a means, com que for an instrument, and por que for a reason.
TL: Tem onde sentar? Você tem com que escrever? Não há por que esperar.
EN: Is there somewhere to sit? Do you have something to write with? There is no reason to wait.
[PAUSE:900]
Não tem como is a frequent conversational conclusion meaning there is no way or it is impossible.
TL: Sem a senha, não tem como entrar.
EN: Without the password, there is no way to get in.
[PAUSE:950]
Read these as availability constructions rather than literal information questions.
SHOW: ter/haver + onde/com que/como/por que + infinitive -> place/tool/means/reason available

[CARD]
TITLE: Que turns a noun phrase or quality into an exclamation
REF: 12.1.1, 12.1.2

In an exclamation, que before a noun means what a or what. Portuguese does not insert the indefinite article used in the English version.
TL: Que surpresa! Que dia bonito!
EN: What a surprise! What a beautiful day!
[PAUSE:900]
Que can also introduce an adjective evaluation. With a noun followed by an adjective, mais may strengthen the highlighted quality.
TL: Que bom te ver! Que situação mais complicada!
EN: How good to see you! What a complicated situation!
[PAUSE:950]
Interrogative and exclamatory que have the same unaccented spelling in Portuguese; punctuation and intonation reveal the force.
SHOW: que + noun -> what a ...! · que + adjective -> how ...! · que + noun + mais + adjective -> stronger quality
Flip for exclamatory quantity and degree.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quanto agrees with a noun; como intensifies a clause
REF: 12.2.1, 12.2.2, 12.3

Before a noun, exclamatory quanto agrees in gender and number and highlights a striking amount.
TL: Quanta gente! Quantas boas lembranças!
EN: What a lot of people! So many good memories!
[PAUSE:900]
Without a noun, quanto can be an invariable adverb. Como before a clause intensifies a verb, adjective, or adverb.
TL: Quanto rimos! Como o tempo passa rápido! Como você fala bem!
EN: How much we laughed! Time passes so quickly! How well you speak!
[PAUSE:950]
These exclamations are common in speech. Portuguese uses no inverted opening mark and gives the exclamative words no extra written accent.
SHOW: quantity + noun -> quanto/a/os/as · verb degree -> quanto · clause degree -> como
