## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A non-finite form still has a job to do
REF: 22.1, 23.1, 24.1

The infinitive, past participle, and gerund do not carry an ordinary person ending. That does not make them interchangeable. Each packages an event in a different way.
TL: Resolver el problema exige paciencia.
EN: Solving the problem requires patience.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Hemos resuelto el problema.
EN: We have solved the problem.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Seguimos buscando otra solución.
EN: We are still looking for another solution.
[PAUSE:1000]
The infinitive names the event, the participle presents a result or builds a compound tense, and the gerund views an activity from within.
SHOW: infinitive = event · participle = result · gerund = unfolding activity
Flip for the decisions that keep the three forms apart.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Start from syntax, not an English ending
REF: 22.1, 23.1, 24.9

An English form ending in ing may correspond to a Spanish infinitive, noun, relative clause, adjective, or gerund. First ask what the phrase does in its sentence.
TL: Leer antes de dormir ayuda a desconectar.
EN: Reading before sleeping helps you switch off.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La mujer que espera junto a la puerta es la directora.
EN: The woman waiting by the door is the director.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Entró sonriendo.
EN: She came in smiling.
[PAUSE:1000]
Leer names an activity, que espera modifies a noun, and sonriendo modifies the manner of a verb. Form follows that relationship.
SHOW: name an event → infinitive · modify a noun → usually relative clause · modify a verb → gerund
Flip to choose between an infinitive and a finite que clause.

[CARD]
TITLE: One understood subject favors the infinitive
REF: 22.2.1, 22.3.2

When one participant performs both actions, Spanish commonly compresses the second action into an infinitive. Its understood subject comes from the governing clause.
TL: Clara prometió llamar esta tarde.
EN: Clara promised to call this afternoon.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Espero terminar el informe mañana.
EN: I hope to finish the report tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Clara is the one who will call, and the speaker is the one who hopes to finish. A perfect infinitive places the compressed event earlier.
TL: Clara afirma haber enviado el informe.
EN: Clara claims to have sent the report.
SHOW: same subject → infinitive · earlier event → haber + participle
Flip when another participant performs the second action.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A distinct subject normally needs a finite clause
REF: 22.2.3, 22.3.2

When the second action belongs to someone else, use que plus a finite verb. Mood follows the governing meaning: wishes, influence, reactions, and pending purpose commonly select the subjunctive.
TL: Clara quiere que Pablo llame esta tarde.
EN: Clara wants Pablo to call this afternoon.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Cerré la ventana antes de que empezara a llover.
EN: I closed the window before it started to rain.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not let an English possessive or object plus ing conceal the second subject. Make the participants explicit before choosing the construction.
SHOW: Clara acts → quiere llamar · Pablo acts → quiere que Pablo llame

[CARD]
TITLE: A governed infinitive may need a fixed link
REF: 22.2.2

The first verb determines whether the infinitive follows directly or through a preposition. Treat the combination as part of the verb's lexical pattern.
TL: Intentaron resolverlo, pero se negaron a improvisar.
EN: They tried to solve it, but refused to improvise.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Dejamos de discutir y empezamos a trabajar.
EN: We stopped arguing and started working.
[PAUSE:1000]
Changing the link can change the meaning. Pensar hacer algo expresses an intention, while pensar en hacerlo means to consider doing it.
SHOW: intentar + infinitive · negarse a · dejar de · pensar en
Flip for motion verbs and attached pronouns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Motion takes a, and pronouns stay with the verb group
REF: 14.3.4, 22.2.2

A verb of motion normally uses a before an infinitive that states the purpose of the movement.
TL: Bajé a recibir a los invitados.
EN: I went downstairs to welcome the guests.
[PAUSE:800]
With many tightly linked verb groups, an unstressed pronoun may attach to the infinitive or move before the finite verb without changing its role.
TL: Voy a explicártelo ahora. Te lo voy a explicar ahora.
EN: I am going to explain it to you now.
[PAUSE:1000]
Keep the whole pronoun cluster together. A reflexive pronoun must still agree with the understood subject of the infinitive.
SHOW: voy a explicártelo = te lo voy a explicar

[CARD]
TITLE: After a preposition, use an infinitive—not a gerund
REF: 22.3.1, 24.9.4

Spanish places an infinitive after prepositions and prepositional phrases, even where English uses an ing form.
TL: Salió sin despedirse y volvió para recoger las llaves.
EN: He left without saying goodbye and returned to collect the keys.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La multaron por conducir demasiado rápido.
EN: They fined her for driving too fast.
[PAUSE:1000]
The preposition contributes the relationship: sin marks absence, para purpose, and por a cause or motive. The infinitive carries any attached object or reflexive pronouns.
SHOW: sin despedirse · para recogerlas · por conducir
Flip for temporal links and subject changes.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Al marks a time boundary; a new subject reopens the clause
REF: 22.3.2, 22.3.3

Al plus an infinitive commonly means when or on doing something. Antes de, después de, hasta, and similar links also take an infinitive when the subject remains the same.
TL: Al abrir la puerta, Julia oyó un ruido.
EN: On opening the door, Julia heard a noise.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Julia esperó hasta que llegó el técnico.
EN: Julia waited until the technician arrived.
[PAUSE:1000]
Julia both opened and heard, so the infinitive is compact. The technician performs the arrival, so a finite clause makes that second subject visible.
SHOW: same subject → al salir / antes de salir · new subject → antes de que salga

[CARD]
TITLE: An infinitive can name an activity
REF: 22.6, 24.9.1

An infinitive phrase can serve as a subject, object, or complement much like a masculine singular noun. English often uses an ing form in the same positions.
TL: Viajar solo requiere cierta confianza.
EN: Travelling alone requires a certain confidence.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Mi actividad favorita es cocinar para amigos.
EN: My favorite activity is cooking for friends.
[PAUSE:1000]
The infinitive can still take its own objects, adverbs, and pronouns. Its subject is normally generic or recovered from context.
SHOW: fumar está prohibido · prefiero caminar · lo difícil es decidir
Flip for articles and agreement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The event is grammatically singular and masculine
REF: 22.6, 22.7

When an infinitive phrase is the subject, the predicative adjective normally appears in masculine singular. A definite article can turn an event into a more concrete style of action or manner.
TL: Es necesario revisar los datos.
EN: Reviewing the data is necessary.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: El hablar tan despacio le daba un aire solemne.
EN: The way he spoke so slowly gave him a solemn air.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not use a gerund merely because English names an activity with ing. The Spanish gerund is primarily adverbial or aspectual.
SHOW: revisar es necesario · el hablar pausado · not a free-standing gerund subject
Eight noun-like infinitive completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: De appears when an expressed subject is easy to act on
REF: 22.10

Compare two structures. If the infinitive phrase itself is the subject, no de appears after the adjective.
TL: Es difícil comprobar estos datos.
EN: It is difficult to verify these data.
[PAUSE:800]
If an expressed noun is the subject of ser but the understood object of the infinitive, adjective plus de presents its suitability or difficulty.
TL: Estos datos son difíciles de comprobar.
EN: These data are difficult to verify.
[PAUSE:1000]
The adjective agrees with the expressed noun; the infinitive keeps its non-finite form.
SHOW: es fácil resolver el problema · el problema es fácil de resolver
Flip for the passive-like interpretation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An active infinitive can carry a passive English reading
REF: 22.5, 22.10

Spanish often leaves the affected thing before an active-form infinitive. English may translate the relationship with a passive infinitive.
TL: Quedan tres solicitudes por revisar.
EN: Three applications remain to be reviewed.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Es una pieza imposible de sustituir.
EN: It is a part that is impossible to replace.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not add a reflexive passive mechanically. Read the role of the expressed noun: solicitudes and pieza are what someone will review or replace.
SHOW: noun + por/de + active infinitive → often passive interpretation
Eight sentence transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Haber keeps its participle invariant
REF: 23.2.1, 18.1.3

Regular past participles end in ado after ar verbs and ido after er or ir verbs. Common irregulars include hecho, dicho, visto, puesto, escrito, vuelto, roto, abierto, and muerto.
TL: Hemos escrito las cartas y las hemos puesto en el sobre.
EN: We have written the letters and put them in the envelope.
[PAUSE:1000]
In a compound tense with haber, the participle does not agree with the subject or a preceding object. Las cartas does not turn escrito into escritas.
SHOW: he escrito · hemos escrito · las he escrito
Flip when the participle behaves as an adjective.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An adjectival participle agrees with its noun
REF: 23.2.1, 23.3

Outside a compound haber tense, a participle can describe a resulting state and then agrees like an adjective.
TL: Las cartas ya están escritas y las puertas permanecen abiertas.
EN: The letters are already written and the doors remain open.
[PAUSE:900]
Some verbs distinguish a verbal participle from a related lexical adjective.
TL: Han despertado al niño, pero la niña sigue despierta.
EN: They have woken the boy, but the girl is still awake.
[PAUSE:1000]
Let the auxiliary diagnose the role before choosing agreement or a double form.
SHOW: haber + escrito · estar + escrita · despertar → despertado / despierto
Eighteen form and agreement decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: A participle clause presents a prior completed state
REF: 23.5

A detached participle can compress a time, cause, or condition whose event is already complete relative to the main clause. It agrees with its own noun.
TL: Terminada la reunión, cerraron la sala.
EN: Once the meeting was over, they closed the room.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Vistas las pruebas, el comité cambió de opinión.
EN: Having seen the evidence, the committee changed its mind.
[PAUSE:1000]
This compact pattern is especially common in written and procedural language. Expand it mentally with una vez que or cuando to confirm the time relationship.
SHOW: participle + noun, main clause · completed before the main event
Flip for forms ending in nte.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Nte forms are lexical adjectives, not productive gerunds
REF: 23.6, 24.3

Forms such as interesante, convincente, and agobiante can modify nouns, but the pattern is not freely available for every verb. Learn each established adjective as vocabulary.
TL: Presentaron una propuesta convincente y un análisis revelador.
EN: They presented a convincing proposal and a revealing analysis.
[PAUSE:900]
These adjectives normally keep one form for masculine and feminine, while changing for plural number.
TL: una situación preocupante; dos situaciones preocupantes
EN: a worrying situation; two worrying situations
[PAUSE:1000]
If no established adjective exists, choose a relative clause or another lexical adjective rather than inventing an nte form.
SHOW: convincente · preocupante · saliente · dictionary-governed, not automatic
Eighteen participle-clause and adjective choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Build the gerund, then check its time relation
REF: 24.2, 24.4.1

Ar verbs normally replace ar with ando; er and ir verbs use iendo. Stem and spelling changes produce forms such as durmiendo, pidiendo, leyendo, oyendo, and construyendo.
TL: Entró hablando por teléfono y salió sonriendo.
EN: She came in talking on the phone and left smiling.
[PAUSE:1000]
The gerund normally overlaps the main event or sits very close to it. It should not introduce a later consequence as though it were the next verb in a narrative.
TL: El edificio se derrumbó y bloqueó la calle.
EN: The building collapsed and blocked the street.
SHOW: simultaneous activity → gerund · later result → finite coordinated verb
Flip for the relationships an adverbial gerund can express.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Context supplies time, method, cause, condition, or concession
REF: 24.4.2, 24.4.3, 24.4.4, 24.4.5

The same gerund form can link its event to the main verb in several ways. Paraphrase it with a finite clause to expose the intended relationship.
TL: Practicando cada día, mejorarás la pronunciación.
EN: By practicing every day, you will improve your pronunciation.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Aun conociendo el riesgo, aceptó el encargo.
EN: Even though she knew the risk, she accepted the assignment.
[PAUSE:1000]
The understood subject normally matches the subject of the main verb. If that link becomes unclear, a finite clause is safer.
SHOW: practicando = si practicas / mediante la práctica · aun sabiendo = aunque sabes

[CARD]
TITLE: A gerund usually modifies a verb, not a noun
REF: 24.3, 24.6

English freely places an ing form after a noun. Careful Spanish normally uses a relative clause because a gerund needs a verbal anchor.
TL: Buscamos una caja que contenga los documentos.
EN: We are looking for a box containing the documents.
[PAUSE:800]
A gerund can qualify the object of verbs of seeing, finding, depicting, or imagining because the main verb supplies that anchor.
TL: Encontraron al guardia durmiendo en la oficina.
EN: They found the guard sleeping in the office.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: bare noun modifier → relative clause · object of perception/finding → gerund possible
Flip for the difference between seeing an event whole and in progress.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Perception contrasts a complete event with an unfolding scene
REF: 22.2.4, 24.7

After verbs such as ver and oír, an infinitive tends to present the perceived event as a complete occurrence. A gerund places the participant inside an activity already in progress.
TL: Vi a Marta cruzar la plaza.
EN: I saw Marta cross the square.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Vi a Marta cruzando la plaza.
EN: I saw Marta while she was crossing the square.
[PAUSE:1000]
When the gerund could attach to the wrong participant, use an infinitive or a finite clause to restore clarity.
SHOW: vi cruzar = whole event · vi cruzando = scene in progress

[CARD]
TITLE: A finite verb plus gerund adds an aspectual lens
REF: 24.8.1, 24.8.2, 24.8.3

Several verbs combine with a gerund to describe the shape of an activity. Andar can suggest repeated or aimless activity, ir gradual development, and llevar a duration continuing to the reference point.
TL: Anda preguntando lo mismo en todas las oficinas.
EN: He keeps going around asking the same thing in every office.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Llevamos dos meses preparando el informe.
EN: We have been preparing the report for two months.
[PAUSE:1000]
These are not decorative alternatives to estar. Each finite verb contributes its own viewpoint.
SHOW: andar = repeated · ir = gradual · llevar + time = duration so far
Flip for continuation, accumulation, and eventual outcome.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Seguir, venir, and acabar trace the activity's path
REF: 24.8.4, 24.8.5, 24.8.6, 24.8.7

Seguir or continuar says that an activity persists. Venir can show a development accumulating over time. Acabar or terminar presents the activity as the eventual outcome.
TL: Seguimos buscando una solución y venimos observando cierta mejora.
EN: We are still seeking a solution and have been observing some improvement.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Después de muchas dudas, acabaron aceptando la propuesta.
EN: After much hesitation, they ended up accepting the proposal.
[PAUSE:1000]
Quedarse plus gerund focuses on remaining in an activity or position.
SHOW: seguir = continue · venir = accumulate · acabar = end up · quedarse = remain
Fifteen aspectual-periphrasis items follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: English ing has no single Spanish translation
REF: 23.6, 24.3, 24.9

Before translating, classify the English form. An activity used as a subject or object often becomes an infinitive. A noun modifier usually becomes a relative clause, an established adjective, or a lexical noun phrase.
TL: Learning languages takes time.
EN: Aprender idiomas lleva tiempo.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: a convincing argument; a sleeping bag
EN: un argumento convincente; un saco de dormir
[PAUSE:1000]
Only use a gerund when it has a Spanish verbal anchor and expresses an unfolding, simultaneous, or aspectually framed activity.
SHOW: activity → infinitive · modifier → adjective/relative clause/noun phrase · verbal circumstance → gerund
Flip for a compact final checklist.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Let the sentence choose the form
REF: 22.3.1, 23.3, 24.9

Ask four questions in order. Is the form naming an event? Is it presenting a completed result? Is it modifying a noun? Or is it describing how an anchored verb unfolds?
TL: Sin revisar los datos, dejaron varias preguntas sin contestar.
EN: Without reviewing the data, they left several questions unanswered.
[PAUSE:900]
Revisar follows a preposition and names an event. Contestar appears in a passive-like infinitive phrase. Neither is a gerund simply because English may use ing.
TL: Salieron comentando los resultados.
EN: They left discussing the results.
[PAUSE:1000]
Here comentando overlaps salieron and has a clear verbal anchor, so the gerund is natural.
SHOW: role first · subject link second · time relation third · lexical convention last
