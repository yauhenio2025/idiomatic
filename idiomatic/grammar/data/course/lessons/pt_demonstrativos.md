## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A demonstrative agrees with the noun it points to
REF: 8.2

The three demonstrative families are este, esse, and aquele. Each one changes for the gender and number of the noun: -e marks masculine singular, -a feminine singular, and -s adds plural.
TL: esta proposta · estes relatórios · aquela sala · aqueles arquivos
EN: this proposal · these reports · that room over there · those files over there
[PAUSE:900]
The same forms can stand before a noun or replace it. When the noun disappears, its gender and number still control the pronoun.
TL: Quero esta pasta, não aquela.
EN: I want this folder, not that one over there.
[PAUSE:950]
Choose the referent first, then carry its agreement onto the pointing word.
SHOW: M SG: este/esse/aquele · F SG: esta/essa/aquela · add -s for plural
Flip for the prepositions that fuse with these forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Em and de contract with every demonstrative series
REF: 8.2

Em and de merge with a following demonstrative. Build neste, nesse, naquele and deste, desse, daquele, with the same feminine and plural endings.
TL: O prazo está neste contrato; os anexos daquele processo chegaram depois.
EN: The deadline is in this contract; the attachments from that case over there arrived later.
[PAUSE:900]
The preposition a contracts only with the aquele family and carries a grave accent.
TL: Dei prioridade àquelas solicitações.
EN: I gave priority to those requests over there.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not leave the preposition separate when the contraction is required.
SHOW: em + esse -> nesse · de + aquela -> daquela · a + aquele -> àquele
Twelve source forms now isolate agreement across the three families.

[CARD]
TITLE: Everyday speech contrasts esse with aquele
REF: 8.3.1, 8.3.2

In ordinary Brazilian speech, esse points near the listener and also does most of the work of English this. Aquele places the referent away from both speaker and listener.
TL: Esse caderno aí é seu? E aquele na estante?
EN: Is that notebook by you yours? And what about that one on the shelf over there?
[PAUSE:900]
Spoken este is uncommon. Add aqui or daqui to esse when you need to mark the speaker's side clearly.
TL: Essa cadeira aqui está livre; essa daí está reservada.
EN: This chair here is free; that one by you is reserved.
[PAUSE:950]
In conversation, let the adverb supply the fine distance contrast instead of forcing a literary three-way system.
SHOW: near speaker in speech -> esse aqui · near listener -> esse aí · remote -> aquele
Flip from physical distance to discourse distance.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Esse recalls; aquele reaches farther back
REF: 8.3.3, 8.3.4

Esse commonly resumes something just mentioned by either participant. It can point to a decision, moment, problem, or other nonphysical referent.
TL: Você mencionou uma falha. Quando percebeu esse problema?
EN: You mentioned a fault. When did you notice that problem?
[PAUSE:900]
Aquele can recover a person, thing, or time treated as remote but already shared. Once it is introduced, esse can keep it active in the conversation.
TL: Lembra daquela pesquisadora? Essa mesma; ela voltou ao laboratório.
EN: Do you remember that researcher? That very one; she returned to the laboratory.
[PAUSE:950]
Distance can be conversational rather than measurable: newly active material tends toward esse, shared remote memory toward aquele.
SHOW: just mentioned -> esse · shared remote memory -> aquele · established again -> esse mesmo
The clean source dialogue follows the movement from remote identification to active reference.

[CARD]
TITLE: Formal writing preserves a job for este
REF: 8.3.2, 8.3.11, 8.3.12

Written Portuguese can maintain the full contrast. Esse points back to material already stated, while este can announce information that follows.
TL: Houve um atraso; esse problema já foi resolvido.
EN: There was a delay; that problem has already been resolved.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: A equipe confirmou este resultado: o sistema está estável.
EN: The team confirmed this result: the system is stable.
[PAUSE:950]
In a formal contrast between two previously named referents, aquele identifies the former and este the latter. This compact pattern belongs to careful writing, not routine conversation.
SHOW: written backward link -> esse · written forward link -> este · former/latter -> aquele/este
Flip for the constructions that let a demonstrative carry a noun phrase.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A demonstrative can select the one that fits
REF: 8.3.5, 8.3.6, 8.3.7, 8.3.8, 8.3.9

Aquele que and its agreeing forms mean the one or those who. A preposition still contracts before the demonstrative when the larger sentence requires it.
TL: O prêmio será entregue àqueles que concluíram o projeto.
EN: The prize will be given to those who completed the project.
[PAUSE:900]
Demonstratives can precede possessives and words such as outro or todos. They also build useful partitive and emphatic frames.
TL: todos esses dados · aquela outra versão · um dia desses · um susto daqueles
EN: all these data · that other version · one of these days · one tremendous fright
[PAUSE:950]
Keep agreement on the demonstrative even when several modifiers share the noun phrase.
SHOW: aquele(s) que = the one(s) who · demonstrative + possessive/other · um ... desses/daqueles
Eight source review choices now test the full written distance contrast.

[CARD]
TITLE: Isto, isso, and aquilo point without naming a noun
REF: 8.4

The neuter demonstratives refer to an unidentified object, a fact, an idea, or a whole statement. They never accompany a noun and have no gender or plural forms.
TL: O que é aquilo perto da janela? Isso é um sensor.
EN: What is that near the window? That is a sensor.
[PAUSE:900]
In speech, isso normally covers both this and that; isto is chiefly written or emphatic. Aquilo preserves the remote contrast.
TL: Não entendi isso. Você pode explicar de novo?
EN: I did not understand that. Can you explain it again?
[PAUSE:950]
If a known noun is being replaced, use an agreeing demonstrative instead. If the referent is not yet classified or is propositional, use the neuter series.
SHOW: unknown thing/idea -> isto/isso/aquilo · known M noun -> este/esse/aquele · no neuter plural
Flip for contraction and emphasis with neuter forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Neuter forms contract and combine with tudo
REF: 8.4.2, 8.4.5

The same prepositions contract with neuter demonstratives: nisto, nisso, naquilo; disto, disso, daquilo; and àquilo.
TL: Não concordo com isso, mas ainda preciso pensar naquilo.
EN: I do not agree with that, but I still need to think about that other matter.
[PAUSE:900]
Tudo before a neuter form means all this or all that. Putting tudo afterward adds colloquial emphasis.
TL: Tudo isso mudou rapidamente. Você guardou isso tudo?
EN: All that changed quickly. Did you keep all of that?
[PAUSE:950]
The contraction identifies the grammatical relation; word order around tudo changes the emphasis, not the referent's number.
SHOW: em + isso -> nisso · de + aquilo -> daquilo · tudo isso · isso tudo

[CARD]
TITLE: Aqui, aí, ali, and lá map the conversation space
REF: 8.5.1, 8.5.2

Aqui locates something by the speaker, and aí locates it by the listener. Ali points to a visible place away from both; lá is farther away, out of sight, or a broader previously named place.
TL: Deixe a caixa aqui; o manual está aí com você; o elevador fica ali.
EN: Leave the box here; the manual is there with you; the elevator is over there in sight.
[PAUSE:900]
Combine these adverbs with esse or aquele when the noun phrase alone would not make the distance precise.
TL: Esse documento aqui é o original; aquele prédio lá é o arquivo central.
EN: This document here is the original; that building over there is the central archive.
[PAUSE:950]
Choose from the speaker-listener geometry before choosing the English word here or there.
SHOW: speaker -> aqui · listener -> aí · visible remote -> ali · distant/out of sight -> lá
Flip for movement and time.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Cá and lá can mark direction, contrast, and time
REF: 8.5.3, 8.5.5, 8.5.6, 8.5.7, 8.5.8

Cá is a movement-oriented alternative to aqui, especially with vir. In a contrast with lá, it identifies the speaker's side.
TL: Venha para cá; os comprovantes estão do lado de lá do balcão.
EN: Come over here; the receipts are on the far side of the counter.
[PAUSE:900]
The spatial system extends into time and discourse. Daqui a introduces a future interval, aí can mark the next event or result, and até lá looks ahead to a remote point.
TL: Daqui a três dias teremos a resposta; até lá, vou aguardar.
EN: In three days we will have the answer; until then, I will wait.
[PAUSE:950]
Movement, location, and time reuse the same viewpoint rather than three unrelated vocabulary lists.
SHOW: vir para cá · side here/there -> de cá/de lá · daqui a + interval · até lá
Seven source adverb choices now test the conversational map.

[CARD]
TITLE: A possessive agrees with the possession, not the possessor
REF: 9.2, 9.3.1

Meu, seu, and nosso change to match the gender and number of the noun possessed. The identity or number of the owner does not supply those endings.
TL: minhas anotações · nosso projeto · suas chaves
EN: my notes · our project · your keys
[PAUSE:900]
The same forms can replace a noun, but a pronominal possessive normally carries the definite article.
TL: Meu relatório está pronto; o seu ainda precisa de revisão.
EN: My report is ready; yours still needs revision.
[PAUSE:950]
Recover the omitted noun before choosing the article and possessive ending.
SHOW: owner -> meu/seu/nosso · possessed noun -> -o/-a/-os/-as · noun omitted -> article + possessive
Flip for the article before an overt noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The article is optional before many possessive noun phrases
REF: 9.3.1, 9.3.6

In conversational subject and object phrases, a definite article before a possessive is often a matter of speaker preference.
TL: Minha irmã chegou cedo. A minha irmã chegou cedo.
EN: My sister arrived early. Both Portuguese versions are possible.
[PAUSE:900]
After a preposition that normally contracts with an article, speech includes the article and its contraction. Formal writing may omit it more freely.
TL: Falamos do nosso projeto e pensamos na sua proposta.
EN: We talked about our project and thought about your proposal.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not confuse optional article choice with possessive agreement; agreement remains mandatory.
SHOW: speech: (o) meu arquivo · de + o nosso -> do nosso · em + a sua -> na sua
Twelve source forms now build the complete article, possessive, and noun phrase.

[CARD]
TITLE: Spoken seu points to the listener
REF: 9.3.2, 9.3.3

In everyday Brazilian Portuguese, seu and sua are understood as your. Express his, hers, and theirs with a noun phrase ending in dele, dela, deles, or delas.
TL: Sua mochila está aqui; a mochila dele ficou no carro.
EN: Your backpack is here; his backpack stayed in the car.
[PAUSE:900]
Written Portuguese can use bare seu for a third-person possessor, but this may be ambiguous. Dele and the other de forms keep the owner explicit in either register.
TL: A diretora apresentou seu relatório, mas o relatório dela ainda causou dúvidas.
EN: The director presented her report, but her report still raised questions.
[PAUSE:950]
In speech, treat seu as second person unless an explicit de phrase says otherwise.
SHOW: spoken your -> seu/sua · his/her/their -> noun + dele/dela/deles/delas · writing permits bare seu
Flip for plural and respectful addressees.

[SIDE]
TITLE: De vocês removes second-person ambiguity
REF: 9.3.4, 9.3.5

When addressing more than one person, use de vocês rather than seu. Respectful address likewise keeps the title inside a de phrase.
TL: A proposta de vocês está pronta? Qual é a opinião da senhora?
EN: Is your proposal ready? What is your opinion, ma'am?
[PAUSE:900]
With a gente, conversational Portuguese allows either nosso or da gente, while the verb still remains singular.
TL: A gente vai apresentar o nosso plano; depois eles avaliam a ideia da gente.
EN: We are going to present our plan; then they will assess our idea.
[PAUSE:950]
Choose the possessor expression from person and register, then make any possessive adjective agree with the possessed noun.
SHOW: plural your -> de vocês · respectful your -> do senhor/da senhora · our with a gente -> nosso or da gente
Eight source completions now contrast the conversational possessor choices.

[CARD]
TITLE: A possessive pronoun carries the hidden noun's agreement
REF: 9.3.1, 9.3.2

When a possessive replaces a noun, its article and ending reveal that noun's gender and number.
TL: Minha mesa fica perto da sua; meus arquivos estão junto dos dela.
EN: My desk is near yours; my files are beside hers.
[PAUSE:900]
The third-person de forms follow an article that represents the omitted noun: o dele, a dela, os deles, as delas. The de phrase itself does not agree.
TL: Nosso resultado foi melhor do que o deles.
EN: Our result was better than theirs.
[PAUSE:950]
Say the hidden noun mentally, select its article, and only then attach the correct ownership expression.
SHOW: hidden carro -> o meu/o dele · hidden pastas -> as suas/as delas
Flip to possession placed after an overt noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: After a noun, a possessive means one connected to that person
REF: 9.3.7, 9.3.8

A possessive after a noun builds the equivalent of English of mine, of yours, or of ours. It still agrees with the noun.
TL: uma colega minha · dois clientes nossos · aquela sugestão sua
EN: a colleague of mine · two clients of ours · that suggestion of yours
[PAUSE:900]
After words such as foto, notícia, lembrança, and saudade, the same position can identify the person represented or concerned rather than an owner.
TL: Recebi notícias suas e encontrei uma foto nossa.
EN: I received news about you and found a photo of us.
[PAUSE:950]
Use the surrounding noun's meaning to distinguish ownership from a relation such as about you or depicting us.
SHOW: noun + agreeing possessive -> of mine/yours/ours · foto nossa -> photo of us
Eight source pronoun completions now require the hidden noun's agreement.

[CARD]
TITLE: Portuguese often uses an article where English uses a possessive
REF: 9.4

When the possessor is the sentence subject and the relationship is obvious, Portuguese normally uses the definite article with body parts, clothing, relatives, and personal belongings.
TL: Clara levantou a mão, vestiu o casaco e pegou a bolsa.
EN: Clara raised her hand, put on her coat, and picked up her bag.
[PAUSE:900]
The same choice works whenever the context makes ownership predictable.
TL: Você pode deixar o carro na garagem.
EN: You can leave your car in the garage.
[PAUSE:950]
Add a possessive only when contrast or ambiguity makes the owner informative.
SHOW: obvious subject-owner -> definite article · contrasted/unclear owner -> possessive
Flip for the indirect-object construction.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An object pronoun can identify the affected owner
REF: 9.4.2

Especially in writing, an indirect object pronoun can identify whose body part or possession is affected while the noun keeps a definite article.
TL: O médico lhe examinou os olhos.
EN: The doctor examined his or her eyes.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: O barulho nos tirou a concentração.
EN: The noise broke our concentration.
[PAUSE:950]
The pronoun marks the affected person; the article presents the affected noun as identifiable in that relation.
SHOW: indirect object + verb + article + possessed noun · owner stays outside the noun phrase

[CARD]
TITLE: Próprio separates ownership from independence
REF: 9.5

Before a noun, próprio reinforces a possessive and agrees with the possession. When the owner is already the subject, the article can replace the possessive.
TL: Apresentamos nossas próprias conclusões. Ela trouxe o próprio computador.
EN: We presented our own conclusions. She brought her own computer.
[PAUSE:900]
After a noun, próprio means an independent one of one's own, without another possessive.
TL: Eles abriram uma empresa própria.
EN: They opened a company of their own.
[PAUSE:950]
Position tells you whether próprio reinforces an explicit owner or characterizes the possession as independent.
SHOW: possessive + próprio + noun · subject-owned -> article + próprio · noun + próprio -> one of one's own
Flip for ownership after ser and two special second-person forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ser, teu, and direct-address seu complete the system
REF: 9.6, 9.7, 9.8

After ser, a possessive can state ownership without an article. Add the article when contrasting a particular item with another.
TL: Esses documentos são meus. Esta cópia é a sua, não a dele.
EN: Those documents are mine. This copy is yours, not his.
[PAUSE:900]
Colloquial teu and tua can replace seu and sua for your. In a direct insult or affectionate label, seu or sua stands before the addressed noun with the force of you.
TL: Esse é teu casaco? Seu distraído, você esqueceu a chave de novo!
EN: Is that your coat? You absent-minded fellow, you forgot the key again!
[PAUSE:950]
Keep ordinary ownership, contrastive ownership, and direct address as separate constructions.
SHOW: X é meu · contrasted -> X é o meu · colloquial your -> teu · direct address -> seu + noun
