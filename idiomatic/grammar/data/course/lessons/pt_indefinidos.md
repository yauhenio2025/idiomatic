## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Todos/as takes the article before a plural noun
REF: 13.1.1, 13.1.2, 13.1.3

Before a plural noun, todos or todas means all and is followed by the definite article. It agrees with the noun in gender.
TL: Todos os documentos chegaram. Todas as janelas estavam abertas.
EN: All the documents arrived. All the windows were open.
[PAUSE:900]
As a pronoun, todos can mean everyone or they all; context supplies the group.
TL: Todos concordaram. Meus colegas chegaram e todos concordaram.
EN: Everyone agreed. My colleagues arrived, and they all agreed.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not copy English by dropping the article before the plural noun.
SHOW: all + plural noun -> todos os / todas as · pronoun -> todos/todas
Flip for the singular patterns with todo.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Todo/a plus a singular noun contrasts whole with every
REF: 13.1.5, 13.1.6, 13.1.7, 13.1.8, 13.1.10

With an article or equivalent determiner, todo or toda means the whole amount. It can also follow the noun.
TL: Li todo o relatório. Trabalhei o dia todo.
EN: I read the whole report. I worked all day.
[PAUSE:900]
Without the article, todo or toda before a singular noun means every. A plural alternative uses todos/as plus the article.
TL: Ela liga todo dia. Ela liga todos os dias.
EN: She calls every day. She calls every day.
[PAUSE:950]
Before an adjective, agreeing todo/a can intensify the resulting state.
TL: Ela ficou toda molhada.
EN: She got completely wet.
SHOW: todo o dia / o dia todo = the whole day · todo dia = every day
The first source set tests this contrast together with tudo.

[CARD]
TITLE: Tudo is an invariable pronoun for everything
REF: 13.2.1, 13.2.2, 13.2.3, 13.2.4

Tudo refers to things collectively and never changes for gender or number. It can mean everything, all of it, or all of them when the referents are things.
TL: Tudo mudou. Preparei a comida, mas eles não comeram tudo.
EN: Everything changed. I prepared the food, but they did not eat it all.
[PAUSE:900]
An adjective that characterizes tudo normally follows de.
TL: Desejo tudo de bom para vocês.
EN: I wish you all the best.
[PAUSE:950]
Use todos or todo with an expressed noun or a group of people; use tudo when no noun is expressed and the meaning is everything.
SHOW: everything/all of it -> tudo · all the files -> todos os arquivos · everyone -> todos/todo mundo
Flip from total scope to distribution among members.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ambos and cada distribute a set in different ways
REF: 13.3.1, 13.3.2, 13.3.4, 13.4.1, 13.4.2, 13.4.4, 13.4.5

Ambos or ambas means both. Before a noun it requires the definite article, though conversation often uses os dois or as duas instead.
TL: Ambas as propostas são viáveis. Já li os dois relatórios.
EN: Both proposals are viable. I have already read both reports.
[PAUSE:900]
Cada is invariable and selects members one by one. Use cada um or cada uma when the noun is not expressed.
TL: Cada aluno recebeu uma senha. Cada uma escolheu um tema.
EN: Each student received a password. Each woman chose a topic.
[PAUSE:950]
Cada also forms rates and intervals.
TL: Os ingressos custam vinte reais cada. O exame é feito a cada seis meses.
EN: The tickets cost twenty reais each. The exam is done every six months.
SHOW: both + noun -> ambos/as os/as · each + noun -> cada · each one -> cada um/uma

[CARD]
TITLE: Qualquer leaves the choice unrestricted
REF: 13.5.1, 13.5.2, 13.5.3, 13.5.4

Before a noun, qualquer means any in the sense of no matter which. The formal plural is quaisquer, but speech often avoids it.
TL: Você pode escolher qualquer caminho. Tomaremos quaisquer medidas necessárias.
EN: You may choose any route. We will take any necessary measures.
[PAUSE:900]
Without a noun, use qualquer um or qualquer uma for any one or either one. Qualquer um can also mean anyone in general.
TL: Qual você prefere? Qualquer um. Qualquer um entende essa regra.
EN: Which do you prefer? Either one. Anyone understands that rule.
[PAUSE:950]
After an indefinite noun, qualquer can mean ordinary or random rather than unrestricted choice.
TL: Este não é um problema qualquer.
EN: This is no ordinary problem.
SHOW: any + noun -> qualquer · any one -> qualquer um/uma · plural formal -> quaisquer
Flip for someone, something, and some amount.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Alguém names a person; algum agrees with a noun
REF: 13.6, 13.7.1, 13.7.2, 13.7.3, 13.8.1, 13.8.3, 13.9.1

Use alguém for an unspecified person. Use algum, alguma, alguns, or algumas before a noun or as an agreeing pronoun.
TL: Preciso de alguém para ajudar. Você prevê algum problema? Tirei algumas fotos.
EN: I need someone to help. Do you foresee any problem? I took some photos.
[PAUSE:900]
For an unspecified thing, alguma coisa is the ordinary spoken choice; algo is more formal. With de plus an adjective, the adjective is masculine singular.
TL: Há alguma coisa de errado aqui. O relatório revela algo importante.
EN: There is something wrong here. The report reveals something important.
[PAUSE:950]
After a noun in a negative sentence, algum strengthens the negative.
TL: Não há motivo algum para preocupação.
EN: There is no reason whatsoever for concern.
SHOW: person -> alguém · noun/agreement -> algum/a(s) · thing -> alguma coisa · formal thing -> algo
The broad source set now tests these choices with ambos, cada, outro, and tal.

[CARD]
TITLE: Outro normally means another without um
REF: 13.10.1, 13.10.2, 13.10.3, 13.10.4

Outro and its agreeing forms mean other or another. For another one, Portuguese normally omits the indefinite article.
TL: Precisamos de outra cadeira. Estas chaves não servem; tente as outras.
EN: We need another chair. These keys do not work; try the others.
[PAUSE:900]
Before a number, use plural outros or outras and agree it with the counted noun.
TL: O projeto levará outras seis semanas.
EN: The project will take another six weeks.
[PAUSE:950]
Reciprocal expressions agree with their participants and preserve the preposition required by the relation.
TL: Os colegas ajudam uns aos outros. Elas gostam uma da outra.
EN: The colleagues help one another. They like each other.
SHOW: another chair -> outra cadeira · another six weeks -> outras seis semanas · each other -> um(a) ... outro(a)
Flip for the formal and idiomatic uses of tal.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Tal points to a kind, a prior mention, or a name
REF: 13.11.1, 13.11.2

Um tal or uma tal can mean such a thing of that kind, while bare tal often points back to the specific thing already mentioned.
TL: Um tal erro seria grave. Como ele cometeu tal erro?
EN: Such an error would be serious. How did he make that error?
[PAUSE:900]
Use tais como to introduce examples and como tal for as such. Do not use tal to intensify an adjective; use tão.
TL: Frutas tais como manga são populares. O aluguel é tão alto.
EN: Fruits such as mangoes are popular. The rent is so high.
[PAUSE:950]
Um tal de introduces someone or something merely identified by a name, often with distance from the speaker.
TL: Um tal de Renato ligou para você.
EN: Someone called Renato phoned you.
SHOW: such a noun -> um(a) tal · this previously mentioned noun -> tal · such as -> tais como · so + adjective -> tão

[CARD]
TITLE: Quantity words agree with nouns but adverbs stay masculine singular
REF: 13.12.1, 13.12.2, 13.12.3

Before a noun, muito, pouco, and tanto agree in gender and number. As pronouns they preserve the agreement of the understood noun.
TL: Temos muita paciência e poucos recursos. Duzentas páginas? Não esperava tantas.
EN: We have a lot of patience and few resources. Two hundred pages? I did not expect so many.
[PAUSE:900]
As adverbs modifying a verb, adjective, or adverb, the forms muito, pouco, and tanto are invariable.
TL: Ela trabalha muito. A tarefa é muito difícil. Choveu tanto ontem.
EN: She works a lot. The task is very difficult. It rained so much yesterday.
[PAUSE:950]
Look for an expressed or understood noun. If there is none, keep the masculine singular adverb.
SHOW: muita água / poucos clientes / tantas páginas · trabalha muito / pouco provável / choveu tanto
Flip for the contrast between scarcity and a small positive amount.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Pouco and um pouco point in different directions
REF: 13.12.2

Pouco without the article presents an amount as small or insufficient. English often translates it as not much or not very.
TL: Dormi pouco. Sobrou pouca comida. O argumento é pouco convincente.
EN: I did not sleep much. Not much food remained. The argument is not very convincing.
[PAUSE:900]
Um pouco presents a small positive amount: a little or a bit.
TL: Dormi um pouco. Falo um pouco de português.
EN: I slept a little. I speak a little Portuguese.
[PAUSE:950]
The article changes the viewpoint, not merely the size of the amount.
SHOW: pouco = little/not much · um pouco = a little/some · pouca + F noun · poucos/poucas + plural noun
The quantity translation set follows after the remaining invariable forms.

[CARD]
TITLE: Mais and menos never agree; bastante usually stays singular
REF: 13.12.4, 13.12.5, 13.12.6

Mais and menos are invariable before singular or plural nouns and as pronouns or adverbs.
TL: Precisamos de mais cadeiras e menos ruído. Quer um pouco mais?
EN: We need more chairs and less noise. Would you like a little more?
[PAUSE:900]
Bastante usually stays singular for plenty of and as an adverb, although agreeing bastantes is possible with plural nouns. Vários and várias agree and mean several.
TL: Há bastante espaço. Gostamos bastante do hotel. Visitamos várias cidades.
EN: There is plenty of space. We really liked the hotel. We visited several cities.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not force gender agreement onto mais, menos, or adverbial bastante.
SHOW: mais cadeiras · menos dinheiro · bastante espaço · bastante útil · vários/várias
Flip for the two Portuguese strategies behind English else.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Else becomes mais for addition and outro for a different choice
REF: 13.14.1, 13.14.2

When else means in addition, use mais with the indefinite expression. Its position can vary with alguém.
TL: Vem mais alguém? Você quer mais alguma coisa? Não sobrou nada mais.
EN: Is anyone else coming? Do you want anything else? Nothing else remained.
[PAUSE:900]
When else means a different person, thing, or place, use an expression with outro.
TL: Falei com outra pessoa. Vamos tentar em outro lugar.
EN: I spoke with someone else. Let us try somewhere else.
[PAUSE:950]
Ask whether the meaning adds another member to the same set or replaces it with a different choice.
SHOW: in addition -> mais alguém / algo mais · different -> outra pessoa / outra coisa / em outro lugar
Flip for a final selection routine across the unit.

[CARD]
TITLE: Let reference, agreement, and scope select the indefinite
REF: 13.1, 13.2, 13.4, 13.5, 13.6, 13.7, 13.8, 13.10, 13.12, 13.14

First identify what is unspecified: a person, a thing, a member of a set, or an amount. Then ask whether a noun is present and whether the form must agree with it.
TL: Alguém deixou alguma coisa em cada mesa.
EN: Someone left something on each table.
[PAUSE:900]
Next decide the scope: total, distributive, unrestricted, small, or additional.
TL: Todos os alunos receberam um arquivo, mas poucos leram tudo.
EN: All the students received a file, but few read everything.
[PAUSE:950]
Finish by checking the structure that Portuguese requires: the article after todos/as, no article in todo dia, agreement before a noun, and invariable adverbs.
SHOW: person -> alguém · thing -> alguma coisa · total -> todo/tudo · each -> cada · any -> qualquer · amount -> muito/pouco/mais/menos
Flip for the article and agreement checks that prevent the most common errors.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Portuguese makes total scope visible in the noun phrase
REF: 13.1, 13.2, 13.3, 13.4

English all can hide several Portuguese structures. Match the intended scope before translating it.
TL: Todo o trabalho terminou. Todos os técnicos saíram. Tudo terminou.
EN: All the work was finished. All the technicians left. Everything ended.
[PAUSE:900]
For distribution, cada keeps the members separate; for a two-member total, ambos/as or os dois/as duas keeps the pair together.
TL: Cada técnico recebeu uma cópia. Ambos os técnicos assinaram.
EN: Each technician received a copy. Both technicians signed.
[PAUSE:950]
Check for an expressed noun, its number, and whether the meaning is total or distributive.
SHOW: whole + SG -> todo/a + determiner · all + PL -> todos/as + article · everything -> tudo · each -> cada
Flip for a final compact contrast in extended contexts.

[CARD]
TITLE: Todo, tudo, and cada encode three different scopes
REF: 13.1, 13.2, 13.4

Todo or toda agrees with an expressed singular noun; tudo replaces the noun phrase and remains invariable; cada distributes over members one by one.
TL: Trabalhei a manhã toda. Resolvi tudo. Revisei cada página.
EN: I worked the whole morning. I solved everything. I reviewed each page.
[PAUSE:900]
With plural total scope, use todos/as plus the article. With recurring singular time expressions, todo/a without an article means every.
TL: Todos os dias reviso cada relatório.
EN: Every day I review each report.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not let the English word all decide the form by itself; reconstruct the Portuguese noun phrase.
SHOW: whole noun -> todo/a · all + plural -> todos/as os/as · everything -> tudo · each -> cada
Flip to close the unit with one decision sequence.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Select the indefinite in four checks
REF: 13.1, 13.2, 13.4, 13.5, 13.6, 13.7, 13.8, 13.10, 13.12, 13.14

First identify the referent: person, thing, member, or amount. Second check whether a noun is spoken or merely understood.
TL: Alguém trouxe algumas caixas, mas falta alguma coisa.
EN: Someone brought some boxes, but something is missing.
[PAUSE:900]
Third choose the scope: total, each, any, other, or additional. Finally apply noun agreement, required articles, and invariable adverb forms.
TL: Cada caixa tem vários itens; todos os itens pesam muito.
EN: Each box has several items; all the items weigh a lot.
[PAUSE:950]
This sequence separates forms that English often compresses into all, any, some, or else.
SHOW: referent -> noun present? -> scope -> article/agreement/adverb check
The final source set revisits todo, tudo, and cada in extended contexts.
