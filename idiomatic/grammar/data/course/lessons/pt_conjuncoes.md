## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: First identify how the two ideas are connected
REF: 26.1, 26.2

A conjunction can coordinate two units of equal rank or make one clause depend on another. That structural choice comes before translation.
TL: A Júlia revisou o texto e o Marcos conferiu os números.
EN: Júlia revised the text and Marcos checked the figures.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Saímos cedo porque a estrada estava congestionada.
EN: We left early because the road was congested.
[PAUSE:950]
E joins two assertions. Porque turns the reason into a subordinate clause.
SHOW: equal rank -> coordination · dependent idea -> subordination
Flip to see why meaning matters more than the English connector.

[SIDE]
TITLE: One English linker can hide several relations
REF: 26.1.3, 26.1.4, 26.2.2, 26.2.11

English so can introduce a conclusion, a result, or a purpose. Portuguese normally makes that distinction explicit.
TL: Estava tarde, então fomos embora.
EN: It was late, so we left.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Falou claramente, de modo que todos entenderam.
EN: She spoke clearly, with the result that everyone understood.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Falou claramente para que todos entendessem.
EN: She spoke clearly so that everyone would understand.
[PAUSE:1000]
The last two clauses differ in both relation and mood: achieved result versus intended outcome.
SHOW: conclusion -> então · actual result -> indicative · purpose -> subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Add and alternate with balanced coordinators
REF: 26.1.1, 26.1.2

Use e for simple addition and ou for an alternative. Paired forms make the balance more prominent.
TL: Trouxe pão e queijo. · Você prefere chá ou café?
EN: She brought bread and cheese. · Do you prefer tea or coffee?
[PAUSE:900]
Tanto ... como means both ... and. Ou ... ou means either ... or.
TL: Tanto a Ana como o Rui participaram. · Ou ligamos agora ou esperamos até amanhã.
EN: Both Ana and Rui took part. · Either we call now or we wait until tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Keep parallel elements in the same grammatical shape on both sides of a paired connector.
SHOW: e · ou · tanto ... como · ou ... ou
Flip for negative addition and replacement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Nem adds a negative; mas sim replaces a rejected option
REF: 26.1.1, 26.1.3

After a negative statement, nem adds another negative element. The paired form nem ... nem means neither ... nor.
TL: Ela não telefonou nem escreveu. · Nem o diretor nem a gerente responderam.
EN: She did not call or write. · Neither the director nor the manager replied.
[PAUSE:900]
Mas adds an ordinary contrast. After explicit negation, mas sim replaces the rejected description with the correct one.
TL: O relatório é curto, mas completo. · Não é um custo, mas sim um investimento.
EN: The report is short but complete. · It is not a cost, but rather an investment.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: negative addition -> nem · contrast -> mas · correction after não -> mas sim
Eight clean coordinating translations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Match conclusions to the register
REF: 26.1.3, 26.1.4

Brazilian speech often uses aí, daí, or então to move from a situation to its consequence. Por isso states the reason for the conclusion more explicitly.
TL: O sistema travou, aí reiniciei o computador. · Choveu muito; por isso, a estrada fechou.
EN: The system froze, so I restarted the computer. · It rained heavily; that is why the road closed.
[PAUSE:950]
Mesmo assim concedes the first fact before continuing with an unexpected conclusion.
TL: O prazo era curto. Mesmo assim, terminamos a revisão.
EN: The deadline was short. Even so, we finished the review.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: spoken consequence -> aí/daí/então · explicit reason -> por isso · concession -> mesmo assim
Flip to formal written alternatives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Formal prose has a wider connector palette
REF: 26.1.3, 26.1.4

Porém, contudo, entretanto, no entanto, and todavia all mark contrast in careful writing. Portanto marks a conclusion, while consequentemente and por conseguinte are still more formal.
TL: A proposta é viável; porém, exige mais tempo.
EN: The proposal is viable; however, it requires more time.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Os dados estavam incompletos; portanto, adiamos a decisão.
EN: The data were incomplete; therefore, we postponed the decision.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not choose a formal connector merely as a synonym. Match its logical job and the register of the surrounding text.
SHOW: written contrast -> porém/no entanto · written conclusion -> portanto

[CARD]
TITLE: Que and se introduce two different kinds of content
REF: 26.2.1.1, 26.2.1.2

Que introduces a statement after verbs of thinking, saying, knowing, or reporting. Portuguese normally keeps que even where English omits that.
TL: Acho que o trem já saiu. · Ela disse que voltaria cedo.
EN: I think the train has already left. · She said she would return early.
[PAUSE:950]
Se introduces an indirect yes-or-no question and then means whether.
TL: Não sei se o trem já saiu.
EN: I do not know whether the train has already left.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: reported content -> que · indirect yes/no question -> se
Flip when the expression before que requires a preposition.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Preserve the preposition required by the governing expression
REF: 26.2.1.1

Some verbs, nouns, and adjectives require a preposition before a que clause. The preposition belongs to the governing expression, not to que itself.
TL: Tenho certeza de que ela vem. · Insistiram em que a reunião fosse adiada.
EN: I am sure that she is coming. · They insisted that the meeting be postponed.
[PAUSE:950]
Test the frame with a noun phrase: certeza de algo, insistir em algo. Then restore the clause with que.
[PAUSE:900]
Que sim and que não can abbreviate a positive or negative reported answer.
TL: Perguntei se ele vinha, e ele disse que sim.
EN: I asked whether he was coming, and he said yes.
SHOW: governing frame + preposition + que · que sim/que não = abbreviated answer

[CARD]
TITLE: Causal connectors present reasons with different discourse status
REF: 26.2.2

Porque gives a direct explanation. Como commonly places a background reason first, while já que treats the reason as available or accepted.
TL: Fechei a janela porque estava frio.
EN: I closed the window because it was cold.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Como estava frio, fechei a janela. · Já que todos chegaram, podemos começar.
EN: Since it was cold, I closed the window. · Since everyone has arrived, we can begin.
[PAUSE:1000]
Pois can also explain, especially after the statement it supports. Uma vez que and visto que are common written choices.
SHOW: direct reason -> porque · background first -> como · accepted premise -> já que
Flip because desde que changes category with its meaning.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The same form can express time or condition
REF: 26.2.2, 26.2.3, 26.2.6

Desde que plus the indicative can mark a starting point in time. With the subjunctive it can mean provided that or as long as.
TL: Trabalho aqui desde que me formei.
EN: I have worked here since I graduated.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Você pode usar o carro, desde que volte antes das dez.
EN: You may use the car provided that you return before ten.
[PAUSE:950]
Uma vez que likewise can introduce a given reason in the indicative or a condition in the subjunctive.
SHOW: starting fact -> desde que + indicative · condition -> desde que + subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Time clauses track whether the event is already actual
REF: 26.2.3

With quando, assim que, logo que, depois que, enquanto, and sempre que, an event presented as actual takes the indicative.
TL: Ela me ligou assim que chegou. · Enquanto esperávamos, tomamos café.
EN: She called me as soon as she arrived. · While we were waiting, we had coffee.
[PAUSE:950]
When the time-clause event is still pending from the viewpoint of the main action, Portuguese uses the future subjunctive or, in a past frame, the imperfect subjunctive.
TL: Ela vai me ligar assim que chegar. · Prometeu ligar assim que chegasse.
EN: She will call me as soon as she arrives. · She promised to call as soon as she arrived.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: actual event -> indicative · pending future -> future subjunctive · pending from past -> imperfect subjunctive
Flip for conjunctions with their own boundary rules.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Antes que and até que do not behave identically
REF: 26.2.3

Antes que always looks toward an unrealized boundary and therefore takes the present or imperfect subjunctive.
TL: Saia antes que comece a chover. · Saímos antes que começasse a chover.
EN: Leave before it starts raining. · We left before it started raining.
[PAUSE:950]
Até que takes the subjunctive while the endpoint is pending, but the indicative can report an endpoint that was actually reached.
TL: Espere até que eu volte. · Esperamos até que ele voltou.
EN: Wait until I return. · We waited until he returned.
[PAUSE:1000]
Mal presents one event as following immediately upon another.
SHOW: antes que -> subjunctive · pending até que -> subjunctive · reached até que -> indicative

[CARD]
TITLE: Concession and contrast keep an obstacle from controlling the result
REF: 26.2.4, 26.2.5

Embora, ainda que, mesmo que, and por mais que take the present or imperfect subjunctive when they concede an obstacle.
TL: Embora esteja cansada, ela vai terminar o relatório.
EN: Although she is tired, she is going to finish the report.
[PAUSE:900]
Se bem que and apesar de que normally take the indicative when they present the obstacle as a fact.
TL: O plano funciona, se bem que é bastante caro.
EN: The plan works, although it is quite expensive.
[PAUSE:1000]
Enquanto and the more written ao passo que contrast two situations without making either one concessive.
SHOW: concessive obstacle -> embora + subjunctive · factual qualification -> se bem que + indicative · contrast -> enquanto
Flip from concession to conditions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Conditions package possibility, restriction, or exception
REF: 26.2.6

Caso, a menos que, a não ser que, contanto que, and conditional desde que take the present or imperfect subjunctive.
TL: Caso precise de ajuda, me avise. · Iremos, a menos que chova.
EN: If you need help, let me know. · We will go unless it rains.
[PAUSE:950]
Se has its own tense patterns: a future condition commonly uses the future subjunctive, while a contrary-to-fact present condition uses the imperfect subjunctive.
TL: Se tiver tempo, eu ligo. · Se tivesse tempo, eu ligaria.
EN: If I have time, I will call. · If I had time, I would call.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: explicit condition/exception -> subjunctive · se + open future -> future subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Comparison, proportion, and conformity answer different questions
REF: 26.2.7, 26.2.8, 26.2.9

Do que introduces the second term of an inequality. Tanto ... como or quanto and tão ... como or quanto build equality.
TL: A prova foi mais curta do que eu esperava. · Ela fala tão depressa quanto o irmão.
EN: The test was shorter than I expected. · She speaks as quickly as her brother.
[PAUSE:950]
À medida que and conforme can track two changes together. Quanto mais ... mais makes the proportional relation explicit.
TL: À medida que a noite avançava, a temperatura caía. · Quanto mais pratico, mais aprendo.
EN: As the night went on, the temperature fell. · The more I practise, the more I learn.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: unequal comparison -> do que · linked change -> à medida que · proportion -> quanto mais ... mais
Flip when conforme reports alignment rather than change.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Conforme and segundo can mean according to what
REF: 26.2.8, 26.2.9

Conforme can present two developments in parallel, but it can also say that an action follows a rule, instruction, or account. Segundo introduces the source of an account.
TL: Fizemos a instalação conforme o técnico explicou.
EN: We performed the installation as the technician explained.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Segundo informaram, a estrada será reaberta amanhã.
EN: According to what they reported, the road will reopen tomorrow.
[PAUSE:950]
Identify whether the clause describes proportional change or conformity with a source.
SHOW: linked development -> conforme/à medida que · alignment/source -> conforme/segundo

[CARD]
TITLE: Purpose and achieved result select different moods
REF: 26.2.10, 26.2.11

Para que and a fim de que introduce an intended outcome and take the present or imperfect subjunctive.
TL: Repeti a instrução para que todos entendessem.
EN: I repeated the instruction so that everyone would understand.
[PAUSE:900]
De forma que, de maneira que, and de modo que take the subjunctive for an aim, but the indicative for an actual consequence.
TL: Repeti a instrução, de modo que todos entenderam.
EN: I repeated the instruction, with the result that everyone understood.
[PAUSE:1000]
The connector family can be identical; the intended-versus-achieved distinction chooses the mood.
SHOW: intended outcome -> subjunctive · achieved consequence -> indicative
Flip to apply that distinction to time clauses too.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Choose mood from viewpoint, not from the connector alone
REF: 26.2.3, 26.2.10, 26.2.11

Some conjunctions always select one mood in a given use. Others change mood with the speaker's viewpoint: actual or pending time, achieved result or intended purpose.
TL: Aproveitei o sol enquanto durou. · Queria aproveitar o sol enquanto durasse.
EN: I enjoyed the sun while it lasted. · I wanted to enjoy the sun for as long as it lasted.
[PAUSE:950]
Read the main verb and the relationship together before choosing the subordinate verb.
[PAUSE:850]
SHOW: relation + time viewpoint -> mood
Nine clean paired mood choices follow; the defective final source item is omitted.

[CARD]
TITLE: A shared subject often favors a preposition plus infinitive
REF: 26.3

When the main and dependent events have the same subject, Portuguese commonly replaces a finite conjunction with the corresponding preposition and an infinitive.
TL: Comemos antes de sair. · Fechou a porta para trabalhar em silêncio.
EN: We ate before leaving. · She closed the door in order to work quietly.
[PAUSE:950]
Compare antes que with antes de, para que with para, and sem que with sem. The finite form introduces its own clause; the prepositional form packages an infinitive.
SHOW: separate finite clause -> conjunction · shared subject -> preposition + infinitive
Flip when the two events have different subjects.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The personal infinitive can keep a different subject
REF: 26.3

Portuguese can use a personal infinitive after antes de, depois de, apesar de, até, para, or sem even when the dependent event has its own subject. This is especially natural in speech.
TL: Vamos terminar antes de os convidados chegarem.
EN: We are going to finish before the guests arrive.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ela saiu sem ninguém perceber.
EN: She left without anyone noticing.
[PAUSE:950]
The personal infinitive keeps the compact prepositional architecture while still identifying the dependent subject.
SHOW: preposition + explicit subject + personal infinitive

[CARD]
TITLE: Mesmo plus gerund offers a spoken concessive frame
REF: 26.4

Brazilian Portuguese often expresses although, even though, or even if with mesmo plus a gerund, particularly in speech.
TL: Mesmo estudando muito, ele ficou nervoso na prova.
EN: Even though he studied a lot, he became nervous in the test.
[PAUSE:900]
An explicit subject may precede the gerund when it differs from the main-clause subject.
TL: Mesmo eles chegando cedo, não vamos mudar o horário.
EN: Even if they arrive early, we are not going to change the schedule.
[PAUSE:1000]
This pattern packages a concession without a finite conjunction such as embora.
SHOW: mesmo + gerund -> spoken concession
Flip for a final selection routine.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Solve a conjunction choice in four passes
REF: 26.1, 26.2, 26.3, 26.4

First name the relation: addition, alternative, contrast, cause, time, condition, concession, comparison, purpose, or result. Next decide between coordination and subordination.
[PAUSE:900]
Then establish whether the subordinate event is actual, pending, intended, or achieved; that determines the mood where the connector allows a contrast. Finally choose the register and decide whether an infinitive or gerund gives a more natural architecture.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Como estava tarde, saímos. · Embora estivesse tarde, continuamos. · Saímos para chegar cedo. · Saímos antes de escurecer.
EN: Since it was late, we left. · Although it was late, we continued. · We left in order to arrive early. · We left before dark.
SHOW: relation -> architecture -> viewpoint/mood -> register
