## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: The infinitive identifies one of three regular conjugations
REF: 15.1, 15.2

Most Portuguese infinitives end in -ar, -er, or -ir. Remove that ending to expose the stem, then attach endings for person, tense, and mood.
TL: falar -> fal- · comer -> com- · decidir -> decid-
EN: to speak · to eat · to decide
[PAUSE:900]
Brazilian Portuguese organizes a tense around four person slots: eu; você, ele, ela; nós; and vocês, eles, elas. A gente uses the third-person singular form.
TL: eu falo · você fala · nós falamos · vocês falam · a gente fala
EN: I speak · you speak · we speak · you all speak · we speak
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: infinitive -> conjugation -> stem -> tense/mood ending -> person
Flip for the present-tense endings shared by regular verbs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Present endings separate -ar from the -er/-ir pair
REF: 15.3

All three conjugations use -o with eu. The third-person singular is -a for -ar but -e for both -er and -ir.
TL: eu falo, como, decido · ela fala, come, decide
EN: I speak, eat, decide · she speaks, eats, decides
[PAUSE:900]
With nós, use -amos, -emos, -imos. With the third-person plural, use -am for -ar and -em for -er or -ir.
TL: nós falamos, comemos, decidimos · eles falam, comem, decidem
EN: we speak, eat, decide · they speak, eat, decide
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: eu -o · ele -a/-e/-e · nós -amos/-emos/-imos · eles -am/-em/-em

[CARD]
TITLE: The imperfect has one -ar series and one -er/-ir series
REF: 15.4

Use -ava with a singular -ar verb and -ia with a singular -er or -ir verb. The same singular form serves eu, você, ele, and ela.
TL: eu falava · ela comia · você decidia
EN: I used to speak · she used to eat · you used to decide
[PAUSE:900]
Nós adds -mos and a written accent: -ávamos or -íamos. The plural forms are -avam and -iam.
TL: nós falávamos · nós comíamos · eles decidiam
EN: we used to speak · we used to eat · they used to decide
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: -ar -> -ava/-ávamos/-avam · -er/-ir -> -ia/-íamos/-iam
Flip to distinguish the completed regular past.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The preterite marks a completed event with stressed endings
REF: 15.5

For eu, use -ei after an -ar stem and -i after an -er or -ir stem. The third-person singular uses -ou, -eu, or -iu.
TL: falei · comi · decidi · ela falou · comeu · decidiu
EN: I spoke · I ate · I decided · she spoke · ate · decided
[PAUSE:900]
The nós forms falamos, comemos, and decidimos look exactly like the present; time context resolves them. The plural preterite ends in -aram, -eram, or -iram.
TL: Ontem falamos com eles. Depois, eles comeram e partiram.
EN: Yesterday we spoke with them. Afterwards, they ate and left.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: eu -ei/-i/-i · ele -ou/-eu/-iu · nós = present form · eles -aram/-eram/-iram

[CARD]
TITLE: The present subjunctive swaps the regular vowel family
REF: 15.6

For -ar verbs, use the e-series; for -er and -ir verbs, use the a-series. There is no separate eu ending: eu, você, ele, and ela share the singular form.
TL: Espero que eu fale. É importante que ela coma. Talvez ele decida.
EN: I hope I speak. It is important that she eat. Perhaps he will decide.
[PAUSE:900]
Add -mos for nós and -m for the third-person plural.
TL: Tomara que falemos. Quero que vocês comam. Talvez eles decidam.
EN: I hope we speak. I want you all to eat. Perhaps they will decide.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: -ar -> -e/-emos/-em · -er/-ir -> -a/-amos/-am
Flip for a reliable way to keep its person endings straight.

[SIDE]
TITLE: One subjunctive stem serves every present-subjunctive person
REF: 15.6, 16.3.1

With a regular verb, remove the infinitive ending and add the opposite vowel series. With many irregular verbs, start instead from the eu present form, remove final -o, and add the a-series.
TL: falar -> fale, falemos, falem · tenho -> tenha, tenhamos, tenham
EN: speak · have, across singular, we, and they forms
[PAUSE:900]
Six frequent verbs do not follow the eu-form shortcut and must be learned directly: dar, estar, haver, ir, saber, and ser.
TL: dê · esteja · haja · vá · saiba · seja
EN: give · be · exist/have · go · know · be
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: regular stem + opposite vowel · many irregulars: eu form minus -o -> a-series · six exceptions

[CARD]
TITLE: The regular imperfect subjunctive uses -asse, -esse, or -isse
REF: 15.7

Attach -asse to an -ar stem, -esse to an -er stem, and -isse to an -ir stem. The singular again serves eu, você, ele, and ela.
TL: se eu falasse · se ela comesse · se você decidisse
EN: if I spoke · if she ate · if you decided
[PAUSE:900]
For nós, add -mos and mark the stressed vowel; for the third-person plural, add -m.
TL: se falássemos · se comêssemos · se decidíssemos · se eles falassem
EN: if we spoke · if we ate · if we decided · if they spoke
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: -ar -> -asse/-ássemos/-assem · -er -> -esse/-êssemos/-essem · -ir -> -isse/-íssemos/-issem
Flip from the form to the environments that call for it.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Past framing and unreal conditions often select the imperfect subjunctive
REF: 15.7, 20.3, 20.4, 20.5, 20.6

A past wish, request, reaction, or evaluation can place the subordinate event in the imperfect subjunctive.
TL: Eu queria que você ficasse. Era importante que eles partissem.
EN: I wanted you to stay. It was important that they leave.
[PAUSE:900]
In a hypothetical condition, an imperfect-subjunctive se-clause commonly pairs with a conditional result.
TL: Se eu tivesse tempo, viajaria mais.
EN: If I had time, I would travel more.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: past trigger + que -> imperfect subjunctive · hypothetical se + imperfect subjunctive -> conditional result

[CARD]
TITLE: The regular future subjunctive starts from the infinitive
REF: 15.8

For a regular verb, the singular future-subjunctive form is identical to the infinitive. Add -mos for nós and -em for the third-person plural.
TL: se eu falar · quando nós comermos · se eles decidirem
EN: if I speak · when we eat · if they decide
[PAUSE:900]
The form presents a future event as a condition, time boundary, or open possibility rather than as a direct prediction.
TL: Quando você chegar, começaremos. Se precisarem, podem ligar.
EN: When you arrive, we will begin. If you need help, you can call.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: regular singular = infinitive · nós + -mos · eles + -em
Flip to separate this finite form from a look-alike infinitive.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A subject and clause frame reveal the future subjunctive
REF: 15.8, 20.4, 20.5

Because falar can be either an infinitive or a singular future subjunctive, use syntax and meaning. After prospective se, quando, assim que, quem, or an open relative expression, a subject-bearing clause can require the future subjunctive.
TL: Se ela falar, eu escuto. Quando falarmos, explicaremos tudo.
EN: If she speaks, I will listen. When we speak, we will explain everything.
[PAUSE:900]
An infinitive instead names the action or follows another verb or preposition without carrying this prospective clause relation.
TL: Quero falar. Antes de falar, pense.
EN: I want to speak. Before speaking, think.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: prospective connector + subject + event -> future subjunctive · named/dependent action -> infinitive

[CARD]
TITLE: Future and conditional endings attach to the whole infinitive
REF: 15.9

The regular future keeps the infinitive and adds -ei, -á, -emos, or -ão.
TL: falarei · comerá · decidiremos · partirão
EN: I will speak · she will eat · we will decide · they will leave
[PAUSE:900]
The conditional also keeps the infinitive and adds -ia for the singular, -íamos for nós, or -iam for the plural.
TL: eu falaria · nós comeríamos · eles decidiriam
EN: I would speak · we would eat · they would decide
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: infinitive + future -ei/-á/-emos/-ão · infinitive + conditional -ia/-íamos/-iam
Flip for the few stem changes and the conversational alternatives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Three future stems shorten, while speech often chooses another construction
REF: 15.1.3, 15.9.1

Dizer, fazer, and trazer use dir-, far-, and trar- before future or conditional endings. Pôr loses its circumflex before those endings.
TL: direi · faria · trariam · poremos
EN: I will say · I would do · they would bring · we will put
[PAUSE:900]
In conversation, ir plus the infinitive commonly replaces the synthetic future, and the imperfect often carries a conditional meaning.
TL: Vou ligar amanhã. Eu aceitava, se fosse possível.
EN: I am going to call tomorrow. I would accept if it were possible.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: dizer -> dir- · fazer -> far- · trazer -> trar- · spoken future -> ir + infinitive

[CARD]
TITLE: Spelling changes preserve a consonant sound before a new ending
REF: 15.12.1

Before e, -car becomes -qu-, -çar becomes -c-, and -gar becomes -gu-. These shifts keep the consonant value stable.
TL: ficar -> fiquei, fique · começar -> comecei, comece · pagar -> paguei, pague
EN: stayed/stay · began/begin · paid/pay
[PAUSE:900]
Before o or a, -cer becomes -ç-, -ger or -gir becomes -j-, and -guer or -guir loses silent u.
TL: descer -> desço, desça · dirigir -> dirijo, dirija · distinguir -> distingo, distinga
EN: descend · drive · distinguish
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: preserve sound: c/qu · ç/c · g/gu · c/ç · g/j · gu/g
Flip for spelling changes that mark stress or separate vowels.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Written accents keep stress and vowel separation visible
REF: 15.12.2, 15.12.3, 15.12.4, 15.12.5

Some stem-stressed forms mark a vowel that must remain prominent or separate: reunir gives reúno; proibir gives proíbo; enxaguar gives enxáguo.
TL: Ela reúne a equipe. A regra proíbe isso. Eu enxáguo o cabelo.
EN: She gathers the team. The rule forbids that. I rinse my hair.
[PAUSE:900]
Verbs in -air and -uir require close attention to hiatus and stress. Construir and destruir have the special third-person singular forms constrói and destrói; doer and roer give dói and rói.
TL: eu saí · nós saíamos · ele constrói · o dente dói
EN: I left · we used to leave · he builds · the tooth hurts
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: change spelling to preserve sound · add accent to preserve stress/hiatus · check the exact ending family

[CARD]
TITLE: Radical-changing -ir verbs alternate only in selected present forms
REF: 16.1

Many stem-e verbs change e to i in eu present and throughout the present subjunctive. Some stem-o verbs similarly change o to u.
TL: prefiro, prefere, preferimos · prefira · durmo, dorme, dormimos · durma
EN: I prefer, she prefers, we prefer · may prefer · I sleep, she sleeps, we sleep · may sleep
[PAUSE:900]
Another group changes u to o in stressed third-person present forms; a smaller e-to-i group extends the change through every stressed present form.
TL: subir -> subo, sobe, subimos, sobem · agredir -> agrido, agride, agredimos, agridem
EN: go up · attack, across present persons
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: learn which positions change · keep unstressed regular positions · do not spread the alternation to every tense
Flip for verbs whose irregularity is smaller and more local.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Semi-irregular verbs are best learned by their diagnostic forms
REF: 16.2

Some families change only one or a few present forms. Verbs in -duzir use -z in the third-person singular; six frequent -iar verbs insert e in stressed present forms.
TL: produzir -> produz · odiar -> odeio, odeia, odiamos, odeiam
EN: produces · I hate, she hates, we hate, they hate
[PAUSE:900]
Ler and crer, rir and sorrir, plus pedir, medir, ouvir, perder, and valer each provide a compact present pattern worth learning as a group.
TL: leio, lê, lemos, leem · ouço, ouve, ouvimos, ouvem · perco, perde, perdemos, perdem
EN: read · hear · lose, across present persons
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: identify the family -> memorize its diagnostic present forms -> keep the remaining tenses regular

[CARD]
TITLE: Two principal parts unlock many irregular forms
REF: 16.3.1

First learn the present indicative, especially eu. For many irregular verbs, eu minus final -o supplies the present-subjunctive stem.
TL: tenho -> tenha · faço -> faça · digo -> diga · vejo -> veja
EN: have · do · say · see, from present to present subjunctive
[PAUSE:900]
Then learn the third-person plural preterite. Remove final -ram to get the base for both imperfect and future subjunctive.
TL: tiveram -> tivesse, tiver · fizeram -> fizesse, fizer · disseram -> dissesse, disser
EN: had/did/said -> imperfect-subjunctive and future-subjunctive stems
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: eu present -> present subjunctive · eles preterite minus -ram -> imperfect/future subjunctive
Flip to organize the high-frequency irregular families.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Learn irregular verbs in related families, not isolated tables
REF: 16.3.2, 16.3.3, 16.3.4, 16.3.5, 16.3.6, 16.3.7

Caber patterns with saber; dizer patterns with trazer in the present; ter patterns with vir through much of the system. Their compounds largely inherit the same irregular stems.
TL: caibo/saiba · digo/trago · tenho/venho · mantenho/intervenho
EN: I fit/may know · I say/bring · I have/come · I maintain/intervene
[PAUSE:900]
Keep ver distinct from vir, and extend the pôr pattern to its compounds.
TL: vemos, vimos, vir · vimos, viemos, vier · ponho, pus, puser · proponho, propus, propuser
EN: we see/saw/if seeing · we come/came/if coming · put/propose across key forms
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: family resemblance saves work · similar-looking ver/vir require separate anchors

[CARD]
TITLE: Irregular present-subjunctive forms still share person endings
REF: 16.3

Once the irregular stem is known, the person pattern is usually stable: singular -a, nós -amos, plural -am for the many eu-derived verbs.
TL: diga, digamos, digam · faça, façamos, façam · tenha, tenhamos, tenham
EN: may say · may do · may have, across singular, we, and they
[PAUSE:900]
Memorize the six exceptional stems directly, including their spelling and accents.
TL: dê, demos, deem · esteja, estejamos, estejam · haja · vá · saiba · seja
EN: may give · may be · may exist · may go · may know · may be
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: irregular stem + regular person series · direct-memory exceptions: dar, estar, haver, ir, saber, ser
Flip to derive the corresponding imperfect-subjunctive forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The preterite plural predicts the irregular imperfect subjunctive
REF: 16.3.1, 20.3, 20.4, 20.5, 20.6

Take the eles preterite, remove -ram, and add -sse for the singular, -ssemos for nós, or -ssem for the plural.
TL: puderam -> pudesse, pudéssemos, pudessem · vieram -> viesse, viéssemos, viessem
EN: could · came, converted to imperfect-subjunctive persons
[PAUSE:900]
Ir and ser share the same preterite fui/foi/fomos/foram, so both produce fosse. Meaning comes from the clause.
TL: Se eu fosse ao Rio, seria turista. Se eu fosse paciente, esperaria.
EN: If I went to Rio, I would be a tourist. If I were patient, I would wait.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: eles preterite -ram + sse/ssemos/ssem · foram -> fosse for both ir and ser

[CARD]
TITLE: The same preterite base yields the irregular future subjunctive
REF: 16.3.1, 20.4, 20.5

Remove -ram from the third-person plural preterite, then use the bare base for the singular, -mos for nós, and -em for the plural.
TL: disseram -> disser, dissermos, disserem · trouxeram -> trouxer, trouxermos, trouxerem
EN: if/when someone says · if/when someone brings, across persons
[PAUSE:900]
This exposes important forms that do not resemble the infinitive.
TL: quando eu tiver · se você fizer · quando eles vierem · se ela vir
EN: when I have · if you do · when they come · if she sees
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: eles preterite minus -ram -> base/base+mos/base+em
Flip for a final workflow that covers both regular and irregular verbs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build a Portuguese verb form in six checks
REF: 15.1, 15.2, 15.3, 15.4, 15.5, 15.6, 15.7, 15.8, 15.9, 15.12, 16.1, 16.2, 16.3

Identify the infinitive and subject. Choose tense and mood from the time relation and clause frame. Then decide whether the verb is regular, radical-changing, semi-irregular, or fully irregular.
[PAUSE:850]
For a regular verb, attach the correct conjugation ending and apply any sound-preserving spelling or accent rule. For an irregular verb, retrieve the right principal part or family stem before adding person endings.
TL: quando nós pagarmos · se eles puderem · espero que ela venha
EN: when we pay · if they can · I hope she comes
[PAUSE:900]
Finish by checking stress, written accents, and look-alikes such as ver versus vir and present versus preterite nós forms.
SHOW: infinitive -> subject -> tense/mood -> verb class -> stem/ending -> spelling/stress check
