## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Six forms, three temporal relations
SVG: de_tempora_c01f.svg
REF: 12

German has six indicative tenses. Do not learn them as six English translations. Organize them by viewpoint. The present, simple past and future place an event around the speaker's now; the perfect, pluperfect and future perfect place one event before a reference point. Hear the first three:
TL: Die Redaktion prüft den Bericht sorgfältig.
EN: The editorial team is checking the report carefully.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Die Redaktion prüfte den Bericht sorgfältig.
EN: The editorial team checked the report carefully.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Die Redaktion hat den Bericht sorgfältig geprüft.
EN: The editorial team has checked the report carefully.
SHOW: first choose the viewpoint · then choose the tense
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The three relative forms
REF: 12, 12.3, 12.4

The remaining forms add a second temporal layer. The pluperfect looks backward from a past point. The future perfect looks backward from a future point. The perfect links a completed event to the chosen viewpoint.
TL: Die Redaktion hatte den Bericht geprüft, bevor die Sitzung begann.
EN: The editorial team had checked the report before the meeting began.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Redaktion wird den Bericht morgen prüfen.
EN: The editorial team will check the report tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Bis morgen Abend wird die Redaktion den Bericht geprüft haben.
EN: By tomorrow evening, the editorial team will have checked the report.
SHOW: pluperfect = before a past point · future perfect = before a future point
That map is the whole unit in miniature. The next cards teach where German chooses differently from English.

[CARD]
TITLE: A past beginning can stay present
SVG: de_tempora_c02f.svg
REF: 12.1.2

When a state or activity began in the past and still holds now, German normally uses the present tense. English reaches for a perfect, often a progressive perfect; German lets the time phrase carry the past starting point.
TL: Seit drei Semestern forscht sie zur Geschichte des Städtebaus.
EN: She has been researching the history of urban planning for three semesters.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Solange ich ihn kenne, arbeitet er äußerst gewissenhaft.
EN: For as long as I have known him, he has worked extremely conscientiously.
SHOW: began before now + still true now → present
Flip the card for the exceptions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Gaps and counted completions switch tense
REF: 12.1.2

Use the perfect when the sentence counts completed episodes up to now, or describes a gap in which something has not happened. But a continuously valid negative state remains present. Ask whether the situation itself continues, or whether you are looking back over completed occurrences.
TL: Seit Monaten habe ich nichts von ihr gehört.
EN: I have not heard anything from her for months.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Seit Jahresbeginn hat der Ausschuss viermal getagt.
EN: The committee has met four times since the beginning of the year.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Seit dem Umzug vermisst sie die Großstadt nicht mehr.
EN: Since moving, she no longer misses the big city.
SHOW: continuous state → present · gap or counted episodes → perfect
Keep the distinction semantic: continuity is not the same thing as a series of completed events.

[CARD]
TITLE: Context usually carries future time
SVG: de_tempora_c03f.svg
REF: 12.1.3

German uses the present for future events whenever a date, plan or shared situation makes the time clear. The finite verb does not need a future marker when the rest of the sentence has already done that work.
TL: Nächsten Donnerstag bespreche ich den Entwurf mit der Projektleitung.
EN: Next Thursday I will discuss the draft with the project management team.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: In zehn Minuten beginnt die Anhörung.
EN: The hearing will begin in ten minutes.
SHOW: clear future context → present is the default
Flip the card for the moment when the future form earns its place.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use the future to remove ambiguity
REF: 12.1.3

Without a future cue, the present may be heard as happening now. The future form can force a forward reading. Even when time is already clear, it can add the speaker's stance: prediction, resolve or intention.
TL: Ich warte vor dem Haupteingang.
EN: I am waiting outside the main entrance.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ich werde vor dem Haupteingang warten.
EN: I will wait outside the main entrance.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Diese Entscheidung wird den Markt grundlegend verändern.
EN: This decision will fundamentally change the market.
SHOW: ambiguity or prediction → explicit future
Six future-to-present transformations follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: The present can dramatize the past
REF: 12.1.4

The historic present moves a past scene under the listener's eyes. German uses it readily in fiction, historical writing, headlines and animated conversation. A past-time cue keeps the timeline clear while the present makes the event feel immediate.
TL: Gestern betrete ich das Büro, und plötzlich schweigen alle.
EN: Yesterday I walk into the office, and suddenly everyone falls silent.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Im letzten Kapitel entdeckt die Ermittlerin den entscheidenden Widerspruch.
EN: In the final chapter, the investigator discovers the decisive contradiction.
SHOW: past anchor + present form = narrative immediacy
Flip the card: ordinary past narration is a register choice.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Written past, conversational perfect
SVG: de_tempora_c04b.svg
REF: 12.2.2

For completed events, written narrative usually advances in the simple past, while everyday conversation usually prefers the perfect, especially in the south. These are not two competing aspects. Very often they tell the same event through different register channels.
TL: Die Ermittlerin öffnete die Mappe und verglich die Unterschriften.
EN: The investigator opened the folder and compared the signatures.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Ermittlerin hat die Mappe geöffnet und die Unterschriften verglichen.
EN: The investigator opened the folder and compared the signatures.
SHOW: written narrative → simple past · conversation → perfect
Common auxiliaries, modal verbs, verbs of saying and passive forms keep the simple past alive in northern speech.

[CARD]
TITLE: Past versus perfect is not English aspect
REF: 12.2.1, 12.2.2

English often builds a sharp meaning contrast between its past and present perfect. German does not copy that boundary. With a completed past event, simple past and perfect can differ mainly in style, rhythm and register.
TL: Im Gespräch erwähnte sie den Vorfall nur beiläufig.
EN: In the conversation, she mentioned the incident only in passing.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Im Gespräch hat sie den Vorfall nur beiläufig erwähnt.
EN: In the conversation, she mentioned the incident only in passing.
SHOW: same event · different register profile
Flip the card for the perfect's strongest semantic home.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The perfect connects evidence to now
REF: 12.2.3

The perfect is especially natural when a past event leaves a visible result, matters to the current situation, has just happened, or belongs to a series reaching the present. Unlike English, German can keep the perfect beside an explicit past-time phrase.
TL: Der Server ist ausgefallen; deshalb können wir die Daten nicht abrufen.
EN: The server has gone down, so we cannot retrieve the data.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Es hat in der Nacht gefroren; die Treppe ist spiegelglatt.
EN: It froze overnight; the staircase is as slick as glass.
SHOW: past event + present evidence → perfect
The form points to relevance, not to an English ban on past-time adverbials.

[CARD]
TITLE: The simple past can hold an interval open
REF: 12.2.4

Shift the viewpoint into the past. If a state or activity had already been continuing when a later past event arrived, German can use the simple past with a duration phrase. English often uses a pluperfect progressive here.
TL: Als die Zusage endlich kam, wartete das Team bereits seit sechs Wochen.
EN: When the approval finally came, the team had already been waiting for six weeks.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Damals leitete sie das Institut und wohnte noch in Bonn.
EN: At that time, she ran the institute and was still living in Bonn.
SHOW: ongoing at a past viewpoint → simple past
Flip the card: a perfect can also look forward.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The perfect can mark future completion
REF: 12.2.5

If a deadline or future context is explicit, German often uses the perfect where English needs a future perfect. In a future time clause, the perfect marks the first event as complete before the next event begins.
TL: Bis Freitag habe ich das Gutachten überarbeitet.
EN: By Friday I will have revised the expert report.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sobald ich die Zahlen geprüft habe, rufe ich dich an.
EN: As soon as I have checked the figures, I will call you.
SHOW: clear future deadline → perfect can replace future perfect
German spends a future auxiliary only when clarity or stance calls for it.

[CARD]
TITLE: The future form adds commitment
REF: 12.3.1

The future form is necessary when a present reading would otherwise win. It is also useful when the speaker wants to make a prediction, intention or firm commitment prominent. The time may be future, but the added value is often stance.
TL: Sie wird diese Haltung nie aufgeben.
EN: She will never give up this stance.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Das wird noch erhebliche Folgen haben.
EN: That is going to have serious consequences down the line.
SHOW: future = forward time made explicit · often plus speaker stance
Flip the card for the future perfect.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Completion before a future checkpoint
SVG: de_tempora_c07b.svg
REF: 12.3.1

The future perfect is relative: its event is future from now but already complete at a later future checkpoint. Build it with the future auxiliary, the past participle, and the infinitive of the perfect auxiliary at the end.
TL: Bis Ende des Monats wird das Labor alle Proben ausgewertet haben.
EN: By the end of the month, the laboratory will have evaluated all the samples.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wenn du ankommst, werde ich den Saal bereits verlassen haben.
EN: When you arrive, I will already have left the hall.
The same form can also present a completed past event as an inference:
TL: Sie wird inzwischen abgereist sein.
EN: She will probably have left by now.
SHOW: completion before a checkpoint · or an inference about the past

[CARD]
TITLE: A future form can mean probably now
SVG: de_tempora_c08f.svg
REF: 12.3.2

German future forms also work as evidence markers. With no real future time at all, the future can present a cautious inference about the present. A probability particle often makes that reading unmistakable.
TL: Er wird noch im Büro sein.
EN: He is probably still at the office.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sie wird wohl die neue Leiterin kennen.
EN: She probably knows the new director.
SHOW: future form + present state = present-time inference
Flip the card to move the inference backward.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Future perfect can mean probably happened
REF: 12.3.2

For an inference about a completed past event, use the future perfect. English often translates it with probably and an ordinary past or perfect. The morphology is future; the meaning is the speaker's distance from the evidence.
TL: Die Sitzung wird länger gedauert haben.
EN: The meeting probably lasted longer.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Er wird den Hinweis wohl übersehen haben.
EN: He has probably overlooked the clue.
SHOW: future perfect + past event = past-time inference
Ten supposition transformations follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Pluperfect orders the past
SVG: de_tempora_c09f.svg
REF: 12.4.1

The pluperfect marks a past before another past. It does not merely mean very long ago. It gives the narrative a past reference point and places one event on the earlier side of it.
TL: Nachdem die Zeugin ihre Aussage geändert hatte, überprüfte die Polizei die Aufnahmen erneut.
EN: After the witness had changed her statement, the police reviewed the recordings again.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Zug war bereits abgefahren, als wir den Bahnsteig erreichten.
EN: The train had already left when we reached the platform.
SHOW: earlier past → pluperfect · later past → simple past
Flip the card for how strictly German maintains that layer.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Maintain the layer when order matters
REF: 12.4.1, 12.4.2

German sometimes relaxes an expected pluperfect to a perfect or simple past for immediacy and style. Colloquial speech can also pile up extra perfect auxiliaries, but those forms remain informal and contested. For controlled standard German, keep one clean pluperfect wherever the sequence needs help.
TL: Erst später verstand er, wie grundlegend sich die Lage verändert hatte.
EN: Only later did he understand how fundamentally the situation had changed.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Danach erklärte sie, warum sie so früh gegangen war.
EN: Afterwards she explained why she had left so early.
SHOW: preserve past-before-past · avoid stacked colloquial auxiliaries
Eight timeline-building exercises follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: German has no progressive inflection
SVG: de_tempora_c10f.svg
REF: 12.5

German does not conjugate a special progressive tense. A plain present or simple past can describe a habit, a state, or an action unfolding right now. Context usually settles the reading.
TL: Mira schreibt einen Leitartikel.
EN: Mira writes an editorial, or Mira is writing an editorial.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Mira schreibt jeden Montag einen Leitartikel.
EN: Mira writes an editorial every Monday.
SHOW: one simple form · context chooses habitual or ongoing
Flip the card for the tools that make ongoing action explicit.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Add an adverb or foreground the process
REF: 12.5

An adverb meaning right now is the lightest explicit cue. A construction meaning to be in the process of doing something is heavier and deliberately foregrounds the unfinished activity. Use it when the process itself matters.
TL: Mira schreibt gerade einen Leitartikel.
EN: Mira is writing an editorial right now.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Mira ist gerade dabei, einen Leitartikel zu schreiben.
EN: Mira is in the middle of writing an editorial.
SHOW: light cue = adverb · strong focus = in-the-process construction
Eleven responses on these cues follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: The progressive toolkit is larger
REF: 12.5

A standard prepositional construction with a nominalized infinitive presents someone as engaged in an activity. A similar construction is widespread in informal speech, especially in western varieties, but remains less neutral in writing.
TL: Als der Alarm losging, war der Techniker beim Kalibrieren der Sensoren.
EN: When the alarm went off, the technician was calibrating the sensors.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Kinder sind draußen am Toben.
EN: The children are outside romping around.
SHOW: standard activity phrase · colloquial regional activity phrase
Flip the card for motion, habits and lexical endpoints.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Particles and verb choice can carry aspect
REF: 12.5

With motion, a small particle can signal that the movement is already under way or imminent. Frequency adverbs separate habits from current events. And verb choice itself can distinguish an unfolding path from a reached endpoint.
TL: Ich komme gleich.
EN: I am coming in a moment.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Die Dozentin beantwortet meistens alle Fragen sofort.
EN: The lecturer usually answers every question immediately.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Als das Wetter umschlug, stiegen die Bergsteiger noch zum Gipfel hinauf.
EN: When the weather changed, the mountaineers were still climbing toward the summit.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Die Gruppe erreichte den Gipfel kurz vor Sonnenuntergang.
EN: The group reached the summit shortly before sunset.
SHOW: local cue or lexical endpoint — no universal progressive form
Six progressive translations follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: A four-question tense procedure
REF: 12.1.2, 12.2, 12.3.2, 12.5

For every tense choice, ask four questions. Where is the viewpoint: now, a past scene, or a future checkpoint? Is the situation continuing or already complete? What register is speaking? Is the form locating time, or expressing prediction and probability?
TL: Seit der Reform arbeitet die Behörde transparenter.
EN: The agency has been operating more transparently since the reform.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: In ihrem Bericht schilderte die Kommission den Ablauf nüchtern.
EN: In its report, the commission gave a sober account of what happened.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Die fehlenden Unterlagen wird wohl niemand vernichtet haben.
EN: Probably no one destroyed the missing documents.
SHOW: viewpoint · completion · register · stance
Flip the card for three English-shaped traps.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Do not translate the English tense label
REF: 12.1.2, 12.1.3, 12.5

An English perfect progressive can become a German present. An English future can become a German present. An English past progressive can become a German simple past plus one local cue. Translate the temporal relation, not the English form name.
TL: Ich wohne seit 2019 in Dresden.
EN: I have been living in Dresden since 2019.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Morgen bin ich mit der Auswertung fertig.
EN: Tomorrow I will be finished with the analysis.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Als die Verbindung abbrach, verhandelte das Team gerade mit dem Lieferanten.
EN: When the connection dropped, the team was negotiating with the supplier.
SHOW: map meaning first · let German choose its own tense
