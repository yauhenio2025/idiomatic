## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: The article agrees with the noun in gender and number
REF: 4.1.1

The definite article has four forms: o for masculine singular, a for feminine singular, os for masculine plural, and as for feminine plural. Choose the noun's lexical gender first, then its number.
TL: O jornal está ao lado da casa.
EN: The newspaper is beside the house.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Os jornais estão ao lado das casas.
EN: The newspapers are beside the houses.
[PAUSE:950]
An ending is only a prediction. Store exceptional nouns with their article: o dia, o mapa, a mão, a foto, and a tribo.
SHOW: M SG -> o · F SG -> a · M PL -> os · F PL -> as
Flip to connect the written forms with ordinary speech.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Definite articles are normally unstressed and link forward
REF: 4.1.2

In ordinary Brazilian speech, unstressed o and os are commonly pronounced with u-like vowels. The feminine a and as retain their a sound.
TL: O homem abriu os livros.
EN: The man opened the books.
[PAUSE:850]
Before a following vowel, h, or voiced consonant, the final s of os or as commonly links to the next word with a z-like sound.
TL: As mulheres e os homens chegaram.
EN: The women and the men arrived.
[PAUSE:950]
The spelling does not change. Hear the article and noun as one short rhythm group while preserving agreement in writing.
SHOW: written o/os · ordinary unstressed sound u/us · plural s + voiced onset -> z-like link

[CARD]
TITLE: A, de, em, and por fuse with a definite article
REF: 4.1.3

The prepositions a, de, em, and por combine with every form of the definite article. The result is written as one word.
TL: Vou ao banco e depois à escola.
EN: I am going to the bank and then to the school.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: O livro está na mesa ao lado dos jornais.
EN: The book is on the table beside the newspapers.
[PAUSE:950]
Keep the article's gender and number visible inside the contraction: ao, aos, à, às; do, dos, da, das; no, nos, na, nas; pelo, pelos, pela, pelas.
SHOW: a + article -> ao/à · de -> do/da · em -> no/na · por -> pelo/pela · add s for plural
Flip for the accent that distinguishes à from a.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Crase writes the meeting of preposition a and article a
REF: 4.1.3

When the syntax requires preposition a and the following feminine noun phrase requires article a or as, Portuguese writes à or às with a grave accent.
TL: Entreguei o livro à professora.
EN: I gave the book to the teacher.
[PAUSE:850]
The accent is not ordinary word stress. It records two grammatical pieces occupying one written form. Masculine ao and aos show the same combination without a grave accent.
TL: Voltamos aos Estados Unidos e fomos à praia.
EN: We returned to the United States and went to the beach.
[PAUSE:950]
First establish the preposition, then establish the article; only then contract them.
SHOW: required a + F article a/as -> à/às · required a + M article o/os -> ao/aos
Seventeen contraction phrases follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Generic nouns commonly take the definite article
REF: 4.1.4

Portuguese often uses a definite article when a noun names a substance, abstract idea, institution, or whole class in general, where English may use no article.
TL: O leite faz bem à saúde.
EN: Milk is good for you.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: A música brasileira é conhecida no exterior.
EN: Brazilian music is well known abroad.
[PAUSE:950]
This pattern also applies readily to plural classes such as os políticos or as crianças. A colloquial generic singular is a separate construction, so do not erase articles merely because English does.
SHOW: whole class/substance/idea -> normally definite article · translate meaning, not article count
Flip for names of languages.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Language names change pattern after language verbs
REF: 4.1.4

A language used as the topic or an ordinary noun normally takes the definite article.
TL: O português tem muitas variedades.
EN: Portuguese has many varieties.
[PAUSE:850]
After verbs closely associated with language ability or study, such as falar, entender, aprender, and estudar, the article is normally absent.
TL: A Marina fala português e entende espanhol.
EN: Marina speaks Portuguese and understands Spanish.
[PAUSE:950]
The second noun has an article here because it is the object of entender; usage permits variation, but the core contrast is topic with article versus closely bound language activity without one.
SHOW: language as topic -> o/a · falar/estudar/aprender + language -> normally zero

[CARD]
TITLE: Place names carry conventional article choices
REF: 4.1.4

Most countries take a definite article, including o Brasil, a China, a Espanha, and os Estados Unidos. A smaller frequent set is normally bare, including Portugal and Moçambique.
TL: A Espanha faz fronteira com Portugal.
EN: Spain borders Portugal.
[PAUSE:850]
Some cities take an article, such as o Rio de Janeiro, o Recife, o Cairo, and o Porto, while Brasília, São Paulo, Paris, and many others are bare.
TL: Ela saiu de Brasília e chegou ao Rio de Janeiro.
EN: She left Brasília and arrived in Rio de Janeiro.
[PAUSE:950]
Learn the article with the place name because the required preposition may contract with it.
SHOW: place class gives a tendency · lexical place name decides · preposition + stored article -> contraction
Flip for Brazilian states and other named entities.

[SIDE]
TITLE: States, companies, streets, and media names often take articles
REF: 4.1.4

Most Brazilian states take an article, but a conventional bare set includes Alagoas, Goiás, Mato Grosso, Mato Grosso do Sul, Minas Gerais, Pernambuco, Rondônia, Roraima, Santa Catarina, São Paulo, and Sergipe.
TL: Ela saiu de Pernambuco e agora mora na Bahia.
EN: She left Pernambuco and now lives in Bahia.
[PAUSE:850]
Companies, shops, streets, television networks, computer programs, and sports teams commonly take a definite article, whose gender often reflects an understood category.
TL: A Microsoft lançou uma versão nova do Windows.
EN: Microsoft released a new version of Windows.
[PAUSE:950]
Treat these as lexical conventions, not as a direct copy of English or Spanish usage.
SHOW: state/name -> learn local convention · named company/street/network/team -> article commonly present

[CARD]
TITLE: Titles and personal names follow relationship and register
REF: 4.1.4

A title followed by a personal name normally takes a definite article when speaking about the person. Direct address omits it.
TL: O doutor Ricardo chegou. Bom dia, doutor Ricardo.
EN: Dr. Ricardo arrived. Good morning, Dr. Ricardo.
[PAUSE:900]
In spoken Brazilian Portuguese, familiar personal names commonly take an article. Historical figures and formal news-style references normally remain bare.
TL: Você conhece a Laura? Shakespeare nasceu em 1564.
EN: Do you know Laura? Shakespeare was born in 1564.
[PAUSE:950]
The article contributes familiarity and discourse framing; it is not part of the person's name.
SHOW: title + name in reference -> article · direct address -> zero · familiar name -> often article · historical/formal name -> zero
Flip for meals, seasons, and numerical labels.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Meals, seasons, labels, and possessives commonly include the article
REF: 4.1.4

Portuguese normally uses the definite article with meals and seasons, and in numerical expressions such as pages, chapters, and transit lines.
TL: Falamos sobre o capítulo quatro depois do almoço.
EN: We talked about chapter four after lunch.
[PAUSE:850]
A possessive adjective can also be preceded by the definite article, especially in neutral or careful usage, although spoken Brazilian Portuguese often omits it.
TL: Carimbaram o seu passaporte?
EN: Did they stamp your passport?
[PAUSE:950]
Days of the week are normally bare, but an article appears when em or a expresses time: na terça, no sábado, aos domingos.
SHOW: meal/season/numerical label -> article · possessive -> article possible · em/a + weekday -> contracted article
Seven hygiene-gated article-usage choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: A body part often takes an article instead of a possessive
REF: 4.1.4

When the subject is the clear possessor, Portuguese commonly uses a definite article before a body part, item of clothing, accessory, or close family relation where English uses a possessive.
TL: Ela lavou o rosto e escovou os dentes.
EN: She washed her face and brushed her teeth.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Ele colocou a mão no bolso e tirou a carteira.
EN: He put his hand in his pocket and took out his wallet.
[PAUSE:950]
The possessor comes from the clause, while the article agrees with the possessed noun. Use an explicit possessive when contrast or ambiguity makes it necessary.
SHOW: clear subject-possessor + inherent item -> definite article · contrast/ambiguity -> possessive can return
Flip to let the article replace a repeated noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The article can stand before a modifier when the noun is understood
REF: 4.1.5

To avoid repeating an established noun, the appropriate definite article can appear before de, que, an adjective, or a possessive. English often uses the one or the ones.
TL: Meu carro é azul; o do meu pai é preto.
EN: My car is blue; my father's is black.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Prefiro a jaqueta verde, não a vermelha.
EN: I prefer the green jacket, not the red one.
[PAUSE:950]
The article still carries the gender and number of the omitted noun, so the intended referent must already be recoverable.
SHOW: known noun -> article + de/que/adjective/possessive · article preserves gender and number
Six complete translation items follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The indefinite article also has four agreement forms
REF: 4.2.1

Use um and uma for a singular countable referent that is not yet identified. The plural forms uns and umas mean some, a few, or a number of.
TL: Comprei um livro e uma revista.
EN: I bought a book and a magazine.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Trouxe uns livros e umas revistas.
EN: I brought some books and some magazines.
[PAUSE:950]
As with the definite forms, select lexical gender before number. English a has no plural, but Portuguese preserves the same four-way agreement pattern.
SHOW: M SG -> um · F SG -> uma · M PL -> uns · F PL -> umas
Flip for contractions with an indefinite article.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Em commonly contracts with um, while de normally stays separate
REF: 4.2.2

Em may combine with the indefinite forms as num, numa, nuns, and numas. This contraction is normal in speech and common in writing, though the uncontracted forms are also possible.
TL: Ele trabalha num prédio no centro.
EN: He works in a building downtown.
[PAUSE:850]
The older written contractions dum, duma, duns, and dumas are rare in current Brazilian usage. Write de um, de uma, de uns, and de umas even though speech links the words closely.
TL: Preciso de uma resposta hoje.
EN: I need an answer today.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not generalize the obligatory definite-article contraction table to every indefinite form.
SHOW: em + um/uma -> num/numa common · de + um/uma -> normally separate in current writing

[CARD]
TITLE: A bare profession or identity classifies the person
REF: 4.2.3.1

After ser, Portuguese normally omits the indefinite article before an unmodified nationality, profession, or religion. The same zero pattern follows tornar-se or virar when they mean become.
TL: Ela é arquiteta e ele se tornou budista.
EN: She is an architect, and he became a Buddhist.
[PAUSE:900]
Como meaning as also takes a bare role noun.
TL: Ele trabalha como porteiro num prédio residencial.
EN: He works as a doorman in a residential building.
[PAUSE:950]
A modifier or a more individuated description can favor an article, because the phrase then describes one kind or instance rather than merely assigning a class.
SHOW: ser/tornar-se/virar + bare identity · como + bare role · characterized instance -> article may return
Flip for generic objects and fixed prepositional descriptions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Generic objects and de or sem descriptions often use zero
REF: 4.2.3.1

An indefinite direct object used generically commonly has no article: ter carro, comprar passagem, procurar emprego. The noun names a kind of object rather than one separately introduced item.
TL: O hotel tem piscina, mas eu não tenho carro.
EN: The hotel has a pool, but I do not have a car.
[PAUSE:850]
After de meaning with or in, and after sem meaning without, an unmodified descriptive noun is also normally bare.
TL: Havia um homem de terno e uma mulher sem chapéu.
EN: There was a man in a suit and a woman without a hat.
[PAUSE:950]
Appositive labels can be bare for the same classificatory reason.
SHOW: generic object -> zero · de/sem + unmodified characteristic -> zero · appositive class -> zero

[CARD]
TITLE: A qualified mass or abstract noun can take an indefinite article
REF: 4.2.3.2

Portuguese often uses um or uma with an otherwise mass or abstract noun when an adjective characterizes a particular quality or manifestation.
TL: O Artur fala um francês perfeito.
EN: Artur speaks perfect French.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: É preciso uma coragem enorme para trabalhar de bombeiro.
EN: Enormous courage is needed to work as a firefighter.
[PAUSE:950]
The article can also turn an abstraction into one act or instance: uma crueldade, uma besteira. Colloquial speech extends this to emphatic amounts such as uma fome or uma chuva.
SHOW: qualified mass/abstract noun -> um/uma · one act or instance -> countable article
Flip for the special meanings of uns and umas.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Uns and umas can mean a few or approximately
REF: 4.2.4

Before an ordinary plural noun, uns or umas presents an indefinite small group.
TL: Preciso comprar umas canetas.
EN: I need to buy some pens.
[PAUSE:850]
Before a number, the plural article commonly means approximately or around.
TL: Recebi uns dez telefonemas.
EN: I received about ten phone calls.
[PAUSE:950]
The same pattern appears with time and distance expressions: daqui a umas duas semanas, a umas oito horas.
SHOW: uns/umas + plural noun -> some/a few · uns/umas + number -> about/around
Ten article-or-zero decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Decide reference first, then form, then contraction
REF: 4.1, 4.2

Start with meaning. Is the noun identifiable or generic, newly introduced, an indefinite group, or a bare classifier? That selects definite, indefinite, or zero.
TL: A médica encontrou uma carteira na rua.
EN: The doctor found a wallet in the street.
[PAUSE:850]
Next retrieve the noun's gender and number. Finally, if a preposition stands before an article, apply the contraction licensed for that pair.
TL: Ela devolveu a carteira ao dono.
EN: She returned the wallet to its owner.
[PAUSE:950]
This order prevents English or Spanish surface forms from deciding too early.
SHOW: reference -> definite/indefinite/zero · gender+number -> form · preposition+article -> contraction
Flip for the geography contrast that must be learned lexically.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Spanish resemblance does not settle a Portuguese place article
REF: 4.1.4

Portuguese and Spanish often disagree about whether a place name takes an article. Retrieve the Portuguese convention directly: a Inglaterra, o Canadá, a China, but Portugal, Angola, Moçambique, and Porto Rico without an article in the cited norm.
TL: Ela viajou de Angola para o Canadá.
EN: She traveled from Angola to Canada.
[PAUSE:850]
Cities and states are equally lexical: o Porto and a Bahia, but Brasília, Nova York, São Paulo, Minas Gerais, and Pernambuco.
TL: Saímos de São Paulo e chegamos à Bahia.
EN: We left São Paulo and arrived in Bahia.
[PAUSE:950]
Store the noun together with its article or zero, then let syntax supply any contraction.
SHOW: Portuguese place name + stored article/zero -> add required preposition -> contract if needed
