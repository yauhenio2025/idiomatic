## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: An adverb is a job, not a shape
SVG: de_adverbien_c01f.svg
REF: 7

German adverbs normally do not decline. That makes them look simple, but their scope can change the architecture of a sentence. One can qualify an action:
TL: Die Gutachterin antwortete ungewöhnlich präzise.
EN: The expert reviewer answered unusually precisely.
[PAUSE:1000]
One can set the viewpoint for an adjective:
TL: Der Vorschlag ist politisch heikel.
EN: The proposal is politically delicate.
[PAUSE:1000]
And one can comment on the whole proposition:
TL: Vermutlich wird der Ausschuss morgen zustimmen.
EN: The committee will probably approve tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1200]
The form stays fixed. What changes is the target the adverb reaches.
SHOW: one fixed form → action · quality · whole proposition
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Diagnose the relation
REF: 7, 7.6

Classify an adverb by the relation it expresses, not by memorizing a flat list. Place answers where; direction answers where to or where from; time answers when or how long; manner answers how; reason answers why; degree answers how much. Several relations can coexist:
TL: Die Delegation tagt heute dort ungewöhnlich lange.
EN: The delegation is meeting there for an unusually long time today.
[PAUSE:1200]
Interrogative adverbs ask for one slot in that system:
TL: Wohin fährt die Delegation anschließend?
EN: Where is the delegation going afterwards?
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Warum reist sie überhaupt ab?
EN: Why is she leaving at all?
[PAUSE:1000]
One further class does not fill a circumstance at all: speaker-attitude adverbs evaluate the proposition. Keep scope in view from the first card.
SHOW: where · direction · time · manner · reason · degree · attitude
Static place comes next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Here, there — and unmarked location
REF: 7.1.1

German has three high-frequency anchors for place. The first points close to the speaker; the second points away and can carry contrastive emphasis; the third is the lighter, less emphatic locator. Hear all three in context:
TL: Hier am Fenster ist es hell; dort beim Regal ist es ruhiger.
EN: It is bright here by the window; over there by the shelf it is quieter.
[PAUSE:1200]
TL: Ist Frau Keller schon da?
EN: Is Ms Keller here yet?
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Leg die Unterlagen bitte dort ab; da stören sie niemanden.
EN: Please put the documents down over there; they will not be in anyone's way there.
[PAUSE:1200]
The last form often marks simple presence, so English may translate it as either here or there. Treat it as unmarked location, not as a rigid distance word.
SHOW: hier = near · dort = pointed distance · da = light locator
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Top, middle and indefinite space
REF: 7.1.2, 7.1.3, 7.1.5

Where English often reaches for nouns such as top, bottom or middle, German prefers adverbs, usually followed by a prepositional phrase:
TL: Ganz oben im Schrank liegt der Ersatzschlüssel.
EN: The spare key is right at the top of the cupboard.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Mitten unter den Akten fand sie eine alte Fotografie.
EN: In the middle of the files, she found an old photograph.
[PAUSE:1000]
Indefinite place forms build a small logical system: some place, every place, no place, another place.
TL: Wir haben überall gesucht, aber der Vertrag war nirgendwo zu finden.
EN: We looked everywhere, but the contract was nowhere to be found.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Vielleicht ist er irgendwo anders abgelegt.
EN: Perhaps it has been filed somewhere else.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not add a direction ending until movement has an actual goal or source.
SHOW: oben · unten · mitten · irgendwo · überall · nirgendwo · anderswo
The surface-and-space contrast comes next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Surface, surrounding space, movement
SVG: de_adverbien_c03f.svg
REF: 7.1.4

English inside and outside hide a three-way German contrast. One pair describes the inner or outer surface of an object:
TL: Die Kanne ist außen feucht, aber innen völlig trocken.
EN: The jug is damp on the outside but completely dry on the inside.
[PAUSE:1200]
A second pair locates someone or something in the surrounding space, either outdoors or within an enclosure:
TL: Draußen warten die Gäste; drinnen wird noch gedeckt.
EN: The guests are waiting outside; inside, the table is still being set.
[PAUSE:1200]
The test is physical. Are you describing a surface, or locating something in a space?
SHOW: surface = außen / innen · space = draußen / drinnen
Flip the card for movement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Crossing the boundary
REF: 7.2.4

Movement across the boundary needs a direction form. In careful speech and writing, the full form can preserve whether motion is toward or away from the reference point:
TL: Komm bitte herein; ich muss gleich wieder hinaus.
EN: Please come in; I have to go out again in a moment.
[PAUSE:1200]
Colloquial northern usage often reduces both directional series to a single initial sound and lets context carry the viewpoint:
TL: Im Gespräch sagt er: „Komm rein und nimm die Kisten mit raus.“
EN: In conversation he says, “Come in and take the crates back out with you.”
[PAUSE:1200]
A surface form still describes orientation rather than a journey:
TL: Das Fenster öffnet sich nach innen.
EN: The window opens inward.
[PAUSE:1000]
Keep the three layers separate: surface, location in space, movement across the boundary.
SHOW: außen/innen ≠ draußen/drinnen ≠ hinaus/herein
An outside-inside choice block comes next.

[CARD]
TITLE: The speaker is the coordinate origin
SVG: de_adverbien_c04f.svg
REF: 7.2

German direction is deictic: it calculates a vector from a reference point. Motion away from that point takes one stem; motion toward it takes the other. The reference point is often the speaker:
TL: Bring bitte die Akte her.
EN: Please bring the file over here.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Nimm die Akte bitte mit hin.
EN: Please take the file over there with you.
[PAUSE:1200]
But a person already established in the scene can become the reference point too:
TL: Von der Empore rief sie zu uns herunter.
EN: From the gallery she called down to us.
[PAUSE:1200]
Do not translate a static English here or there. First draw the direction relative to the active point of view.
SHOW: hin = away from the reference point · her = toward it
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Position, goal, source
REF: 7.2.1

The question system makes the three coordinates explicit. One asks for position, one for a goal, and one for a source:
TL: Wo stellst du die leere Kiste hin?
EN: Where are you putting the empty crate?
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wo kommt dieser Schlüssel her?
EN: Where did this key come from?
[PAUSE:1000]
In speech, the direction element often separates and moves to the clause end. A figurative source, however, stays fused:
TL: Woher kennst du diese Formulierung?
EN: How do you know that wording?
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Daher rührt seine Skepsis.
EN: That is the source of his skepticism.
[PAUSE:1000]
The literal forms can split because they behave like separable direction pieces. The figurative forms are single lexical adverbs.
SHOW: position: wo · goal: wohin / wo … hin · source: woher / wo … her
A place-and-direction translation block follows.

[CARD]
TITLE: Direction compounds form a grid
SVG: de_adverbien_c05f.svg
REF: 7.2.4

Add a spatial base to the two deictic stems and German gets a systematic direction grid: up, down, in, out and across, each viewed as away or toward. The prepositional phrase can name the path while the compound names its vector:
TL: Sie trug die Proben ins Labor hinauf.
EN: She carried the samples up to the laboratory.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Techniker kam mit dem Ersatzteil zu uns herunter.
EN: The technician came down to us with the replacement part.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Licht drang durch den Türspalt herein.
EN: Light came in through the gap in the door.
[PAUSE:1200]
The apparent repetition is functional: the phrase supplies the route or endpoint; the compound keeps the direction prominent.
SHOW: base direction + hin/her = a speaker-centered vector
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Direction can be the focus
REF: 7.2.4, 7.2.5

Compare a bare motion verb with one carrying an explicit direction compound:
TL: Der Kurier lief in den Innenhof.
EN: The courier ran into the inner courtyard.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Kurier lief in den Innenhof hinein.
EN: The courier ran right into the inner courtyard.
[PAUSE:1200]
The second version foregrounds the inward path. Everyday speech often uses reduced direction forms freely:
TL: Wir gehen kurz raus und bringen danach die Stühle rein.
EN: We are going outside for a moment and will bring the chairs in afterwards.
[PAUSE:1000]
Simple prefixes frequently carry lexical or figurative meanings instead:
TL: Ohne zusätzliche Mittel kommt das Projekt nicht aus.
EN: The project cannot manage without additional funding.
[PAUSE:1000]
So read the whole verb: a direction compound may be literal, while its shorter cousin can be a different lexical item.
SHOW: motion verb = event · direction compound = path in focus
Now move from space to time.

[CARD]
TITLE: Time has three axes
SVG: de_adverbien_c06f.svg
REF: 7.3, 7.3.1

Time adverbs do three different jobs. They can locate a point, measure a span, or state recurrence. Keep those axes separate:
TL: Der Bericht ist gestern eingetroffen.
EN: The report arrived yesterday.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Verhandlungen dauerten monatelang.
EN: The negotiations went on for months.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Freitags arbeitet das Labor nur bis Mittag.
EN: On Fridays, the laboratory works only until noon.
[PAUSE:1200]
The long suffix turns a time unit into a duration; the day ending turns a named day into a recurring schedule.
SHOW: point = when · duration = how long · frequency = how often
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: One morning or every morning?
REF: 7.3.2

A day anchor plus a period of the day identifies one occasion:
TL: Heute Morgen war die Zufahrt gesperrt.
EN: The access road was closed this morning.
[PAUSE:1000]
The evening and the night are distinct periods:
TL: Heute Abend besprechen wir den Entwurf; heute Nacht bleibt das System offline.
EN: This evening we will discuss the draft; tonight the system will remain offline.
[PAUSE:1200]
A day or period with the recurring ending describes a habit or schedule:
TL: Montagmorgens ist der erste Zug fast leer.
EN: On Monday mornings, the first train is almost empty.
[PAUSE:1000]
For one future morning, use a future day anchor instead:
TL: Morgen früh öffnet die Werkstatt früher als sonst.
EN: Tomorrow morning, the workshop will open earlier than usual.
[PAUSE:1000]
The contrast is occasion versus recurrence, not merely singular versus plural.
SHOW: heute Morgen = one occasion · morgens = recurring · morgen früh = future occasion
The sharper time-word contrasts come on the next card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Three different kinds of then
REF: 7.3.3

English then collapses several relations. For a past period viewed as a setting, German uses a dedicated past-time adverb:
TL: Damals kannte niemand das Ausmaß des Schadens.
EN: At that time, nobody knew the extent of the damage.
[PAUSE:1000]
For sequence or consequence, use the ordinary next-step adverb:
TL: Zuerst prüfen wir die Zahlen, dann entscheiden wir.
EN: First we check the figures, then we decide.
[PAUSE:1200]
A near-identical form in a question is not temporal at all; it adds conversational interest or insistence:
TL: Was genau hat dich denn überzeugt?
EN: What exactly was it that convinced you?
[PAUSE:1200]
Past setting, sequence, and question tone are three separate systems. Do not let the English spelling merge them.
SHOW: damals = past setting · dann = sequence · denn = question tone
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Before, after, recently
REF: 7.3.4, 7.3.5

Used without a noun or clause, before and after need adverbs rather than prepositions or conjunctions:
TL: Ich muss vorher noch die Rechtsabteilung anrufen.
EN: I still have to call the legal department beforehand.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Nach der Sitzung schickte sie die Notizen; danach fuhr sie nach Hause.
EN: After the meeting, she sent the notes; afterwards, she drove home.
[PAUSE:1200]
Recent time is divided more finely. One form points to a recent occasion; another describes a recent new tendency:
TL: Neulich begegnete ich unserer früheren Dozentin.
EN: I ran into our former lecturer recently.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Neuerdings wird die Antwort automatisch erstellt.
EN: These days, the reply is generated automatically.
[PAUSE:1000]
A phrase with since presents a state that began recently and still holds:
TL: Seit kurzem leitet er das Berliner Büro.
EN: He has been in charge of the Berlin office for only a short time.
[PAUSE:1200]
Choose by temporal contour: event, new tendency, or continuing state.
SHOW: recent event · new tendency · state continuing to now

[CARD]
TITLE: German does not need an -ly ending
SVG: de_adverbien_c08f.svg
REF: 7.4.1

Most German adjectives and participles can do adverbial work without changing form. Before a noun, an adjective takes the ending required by the noun phrase:
TL: Wir brauchen eine klare Formulierung.
EN: We need clear wording.
[PAUSE:1000]
Attached to an action, the same stem has no adjective ending:
TL: Wir müssen den Einwand klar formulieren.
EN: We must formulate the objection clearly.
[PAUSE:1200]
An adverb can itself be qualified by another uninflected form:
TL: Die Sprecherin formulierte den Einwand erstaunlich klar.
EN: The speaker formulated the objection remarkably clearly.
[PAUSE:1200]
Do not search for a German suffix corresponding to English L Y. Use position and scope to identify the job.
SHOW: before a noun → adjective ending · with an action → fixed form
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Manner and viewpoint share a shape
REF: 7.4.1

A manner adverb tells you how the action was performed. A viewpoint adverb tells you within which frame the whole assessment is valid. They can stand together:
TL: Finanziell handelte die Behörde vernünftig.
EN: Financially, the authority acted sensibly.
[PAUSE:1200]
The first form sets the frame; the second describes the response. With a manner adverb, negation can target the manner while leaving the event intact:
TL: Die Zeugin antwortete nicht ausführlich.
EN: The witness did not answer in detail.
[PAUSE:1200]
She did answer; the answer was not detailed. Viewpoints can contrast explicitly:
TL: Juristisch ist der Vertrag unwirksam, politisch bleibt er jedoch brisant.
EN: Legally, the contract is invalid, but politically it remains contentious.
[PAUSE:1200]
Ask whether the word describes the action or selects the lens through which the proposition is judged.
SHOW: manner = how · viewpoint = within which frame
Speaker attitude is the next layer of scope.

[CARD]
TITLE: Attitude scopes over the proposition
SVG: de_adverbien_c09f.svg
REF: 7.4.2

An attitude adverb reports the speaker's assessment of the whole proposition: evidence, probability, regret, expectation. Contrast that with manner:
TL: Die Frist wurde sorgfältig berechnet.
EN: The deadline was calculated carefully.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Offenbar wurde die Frist falsch berechnet.
EN: Apparently, the deadline was calculated incorrectly.
[PAUSE:1200]
The first tells us how the calculation happened. The second tells us how the speaker knows or judges the entire claim. Another probability judgment:
TL: Wahrscheinlich wird der Einspruch keinen Erfolg haben.
EN: The appeal will probably not succeed.
[PAUSE:1000]
These adverbs can answer a yes-or-no question, but they do not answer how:
TL: Kommt die Ministerin? Vermutlich.
EN: Is the minister coming? Probably.
[PAUSE:1200]
Negation belongs inside their scope; it does not normally negate the attitude word itself.
SHOW: attitude adverb → speaker's stance toward the whole claim
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: German compresses whole English clauses
REF: 7.4.4

German often uses a compact adverb where idiomatic English chooses a verb or subordinate clause. This is not ornament; it is a major route to concise, native-like phrasing:
TL: Hoffentlich einigen sich beide Seiten noch heute.
EN: I hope both sides reach an agreement today.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Leider können wir den Termin nicht verschieben.
EN: I am afraid we cannot postpone the appointment.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Vorsitzende reist angeblich morgen ab.
EN: The chair is said to be leaving tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Preference works the same way, with adverbs attached directly to the activity:
TL: Sie liest abends gern Essays, hört aber lieber Interviews.
EN: She likes reading essays in the evening, but prefers listening to interviews.
[PAUSE:1200]
When English says I hope, I am afraid, is said to, likes, or prefers, first test whether German wants an adverb instead.
SHOW: English clause or verb → compact German adverb

[CARD]
TITLE: Reason adverbs link propositions
REF: 7.4.3, 7.6

Reason adverbs package cause, consequence, concession, condition and purpose. They connect ideas while occupying an ordinary clause position:
TL: Die Daten waren unvollständig; deshalb wurde die Abstimmung vertagt.
EN: The data were incomplete; therefore, the vote was postponed.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Warnung kam spät; trotzdem ließ sich der Fehler noch beheben.
EN: The warning came late; nevertheless, the error could still be corrected.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Falls der Server ausfällt, verwenden wir gegebenenfalls die Sicherungskopie.
EN: If the server fails, we will use the backup copy if necessary.
[PAUSE:1200]
Reason questions also encode register and purpose:
TL: Weshalb wurde der Zugang gesperrt, und wozu dient die neue Prüfung?
EN: Why was access blocked, and what is the new check for?
[PAUSE:1200]
Learn the logical relation with the word: result, concession, fallback, or purpose.
SHOW: cause → result · obstacle → concession · condition → fallback
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Different from is not otherwise
REF: 7.4.5

Two common adverbs overlap around English else, but they have distinct cores. Use the first for a different manner or a different identity:
TL: Wir müssen das Verfahren anders organisieren.
EN: We have to organize the procedure differently.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Niemand anders kann die Zahlung freigeben.
EN: Nobody else can authorize the payment.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use the second for an additional item or for the consequence otherwise:
TL: Hat sonst noch jemand Einwände?
EN: Does anyone else have objections?
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Beeil dich, sonst schließt der Schalter.
EN: Hurry up, otherwise the counter will close.
[PAUSE:1200]
If different is central, choose the different-family. If the meaning is in addition, apart from that, or otherwise, choose the consequence-family.
SHOW: anders = different · sonst = in addition / otherwise
The productive suffixes come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: The manner factory
REF: 7.4.6

The suffix built on the noun for way is highly productive. Attached to nouns or verb stems, it creates manner, distribution and approximation adverbs:
TL: Die Unterlagen wurden schrittweise digitalisiert.
EN: The documents were digitized step by step.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Beratungsraum kann stundenweise gebucht werden.
EN: The consultation room can be booked by the hour.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Ausnahmsweise tagt der Ausschuss online.
EN: By way of exception, the committee is meeting online.
[PAUSE:1200]
Read the base literally where possible: in steps, by the hour, by way of exception. That makes new formations interpretable rather than arbitrary.
SHOW: noun or verb stem + -weise → manner · distribution · approximation
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The attitude factory adds a hinge
REF: 7.4.6

A related formation inserts an extra element and normally turns an adjective or participle into a speaker comment. Compare plain manner with proposition-level judgment:
TL: Der Gutachter antwortete vernünftig.
EN: The reviewer answered sensibly.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Vernünftigerweise verlangte er einen zweiten Test.
EN: Sensibly enough, he requested a second test.
[PAUSE:1200]
The first describes the answer. The second endorses the decision. The same scope contrast appears here:
TL: Die Maschine lief merkwürdig ruhig.
EN: The machine ran strangely quietly.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Merkwürdigerweise lief die Maschine völlig geräuschlos.
EN: Strangely enough, the machine ran completely silently.
[PAUSE:1200]
These attitude forms do not become adjectives. The extra hinge signals a comment on the proposition, not a quality inside a noun phrase.
SHOW: -weise = manner · -erweise = speaker comment
A reformulation block on concise adverbs follows.

[CARD]
TITLE: Degree controls the scale
REF: 7.5

Degree adverbs amplify, soften or set a boundary. They most often modify adjectives and other adverbs:
TL: Der Entwurf ist ziemlich ausgereift, aber noch lange nicht gut genug.
EN: The draft is fairly mature, but still nowhere near good enough.
[PAUSE:1200]
One especially common intensifier can also modify verbs directly:
TL: Diese Entscheidung überrascht mich sehr.
EN: This decision surprises me very much.
[PAUSE:1000]
It also follows words asking or setting degree:
TL: Wie sehr belastet die Verzögerung das Projekt?
EN: How much is the delay affecting the project?
[PAUSE:1000]
But comparative forms normally take the quantity word instead:
TL: Die neue Lösung ist viel stabiler als die alte.
EN: The new solution is much more stable than the old one.
[PAUSE:1200]
Think scale position: slight, sufficient, extreme, almost, or far from the threshold.
SHOW: sehr with verbs and degree frames · viel with comparatives
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Adverbs climb the comparison ladder
SVG: de_adverbien_c12b.svg
REF: 7.7

Quality and quantity adverbs form a comparative like adjectives, but their ordinary superlative uses a fixed prepositional frame:
TL: Jana argumentiert präzise, Leon präziser, und Mira am präzisesten.
EN: Jana argues precisely, Leon more precisely, and Mira most precisely.
[PAUSE:1200]
TL: Von allen Kandidaten reagierte sie am schnellsten.
EN: Of all the candidates, she reacted fastest.
[PAUSE:1000]
High-frequency adverbs preserve important irregular ladders:
TL: Er arbeitet gern allein, lieber im kleinen Team und am liebsten mit seiner langjährigen Kollegin.
EN: He likes working alone, prefers working in a small team, and most enjoys working with his long-standing colleague.
[PAUSE:1200]
The common frequency adverb has a comparative, but its usual superlative switches stems:
TL: Diese Variante wird öfter gewählt, aber am häufigsten erscheint die Standardform.
EN: This variant is chosen fairly often, but the standard form appears most frequently.
[PAUSE:1200]
And quantity comparatives stay uninflected before nouns:
TL: Wir brauchen weniger Papier und mehr Geduld.
EN: We need less paper and more patience.
[PAUSE:1000]
The system closes where it began: the adverb's form is fixed by its function, not by agreement.
SHOW: regular: -er → am -sten · irregular: lieber → am liebsten
