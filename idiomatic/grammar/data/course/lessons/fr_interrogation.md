## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: First decide what kind of answer you need
REF: 14.1

A yes-or-no question asks whether a whole proposition is true. An information question replaces one missing element with a question word or phrase.
TL: Est-ce que Nora vient demain? — Oui.
EN: Is Nora coming tomorrow? — Yes.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Quand Nora vient-elle? — Demain.
EN: When is Nora coming? — Tomorrow.
SHOW: whole proposition → yes/no · missing element → information question
Flip to choose a register before you choose a word order.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Question forms carry register
REF: 14.2, 14.3

French often offers a conversational form, a neutral all-purpose form, and a formal form for the same question. The message can stay constant while the syntax marks the setting.
TL: Tu pars? · Est-ce que tu pars? · Pars-tu?
EN: Are you leaving? — conversational, neutral, and formal.
[PAUSE:900]
The neutral est-ce que pattern is the safest default across speech and writing. Inversion belongs especially to careful speech and writing.
SHOW: choose meaning first · then choose a form that fits the setting

[CARD]
TITLE: Build yes-or-no questions three ways
REF: 14.2.1, 14.2.2, 14.2.3

In conversation, keep statement order and raise the intonation. For a register-neutral question, put est-ce que before the statement. For a formal question with a pronoun subject, invert the finite verb and pronoun.
TL: Elle reste ici? · Est-ce qu'elle reste ici? · Reste-t-elle ici?
EN: Is she staying here?
[PAUSE:900]
All three ask about the same proposition. Only the framing changes.
SHOW: statement + rise · est-ce que + statement · verb-pronoun inversion
Flip for a noun subject in formal inversion.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Keep a noun subject and echo it with a pronoun
REF: 14.2.3, 14.2.4

A noun or proper-name subject stays before the verb. Add an agreeing subject pronoun after the finite verb; do not simply move the noun behind the verb.
TL: Pierre est-il prêt? · Les voyageuses sont-elles arrivées?
EN: Is Pierre ready? Have the women travellers arrived?
[PAUSE:900]
Insert written -t- when inversion would place two vowel sounds together and the verb supplies no t or d.
TL: Julie viendra-t-elle? · A-t-il compris?
EN: Will Julie come? Has he understood?
SHOW: noun subject + verb + agreeing pronoun · vowel-vowel → -t-

[CARD]
TITLE: Treat inversion with je as a restricted formal pattern
REF: 14.2.5, 14.2.6

Present-tense inversion with je is natural only with a small set of common forms such as ai-je, suis-je, puis-je, dois-je, vais-je, and dis-je. Elsewhere, prefer est-ce que.
TL: Puis-je entrer? · Est-ce que je prends le bus?
EN: May I come in? Am I taking the bus?
[PAUSE:900]
For an English-style tag question, French has the single invariable phrase n'est-ce pas.
TL: Vous habitez ici, n'est-ce pas?
EN: You live here, don't you?
SHOW: learned je inversion · otherwise est-ce que · tag → n'est-ce pas
Flip to answer a negative question without ambiguity.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Oui confirms; si contradicts a negative
REF: 14.2.8

Answer an affirmative yes-or-no question with oui or non. In response to a negative question, si rejects the negative and non confirms it.
TL: Tu viens? — Oui. · Tu ne viens pas? — Si, je viens.
EN: Are you coming? — Yes. Aren't you coming? — Yes, I am.
[PAUSE:900]
Merci alone after an offer normally means no, thank you. Use je veux bien, volontiers, or s'il vous plaît for yes, please.
SHOW: affirmative yes → oui · contradict a negative → si · no → non

[CARD]
TITLE: Build information questions along the same register scale
REF: 14.3.1, 14.3.2, 14.3.3, 14.3.5

Conversational French can leave the question phrase in place or front it without further changes. Neutral French fronts it before est-ce que. Formal French fronts it and uses inversion.
TL: Tu pars quand? · Quand tu pars? · Quand est-ce que tu pars? · Quand pars-tu?
EN: When are you leaving?
[PAUSE:1000]
The very informal fronted-statement pattern is common in speech but is not the best general writing default.
SHOW: in place · fronted + statement order · fronted + est-ce que · fronted + inversion
Flip for what at the conversational and neutral ends of the scale.

[SIDE]
TITLE: What changes shape when it moves
REF: 14.3.1, 14.3.2, 14.3.3, 14.6.2

Use stressed quoi when what stays in place or follows a preposition. Use qu'est-ce que for a neutral fronted direct-object question; formal que requires inversion with a pronoun subject.
TL: Tu cherches quoi? · À quoi penses-tu?
EN: What are you looking for? What are you thinking about?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Qu'est-ce que vous cherchez? · Que cherchez-vous?
EN: What are you looking for?
SHOW: in place / after preposition → quoi · neutral object → qu'est-ce que · formal → que + inversion
Forty-one clean response and transformation items begin after this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ask who or what by tracking subject and object
REF: 14.3.4, 14.3.6

For a person, qui can be the subject without inversion. The expanded neutral forms make the roles visible: qui est-ce qui for a person as subject and qui est-ce que for a person as direct object.
TL: Qui parle? · Qui est-ce qui parle? · Qui est-ce que Nora invite?
EN: Who is speaking? Who is speaking? Whom is Nora inviting?
[PAUSE:900]
Do not add a second subject pronoun after subject qui.
SHOW: person subject → qui / qui est-ce qui · person object → qui est-ce que
Flip for a thing as subject or direct object.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Qui marks the grammatical role for a thing
REF: 14.3.4, 14.3.6

For a non-person subject, use qu'est-ce qui. For a non-person direct object, use qu'est-ce que. The final qui or que tells whether the missing element is subject or object.
TL: Qu'est-ce qui a causé le bruit? · Qu'est-ce que Nora a entendu?
EN: What caused the noise? What did Nora hear?
[PAUSE:900]
Formal object que can invert with a pronoun subject: Qu'a-t-elle entendu? With a noun subject, prefer qu'est-ce que or a licensed stylistic inversion.
SHOW: thing subject → qu'est-ce qui · thing object → qu'est-ce que

[CARD]
TITLE: Inversion leaves clitics and negation in their normal slots
REF: 14.4, 14.5

Only the finite verb and subject relationship changes. Unstressed object pronouns remain before the verb, and ne plus the negative word still surrounds the finite verb complex.
TL: Le lui avait-il prêté? · Pourquoi me l'ont-ils donné?
EN: Had he lent it to her? Why did they give it to me?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: N'as-tu jamais fait cela? · Pourquoi ne leur écrivent-ils plus?
EN: Have you never done that? Why don't they write to them any more?
SHOW: object clitics stay before the verb · ne ... pas/jamais/plus keeps its frame
Flip for inversion without a repeated pronoun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Stylistic inversion is elegant but tightly licensed
REF: 14.3.7

Formal French can place a noun subject after the whole verb group without inserting a pronoun. It works freely with an intransitive verb that has no complement, or when the questioned phrase itself is the direct object.
TL: Où est allée Christine? · Quel vin recommande le patron?
EN: Where did Christine go? Which wine does the manager recommend?
[PAUSE:900]
It is not available with pourquoi, and extra complements can block it. When unsure, use noun-subject plus agreeing-pronoun inversion or est-ce que.
SHOW: licensed formal pattern → verb group + noun subject · never with pourquoi

[CARD]
TITLE: Keep a preposition attached to qui or quoi
REF: 14.6.1, 14.6.2

Qui covers a person as subject, object, or object of a preposition. Quoi is the stressed form used after a preposition when the target is not a person.
TL: À qui avez-vous écrit? · De qui parlent-ils?
EN: To whom did you write? Who are they talking about?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: À quoi pense-t-elle? · Sur quoi peut-on compter?
EN: What is she thinking about? What can one rely on?
SHOW: preposition + person → qui · preposition + thing → quoi
Flip to distinguish quel from lequel.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quel accompanies a noun; lequel stands for a choice
REF: 14.6.3, 14.6.4

Quel agrees with the noun it introduces. Lequel agrees with a noun already understood and asks which one or which ones from a set.
TL: Quel livre préférez-vous? · Dans quelle ville habite-t-elle?
EN: Which book do you prefer? In which town does she live?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Lequel de ces livres préférez-vous? · Lesquelles sont prêtes?
EN: Which of these books do you prefer? Which ones are ready?
SHOW: quel + noun · lequel replaces the chosen noun · both agree in gender and number

[CARD]
TITLE: Combien counts; comment asks the manner
REF: 14.6.5, 14.6.6, 14.6.8

Combien can stand alone or introduce de plus a noun. When combien alone is a direct object, keep the pronoun en.
TL: Combien de voyageurs sont arrivés? · Combien en as-tu vus?
EN: How many travellers arrived? How many did you see?
[PAUSE:900]
Comment asks how something happens or how someone is. French does not normally build how plus adjective by putting comment or combien before that adjective.
TL: Comment allez-vous? · De quelle taille est la table?
EN: How are you? How big is the table?
SHOW: quantity → combien (de) · manner → comment · how + adjective → recast the phrase
Flip for the common time, place, and reason words.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Où and quand allow patterns that pourquoi rejects
REF: 14.6.7

Où, quand, and pourquoi work with ordinary conversational, est-ce que, and pronoun-inversion patterns. With être, où and quand commonly use stylistic inversion.
TL: Où est le portefeuille? · Quand est son anniversaire?
EN: Where is the wallet? When is his birthday?
[PAUSE:800]
Pourquoi cannot use stylistic inversion. With a noun subject, use est-ce que or keep the noun and echo it with a pronoun.
TL: Pourquoi est-ce que Pierre est parti? · Pourquoi Pierre est-il parti?
EN: Why did Pierre leave?
SHOW: où / quand + être → stylistic inversion common · pourquoi → never stylistic inversion

[CARD]
TITLE: Indirect questions restore statement word order
REF: 14.7, 14.7.1

After verbs such as demander, se demander, savoir, comprendre, dire, or expliquer, the embedded question has no subject-verb inversion and no est-ce que.
TL: Où sont-ils? → Je ne sais pas où ils sont.
EN: Where are they? → I do not know where they are.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Pourquoi Marie est-elle revenue? → Dites-moi pourquoi Marie est revenue.
EN: Why did Marie return? → Tell me why Marie returned.
SHOW: reporting frame + question word + subject + verb
Flip for an embedded yes-or-no question.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Si introduces an indirect yes-or-no question
REF: 14.7.2

When a direct yes-or-no question becomes indirect, replace its question framing with si, meaning whether or if, and use statement order.
TL: Julie viendra-t-elle? → Je me demande si Julie viendra.
EN: Will Julie come? → I wonder whether Julie will come.
[PAUSE:900]
This interrogative si is not a hypothetical if-clause. Future and conditional forms may follow it when the meaning requires them.
SHOW: indirect yes/no → si + subject + verb

[CARD]
TITLE: Ce qui and ce que replace the expanded what forms
REF: 14.7.3

Direct qu'est-ce qui becomes ce qui inside an indirect question. Direct que or qu'est-ce que becomes ce que.
TL: Qu'est-ce qui est arrivé? → Elle demande ce qui est arrivé.
EN: What happened? → She asks what happened.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Qu'est-ce que Pierre fera? → Je ne sais pas ce que Pierre fera.
EN: What will Pierre do? → I do not know what Pierre will do.
SHOW: qu'est-ce qui → ce qui · que / qu'est-ce que → ce que
Flip for all the other information words.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Other question words survive the move indoors
REF: 14.7.1, 14.7.3

Qui, à qui, de quoi, quel, lequel, quand, où, comment, and pourquoi keep their identity in an indirect question. What disappears is direct-question syntax.
TL: À qui écrivait-il? → Elle demande à qui il écrivait.
EN: To whom was he writing? → She asks to whom he was writing.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Laquelle lui plaît? → Je ne sais pas laquelle lui plaît.
EN: Which one does he like? → I do not know which one he likes.
SHOW: keep the question phrase · remove inversion and est-ce que

[CARD]
TITLE: Let the reporting tense control the embedded tense
REF: 14.7.4

After a present, future, or conditional reporting verb, the original tense normally remains. A past reporting verb can shift a present to the imperfect and a past to the pluperfect.
TL: Elle ne sait pas s'il chante. · Elle ne savait pas s'il chantait.
EN: She does not know whether he sings. She did not know whether he sang.
[PAUSE:900]
A future viewed from a past reporting point normally becomes conditional.
TL: Chantera-t-il? → Elle ne savait pas s'il chanterait.
EN: Will he sing? → She did not know whether he would sing.
SHOW: present reporting frame → tense stays · past frame → backshift when required
Flip for the full construction sequence.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build every question in three decisions
REF: 14.1, 14.2, 14.3, 14.7

First decide whether the missing answer is the whole proposition or one element. Second identify the missing element's role: subject, object, prepositional object, quantity, place, time, manner, or reason. Third choose direct or indirect syntax and the register.
TL: Qu'est-ce qui a changé? · Pourquoi est-ce que cela a changé? · Je demande pourquoi cela a changé.
EN: What changed? Why did that change? I am asking why that changed.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not translate an English question word in isolation. Build the French question from answer type, grammatical role, register, and clause type.
SHOW: answer type → role → direct/indirect + register
