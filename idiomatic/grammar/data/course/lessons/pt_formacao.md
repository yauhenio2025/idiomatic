## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A suffix can describe both size and attitude
REF: 28.1, 28.2

Brazilian Portuguese uses expressive suffixes to do more than make a noun smaller or larger. The new form can also sound affectionate, pleasant, emphatic, admiring, or dismissive.
TL: casa → casinha · mesa → mesona
EN: house → little house · table → big table
[PAUSE:900]
The surrounding sentence decides whether literal size or the speaker's attitude matters most.
TL: Que tal uma cervejinha gelada?
EN: How about a nice cold beer?
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: base + suffix -> size and speaker attitude
Flip for the most regular diminutive pattern.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Replace a final unstressed vowel with -inho or -inha
REF: 28.1.1

When a noun or adjective ends in unstressed o, a, or e, remove that vowel and choose inho or inha according to gender.
TL: dedo → dedinho · casa → casinha · dente → dentinho
EN: finger → little finger · house → little house · tooth → little tooth
[PAUSE:950]
An adjective follows the noun it describes.
TL: O quarto ficou quentinho. · A sopa ficou quentinha.
EN: The room became nice and warm. · The soup became nice and warm.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: unstressed -o/-a/-e -> -inho/-inha

[CARD]
TITLE: Preserve the consonant sound at the join
REF: 28.1.1

Removing the final vowel can put c or g before i. Change the spelling when necessary so the original hard sound survives.
TL: saco → saquinho · lago → laguinho
EN: bag → little bag · lake → little lake
[PAUSE:950]
The spelling change is mechanical: qu keeps the hard c sound, and gu keeps the hard g sound.
TL: macaco → macaquinho · brega → breguinha
EN: monkey → little monkey · tacky → slightly tacky
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: c + i -> qu + i · g + i -> gu + i
Flip for bases ending in s or z.

[SIDE]
TITLE: After final s or z, add -inho or -inha
REF: 28.1.1

A noun or adjective ending in s or z keeps that consonant and adds the ordinary diminutive ending.
TL: japonês → japonesinho · voz → vozinha
EN: Japanese man → little Japanese guy · voice → little voice
[PAUSE:950]
The suffix carries the spoken stress, so an earlier written accent can disappear.
TL: lápis → lapisinho
EN: pencil → little pencil
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: final -s/-z + -inho/-inha · suffix stress removes the old accent

[CARD]
TITLE: Use -zinho or -zinha after the other endings
REF: 28.1.1

Add zinho or zinha after a stressed vowel, a nasal vowel, l, m, or r. The same pattern applies after io and ia.
TL: café → cafezinho · papel → papelzinho · flor → florzinha
EN: coffee → small black coffee · paper → scrap of paper · flower → little flower
[PAUSE:950]
TL: fio → fiozinho · tia → tiazinha
EN: thread → little thread · aunt → auntie
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: stressed vowel, nasal, -l/-m/-r, -io/-ia -> -zinho/-zinha
Flip for two spelling consequences.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Move the accent and build plurals from the plural stem
REF: 28.1.1

The i of the diminutive suffix receives the stress, so the base normally loses another written accent.
TL: pá → pazinha · bebê → bebezinho · pássaro → passarinho
EN: shovel → little shovel · baby → little baby · bird → little bird
[PAUSE:950]
For plural diminutives of words ending in l or ão, start from the plural stem before adding zinhos or zinhas.
TL: papel → papéis → papeizinhos · pão → pães → pãezinhos
EN: paper → papers → little papers · bread → loaves → bread rolls
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: base accent drops · plural stem + -zinhos/-zinhas

[CARD]
TITLE: Diminutives can make a noun warm, familiar, or inviting
REF: 28.1.2

Literal smallness is only the starting point. A diminutive often adds affection or presents something as pleasant and approachable.
TL: Tem uma manchinha nessa camisa.
EN: There is a little stain on this shirt.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Estou com saudades da minha mãezinha.
EN: I miss my dear mother.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Vamos tomar um cafezinho?
EN: Shall we have a little coffee?
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: smallness · affection · friendly invitation
The source formation drill follows this card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: With qualities, the suffix adjusts the degree
REF: 28.1.2

On an adjective or an adverb-like form, a diminutive can mean that a desirable state is exactly right.
TL: A roupa já está sequinha. · A mesa cabe direitinho aqui.
EN: The laundry is already good and dry. · The table fits exactly here.
[PAUSE:950]
With an unpleasant quality, it can soften the judgment rather than make it affectionate.
TL: Está friozinho lá fora. · A festa foi chatinha.
EN: It is chilly outside. · The party was a bit dull.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: desirable degree -> just right · unpleasant degree -> somewhat

[CARD]
TITLE: Some diminutives become dictionary words
REF: 28.1.2

A frequent diminutive can develop a conventional meaning that is not simply the base plus small.
TL: carrinho · cafezinho · bondinho
EN: cart or toy car · small black coffee · cable car
[PAUSE:1000]
Context can still reactivate a literal reading. Carrinho may name a cart, a toy car, or simply a little car.
TL: O carrinho de compras está na entrada.
EN: The shopping cart is at the entrance.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: productive form can become a stored lexical meaning
Flip to the augmentative.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Start augmentatives with -ão, -ona, -zão, or -zona
REF: 28.2.1

After unstressed o, a, or e, replace the vowel with ão or ona. After many other endings, add zão or zona.
TL: dedo → dedão · mesa → mesona · pai → paizão · mãe → mãezona
EN: finger → thumb or big toe · table → big table · father → devoted father · mother → devoted mother
[PAUSE:1000]
Some words ending in l or r instead take ão or ona directly.
TL: papel → papelão · colher → colherona
EN: paper → cardboard · spoon → large spoon
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: common pattern first · confirm the established word

[CARD]
TITLE: Augmentatives are emphatic but less predictable
REF: 28.2.1, 28.2.2

The augmentative highlights size, intensity, or an impressive quality. Its form is less regular than the diminutive, and a feminine base can produce a masculine derivative.
TL: Que narigão! · Aquela estante é pesadona.
EN: What a huge nose! · That bookcase is really heavy.
[PAUSE:950]
TL: cabeça → cabeção · mulher → mulherão
EN: head → big head · woman → striking woman
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: emphasis + lexical convention · gender may shift
Flip for augmentatives whose meanings have specialized.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Do not decode every augmentative literally
REF: 28.2.2

Some common forms have settled into distinct meanings.
TL: caixa → caixão · papel → papelão
EN: box → coffin · paper → cardboard
[PAUSE:900]
TL: sacola → sacolão · mochila → mochilão
EN: carrier bag → fruit and vegetable market · backpack → backpacking tour
[PAUSE:950]
Learn these as words while keeping the productive pattern available for transparent new cases.
SHOW: analyze the suffix · then verify the lexical meaning

[CARD]
TITLE: -aço praises; -eco disparages
REF: 28.3, 28.4

The suffix aço can turn a noun into an enthusiastic evaluation.
TL: Que golaço! · Foi um jogaço.
EN: What a great goal! · It was a fantastic game.
[PAUSE:950]
The suffix eco has the opposite flavor with a limited group of nouns: it expresses poor quality or contempt.
TL: um filmeco · um jornaleco
EN: a really bad movie · a low-quality newspaper
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: -aço -> appreciation · -eco -> contempt
Flip from expressive size to events.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A feminine participle can name a brief action
REF: 28.5.1, 28.5.2

Portuguese can turn the feminine past-participle form into a noun ending in ada or ida.
TL: olhar → uma olhada · ler → uma lida
EN: to look → a look · to read → a read
[PAUSE:950]
Combine the noun with dar to present the action as one bounded event.
TL: Vou dar uma olhada no relatório.
EN: I am going to take a look at the report.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: verb -> feminine event noun · dar uma + event

[CARD]
TITLE: Introduce the event noun's target with em
REF: 28.5.2

When an ada or ida event noun has an object, em introduces that target. The contraction then follows the ordinary gender and number rules.
TL: Dei uma lida no jornal.
EN: I had a read of the newspaper.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Vou dar uma limpadinha nesses vidros.
EN: I am going to give these windows a bit of a clean.
[PAUSE:950]
A diminutive can make the action sound brief, light, or informal.
SHOW: dar uma + event + em + target
Flip when the base is an object rather than a verb.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Instrument + -ada names a blow or impact
REF: 28.6.1

Adding ada to the name of an object can create a noun for a blow delivered with it.
TL: martelo → martelada · pedra → pedrada
EN: hammer → blow with a hammer · stone → blow with a stone
[PAUSE:900]
Some established forms translate more naturally as a particular injury or action.
TL: faca → facada · pau → paulada
EN: knife → knife wound · stick → blow with a stick
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: instrument noun + -ada -> impact noun

[CARD]
TITLE: The verb frames the impact from either side
REF: 28.6.2

Use dar uma with em to present the person delivering a blow. Use levar uma to present the person receiving it.
TL: O assaltante deu uma coronhada no gerente.
EN: The robber pistol-whipped the manager.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: O jogador levou uma bolada na cara.
EN: The player was hit in the face with the ball.
[PAUSE:950]
The plural phrase a pauladas presents the means as repeated blows.
TL: Quebraram a porta a pauladas.
EN: They broke the door by hitting it with sticks.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: dar uma ... em -> deliver · levar uma ... -> receive · a ...s -> repeated means
Flip for a third family ending in ada.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Living beings + -ada can name an informal group
REF: 28.7.1, 28.7.2

With a noun for a living being, ada can form an informal collective.
TL: criança → criançada · mulher → mulherada · cachorro → cachorrada
EN: child → kids · woman → women · dog → dogs
[PAUSE:950]
Rapaz uses an extra i in the established form rapaziada. These collectives often take the definite article or serve as forms of address.
TL: Vamos embora, rapaziada.
EN: Let us get going, everyone.
[PAUSE:950]
They are informal and can sound affectionate, neutral, or contemptuous according to context.
SHOW: living-being noun + -ada -> informal collective

[CARD]
TITLE: Read a derived word in three passes
REF: 28.1, 28.2, 28.3, 28.4, 28.5, 28.6, 28.7

First identify the base and the visible suffix. Next choose the suffix's productive contribution: size, evaluation, a bounded action, an impact, or a group.
[PAUSE:900]
Finally test the result against context, because familiar derivatives can acquire a stored meaning or a special tone.
TL: cafezinho · papelão · uma olhada · uma pedrada · a criançada
EN: small black coffee · cardboard · a look · a blow with a stone · the kids
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: base -> suffix pattern -> contextual meaning
Flip for the production routine.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build only after checking the final sound and the intended tone
REF: 28.1, 28.2, 28.3, 28.4, 28.5, 28.6, 28.7

For a diminutive, decide between inho or inha and zinho or zinha from the ending of the base, then apply the stress and plural rules. For an augmentative, begin with the common pattern but verify the established form.
[PAUSE:900]
For ada, ask what the base denotes. A verb can yield a brief event, an object can yield an impact, and a living being can yield an informal collective.
TL: lago → laguinho · pai → paizão · ler → uma lida · criança → criançada
EN: lake → little lake · father → devoted father · read → a read · child → kids
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: sound -> suffix -> spelling -> context -> tone
