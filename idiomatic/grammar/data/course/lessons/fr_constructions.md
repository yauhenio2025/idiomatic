## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Learn a verb together with the complement frame it licenses
REF: 8.1, 8.2, 8.3, 8.4, 8.5

A French verb can be intransitive, directly transitive, indirectly transitive, or ditransitive. The frame tells you whether the verb has no object, a bare direct object, a prepositional object, or both kinds of object.
TL: Jeanne part; Malik ferme la porte; Yvon hérite d'une fortune; Hervé offre des fleurs à Léa.
EN: Jeanne is leaving; Malik closes the door; Yvon inherits a fortune; Hervé gives flowers to Léa.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not learn only the English gloss. Store the French infinitive with its construction, because two apparent synonyms may license different complements.
SHOW: partir · fermer qc · hériter de qc · offrir qc à qn
Flip for verbs whose frame changes with their meaning.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A changed frame can create a changed meaning
REF: 8.3.1, 8.3.3

Some verbs allow an understood object, while others alternate between an intransitive event and a transitive action. Ask whether a participant acts, changes state, or causes something else to change.
TL: Le bureau ferme à dix-huit heures; le gardien ferme le bureau à dix-huit heures.
EN: The office closes at six; the caretaker closes the office at six.
[PAUSE:900]
The first clause has no object. In the second, la porte is the direct object. That structural difference matters for pronouns, compound-tense auxiliaries, and the passive.
TL: La voiture ralentit; le conducteur ralentit la voiture.
EN: The car slows down; the driver slows the car down.
SHOW: no object → intransitive · cause + direct object → transitive

[CARD]
TITLE: Most compound forms use avoir, but a small intransitive set uses être
REF: 8.2.1, 8.2.2

Most intransitive verbs form compound tenses with avoir. A smaller, high-frequency set of motion or change-of-state verbs uses être, and its participle agrees with the subject.
TL: Les prix ont augmenté; Claire est arrivée; ses collègues sont partis.
EN: Prices rose; Claire arrived; her colleagues left.
[PAUSE:900]
Learn the auxiliary as part of the verb's intransitive construction. Do not infer it merely from the English meaning or from the presence of movement.
SHOW: most intransitives → avoir · selected intransitives → être + subject agreement
Eighteen clean auxiliary-choice drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A transitive use switches an être verb to avoir
REF: 8.3.2, 8.3.4

When a normally être-taking movement verb is used with a direct object, the construction is transitive and the compound tense takes avoir.
TL: Zoé est sortie; Zoé a sorti les poubelles.
EN: Zoé went out; Zoé took out the bins.
[PAUSE:900]
The same contrast appears with monter, descendre, rentrer, retourner, and related verbs. Locate the direct object before choosing the auxiliary.
TL: Ils sont descendus au sous-sol; ils ont descendu les cartons.
EN: They went down to the basement; they took the boxes down.
SHOW: subject moves → être · subject moves an object → avoir

[CARD]
TITLE: French directness does not always match English directness
REF: 8.3.5, 8.4.1

Some French verbs take a bare direct object where English uses a preposition. Others require à or de where English uses a direct object. The translation alone cannot determine the frame.
TL: Nous écoutons la radio; elle attend son frère; il obéit à ses parents.
EN: We listen to the radio; she waits for her brother; he obeys his parents.
[PAUSE:900]
Test the French construction with a noun before replacing that noun with a pronoun. A direct object becomes le, la, les; an à-object may become lui or leur, depending on the noun.
SHOW: écouter qc · attendre qn · obéir à qn
Flip for de-objects and changes of meaning.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The preposition belongs to the French verb, not to its translation
REF: 8.4, 8.4.1

Indirectly transitive verbs select their own preposition. Even related meanings can use different frames, and changing the preposition can change the sense.
TL: Elle pense à son examen; que pensez-vous de sa réponse?
EN: She is thinking about her exam; what do you think of her answer?
[PAUSE:900]
Learn a minimal phrase with each use. This prevents English from supplying a preposition where French has none, or removing one that French requires.
TL: Il croit cette histoire, mais il ne croit pas au hasard.
EN: He believes this story, but he does not believe in chance.
SHOW: lexical meaning + complement type + preposition → one stored construction

[CARD]
TITLE: A ditransitive verb keeps one direct and one prepositional object
REF: 8.5, 8.5.1

Ditransitive verbs combine a direct object with an object introduced by à or de. French does not copy the English double-object order by dropping the preposition.
TL: Le patron accorde deux jours de congé aux ouvriers.
EN: The manager grants the workers two days of leave.
[PAUSE:900]
Deux jours de congé is what is granted, so it is direct. Aux ouvriers names the recipients and keeps à. Build the French frame before arranging the nouns.
TL: Elle remplit le seau d'eau; il cache la vérité à ses parents.
EN: She fills the bucket with water; he hides the truth from his parents.
SHOW: verb + qc + à/de + qn/qc · never verb + recipient + thing without a preposition
Six clean ditransitive drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Pronouns preserve the two-object architecture
REF: 8.5, 3.2.12, 3.2.16

When both complements become pronouns, identify their roles first. Direct-object and indirect-object pronouns occupy different slots even when English uses two bare noun phrases.
TL: Elle offre les billets à Nora; elle les lui offre.
EN: She offers the tickets to Nora; she offers them to her.
[PAUSE:900]
A de-object may instead be represented by en. The reliable sequence is to reconstruct the full noun frame, classify each complement, and only then choose and order pronouns.
TL: Il remplit le verre de jus; il en remplit le verre.
EN: He fills the glass with juice; he fills the glass with it.
SHOW: reconstruct nouns → label direct/prepositional → choose pronouns → place them

[CARD]
TITLE: The passive promotes only a direct object
REF: 8.6.1, 8.6.3

To form a passive, move the active direct object into subject position and insert être in the original tense. The lexical verb becomes a past participle.
TL: Un éditeur a acheté les droits; les droits ont été achetés par un éditeur.
EN: A publisher bought the rights; the rights were bought by a publisher.
[PAUSE:900]
An indirect or prepositional object cannot be promoted. With offrir quelque chose à quelqu'un, the thing may become the passive subject, but the recipient must retain à.
TL: On a offert un prix à Lina; un prix a été offert à Lina.
EN: They offered Lina a prize; a prize was offered to Lina.
SHOW: direct object → subject + être + participle · prepositional object stays prepositional
Seventeen clean active-to-passive drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Passive agreement follows the promoted subject
REF: 8.6.1

Because the passive auxiliary is être, the past participle agrees in gender and number with the new grammatical subject.
TL: La décision a été annoncée; les mesures ont été annoncées.
EN: The decision was announced; the measures were announced.
[PAUSE:900]
An expressed agent normally follows par. De is more likely when the phrase describes a state, feeling, or enduring relation rather than a deliberate action.
TL: La route a été bloquée par les manifestants; cette actrice est aimée du public.
EN: The road was blocked by the demonstrators; this actress is loved by the public.
SHOW: être agrees with subject · par → agent/action · de → state or relation

[CARD]
TITLE: A grammatical passive can still be unnatural
REF: 8.6.2, 8.6.3

Only a direct object is structurally eligible for promotion, and even then the resulting viewpoint must make sense. French rejects the English pattern that promotes a recipient from a double-object construction.
TL: On a envoyé une lettre à Jules; une lettre a été envoyée à Jules.
EN: Someone sent Jules a letter; a letter was sent to Jules.
[PAUSE:900]
Keep à Jules. A sentence modeled on Jules was sent a letter would falsely treat the recipient as the direct object.
SHOW: structural eligibility + sensible viewpoint → acceptable passive
Flip for the active alternatives that French often prefers.

[SIDE]
TITLE: French often replaces an English passive with on, se, or an impersonal frame
REF: 8.6.4, 8.7.6

French uses the passive less freely than English. An active clause with on removes a specific agent; a pronominal construction can describe a general process; an impersonal expression can background the actor.
TL: On vend ces billets en ligne; ces billets se vendent rapidement.
EN: These tickets are sold online; these tickets sell quickly.
[PAUSE:900]
Choose according to meaning. On suggests an unspecified human agent, while passive se often presents a property, norm, or process associated with the subject.
TL: Il est interdit de stationner ici.
EN: Parking is prohibited here.
SHOW: unspecified people → on · general process/property → se · rule/event framing → impersonal

[CARD]
TITLE: Pronominal verbs carry a subject-matching unstressed pronoun
REF: 8.7, 8.7.1, 8.7.3, 8.7.5

A pronominal verb uses me, te, se, nous, or vous with the finite verb. Its meaning may be reflexive, reciprocal, inherently pronominal, or lexical without a transparent self meaning.
TL: Lina se lave; les collègues se saluent; Hugo s'évanouit; nous nous souvenons du rendez-vous.
EN: Lina washes herself; the colleagues greet each other; Hugo faints; we remember the appointment.
[PAUSE:900]
The pronoun's presence does not prove a literal reflexive meaning. Learn inherently pronominal verbs with se in the dictionary form and classify the meaning in context.
SHOW: reflexive · reciprocal · inherently/lexically pronominal · passive use
Flip for body-part constructions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Body parts normally take an article, not a possessive
REF: 8.7.2, 2.2.8

When the subject acts on their own body, French normally uses a pronominal verb and a definite article before the body part.
TL: Je me lave les mains; elle s'est cassé la jambe.
EN: I wash my hands; she broke her leg.
[PAUSE:900]
The body part is the direct object, so the reflexive pronoun represents the affected person indirectly. That analysis later determines past-participle agreement.
TL: Ils se brossent les dents avant de partir.
EN: They brush their teeth before leaving.
SHOW: subject + reflexive pronoun + verb + definite article + body part

[CARD]
TITLE: Decide whether se is direct or indirect by rebuilding the base frame
REF: 8.7.1, 8.7.5

Remove the pronominal form and ask how the non-pronominal verb relates the participants. If it is cacher quelqu'un, se is direct; if it is parler à quelqu'un, se is indirect.
TL: Nora se cache; Nora cache Nora. Nora se parle; Nora parle à Nora.
EN: Nora hides herself; Nora talks to herself.
[PAUSE:900]
This is a grammatical test, not a translation test. The same direct-versus-indirect distinction applies when a plural subject gives a reciprocal reading.
TL: Elles se voient; elles se téléphonent.
EN: They see each other; they telephone each other.
SHOW: base verb + qn → direct se · base verb + à qn → indirect se

[SIDE]
TITLE: L'un l'autre makes a reciprocal relation explicit
REF: 8.7.5

A plural pronominal construction can sometimes be interpreted reflexively or reciprocally. Add the appropriate form of l'un l'autre when the each-other meaning must be unmistakable.
TL: Les deux témoins se critiquent l'un l'autre.
EN: The two witnesses criticize each other.
[PAUSE:900]
The preposition required by the base verb remains before the second element. Gender and number also affect the expression.
TL: Les deux candidates parlent l'une à l'autre; les équipes se méfient les unes des autres.
EN: The two candidates talk to each other; the teams distrust one another.
SHOW: direct relation → l'un l'autre · à/de relation → l'un à/de l'autre

[CARD]
TITLE: Pronominal compound tenses use être, but agreement tracks a direct object
REF: 8.7.7

All pronominal verbs form compound tenses with être. Unlike ordinary être verbs, however, their participle agrees when the pronominal object is direct, not simply because it matches the subject.
TL: Elle s'est lavée; ils se sont rencontrés; elles se sont parlé.
EN: She washed herself; they met each other; they spoke to each other.
[PAUSE:900]
In the first two examples the pronoun is direct, so agreement appears. Parler takes à, making se indirect, so parlé remains unchanged.
SHOW: être auxiliary · direct se → agreement · indirect se → no agreement
Twenty-three clean classification and agreement drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An explicit direct object can block agreement with se
REF: 8.7.2, 8.7.7

With a body part or another direct object after the verb, se is normally indirect and the participle does not agree with it.
TL: Elle s'est lavé les mains; ils se sont offert des billets.
EN: She washed her hands; they gave themselves tickets.
[PAUSE:900]
A preceding direct object can trigger agreement under the general object-agreement rule. Reconstruct the complements and locate the direct object before judging the ending.
TL: Les mains qu'elle s'est lavées; les lettres qu'ils se sont écrites.
EN: The hands she washed; the letters they wrote to each other.
SHOW: following direct object → no agreement with se · preceding direct object → agreement with that object

[CARD]
TITLE: Passive se presents a process without naming an agent
REF: 8.7.6

A pronominal construction can express the equivalent of an English passive, especially for general procedures, properties, and customary actions.
TL: Ce fromage se mange chaud; les billets se vendent à l'entrée.
EN: This cheese is eaten hot; tickets are sold at the entrance.
[PAUSE:900]
The grammatical subject is not acting on itself. It is the entity affected by a generalized process, and the agent is left unexpressed.
TL: Cette opération s'effectue en quelques minutes.
EN: This operation can be carried out in a few minutes.
SHOW: affected subject + se + active-form verb → generalized passive meaning
Eight clean passive-versus-lexical judgments follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Se faire and se laisser focus on what befalls the subject
REF: 8.7.4

Se faire and se laisser followed by an infinitive describe an event that the subject causes, permits, or experiences, sometimes without intending it.
TL: Elle s'est fait couper les cheveux; il s'est laissé convaincre.
EN: She had her hair cut; he let himself be persuaded.
[PAUSE:900]
Translate the whole construction, not each word separately. Context decides whether it means arranging an event, allowing it, or suffering its result.
TL: Il s'est fait voler son vélo dans la cour.
EN: He had his bicycle stolen in the courtyard.
SHOW: se faire + infinitive → cause/experience · se laisser + infinitive → allow/yield

[CARD]
TITLE: Impersonal il fills subject position without referring to anyone
REF: 8.8, 8.8.1, 8.8.2, 8.8.3

An impersonal verb uses il as a grammatical subject with no person or thing as its referent. Weather verbs, falloir, and il y a are central patterns.
TL: Il pleut; il faut deux signatures; il faut répondre; il y a une erreur.
EN: It is raining; two signatures are needed; one must answer; there is an error.
[PAUSE:900]
Falloir can take a noun, an infinitive, or a clause with the subjunctive. An indirect-object pronoun can name the person affected by the necessity.
TL: Il me faut une copie; il faut que vous répondiez aujourd'hui.
EN: I need a copy; you must reply today.
SHOW: il + weather verb · il faut + noun/infinitive/que + subjunctive · il y a + noun
Seven clean impersonal-frame drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Il s'agit de is fixed as an impersonal construction
REF: 8.8.4, 8.8.5

Il s'agit de can introduce a noun or infinitive, but its il cannot be replaced by a personal subject. To say what a book or discussion concerns, keep the impersonal frame.
TL: Dans ce chapitre, il s'agit des constructions verbales; il s'agit de choisir le bon complément.
EN: This chapter is about verb constructions; the task is to choose the right complement.
[PAUSE:900]
Some normally personal verbs can also appear impersonally, often with the logical subject later in the clause. Recognize the structure before translating word order.
TL: Il manque deux dossiers; il est arrivé un incident curieux.
EN: Two files are missing; a curious incident occurred.
SHOW: il s'agit de + noun/infinitive · impersonal il + verb + delayed logical subject
