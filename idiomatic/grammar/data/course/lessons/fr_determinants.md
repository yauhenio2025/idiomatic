## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A determiner tells the hearer how to locate a noun
REF: 2, 2.1

French determiners do more than display gender and number. They present a referent as identifiable, as one unspecified countable item, as an unbounded quantity, as pointed out, or as anchored to a possessor.
TL: Passe-moi la fourchette qui est près de toi.
EN: Pass me the fork that is near you.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Passe-moi une fourchette; n'importe laquelle conviendra.
EN: Pass me a fork; any one will do.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first request assumes one identifiable fork. The second deliberately leaves the choice open.
SHOW: definite · indefinite · partitive · demonstrative · possessive
Flip for the core inventory.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Match meaning before matching form
REF: 2, 2.1

The definite series is le, la, les; the singular indefinite forms are un and une, with plural des. Partitives use du, de la, de l' and sometimes plural des.
TL: J'ai acheté une bouteille et du lait.
EN: I bought a bottle and some milk.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Cette bouteille est à mon frère.
EN: This bottle belongs to my brother.
[PAUSE:1000]
Cette contrasts the bottle with alternatives; mon anchors frère to the speaker. Form follows the noun's gender, number and initial sound, but the intended discourse status comes first.
SHOW: decide status → retrieve series → agree with noun

[CARD]
TITLE: Elision responds to the next spoken onset
REF: 2.1.1

Immediately before a vowel sound or h muet, le and la become l', while du and de la become de l'. Gender still exists even though the short form hides it.
TL: l'univers, l'électricité, de l'acier, de l'eau
EN: the universe, electricity, some steel, some water
[PAUSE:1000]
An h aspiré begins no audible h sound, but it blocks elision and liaison as if it were a consonant.
TL: le héros, la haine, du hachis
EN: the hero, hatred, some minced meat
SHOW: vowel or h muet → elide · h aspiré → keep full form
Flip for contractions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: À and de fuse only with le and les
REF: 2.1.1, 2.2.1

The prepositions à and de fuse with masculine singular le and with plural les: au, aux, du, des. They remain separate from la and l'.
TL: Nous allons au musée puis à la bibliothèque.
EN: We are going to the museum and then to the library.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle revient des bureaux et de l'université.
EN: She is coming back from the offices and the university.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not confuse fused des, meaning de + les, with indefinite or partitive des. Meaning and syntax identify which one is present.
SHOW: à + le/les → au/aux · de + le/les → du/des

[CARD]
TITLE: The definite article marks shared identifiability
REF: 2.2

Use le, la or les when the context makes a referent uniquely identifiable. French also uses the definite article for a class, a unique phenomenon or an abstract quality in general where English often uses no article.
TL: La patience facilite le travail en équipe.
EN: Patience makes teamwork easier.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Les abeilles jouent un rôle essentiel dans cet écosystème.
EN: Bees play an essential role in this ecosystem.
[PAUSE:1000]
The article presents patience and bees as whole concepts, not as newly introduced instances.
SHOW: identifiable referent · whole class · general quality
Flip for names and conventional uses.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Proper names and routines follow French conventions
REF: 2.2.2, 2.2.3, 2.2.4, 2.2.5, 2.2.7

Countries, regions and languages commonly take the definite article, though some place names omit it and a language after parler normally appears bare.
TL: La Croatie se trouve en Europe; on y parle croate.
EN: Croatia is in Europe; Croatian is spoken there.
[PAUSE:900]
Seasons, titles and quoted rates also have established patterns.
TL: Monsieur le Maire vend ces affiches cinq euros le rouleau.
EN: The mayor sells these posters at five euros a roll.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn the construction rather than mechanically copying an English article.
SHOW: translate the convention, not the visible English form
Twelve clean definite-article decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Body-part possession is often inferred
REF: 2.2.8

When an action clearly identifies whose body is affected, French normally uses the definite article and often an object or reflexive pronoun instead of an English-style possessive.
TL: Je me suis fracturé la jambe.
EN: I broke my leg.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle lui serre la main.
EN: She shakes his hand.
[PAUSE:1000]
If the body part itself is the subject, a possessive is usual: Ma tête me fait mal.
SHOW: known possessor in the clause → often definite body part
Flip for distributive number.

[SIDE]
TITLE: One each is normally singular in French
REF: 2.2.9

When several people each have one body part, garment or personal attribute, French often keeps the possessed noun singular.
TL: Les élèves ont levé la main droite.
EN: The pupils raised their right hands.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ils ont tous accroché leur manteau dans l'entrée.
EN: They all hung their coats in the entrance hall.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use the plural only when each person has more than one relevant item or when the context genuinely refers to a plural set.
SHOW: several possessors × one item each → singular item
Seven number-sensitive translations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Indefinite des disappears after governing de
REF: 2.3.2, 2.3.3

When a construction already supplies the preposition de, a following indefinite or partitive phrase loses its determiner.
TL: Nous avons besoin d'argent et de conseils fiables.
EN: We need money and reliable advice.
[PAUSE:900]
The underlying ideas are de l'argent and des conseils, but besoin de supplies the governing de. The result is not *de de l'argent or *de des conseils.
SHOW: governing de + indefinite/partitive phrase → bare de
Flip for a definite noun phrase.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Definite les survives by fusing with de
REF: 2.3.2, 2.3.3

A definite plural phrase remains definite after de, producing the fused form des.
TL: Nous avons besoin des conseils que vous avez préparés.
EN: We need the advice that you prepared.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Elle parle de projets concrets, puis des projets de son équipe.
EN: She talks about concrete projects, then about her team's projects.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first de marks an indefinite class; the second des is de + les and points to an identifiable set.
SHOW: de + indefinite des → de · de + definite les → des
Eight de-versus-des decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: D'autres and des autres encode different structures
REF: 2.3.4

D'autres usually means other or others without identifying a closed set. Les autres identifies the remainder, and des autres is de + les autres.
TL: Certains restent ici; d'autres partent demain.
EN: Some are staying here; others are leaving tomorrow.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Je connais l'avis des autres membres du comité.
EN: I know the opinion of the other committee members.
[PAUSE:1000]
Ask whether de is indefinite packaging or a preposition governing an identifiable remainder.
SHOW: d'autres = others · les autres = the others · des autres = of the others
Flip for adjective placement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Careful writing often uses de before an adjective
REF: 2.3.5

Plural indefinite des commonly becomes de when an adjective precedes the noun in formal written French.
TL: Ils ont proposé de nouvelles méthodes.
EN: They proposed new methods.
[PAUSE:800]
Established adjective-noun units may retain des.
TL: Nous avons visité des grands magasins et acheté des petits pois.
EN: We visited department stores and bought peas.
[PAUSE:1000]
Spoken usage varies, but the course exercises target the careful written contrast.
SHOW: des + prenominal adjective → usually de · lexical unit may keep des
Eight clean formal-style choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The partitive packages an unbounded amount
REF: 2.4

Use du, de la or de l' when a mass noun or abstract noun denotes an unspecified quantity. English often uses some or no visible article.
TL: Versez de l'huile et ajoutez du sel.
EN: Pour in some oil and add salt.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle a de la patience et du courage.
EN: She has patience and courage.
[PAUSE:1000]
Plural des can also package nouns that are grammatically plural but mass-like in meaning.
SHOW: unspecified amount, substance or quality → partitive
Flip for established activity expressions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Faire often selects a partitive activity phrase
REF: 2.4.1

Many activity expressions use faire followed by a partitive noun phrase.
TL: Nous faisons du sport le samedi.
EN: We do sport on Saturdays.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle fait de la politique depuis plusieurs années.
EN: She has been active in politics for several years.
[PAUSE:1000]
The partitive is part of the French construction. Store the verb and noun phrase together, then apply ordinary contraction and elision.
SHOW: faire du · faire de la · faire de l'
Three clean whole-sentence partitive translations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Negation usually reduces an indefinite object to de
REF: 2.5

After ne...pas, ne...jamais, ne...plus and ne...guère, an indefinite or partitive direct object normally takes de.
TL: J'achète du chocolat, mais je n'achète jamais de bonbons.
EN: I buy chocolate, but I never buy sweets.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Elle a trouvé une place; il n'a pas trouvé de place.
EN: She found a space; he did not find a space.
[PAUSE:1000]
The reduction belongs to the scope of negation, not to every de phrase in a negative sentence.
SHOW: negated indefinite direct object → de
Flip for the three main exceptions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Être, one single item and contrast preserve the article
REF: 2.5

After être, the indefinite form remains. It also remains when the meaning insists on not one single item or explicitly contrasts one noun with another.
TL: Ce n'est pas une erreur.
EN: It is not a mistake.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Elle n'a pas fumé une seule cigarette.
EN: She did not smoke a single cigarette.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Je ne cherche pas un tournevis, mais une perceuse.
EN: I am not looking for a screwdriver, but a drill.
[PAUSE:1000]
Diagnose the construction before replacing an article.
SHOW: être · not a single · explicit contrast → retain article
Ten negative transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Zero article is licensed by a construction
REF: 2.6

French omits the article in many established noun constructions. A noun linked by de may classify another noun rather than identify a particular referent.
TL: une odeur de roses, un carnet d'adresses
EN: a smell of roses, an address book
[PAUSE:900]
Participles and set prepositional phrases can behave similarly.
TL: des lunettes cerclées d'or; voyager en train
EN: gold-rimmed glasses; to travel by train
[PAUSE:1000]
The absence is grammatical, not an invitation to omit articles freely.
SHOW: identify the licensing construction before using zero article
Flip for roles, apposition and lists.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Bare role nouns describe status, not a classified instance
REF: 2.6.6, 2.6.7, 2.6.8

An unmodified profession, nationality or role after être, devenir and similar verbs often appears bare. Modification can restore an article.
TL: Elle est devenue architecte, puis une architecte reconnue.
EN: She became an architect, then a recognized architect.
[PAUSE:900]
Appositions and coordinated lists may also omit articles when the whole construction licenses it.
TL: Versailles, palais de Louis XIV, attire des visiteurs du monde entier.
EN: Versailles, Louis XIV's palace, attracts visitors from around the world.
[PAUSE:1000]
Contrast classification with reference to a particular person or object.
SHOW: bare classification · modified or identified instance
Five clean article-omission translations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Demonstratives point by contrast
REF: 2.7, 2.7.1

Ce accompanies a masculine singular noun beginning with a consonant sound, cet a masculine noun beginning with a vowel sound or h muet, cette a feminine singular noun, and ces any plural noun.
TL: ce dossier, cet ancien dossier, cette ébauche, ces documents
EN: this file, this old file, this draft, these documents
[PAUSE:1000]
The forms can mean this or that; context supplies distance. Add -ci or -là when an explicit contrast is needed.
TL: Comparez ce rapport-ci avec ce rapport-là.
EN: Compare this report with that report.
SHOW: ce/cet/cette/ces + noun · -ci/-là for explicit contrast
Flip for reference across sentences.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A demonstrative can turn prior discourse into a noun phrase
REF: 2.7.1

A demonstrative often presents a newly mentioned event or person as the identifiable topic of the next sentence.
TL: Il a refusé de répondre. Cette réaction a surpris le comité.
EN: He refused to answer. This reaction surprised the committee.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: On lui présenta un inconnu. Cet homme connaissait déjà son nom.
EN: He was introduced to a stranger. This man already knew his name.
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose the demonstrative when the intended contrast or discourse link matters, not merely because English has the.
SHOW: prior mention → demonstrative recategorization

[CARD]
TITLE: Possessives agree with the possessed noun
REF: 2.8

Mon, ma, mes and the other possessive determiners match the gender and number of the noun that follows, not the possessor.
TL: Paul cherche sa veste; Marie cherche aussi sa veste.
EN: Paul is looking for his jacket; Marie is also looking for her jacket.
[PAUSE:900]
Before a feminine singular vowel sound or h muet, use mon, ton or son to avoid hiatus.
TL: mon amie, ton opinion, son histoire
EN: my friend, your opinion, his or her story
[PAUSE:1000]
The possessive form alone may not reveal the possessor's natural gender; context does that work.
SHOW: possessor chooses person · possessed noun chooses form
Flip for possessive pronouns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Possessive pronouns replace the whole noun phrase
REF: 2.8

Le mien, la mienne, les miens and les miennes agree with the omitted possessed noun. The corresponding series includes le tien, le sien, le nôtre, le vôtre and le leur.
TL: Ma voiture est garée à côté de la vôtre.
EN: My car is parked next to yours.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Vos enfants ont le même âge que les nôtres.
EN: Your children are the same age as ours.
[PAUSE:1000]
The definite article is part of the possessive pronoun and changes for gender and number.
SHOW: determiner + noun · article + possessive pronoun
