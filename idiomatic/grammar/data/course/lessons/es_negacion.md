## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: No normally stands before what it negates
REF: 27.2.1

To negate a finite verb, place no immediately before the verb group.
TL: Marta no trabaja los viernes.
EN: Marta does not work on Fridays.
[PAUSE:800]
Object clitics remain attached to the verb group, so no stands before them rather than splitting them from the verb.
TL: Marta no me lo explicó.
EN: Marta did not explain it to me.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: no + clitics + finite verb · no me lo explicó
Flip to see how placement changes the scope.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Place no at the level you mean to reject
REF: 27.2.1

No before the finite verb rejects the whole assertion. Before an infinitive, noun, or adjective, it can reject only that element.
TL: Intentó no interrumpir la reunión.
EN: She tried not to interrupt the meeting.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: No todos aceptaron la propuesta.
EN: Not everyone accepted the proposal.
[PAUSE:1000]
Compare no intentó interrumpir: that denies the attempt, not merely the interruption.
SHOW: no intentó = no attempt · intentó no interrumpir = attempt to avoid interrupting
Flip for negative words around the verb.

[CARD]
TITLE: A preverbal negative word replaces no
REF: 27.3

When nadie, nada, nunca, jamás, ninguno, or tampoco appears before the finite verb, it supplies the negative force by itself.
TL: Nadie respondió. Nunca llegan tarde.
EN: Nobody answered. They never arrive late.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not add no before the verb in this order.
SHOW: nadie respondió · nunca llegan · no extra no
Flip when the negative word follows the verb.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A postverbal negative word requires no
REF: 27.3

When the negative word follows the finite verb, place no before the verb as well. This negative concord expresses one semantic negation; it is not an affirmative double-negative reading.
TL: No respondió nadie. No encontraron nada.
EN: Nobody answered. They did not find anything.
[PAUSE:900]
After the first negative, later compatible elements normally stay negative too.
TL: No dijo nada a nadie nunca.
EN: She never said anything to anyone.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: before verb → nadie vino · after verb → no vino nadie
Fifteen clean nada, nadie, and ninguno choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Nada and nadie name different missing participants
REF: 27.5.1, 27.5.2, 27.5.3

Nada refers to no thing or nothing; nadie refers to no person or nobody. Their position determines whether no is also required.
TL: Nadie trajo nada.
EN: Nobody brought anything.
[PAUSE:800]
After a preposition, keep the preposition before the negative pronoun.
TL: No hablé con nadie de nada.
EN: I did not speak with anyone about anything.
[PAUSE:1000]
Nada can also intensify an adjective or adverb.
TL: La tarea no es nada fácil.
EN: The task is not at all easy.
SHOW: person → nadie · thing/amount → nada
Flip for ninguno.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ninguno agrees with the noun it excludes
REF: 27.5.5

Ninguno can stand alone, and its adjectival forms ningún and ninguna accompany a singular noun.
TL: Ninguno de los candidatos llamó. No recibimos ninguna respuesta.
EN: None of the candidates called. We did not receive any reply.
[PAUSE:900]
Use ningún before an ordinary masculine singular noun, but write ninguna before a feminine noun even when it begins with stressed a.
TL: No encontraron ningún error ni ninguna arma.
EN: They did not find any error or any weapon.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: ninguno solo · ningún problema · ninguna respuesta · ninguna arma
Flip for ni.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ni links alternatives inside a negative frame
REF: 27.5.4

Ni means nor or not even according to its position. A series can repeat ni before every coordinated element.
TL: No habla ni francés ni italiano.
EN: She speaks neither French nor Italian.
[PAUSE:800]
At the beginning, ni siquiera can focus the least expected possibility and supplies the preverbal negative force.
TL: Ni siquiera Ana conocía la respuesta.
EN: Not even Ana knew the answer.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: no ... ni X ni Y · ni siquiera + verb
Flip for tampoco and short replies.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Tampoco is the negative partner of también
REF: 27.5.9, 27.5.10

Use tampoco for not either, neither, or nor. Before the verb it needs no extra no; after the verb it appears inside a clause already negated by no.
TL: Yo tampoco voy. No voy tampoco.
EN: I am not going either.
[PAUSE:900]
En absoluto can mean not at all in a negative clause or serve as a complete negative reply.
TL: —¿Te molesta? —En absoluto.
EN: Does it bother you? Not at all.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: también adds · tampoco rejects too · en absoluto strengthens or answers
Twelve hygiene-gated general completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Negative words can mean any or ever without negating
REF: 27.4

In questions, comparisons, conditions, and clauses governed by doubt, impossibility, or another non-assertive meaning, nadie, nada, ninguno, and nunca can correspond to anyone, anything, any, or ever.
TL: ¿Quién ha visto nunca algo semejante?
EN: Who has ever seen anything like that?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Es imposible que nadie recuerde todos los detalles.
EN: It is impossible for anyone to remember every detail.
[PAUSE:1000]
The licensing context, not the dictionary form alone, determines whether the whole sentence is negative.
SHOW: asserted negative · question · comparison · non-assertive clause
Flip for nunca and jamás.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Nunca and jamás share a core but not every context
REF: 27.5.6

Both normally mean never. Jamás is often stronger, and nunca jamás is emphatic.
TL: Jamás revelaré la contraseña. No la revelaré nunca.
EN: I will never reveal the password. I will never reveal it.
[PAUSE:900]
In comparisons meaning ever, use nunca rather than jamás.
TL: Ahora trabaja más que nunca.
EN: Now she works more than ever.
[PAUSE:1000]
As elsewhere, preverbal nunca or jamás replaces no, while a postverbal form requires it.
SHOW: nunca lo vi · no lo vi nunca · más que nunca
Flip from negative grammar to English no.

[CARD]
TITLE: English no is not always Spanish no
REF: 27.2.2

English uses no as a determiner and in many short labels. Spanish often recasts the idea as a clause, a prepositional phrase, or a conventional expression.
TL: No hay entradas. Nos quedamos sin entradas.
EN: There are no tickets. We have no tickets left.
[PAUSE:900]
A posted prohibition commonly uses prohibido or a bare infinitive.
TL: Prohibido fumar. No tocar.
EN: No smoking. Do not touch.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: no hay · sin · prohibido + infinitive · no + infinitive
Flip for idiomatic refusals and absence.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Rebuild the message instead of copying the determiner
REF: 27.2.2

Choose a Spanish structure that states what is absent, impossible, prohibited, or rejected.
TL: No tengo tiempo. No hay por qué discutir.
EN: I have no time. There is no need to argue.
[PAUSE:900]
Short emphatic refusals use expressions such as ni hablar, de ninguna manera, or de eso nada.
TL: —¿Vas a darle la clave? —¡Ni hablar!
EN: Are you going to give him the password? No way!
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: identify meaning → choose the Spanish construction
Four unflagged translation prompts follow; uncertain alternatives are omitted.

[CARD]
TITLE: Apenas can mean hardly or as soon as
REF: 27.5.7

As an adverb, apenas means hardly, scarcely, or barely. Before the verb it supplies negative-like force without no.
TL: Apenas conozco a los vecinos.
EN: I hardly know the neighbors.
[PAUSE:800]
As a time subordinator, apenas means as soon as or scarcely when.
TL: Apenas termine la reunión, te llamaré.
EN: As soon as the meeting ends, I will call you.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not treat every apenas as a simple replacement for no.
SHOW: degree → hardly · time link → as soon as
Flip for the apparently redundant no.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Learn to recognize redundant no before producing it
REF: 27.2.4

Some varieties, especially in Spain, allow an optional no that does not reverse the meaning in comparisons, emphatic exclamations, and certain future-oriented hasta que clauses.
TL: No salgas hasta que no termine la tormenta.
EN: Do not go out until the storm ends.
[PAUSE:900]
The same no is not freely available in every clause, and in other contexts it expresses a real negation. When in doubt, use the ordinary construction whose logic is clear.
TL: Me quedaré hasta que termine la película.
EN: I will stay until the film ends.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: restricted optional pattern · not a general insertion rule
The source's judgment-dependent redundant-no exercise is deliberately excluded.

[CARD]
TITLE: Nomás is regional and context-sensitive
REF: 27.6

Across Latin America, nomás has several colloquial meanings. Context can make it equivalent to justo, mismo, solo, sin más, or en cuanto rather than to a literal negation.
TL: El banco está en la esquina nomás.
EN: The bank is right on the corner.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La vi ayer nomás.
EN: I saw her only yesterday.
[PAUSE:1000]
It can also soften an invitation or connect an event in time.
TL: Pase nomás. Lo haré nomás llegue.
EN: Do come in. I will do it as soon as she arrives.
SHOW: regional nomás → infer meaning from position and context
Four clean interpretation prompts follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build a negative sentence in three passes
REF: 27.2.1, 27.3, 27.4

First decide the intended meaning and scope. Next choose no or a more specific negative expression. Finally place that expression relative to the finite verb and apply negative concord.
TL: Nadie encontró nada. No encontró nada ninguno de ellos.
EN: Nobody found anything. None of them found anything.
[PAUSE:900]
Then check for a special environment: a question or comparison may license an any or ever reading, while regional and fixed expressions may require a different translation altogether.
TL: ¿Has visto alguna vez algo así? Nunca.
EN: Have you ever seen anything like that? Never.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: meaning and scope → form → position → concord → special context
Review the contrasts before returning to the exercises.
