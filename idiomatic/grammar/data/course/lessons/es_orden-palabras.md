## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Begin with a neutral frame, then ask what is prominent
REF: 42.1.1, 42.1.2

Subject–verb–object is the neutral starting point for an ordinary transitive statement, but Spanish moves constituents more readily than English to organize topic and focus.
TL: La comisión publicará el informe mañana.
EN: The committee will publish the report tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
The same event can begin from the report when it is already under discussion.
TL: El informe lo publicará la comisión mañana.
EN: As for the report, the committee will publish it tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not treat every possible order as interchangeable. The chosen order tells the listener what is already active and what arrives as news.
SHOW: neutral S–V–O · active topic early · focused news later
Flip for the first structural change to the neutral frame.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An unstressed object pronoun moves before the finite verb
REF: 42.1.3, 42.7.4

A full noun object normally follows the verb, but an unstressed object pronoun normally stands immediately before a finite verb.
TL: Jorge vendió el coche. Jorge lo vendió ayer.
EN: Jorge sold the car. Jorge sold it yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
With an infinitive or gerund, the pronoun can often attach to the non-finite form or stand before the governing finite verb.
TL: Voy a explicártelo. Te lo voy a explicar.
EN: I am going to explain it to you. I am going to explain it to you.
[PAUSE:1000]
The clitic and its verb form one compact grammatical unit.
SHOW: full object after verb · clitic before finite verb · optional attachment to infinitive or gerund

[CARD]
TITLE: Keep a relative clause beside its antecedent
REF: 42.2.1

Spanish strongly resists placing the main predicate between a noun and the relative clause that modifies it.
TL: Ha llegado una investigadora que conoce bien el archivo.
EN: A researcher who knows the archive well has arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
The complete noun phrase may instead come first when it does not leave an awkwardly delayed main verb.
TL: La investigadora que conocimos ayer presentará los resultados esta tarde.
EN: The researcher whom we met yesterday will present the results this afternoon.
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose the frame that keeps the antecedent and its modifier visibly connected.
SHOW: verb + [noun + relative] · or [noun + relative] + verb · never split the pair
Flip to arrange the inside of the relative clause.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Inside a relative clause, avoid a stranded verb
REF: 42.2.2, 42.2.3

When an expressed subject follows the relative verb, the verb stays close to the relative link and the heavier subject can arrive afterward.
TL: Ese es el proyecto que aprobó el consejo municipal.
EN: That is the project that the city council approved.
[PAUSE:900]
A light subject before the verb can also be natural, especially when context favors it; the principle is proximity and balance, not automatic inversion.
TL: Este es el artículo que Ana recomendó durante la reunión.
EN: This is the article that Ana recommended during the meeting.
[PAUSE:1000]
With both layers present, Spanish may use a verb before the main subject and another before the relative subject.
TL: Ganará el equipo que cometa menos errores.
EN: The team that makes fewer mistakes will win.
SHOW: relative + verb + heavy subject · preserve proximity · balance the whole sentence

[CARD]
TITLE: A question word normally draws the verb forward
REF: 42.3.1

After an interrogative phrase, standard neutral Spanish normally places the finite verb before an expressed subject.
TL: ¿Cuándo llegará la delegación?
EN: When will the delegation arrive?
[PAUSE:900]
Keep any governing preposition with the interrogative phrase.
TL: ¿Con quién habló la directora?
EN: Who did the director speak with?
[PAUSE:1000]
Spanish needs no English-style do-support. Build directly from interrogative phrase, verb, and the remaining constituents.
SHOW: interrogative phrase + verb + subject · governing preposition stays in front
Flip to embed the same information gap.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An indirect question preserves the internal order
REF: 42.3.1

Embedding removes the question marks when the whole sentence is a statement, but it does not move the interrogative verb to an English-style final position.
TL: No sabemos cuándo llegará la delegación.
EN: We do not know when the delegation will arrive.
[PAUSE:900]
The interrogative word keeps its written accent because it still identifies missing information.
TL: Me pregunto dónde guardó Marta las llaves.
EN: I wonder where Marta put the keys.
[PAUSE:1000]
Preserve the question phrase and its clause architecture inside the larger statement.
SHOW: no outer question marks · accent retained · internal verb–subject order retained
Nine clean direct-question drills follow; implicit continuations are excluded.

[CARD]
TITLE: Exclamatives place the scale first
REF: 42.3.1

Qué, cómo, and cuánto introduce the quality, manner, or amount that carries the exclamatory force. The verb normally precedes an expressed subject after them.
TL: ¡Qué bien canta Lucía!
EN: Lucía sings so well!
[PAUSE:900]
TL: ¡Cuántos errores contiene el borrador!
EN: The draft contains so many errors!
[PAUSE:900]
TL: ¡Cómo ha cambiado el barrio!
EN: How the neighborhood has changed!
[PAUSE:1000]
The accent and opening punctuation belong to the exclamatory construction, not to ordinary relative or connective uses.
SHOW: ¡qué + quality! · ¡cómo + event! · ¡cuánto + amount!
Flip for the regional boundary on interrogative order.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Learn the neutral pattern without erasing regional Spanish
REF: 42.3.4

Verb–subject order after a question word is the broadly neutral standard. In parts of the Caribbean and some nearby varieties, subject–verb order is an established regional pattern.
TL: ¿Cómo se enteró usted de la noticia?
EN: How did you find out about the news?
[PAUSE:900]
A learner can use the neutral pattern consistently while recognizing regional forms as grammatical in their speech communities.
TL: ¿Qué piensas tú del resultado?
EN: What do you think about the result?
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat regionally marked order as variation, not as random movement or a universal error.
SHOW: broad neutral: question phrase + verb + subject · regional S–V patterns exist
Eight hygiene-gated exclamation transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: An opening adverbial often licenses verb–subject order
REF: 42.5

After an initial time, place, or scene-setting phrase, verb–subject order is common, especially with an intransitive verb and a subject that introduces new information.
TL: A las seis llegó el último tren.
EN: The last train arrived at six.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: En el patio esperaban varios periodistas.
EN: Several journalists were waiting in the courtyard.
[PAUSE:900]
Subject–verb order may also be possible. Rhythm, transitivity, and information structure determine which version fits.
TL: A las seis, el último tren seguía detenido en la estación anterior.
EN: At six, the last train was still stopped at the previous station.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: opening scene + verb + new subject · inversion common, not mechanical
Flip for two environments with a stronger inversion preference.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Dialogue tags and que-imperatives favor verb first
REF: 42.4, 42.6

When a written dialogue identifier follows the quotation, Spanish places the reporting verb before its subject.
TL: «Volveremos mañana», respondió la directora.
EN: “We will return tomorrow,” the director replied.
[PAUSE:900]
Third-person commands introduced by que also commonly place the verb before the expressed subject.
TL: Que pase el siguiente candidato.
EN: Have the next candidate come in.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Que no vuelva Pablo a interrumpir la sesión.
EN: Do not let Pablo interrupt the meeting again.
[PAUSE:1000]
These are learned constructions with their own preferred order.
SHOW: quotation + reporting verb + subject · que + command verb + subject

[CARD]
TITLE: A preposition stays with its complement
REF: 42.7.1

Spanish does not strand a preposition at the end of a relative or interrogative clause. Move the preposition together with the relative or question word.
TL: Este es el despacho en el que trabaja la traductora.
EN: This is the office that the translator works in.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: ¿A quién enviaste la copia?
EN: Who did you send the copy to?
[PAUSE:900]
The same integrity applies before an infinitive: only a very small class of short words can intervene between the preposition and its infinitive.
TL: Cancelaron la visita por no poder entrar en el edificio.
EN: They canceled the visit because they could not enter the building.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: preposition + relative · preposition + interrogative · preposition + infinitive
Flip from government to fixed verbal units.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Do not split a set phrase to place an adverb
REF: 42.7.2

Periphrases and lexical verb phrases keep their core pieces together. Put an adverb before or after the complete unit instead of inserting it inside.
TL: Las medidas se llevarán a cabo inmediatamente.
EN: The measures will be carried out immediately.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: La portavoz se dio cuenta enseguida del error.
EN: The spokesperson noticed the error immediately.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El informe tiene que estar listo mañana.
EN: The report has to be ready tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn the verbal expression as a unit, then place surrounding information around it.
SHOW: llevar a cabo · darse cuenta de · tener que + infinitive

[CARD]
TITLE: Keep auxiliaries and clitics in compact chains
REF: 42.7.3, 42.7.4

Do not insert an adverb between haber and its participle. Place it before the auxiliary or after the participle phrase.
TL: Siempre he defendido esa propuesta. He defendido siempre esa propuesta.
EN: I have always supported that proposal. I have always supported that proposal.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Nunca habían visto el mar.
EN: They had never seen the sea.
[PAUSE:900]
Likewise, adjacent unstressed pronouns cannot be separated from one another or from the finite verb.
TL: Se lo explicaré después de la reunión.
EN: I will explain it to her after the meeting.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: no insertion inside haber + participle · clitic cluster + verb
Flip for affirmative commands and coordination.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Pronoun placement follows the verb form
REF: 42.7.4

With an affirmative command, attach the clitic cluster to the verb. With a negative command, place it before the verb.
TL: Explícaselo ahora. No se lo expliques todavía.
EN: Explain it to her now. Do not explain it to her yet.
[PAUSE:900]
To coordinate stressed people or things, use a single clitic for the grammatical object and add the full coordinated phrase separately.
TL: Los vi a él y a ella en la entrada.
EN: I saw him and her at the entrance.
[PAUSE:1000]
Never insert y or o inside a cluster of unstressed pronouns.
SHOW: affirmative command + attached clitics · negative + preverbal clitics · coordinate full phrases

[CARD]
TITLE: Keep a modifier close to what it modifies
REF: 42.7.5, 42.7.7

An adjectival phrase should sit beside its noun so that its attachment is clear.
TL: Encontraron una carpeta cubierta de polvo en el almacén.
EN: They found a folder covered in dust in the storeroom.
[PAUSE:900]
A compound noun joined by de also stays intact.
TL: Compró un lápiz de memoria barato.
EN: She bought an inexpensive flash drive.
[PAUSE:900]
Apposition follows the same proximity principle.
TL: Abrió la puerta Lucía, la nueva coordinadora del proyecto.
EN: Lucía, the new project coordinator, opened the door.
[PAUSE:1000]
Word order should prevent a modifier from appearing to attach to the nearest wrong noun.
SHOW: noun + adjectival phrase · compound noun kept whole · noun + apposition
Flip to apply proximity to adverbs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The location of a modifier controls its reading
REF: 42.7.5, 42.8.1

Move a modifier only together with the phrase it belongs to. A late phrase can otherwise seem to describe the wrong participant or event.
TL: Entrevistaron a una médica especializada en salud pública.
EN: They interviewed a doctor specializing in public health.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Publicaron ayer el informe revisado por la comisión.
EN: Yesterday they published the report revised by the committee.
[PAUSE:1000]
The safest order makes each dependency obvious on first reading.
SHOW: preserve phrase boundaries · place modifiers beside their heads

[CARD]
TITLE: Put an adverb near the event it measures
REF: 42.8.1, 42.8.2

Spanish adverbs are mobile, but a useful default is immediate proximity to the verb or phrase they modify.
TL: La comisión aprobó ayer el presupuesto.
EN: The committee approved the budget yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
Unlike ordinary English order, Spanish readily places an adverb between the verb and a full noun object.
TL: Revisaron cuidadosamente todas las solicitudes.
EN: They carefully reviewed all the applications.
[PAUSE:900]
Other positions may be grammatical when scope and rhythm remain clear.
TL: La comisión aprobó el presupuesto ayer.
EN: The committee approved the budget yesterday.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: adverb close to its target · verb–adverb–object is available · scope decides
Flip to prevent attachment ambiguity.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Move time toward the verb it belongs to
REF: 42.8.2

A final English adverb may ambiguously modify the main event or a nearby relative clause. Spanish can resolve the reading by placing it beside the intended verb.
TL: Vi ayer a la científica que recibió el premio.
EN: Yesterday I saw the scientist who received the prize.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Vi a la científica que recibió ayer el premio.
EN: I saw the scientist who received the prize yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
Time often precedes place, although both orders can be possible when the dependencies are clear.
TL: Fuimos ayer al archivo. Fuimos al archivo ayer.
EN: We went to the archive yesterday. We went to the archive yesterday.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: main-event time beside main verb · relative-event time inside relative clause

[CARD]
TITLE: Put the topic first and resume a definite object
REF: 42.9.1

The topic is the entity or setting that the utterance treats as already active. It often comes first even when it is not the grammatical subject.
TL: Los viernes trabajo desde casa.
EN: On Fridays I work from home.
[PAUSE:900]
A fronted definite object is normally echoed by an unstressed object pronoun.
TL: Ese informe ya lo leyó Marta.
EN: Marta has already read that report.
[PAUSE:900]
A bare, non-specific noun can be fronted without the resumptive pronoun.
TL: Dinero no tengo, pero tiempo sí.
EN: I have no money, but I do have time.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: topic first · definite fronted object + clitic · bare non-specific noun may stand alone
Flip for stressed subject pronouns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A Spanish subject pronoun already carries contrast
REF: 42.9.1

Because the verb ending identifies the subject, an overt subject pronoun is available to mark contrast or a deliberate change of topic.
TL: Yo preparo los datos y tú redactas las conclusiones.
EN: I will prepare the data, and you will draft the conclusions.
[PAUSE:900]
Spanish does not normally need an English- or French-style repeated topic plus subject pronoun.
TL: En cuanto a mí, prefiero esperar.
EN: As for me, I prefer to wait.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: La mostaza la detesto.
EN: Mustard, I hate it.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use an explicit pronoun because it contributes contrast, not merely because English writes one.
SHOW: verb ending identifies subject · overt pronoun adds contrast

[CARD]
TITLE: New subjects often arrive after intransitive verbs
REF: 42.9.2

With an event, arrival, appearance, or change, an indefinite subject often supplies the new information and naturally follows the verb.
TL: Ha ocurrido un error.
EN: An error has occurred.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Llamó una amiga de Clara.
EN: A friend of Clara called.
[PAUSE:900]
When that subject is already the topic and the predicate supplies the news, subject–verb order becomes natural.
TL: El mensajero ya ha llegado.
EN: The courier has already arrived.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: event + new indefinite subject → V–S · established subject + new event → S–V
Flip for transitive clauses and the final decision rule.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Context outranks a mechanical word-order formula
REF: 42.9.2, 42.9.3

In a neutral transitive statement, subject–verb–object remains the dependable baseline.
TL: Ana revisó el contrato.
EN: Ana reviewed the contract.
[PAUSE:900]
Front the object when it is the topic and resume it with a clitic; leave the focused object later when it is the answer to what.
TL: El contrato lo revisó Ana. Ana revisó el contrato nuevo.
EN: As for the contract, Ana reviewed it. Ana reviewed the new contract.
[PAUSE:1000]
Before moving anything, ask three questions: What is the fixed grammatical unit? What is already the topic? What is the new or contrasted information?
SHOW: preserve units · topic early · news and contrast receive prominence
