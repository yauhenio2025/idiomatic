## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Treat negation as a two-part frame
REF: 16.1, 16.4

Most French sentence negators have a preverbal ne and a second element that carries the main meaning. The second element may mean not, never, no longer, nothing, nobody, or only.
TL: Je ne reconnais pas ce quartier.
EN: I do not recognize this neighbourhood.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Personne n'a contesté la décision.
EN: Nobody challenged the decision.
[PAUSE:900]
The surface shapes differ, but the same question organizes them: what is negated, and where does each negative element belong?
SHOW: choose the meaning -> locate its scope -> build the negative frame
Flip for the difference between speech and careful writing.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Spoken French often drops ne; careful writing keeps it
REF: 16.4

In ordinary conversation, speakers very often omit ne, especially with pas. The second element then carries the audible negation. In careful prose and formal speech, retain the complete frame.
TL: Je n'ai pas compris votre remarque.
EN: I did not understand your comment.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: J'ai pas compris ta remarque.
EN: I didn't understand your comment.
[PAUSE:900]
The second version is normal informal speech, not the controlled written model. This course keeps ne in full sentences unless it is explicitly contrasting registers.
SHOW: careful French -> ne ... negator · informal speech -> ne often disappears

[CARD]
TITLE: Anchor ne to the finite verb
REF: 16.2.1, 16.6.1

Ne comes before the finite verb and before any unstressed object pronouns. With a simple tense, pas and light negative adverbs follow that finite verb.
TL: Elle ne leur répond jamais directement.
EN: She never answers them directly.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nous ne retrouvons plus ce dossier.
EN: We can no longer find that file.
[PAUSE:900]
In a compound tense, pas, jamais, plus, guère, and direct-object rien normally follow the auxiliary rather than the past participle.
TL: Nous ne l'avons toujours pas retrouvé.
EN: We still have not found it.
SHOW: ne + clitics + finite verb · auxiliary + pas/jamais/plus/rien + participle
Flip when the negated verb is an infinitive.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Put most infinitive negation before the infinitive
REF: 16.2.2, 16.5

With an infinitive, ne together with pas, jamais, plus, or rien normally forms a block before the infinitive. In a compound infinitive, that block normally precedes avoir or être.
TL: Il promet de ne plus intervenir.
EN: He promises not to intervene any longer.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle regrette de ne rien avoir noté.
EN: She regrets not having written anything down.
[PAUSE:900]
Aucun and personne behave more like noun phrases and follow the verbal material. Questions and negative imperatives keep the same ordering principles as statements.
TL: Je préfère ne voir personne aujourd'hui.
EN: I would rather not see anyone today.
SHOW: ne pas/plus/jamais/rien + infinitive · ne + infinitive + personne/aucun

[CARD]
TITLE: After basic negation, an indefinite object usually becomes de
REF: 16.6.1, 16.6.2

When ne ... pas negates a direct object introduced by un, une, du, de la, or des, the article normally collapses to de. The negation denies the existence or quantity of that object.
TL: Nous n'avons pas reçu de réponse.
EN: We did not receive an answer.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle ne boit pas de café l'après-midi.
EN: She does not drink coffee in the afternoon.
[PAUSE:900]
The article remains after être, when it means not a single one, or when the noun is contrasted with another choice.
TL: Ce n'est pas une erreur.
EN: It is not a mistake.
SHOW: negated indefinite direct object -> de · être, not one, or contrast -> article may remain
Flip from sentences to individual phrases.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Pas and non can target less than a sentence
REF: 16.6.3, 16.6.4

Pas alone can reject a noun phrase, adjective, adverb, or conjunction without building a full ne ... pas frame. Formal contrast may instead use non or non pas.
TL: Pas cette semaine, mais la suivante.
EN: Not this week, but the following one.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il faut vérifier, non pas deviner.
EN: We must verify, not guess.
[PAUSE:900]
If a pas phrase itself becomes the subject of a finite negative sentence, careful French supplies ne before the verb.
TL: Pas un témoin ne s'est manifesté.
EN: Not a single witness came forward.
SHOW: phrase focus -> pas · formal contrast -> non (pas) · negative subject -> ne + verb

[CARD]
TITLE: Ne ... que restricts the constituent after que
REF: 16.2.1, 16.7

Ne ... que means only. Put que immediately before the person, object, time, or prepositional phrase being restricted. Moving que changes the scope.
TL: Elle ne consulte que les dossiers urgents.
EN: She consults only the urgent files.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle n'a répondu qu'à Léa.
EN: She replied only to Léa.
[PAUSE:900]
Que may therefore sit far from the finite verb. It contracts to qu' before a vowel sound, but ne stays attached to the verb.
SHOW: ne + verb ... que + restricted constituent
Flip for the difference between restriction and negation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Restriction can combine with true negators
REF: 16.7, 16.3

Ne ... que is restrictive, not logically negative, so ordinary articles do not change to de. It can also combine with pas, jamais, or plus to produce more precise scope.
TL: Je ne l'ai vu qu'une fois.
EN: I saw him only once.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il n'a pas que des défauts.
EN: He does not have only faults.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle ne parle jamais que de son travail.
EN: She never talks about anything except her work.
[PAUSE:900]
The sequence ne fait que plus an infinitive often means does nothing but, with a repeated or excessive action.
SHOW: only -> ne ... que · not only -> ne ... pas que · nothing except -> ne ... jamais que

[CARD]
TITLE: Aucun agrees with the noun it excludes
REF: 16.8

Aucun or aucune means no or not any and normally remains singular. It stands directly before its noun and agrees in gender.
TL: Je n'ai reçu aucune confirmation.
EN: I received no confirmation.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Aucune donnée ne permet de conclure.
EN: No data point allows us to draw a conclusion.
[PAUSE:900]
As a subject, aucun comes first and ne still precedes the verb. It can also stand alone or take a complement introduced by de.
TL: Aucun de ces scénarios ne me convainc.
EN: None of these scenarios convinces me.
SHOW: aucun + masculine noun · aucune + feminine noun · subject aucun ... ne
Flip for formal nul and negation after sans.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Nul is formal; sans reverses the polarity
REF: 16.8, 16.15

Negative nul or nulle belongs mainly to formal and legal French. Everyday French normally prefers aucun or pas de. After sans, negative-looking words acquire the positive-polarity meanings any, ever, anything, or anyone, and no ne is added.
TL: Nulle objection n'a été formulée.
EN: No objection was raised.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il est parti sans aucune explication.
EN: He left without any explanation.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nous avons résolu le problème sans rien modifier.
EN: We solved the problem without changing anything.
SHOW: ordinary no -> aucun · formal no -> nul · sans + negative word -> any/ever

[CARD]
TITLE: Jamais follows the finite verb or auxiliary
REF: 16.2.1, 16.9

In a simple tense, jamais follows the finite verb. In a compound tense, it normally follows the auxiliary and precedes the participle.
TL: Elle ne consulte jamais son téléphone en réunion.
EN: She never checks her phone during meetings.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nous n'avions jamais rencontré cette difficulté.
EN: We had never encountered this difficulty.
[PAUSE:900]
An indefinite direct object after jamais normally takes de, just as it does after pas.
TL: Il n'achète jamais de journaux.
EN: He never buys newspapers.
SHOW: finite verb + jamais · auxiliary + jamais + participle · jamais + de + object
Flip when jamais moves to the front or loses ne.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Initial jamais adds emphasis without inversion
REF: 16.9

Jamais can open a sentence for emphatic never. Unlike English, French does not invert the subject and verb. Without ne, jamais can also mean never on its own or ever in questions and hypothetical frames.
TL: Jamais je n'accepterai un tel compromis.
EN: Never will I accept such a compromise.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Avez-vous jamais envisagé cette solution ?
EN: Have you ever considered this solution?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Si jamais vous changez d'avis, appelez-moi.
EN: If you ever change your mind, call me.
SHOW: emphatic never -> jamais + subject, no inversion · question/si -> jamais can mean ever

[CARD]
TITLE: Plus means no longer, not not more
REF: 16.2.1, 16.10

Ne ... plus says that a former state or habit has ended. Do not add pas. The sequence ne ... pas plus instead compares quantities or degrees and means not more.
TL: Je ne travaille plus le dimanche.
EN: I no longer work on Sundays.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il ne voyage pas plus que moi.
EN: He does not travel any more than I do.
[PAUSE:900]
An indefinite direct object after plus normally becomes de. Non plus supplies either or neither after another negative statement.
TL: Léa n'a plus de batterie, et moi non plus.
EN: Léa has no battery left, and neither do I.
SHOW: no longer -> ne ... plus · not more -> ne ... pas plus · neither/either -> non plus
Flip from no longer to hardly.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Guère is the careful-register hardly
REF: 16.11

Ne ... guère means hardly or scarcely and is commonest in careful or literary French. In everyday language, à peine is often more natural and does not take ne.
TL: Cette hypothèse ne paraît guère plausible.
EN: This hypothesis hardly seems plausible.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Cette hypothèse paraît à peine plausible.
EN: This hypothesis seems barely plausible.
[PAUSE:900]
Guère also triggers de before an indefinite direct object or quantity noun.
TL: Il n'y a guère de différence entre les deux versions.
EN: There is hardly any difference between the two versions.
SHOW: careful hardly -> ne ... guère · everyday barely -> à peine · guère de + noun

[CARD]
TITLE: Rien and personne occupy different object positions
REF: 16.2.1, 16.12, 16.13

As a direct object, rien behaves like a light negative adverb and follows the finite verb or auxiliary. Personne behaves like a full noun phrase and stays after the participle in a compound tense.
TL: Je n'ai rien compris.
EN: I did not understand anything.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Je n'ai rencontré personne.
EN: I did not meet anyone.
[PAUSE:900]
After a preposition, both remain in the ordinary prepositional position.
TL: Je ne parle de rien à personne.
EN: I speak about nothing to anyone.
SHOW: auxiliary + rien + participle · auxiliary + participle + personne · preposition + either
Flip when nothing or nobody is the subject or has a modifier.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Subjects lead; modifiers use de plus masculine singular
REF: 16.12, 16.13

As subjects, rien and personne stand before ne and the verb. A following adjective is linked with de and remains masculine singular, regardless of what English suggests.
TL: Rien ne bouge dans le couloir.
EN: Nothing is moving in the corridor.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Personne ne répond à cette adresse.
EN: Nobody answers at this address.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nous n'avons trouvé personne de disponible.
EN: We found nobody available.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Rien d'urgent n'a été signalé.
EN: Nothing urgent was reported.
SHOW: subject rien/personne + ne · rien/personne de + masculine singular adjective

[CARD]
TITLE: Ni ... ni coordinates the exact items rejected
REF: 16.14

Ne ... ni ... ni means neither ... nor. Put ni before each coordinated noun phrase, adjective, participle, or prepositional phrase, and repeat any preposition that belongs to both terms.
TL: Elle ne boit ni café ni thé.
EN: She drinks neither coffee nor tea.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il ne pense ni à l'échec ni au succès.
EN: He thinks neither about failure nor about success.
[PAUSE:900]
When a coordinated pair is the subject, it comes before ne. Agreement follows the meaning and number of that pair.
TL: Ni le budget ni le calendrier ne posent problème.
EN: Neither the budget nor the schedule poses a problem.
SHOW: ne ... ni X ni Y · repeat shared prepositions · subject ni X ni Y ... ne
Flip when whole verbs or an existing negative frame are coordinated.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Repeat ne across coordinated finite verbs
REF: 16.14, 16.15

When ni links two finite verbs, each verbal unit receives ne. After an existing negative, French also uses ni where English may say or, nor, or and.
TL: Il ne confirme ni ne dément cette information.
EN: He neither confirms nor denies this information.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Je n'ai vu ni Marc ni sa sœur.
EN: I saw neither Marc nor his sister.
[PAUSE:900]
After sans, ni can coordinate infinitives or noun phrases without adding a finite negative frame.
TL: Elle est partie sans prévenir ni laisser d'adresse.
EN: She left without warning anyone or leaving an address.
SHOW: finite V1 ne ... ni ne V2 · existing negative + ni · sans ... ni, no ne

[CARD]
TITLE: Multiple negatives accumulate in a controlled order
REF: 16.3

French can combine several negative elements without cancelling them. Around a compound tense, light elements such as jamais, plus, and rien normally come before the participle, while personne stays after it.
TL: Elle n'a jamais rien révélé.
EN: She never revealed anything.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il ne confie plus rien à personne.
EN: He no longer tells anyone anything.
[PAUSE:900]
Plus jamais is also common for never again, and it can still be followed by rien.
TL: Elle ne publiera plus jamais rien sur ce sujet.
EN: She will never publish anything on this subject again.
SHOW: jamais/plus + rien + participle · participle + personne · negatives reinforce
Flip for combinations that change scope rather than simply intensifying it.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Read every added negator for its own scope
REF: 16.3, 16.7

Pas plus que and pas que are not the same as plus and que alone. Likewise, adding jamais or plus to a restrictive que describes when the restriction holds.
TL: Je n'ai pas que des critiques à formuler.
EN: I do not have only criticisms to make.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nous ne recevons plus que deux candidatures par mois.
EN: We now receive only two applications per month.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il ne commande jamais que des plats végétariens.
EN: He never orders anything but vegetarian dishes.
[PAUSE:900]
Build the sequence from meaning, then verify the established order. Do not translate a string of English negative words mechanically.
SHOW: pas que -> not only · plus que -> only ... left · jamais que -> never anything but

[CARD]
TITLE: Expletive ne does not negate its clause
REF: 16.16

In careful French, a subordinate clause after fear, prevention, avant que, or certain comparisons may contain a ne that contributes no negative meaning. The subjunctive or comparison licenses it; the surrounding sentence supplies the interpretation.
TL: Je crains qu'elle ne découvre l'erreur.
EN: I am afraid that she will discover the error.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Fermez la fenêtre avant que la salle ne refroidisse.
EN: Close the window before the room cools down.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Le résultat est meilleur que nous ne l'espérions.
EN: The result is better than we hoped.
[PAUSE:900]
None of these ne forms means not. They are optional markers of elevated style.
SHOW: fear/prevention/comparison + ne -> formal, often optional, not negative
Flip for the different case in which ne really does negate by itself.

[SIDE]
TITLE: With a few verbs, formal French may omit pas
REF: 16.6.2, 16.16

A small group of verbs can carry genuine negation with ne alone in formal writing. Common environments include cesser de, oser, pouvoir, and savoir in special modal uses.
TL: Il ne cesse de poser des questions.
EN: He does not stop asking questions.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Je n'ose contredire la directrice.
EN: I do not dare contradict the director.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Je ne saurais vous garantir ce délai.
EN: I could not guarantee that deadline to you.
[PAUSE:900]
Unlike expletive ne, these examples are genuinely negative. Outside established formal patterns, use the complete modern negator rather than dropping pas at will.
SHOW: expletive ne -> no negative force · licensed ne alone -> real formal negation
