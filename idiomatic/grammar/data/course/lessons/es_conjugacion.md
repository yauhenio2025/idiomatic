## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A finite verb is a bundle of coordinates
REF: 16.1, 16.2, 16.3

A Spanish finite form normally identifies person and number while also locating an event in tense and mood. Start by separating the stem from the ending instead of memorizing each form as an isolated word.
TL: Revisaremos el contrato cuando llegue la asesora.
EN: We will review the contract when the adviser arrives.
[PAUSE:1000]
Revisaremos marks first-person plural future indicative. Llegue marks third-person singular present subjunctive. One sentence can therefore coordinate two different morphological systems.
SHOW: stem + person/number + tense/mood
Flip for the three conjugations.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The infinitive vowel predicts the regular pattern
REF: 16.1, 16.3

Spanish sorts infinitives into ar, er and ir conjugations. The stem comes from removing that ending, and regular forms attach the corresponding paradigm.
TL: Mientras Clara trabaja, nosotros comemos y los niños escriben.
EN: While Clara works, we eat and the children write.
[PAUSE:900]
The er and ir systems overlap heavily, but they are not interchangeable. Their differences surface clearly in forms such as the present plural, the infinitive, and the participle.
TL: Preferimos vivir cerca, pero debemos vender la casa.
EN: We prefer to live nearby, but we must sell the house.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: hablar → habl- · comer → com- · vivir → viv-
Flip for spelling changes.

[CARD]
TITLE: Spelling changes preserve a sound
REF: 16.4.1, 16.4.2, 16.4.3, 16.4.6, 16.4.7

Many apparent irregularities are orthographic repairs. Before e, z becomes c, hard c becomes qu, and g may need u to keep its hard sound.
TL: Crucé la plaza, busqué la entrada y pagué en efectivo.
EN: I crossed the square, looked for the entrance, and paid in cash.
[PAUSE:1000]
Before o or a, verbs in ger or gir replace g with j. Verbs in guir usually drop the silent u in the same environment.
TL: Protejo los archivos para que todos sigan el procedimiento.
EN: I protect the files so that everyone follows the procedure.
SHOW: crucé · busqué · pagué · protejo · siga
Flip for written accents and y.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Stress can make the spelling visible
REF: 16.4.4, 16.4.10, 16.4.11, 16.9

A written accent or dieresis can preserve the intended syllable structure or pronunciation. The contrast is morphological: the mark appears where stress or the following vowel requires it.
TL: La coordinadora averigüa por qué la empresa continúa aislando los datos.
EN: The coordinator finds out why the company continues to isolate the data.
[PAUSE:900]
An unstressed i between vowels is often written y, while combinations after ñ or ll lose an expected i.
TL: El constructor leyó el informe mientras el campanero tañía al fondo.
EN: The builder read the report while the bell-ringer was ringing bells in the distance.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: averigüe · continúa · construyó · tañó
Ten spelling diagnoses follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Stem change has a stress footprint
REF: 16.6

Stem-changing verbs keep regular endings but alternate a vowel inside the stem. In the present tense, the change normally appears when that stem vowel is stressed and disappears in nosotros and vosotros forms.
TL: La directora cierra la sesión, pero nosotros cerramos el edificio más tarde.
EN: The director closes the session, but we close the building later.
[PAUSE:1000]
The infinitive alone does not reliably predict whether a verb changes. Learn a model form with each lexical item.
TL: El museo renueva la sala, pero la empresa innova sin descanso.
EN: The museum renovates the room, but the company innovates continuously.
SHOW: cerrar → cierro, cerramos · lexical pattern, regular endings
Flip for the special reach of ir verbs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Some ir verbs carry the change into the past
REF: 16.6.5, 16.6.7, 16.7.3

In the third persons of the preterite, many stem-changing ir verbs use a reduced change: e becomes i, and o becomes u. That changed third-person stem also feeds the imperfect subjunctive.
TL: Marta pidió ayuda y los técnicos durmieron en el aeropuerto.
EN: Marta asked for help, and the technicians slept at the airport.
[PAUSE:1000]
A verb like sentir combines patterns: present siento but sentimos, preterite sintió, and imperfect subjunctive sintiera or sintiese.
TL: No creía que el público sintiera tanta incertidumbre.
EN: I did not believe that the public felt so much uncertainty.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: pedir → pidió → pidiera · dormir → durmió → durmiera
Flip for irregular families.

[CARD]
TITLE: Irregular forms cluster into families
REF: 16.5, 16.10

Spanish has a small core of genuinely irregular verbs, plus large families built from recurring patterns. Compounds normally inherit the irregularity of their base.
TL: Propuse una alternativa porque el plazo no satisfizo a nadie.
EN: I proposed an alternative because the deadline satisfied no one.
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat an irregular principal part as a productive key. Poner gives pongo, pondré and puse; proponer preserves those stems with its prefix.
TL: Si posponen la reunión, propondré otra fecha.
EN: If they postpone the meeting, I will propose another date.
SHOW: base pattern + prefix · poner → proponer · hacer → deshacer
Flip for the first-person present.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The yo form predicts more than itself
REF: 16.7.1, 16.7.5, 16.10

Many irregular presents isolate their special stem in the first-person singular: salgo, conozco, traduzco, quepo. That yo form often becomes the base of the present subjunctive.
TL: Traduzco el resumen para que los socios conozcan la propuesta.
EN: I translate the summary so that the partners know the proposal.
[PAUSE:1000]
Four high-frequency yo forms end in y and must simply be anchored: doy, estoy, voy and soy.
TL: Hoy estoy disponible y voy a dar una respuesta definitiva.
EN: Today I am available, and I am going to give a definitive answer.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: salgo → salga · conozco → conozca · doy, estoy, voy, soy
Flip for the strong preterite.

[CARD]
TITLE: Strong preterites use their own stem
REF: 16.7.3

Many common irregular verbs replace the infinitive stem in the preterite. The strong endings are unstressed, so the first and third singular forms carry no written accent.
TL: Ayer tuve tiempo, hice la revisión y puse una nota al margen.
EN: Yesterday I had time, completed the review, and put a note in the margin.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn the stem and attach the family endings: e, iste, o, imos, isteis, ieron. The forms of ser and ir share the fui series and rely on context.
TL: La visita fue breve, pero fuimos hasta la sede nueva.
EN: The visit was brief, but we went as far as the new headquarters.
SHOW: tuv-e · hic-e/hiz-o · pus-e · fu-i
Flip for j-stems.

[SIDE]
TITLE: J-stems shorten the plural ending
REF: 16.7.3

Preterite stems ending in j take eron, not ieron, in the third-person plural. This applies to decir, traer and the whole ducir family.
TL: Los testigos dijeron la verdad y trajeron los documentos originales.
EN: The witnesses told the truth and brought the original documents.
[PAUSE:900]
The spelling is part of the paradigm, not an optional pronunciation guide.
TL: Las nuevas medidas redujeron el consumo y condujeron a un acuerdo.
EN: The new measures reduced consumption and led to an agreement.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: dijeron · trajeron · produjeron · condujeron
Sixteen irregular-preterite transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Future and conditional share one stem list
REF: 16.7.4

Regular future and conditional endings attach to the complete infinitive. The future uses the accented endings é, ás, á, emos, éis and án; the conditional uses the imperfect endings of er and ir verbs.
TL: Mañana revisaremos las cifras; con más tiempo, explicaríamos cualquier discrepancia.
EN: Tomorrow we will review the figures; with more time, we would explain any discrepancy.
[PAUSE:1000]
Both tenses therefore share the same irregular stem. Once the stem is secure, the choice between future and conditional is entirely in the ending.
SHOW: infinitive/stem + future endings · same stem + ía endings
Flip for the twelve irregular stems.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Twelve verbs compress the infinitive
REF: 16.7.4

Some stems drop a vowel, some insert d, and decir and hacer contract further. Their compounds inherit the same base.
TL: El informe cabrá en una página, pero tendremos que resumir los anexos.
EN: The report will fit on one page, but we will have to summarize the appendices.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Si mantuvieran el formato, yo podría rehacer la tabla y saldría antes.
EN: If they kept the format, I could redo the table and would leave earlier.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: cabr-, habr-, sabr- · pondr-, tendr-, saldr- · dir-, har-
Eleven future-or-conditional choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Build the present subjunctive from yo
REF: 16.7.5

For most verbs, remove the o from the first-person present indicative and add the opposite thematic vowel. Ar verbs take e endings; er and ir verbs take a endings.
TL: Conviene que entregues el informe antes de que salga la directora.
EN: You should submit the report before the director leaves.
[PAUSE:1000]
This rule carries yo-form irregularities and spelling repairs into the whole subjunctive paradigm.
TL: Dudo que traduzcan el anexo sin que alguien les explique el contexto.
EN: I doubt that they will translate the appendix unless someone explains the context to them.
SHOW: entrego → entregue · salgo → salga · traduzco → traduzca
Flip for the six independent stems.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Six present subjunctives need their own anchor
REF: 16.2, 16.7.5

Dar, estar, haber, ir, saber and ser do not follow the ordinary yo-minus-o route. Anchor them as dé, esté, haya, vaya, sepa and sea.
TL: Espero que haya sitio y que todos sepan dónde está la entrada.
EN: I hope there is room and that everyone knows where the entrance is.
[PAUSE:900]
Stem-changing verbs preserve their normal alternation, with extra reduced changes in nosotros and vosotros for many ir verbs.
TL: Prefiero que empecemos ahora y que los invitados se sientan cómodos.
EN: I prefer us to begin now and for the guests to feel comfortable.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: dé · esté · haya · vaya · sepa · sea
Fifteen present-subjunctive completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The imperfect subjunctive grows from the preterite
REF: 16.7.6

Take the third-person plural preterite, remove ron, and add the ra or se series. This derivation preserves every irregularity already visible in that preterite stem.
TL: Me sorprendió que pidiera ayuda y que produjese un informe tan detallado.
EN: I was surprised that she asked for help and produced such a detailed report.
[PAUSE:1000]
The two series are interchangeable in ordinary subjunctive use, though the ra forms are more frequent.
TL: Querían que fuéramos con ellos; también sería posible decir que fuésemos.
EN: They wanted us to go with them; it would also be possible to use the se form.
SHOW: ellos preterite minus -ron + -ra/-se endings
Flip for stress and the nosotros accent.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The derivation preserves a stable stress pattern
REF: 16.7.6, 16.9

The ra and se endings carry predictable stress. In the nosotros form, write an accent on the vowel immediately before ramos or semos.
TL: El comité exigió que tuviéramos cifras fiables y redujéramos el margen de error.
EN: The committee demanded that we have reliable figures and reduce the margin of error.
[PAUSE:900]
In a natural conditional sentence, the main clause normally uses the conditional rather than a second ra form.
TL: Si supiéramos la causa, propondríamos una solución concreta.
EN: If we knew the cause, we would propose a concrete solution.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: tuviera → tuviéramos · dijese → dijésemos
Flip for commands.

[CARD]
TITLE: The affirmative tú command is a small patchwork
REF: 16.7.8, 21.2.2

Most affirmative tú commands use the third-person singular present indicative. Eight common verbs instead use compact forms that must be learned as a set.
TL: Ven temprano, pon las carpetas aquí y haz una copia para Marta.
EN: Come early, put the folders here, and make a copy for Marta.
[PAUSE:1000]
The remaining core forms are di, sal, sé, ten and ve. Reflexive ir is normally vete.
TL: Sé prudente, ten paciencia y vete cuando termine la reunión.
EN: Be prudent, be patient, and leave when the meeting ends.
SHOW: ven · di · sal · haz · ten · ve · pon · sé
Flip for the rest of the command system.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Other commands recruit the subjunctive
REF: 16.7.8, 21.2.2

Negative tú commands and both usted and ustedes commands use present-subjunctive forms. This makes the yo-form strategy immediately useful.
TL: No traduzcas esa sección todavía; traduzca primero el resumen, señora.
EN: Do not translate that section yet; translate the summary first, ma'am.
[PAUSE:900]
Affirmative commands attach object pronouns, while negative commands place them before the verb. Attachment may trigger a written accent.
TL: Dímelo ahora, pero no se lo digas a los demás.
EN: Tell me now, but do not tell the others.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: dímelo · no me lo digas · traduzca · traduzcan
Nine affirmative-command choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The imperfect is regular almost everywhere
REF: 16.7.2

Remove the infinitive ending and add aba for ar verbs or ía for er and ir verbs. These endings remain stable across person and number.
TL: Mientras analizábamos los datos, el equipo discutía las excepciones.
EN: While we were analysing the data, the team was discussing the exceptions.
[PAUSE:1000]
Only ser, ir and ver have special imperfect stems: era, iba and veía.
TL: Cuando era estudiante, iba a la biblioteca y veía a Clara cada jueves.
EN: When I was a student, I went to the library and saw Clara every Thursday.
SHOW: hablaba · comía · vivía · era · iba · veía
Flip for compound forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Compound tenses isolate the variable part
REF: 16.2, 16.7.10, 16.13

Every compound tense combines a finite form of haber with an invariable past participle. Change haber to locate the event; do not make the participle agree with the subject.
TL: Ellas habían revisado las cifras y habrán enviado el informe antes del viernes.
EN: They had reviewed the figures and will have sent the report before Friday.
[PAUSE:1000]
Spanish never selects ser merely because a verb describes movement or change of state. The auxiliary remains haber.
TL: Cuando lleguemos, los invitados ya habrán venido.
EN: When we arrive, the guests will already have come.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: finite haber + invariable participle · habían revisado · habrán venido
Flip for regional forms and register.

[CARD]
TITLE: Regional competence includes forms you may not produce
REF: 16.2, 16.7.1, 16.7.8, 16.8

Spain uses vosotros routinely, while Latin America normally uses ustedes. Voseo regions pair vos with locally established present and imperative forms.
TL: Vos sabés que podés venir cuando quieras.
EN: You know that you can come whenever you like.
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose one coherent address system for your own speech, but learn to recognize the others without treating them as errors.
TL: Vosotros tenéis la llave; ustedes pueden esperar en la sala.
EN: You have the key; you can wait in the room.
SHOW: Spain: vosotros · Latin America: ustedes · voseo: regional system
Flip for popular regularizations.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Frequency does not erase the standard contrast
REF: 16.8

Popular speech often regularizes difficult paradigms, adds s to a second-person preterite, or pluralizes existential haber. These forms are useful to recognize but remain unsuitable for careful international writing.
TL: En el informe formal escribimos dijiste, hubo problemas y quepo aquí.
EN: In the formal report we write you said, there were problems, and I fit here.
[PAUSE:1000]
The goal is not to flatten regional variety. It is to distinguish established regional standards from stigmatized analogical forms.
TL: Una forma regional coherente no es lo mismo que una regularización estigmatizada.
EN: A coherent regional form is not the same as a stigmatized regularization.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: recognize variation · preserve register · produce a coherent system
End of the conjugation lesson.
