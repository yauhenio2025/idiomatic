## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Start from subject, verb, and object
REF: 27.1

The neutral order of a Portuguese statement is often the familiar subject–verb–object pattern. Keep it unless information structure or a more specific placement rule gives you a reason to move something.
TL: A Marina revisou o contrato.
EN: Marina reviewed the contract.
[PAUSE:850]
Time and place expressions can frame that core without changing the roles.
TL: Ontem a Marina revisou o contrato no escritório.
EN: Yesterday Marina reviewed the contract at the office.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: subject + verb + object · add context around the core
Flip for the first important difference from English.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Do not put a neutral manner adverb between subject and verb
REF: 27.1

English readily places a manner adverb before the lexical verb. Neutral Portuguese normally puts it immediately after the verb and before a direct object.
TL: A Marina revisou cuidadosamente o contrato.
EN: Marina carefully reviewed the contract.
[PAUSE:900]
The adverb describes how the action was carried out; it does not split the subject from the verb.
TL: O técnico verificou rapidamente os dados.
EN: The technician quickly checked the data.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: subject + verb + manner + direct object

[CARD]
TITLE: Final position gives the manner extra weight
REF: 27.1

A manner adverb can follow the direct object when the way something happened deserves special emphasis.
TL: A Marina revisou o contrato cuidadosamente.
EN: Marina reviewed the contract carefully.
[PAUSE:850]
Compare the neutral order, where the object receives the later position.
TL: A Marina revisou cuidadosamente o contrato.
EN: Marina carefully reviewed the contract.
[PAUSE:950]
Both orders can be grammatical. The later element normally carries more informational weight.
SHOW: neutral manner -> verb + adverb + object · emphatic manner -> verb + object + adverb
Flip for short indirect-object phrases.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A short recipient phrase follows the same contrast
REF: 27.1

A short indirect object introduced by a or para commonly follows the verb and precedes the direct object in a neutral statement.
TL: O diretor entregou aos candidatos o resultado.
EN: The director gave the candidates the result.
[PAUSE:900]
Putting the direct object first can make the recipient feel more prominent or contrastive.
TL: O diretor entregou o resultado aos candidatos, não aos jornalistas.
EN: The director gave the result to the candidates, not to the journalists.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: neutral recipient -> verb + recipient + direct object · focused recipient -> later

[CARD]
TITLE: New subjects often arrive after an intransitive verb
REF: 27.1

When an intransitive verb introduces a subject as new information, Portuguese commonly places that subject after the verb.
TL: Chegou uma encomenda para você.
EN: A parcel arrived for you. / There is a parcel for you.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Apareceu um erro no relatório.
EN: An error appeared in the report. / There was an error in the report.
[PAUSE:950]
English often uses there to postpone the new subject. Portuguese can let the verb introduce it directly.
SHOW: intransitive verb + new subject
Flip when the subject is already known.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A known subject returns naturally to the front
REF: 27.1

Once the subject is established, subject–verb order is the ordinary continuation.
TL: Chegou uma encomenda para você. A encomenda está na recepção.
EN: A parcel arrived for you. The parcel is at reception.
[PAUSE:900]
The first sentence presents the parcel; the second takes it as the topic.
TL: Surgiu um problema ontem. O problema já foi resolvido.
EN: A problem arose yesterday. The problem has already been solved.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: new information -> after the verb · established topic -> before the verb

[CARD]
TITLE: Headlines can delay the subject for impact
REF: 27.1

Journalistic Portuguese often begins with the event and its setting, then reveals the subject at the end.
TL: Começa hoje em Recife a feira internacional de tecnologia.
EN: The international technology fair begins today in Recife.
[PAUSE:900]
The same effect appears in the passive.
TL: Foi inaugurada ontem a nova biblioteca municipal.
EN: The new municipal library was opened yesterday.
[PAUSE:950]
This is a presentational choice, not a rule that every subject should follow its verb.
SHOW: event + setting + newsworthy subject
Flip to turn statements into ordinary yes-or-no questions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Yes-or-no questions normally keep statement order
REF: 27.2

Portuguese does not normally invert the subject and verb or add an auxiliary equivalent to English do. Speech uses question intonation; writing uses the question mark.
TL: A Lívia trabalha aqui. · A Lívia trabalha aqui?
EN: Lívia works here. · Does Lívia work here?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Vocês receberam a mensagem?
EN: Did you receive the message?
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: statement order + question intonation or ? · no do-support

[CARD]
TITLE: Most question words do not trigger English-style inversion
REF: 27.2

With an ordinary subject and verb, place the interrogative phrase first and keep the Portuguese clause intact.
TL: Por que a Lívia adiou a reunião?
EN: Why did Lívia postpone the meeting?
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Que livro o Rafael comprou?
EN: Which book did Rafael buy?
[PAUSE:950]
There is no inserted auxiliary and no general rule that moves the subject behind the verb.
SHOW: question phrase + subject + verb · no English do/did
Flip for a narrower pattern where verb–subject order is natural.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Be-like verbs often precede a noun subject after a question word
REF: 27.2

With ser, estar, ficar, and verbs of similar meaning, a noun subject commonly follows the verb.
TL: Onde fica a estação central? · Como estão os preparativos?
EN: Where is the central station? · How are the preparations going?
[PAUSE:900]
With a pronoun subject, both orders can occur.
TL: Onde ela está? · Onde está ela?
EN: Where is she?
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: interrogative + be-like verb + noun subject · pronoun order can vary

[CARD]
TITLE: Treat broad inversion as a formal exception
REF: 27.2

Formal writing can place the subject after a wider range of verbs, but that order sounds marked outside an appropriate register.
TL: Teria o autor previsto essa consequência?
EN: Had the author foreseen that consequence?
[PAUSE:900]
In ordinary conversation, keep the subject before the verb.
TL: O autor teria previsto essa consequência?
EN: Would the author have foreseen that consequence?
[PAUSE:950]
Do not copy English question inversion mechanically. Use the ordinary order unless a specific Portuguese pattern or formal style motivates the change.
SHOW: everyday question -> subject + verb · formal prose may invert
Flip to indirect questions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An indirect question is a clause inside a larger sentence
REF: 27.3

The opening expression does not turn the embedded question into English statement order by translation. Build the Portuguese embedded clause according to its own pattern.
TL: Você sabe onde fica a estação central?
EN: Do you know where the central station is?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Não sei por que a Lívia adiou a reunião.
EN: I do not know why Lívia postponed the meeting.
[PAUSE:950]
The locative question uses verb–noun order; the ordinary transitive clause keeps subject–verb order.
SHOW: reporting frame + interrogative + appropriate Portuguese clause order

[CARD]
TITLE: A noun subject often follows an intransitive verb in an indirect question
REF: 27.3

After an interrogative phrase, a noun subject commonly follows an intransitive or be-like verb.
TL: Você sabe a que horas começa a palestra?
EN: Do you know what time the lecture starts?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ela perguntou onde estava a chave do arquivo.
EN: She asked where the archive key was.
[PAUSE:950]
The order supports the same presentational logic seen in statements: the verb introduces or locates the noun subject.
SHOW: interrogative + intransitive/be-like verb + noun subject

[SIDE]
TITLE: Keep the outer and inner questions separate
REF: 27.2, 27.3

First identify the outer frame: você sabe, pode me dizer, ela perguntou. Then locate the interrogative expression that opens the embedded clause.
[PAUSE:850]
Finally choose the inner order. Use verb–noun order with the relevant intransitive or be-like pattern; otherwise do not invent English-style inversion.
TL: Pode me dizer onde fica a entrada? · Você sabe quando a equipe chega?
EN: Can you tell me where the entrance is? · Do you know when the team arrives?
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: outer frame -> interrogative -> inner verb pattern -> noun/pronoun subject

[CARD]
TITLE: Let information structure choose between two grammatical orders
REF: 27.1

Word order is not only a template. It tells the listener what is already established and what is being introduced or emphasized.
TL: A Beatriz leu atentamente o parecer.
EN: Beatriz carefully read the opinion.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: A Beatriz leu o parecer atentamente.
EN: Beatriz read the opinion carefully.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Chegou o ônibus. · O ônibus chegou atrasado.
EN: The bus arrived. · The bus arrived late.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: later position -> stronger focus or newer information
Flip for a final decision routine.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build Portuguese order in four checks
REF: 27.1, 27.2, 27.3

Start with the neutral subject–verb–object core. Place a neutral manner expression directly after the verb, or later when it needs emphasis.
[PAUSE:850]
Next ask whether an intransitive verb is introducing a new subject; if so, verb–subject order may be natural. For a question, avoid automatic English inversion. Finally, in an indirect question, choose the embedded order from the Portuguese verb and subject type.
TL: O guia explicou calmamente o percurso. · Surgiu uma dúvida. · Onde fica a saída? · Você sabe quando começa a visita?
EN: The guide calmly explained the route. · A question arose. · Where is the exit? · Do you know when the visit begins?
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: neutral core -> manner/focus -> new subject -> question pattern
