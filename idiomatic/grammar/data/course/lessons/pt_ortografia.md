## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Portuguese spelling is systematic, but sound still depends on position
REF: 1, 1.5.1

Brazilian Portuguese spelling usually narrows a letter to a small set of sounds. Position often makes the choice: c before a behaves differently from c before e, and final unstressed vowels behave differently from stressed ones.
TL: Como se escreve o seu sobrenome?
EN: How is your surname spelled?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Meu sobrenome se escreve com gê e agá.
EN: My surname is spelled with g and h.
[PAUSE:900]
Some details remain lexical, especially open or closed stressed e and o, the sound of intervocalic x, and whether u is heard in gue, gui, que, or qui. Learn the position rules first, then verify the lexical edges.
SHOW: position rules -> strong first reading · lexical edge -> check reliable audio
Flip to spell names with Portuguese letter names.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Letter names are part of practical spoken Portuguese
REF: 1.1

The alphabet contains the same twenty-six letters as English, but its letter names are Portuguese. The least guessable names include agá for h, jota for j, quê for q, erre for r, xis for x, and ípsilon for y.
TL: Pode soletrar, por favor?
EN: Can you spell that, please?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ana: a-ene-a. Silva: esse-i-ele-vê-a.
EN: Ana: A-N-A. Silva: S-I-L-V-A.
[PAUSE:900]
K, w, and y occur mainly in foreign words and proper names, but they belong to the official alphabet and matter whenever a name, code, or address is dictated.
SHOW: h = agá · j = jota · q = quê · r = erre · x = xis · y = ípsilon

[CARD]
TITLE: C and g change before front vowels
REF: 1.2, 1.5.1

C has its k-like sound before a, o, and u, but its s-like sound before e and i. The cedilla gives c the s-like value before a, o, or u. G is hard before a, o, and u and has the sound heard in gente before e and i.
TL: Cecília comprou café e açúcar.
EN: Cecília bought coffee and sugar.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Gustavo guarda o gelo na geladeira.
EN: Gustavo keeps the ice in the refrigerator.
[PAUSE:900]
Before e or i, gu and qu normally preserve the hard consonant while u is silent. In a lexical set such as aguentar and frequente, u is pronounced; modern spelling no longer marks that difference.
SHOW: ca/co/cu -> hard c · ce/ci and ça/ço/çu -> s sound · ge/gi -> soft g
Flip for the two-letter spelling units.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ch, lh, nh, qu, and gu must be read as groups
REF: 1.1, 1.2

H is silent at the beginning of most native words, but it forms three important groups. Ch has the sound at the start of chuva, lh the palatal sound in filho, and nh the nasal palatal sound in vinho.
TL: O churrasco leva queijo e molho.
EN: The barbecue includes cheese and sauce.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Minha filha conhece aquele caminho.
EN: My daughter knows that path.
[PAUSE:900]
Qu before a or o usually includes a w-like glide, as in quatro and qual. Before e or i it is commonly k-like, as in queijo. Read the whole group before assigning a sound to each written letter.
SHOW: ch · lh · nh · qu/gu before e or i -> read as a unit

[CARD]
TITLE: R has a strong position contrast and a regional margin
REF: 1.2

Between vowels, a single r has the short tapped or trilled value heard in caro. Written rr gives the stronger h-like sound heard in carro. That strong value also occurs at the beginning of a word and after m, n, l, or s.
TL: O carro vermelho parou perto do rio.
EN: The red car stopped near the river.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: O genro da Marta mora em Israel.
EN: Marta's son-in-law lives in Israel.
[PAUSE:900]
The spelling contrast between caro and carro can distinguish words, so the doubled letter is never decorative.
SHOW: vowel-r-vowel -> short r · initial r / rr / after m n l s -> strong r
Flip for syllable-final and infinitive r.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Final r is where region and speech style become audible
REF: 1.2, 1.5.6

R before a consonant or at the end of a non-verb word varies strongly across Brazil: tapped, guttural, retroflex, and weakened realizations all occur. Do not mistake a regional realization for a spelling change.
TL: A tarde ficou muito quente.
EN: The afternoon became very hot.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Vou fazer isso agora.
EN: I am going to do that now.
[PAUSE:900]
The final r of an infinitive is often not pronounced in ordinary speech, but it can reappear before a following vowel. Keep writing the infinitive ending even when casual pronunciation reduces it.
SHOW: syllable-final r -> regional · infinitive -r -> often silent in speech, always written

[CARD]
TITLE: Single and double s contrast between vowels
REF: 1.2, 1.5.7

At the beginning of a word, after a consonant or nasal vowel, and before a voiceless consonant, s is normally unvoiced. Between vowels, a single s is voiced, while ss remains unvoiced.
TL: Rosa vai casar no sábado.
EN: Rosa is going to get married on Saturday.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Esse assunto é muito sério.
EN: That subject is very serious.
[PAUSE:900]
The single-versus-double contrast is visible in pairs such as casar and cassar. Portuguese simplified most historical doubled consonants; rr and ss are the productive spelling contrasts that remain.
SHOW: vowel-s-vowel -> z sound · vowel-ss-vowel -> s sound
Flip for s at a word boundary and the variable letter x.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Phrase boundaries can change s, while x is often lexical
REF: 1.2, 1.5.1

At the end of a word, s is normally unvoiced in isolation. Before a following vowel or voiced consonant in the same phrase, it becomes voiced and the words run together.
TL: As árvores ficam perto das casas.
EN: The trees are near the houses.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Eles estão visitando os museus.
EN: They are visiting the museums.
[PAUSE:900]
X has several possible values. Position supplies useful tendencies, but words such as caixa, exame, próximo, and táxi show why an unfamiliar intervocalic x sometimes has to be learned with the word.
SHOW: final s + voiced onset -> z sound · x -> position helps, dictionary settles edges
Eight printed-key voiced-s identifications follow; five unmatched choices are excluded.

[CARD]
TITLE: Oral vowels reduce in unstressed final position
REF: 1.3

Stressed e and o each have an open and a close value. If no written accent appears, spelling often does not reveal which value a particular word needs.
TL: O corte foi feito na antiga corte.
EN: The cut was made at the old court.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ela tomou café cedo.
EN: She drank coffee early.
[PAUSE:900]
In unstressed final position, written e is commonly pronounced like i and written o like u in Brazilian Portuguese. That reduction also occurs before final s.
SHOW: stressed e/o -> open or close · final unstressed e/o -> commonly i/u
Flip to separate oral and nasal vowels.

[SIDE]
TITLE: M, n, and the tilde can signal nasal vowels
REF: 1.4

A tilde marks nasalization directly. M or n after a vowel in the same syllable also nasalizes that vowel rather than contributing an ordinary final consonant sound.
TL: Minha irmã também compra pão.
EN: My sister also buys bread.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: O campo fica longe do centro.
EN: The field is far from the center.
[PAUSE:900]
Use m at the end of many words and before b or p; n occurs before other consonants. Common nasal diphthongs include ão, ãe, õe, final stressed em, and the verb ending written am when it is unstressed.
SHOW: tilde or vowel + m/n in one syllable -> nasal vowel · m/n not a separate stop

[CARD]
TITLE: Brazilian speech can insert vowels that spelling does not show
REF: 1.5.3, 1.5.4, 1.5.5

Brazilian Portuguese allows only a limited set of consonant clusters. In many other written clusters, speakers insert an i-like vowel to make the word pronounceable.
TL: O advogado observou o ritmo da reunião.
EN: The lawyer observed the rhythm of the meeting.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: A psicóloga explicou o problema.
EN: The psychologist explained the problem.
[PAUSE:900]
The same repair can appear after a foreign final consonant, and before an initial s plus consonant in a borrowed word. It belongs to pronunciation, not ordinary spelling.
SHOW: difficult written cluster -> possible inserted i sound · spelling stays unchanged
Flip to link this repair to d and t.

[SIDE]
TITLE: D and t often palatalize before an i sound
REF: 1.2, 1.5.3

Before a phonetic i sound, d and t commonly acquire the palatalized values heard in dia and tia. Because final unstressed e is often pronounced like i, the pattern also appears in words such as sede and forte.
TL: O dia foi importante para o time.
EN: The day was important for the team.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: A internet funciona durante a noite.
EN: The internet works during the night.
[PAUSE:900]
Speakers and regions vary, so non-palatalized d and t are not spelling errors. The key is that the pronunciation responds to the sound that follows, including an inserted i-like vowel.
SHOW: d/t + pronounced i -> common palatalization · region can vary

[CARD]
TITLE: Divide syllables before deciding where stress falls
REF: 1.6, 1.7

Every Portuguese syllable has a vowel nucleus. Two adjacent consonants normally divide between syllables, including rr and ss. Groups such as ch, lh, nh, common consonant-plus-l or consonant-plus-r clusters, and gu or qu stay together at the start of the next syllable.
TL: ca-mi-nho · car-ro · dis-tin-guir
EN: path · car · to distinguish
[PAUSE:800]
Diphthongs and triphthongs are not split. Once the syllables are clear, the accent rules can identify the default stressed syllable.
SHOW: divide consonants · keep legal onset groups · never split a diphthong
Flip for the two default stress patterns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Word endings predict the default stress
REF: 1.7.1, 1.7.2

Words ending in a, e, o, am, em, or ens, with optional final s where appropriate, normally stress the penultimate syllable without a written accent.
TL: ca-SA · jo-VENS · de-CI-dem
EN: house · young people · they decide
[PAUSE:800]
Words ending in i, u, l, n, r, x, z, and several nasal or diphthongal endings normally stress the final syllable unless a written accent overrides the default.
TL: ca-pi-TAZ · co-MER · or-ga-ni-za-ÇÕES
EN: foreman · to eat · organizations
[PAUSE:900]
Stress is predictable only after you identify the ending and any written accent.
SHOW: common vowel endings -> penultimate · listed consonant/nasal endings -> final

[CARD]
TITLE: Written accents mark exceptions and vowel quality
REF: 1.7.3, 1.7.4, 1.7.5

A stressed final a, e, or o is written with an accent. Every antepenultimate stress is also written. When accented e or o is open, use the acute accent; when it is close, use the circumflex.
TL: café · robô · lógico · ônibus
EN: coffee · robot · logical · bus
[PAUSE:800]
An accent can also place stress on the penultimate syllable where the ending would otherwise predict final stress.
TL: âmbar · tênis · órgão
EN: amber · tennis shoes · organ
[PAUSE:900]
The accent therefore does two jobs: it locates exceptional stress and, for e or o, can identify open versus close quality.
SHOW: final stressed a/e/o -> accent · antepenultimate -> accent · é/ó open, ê/ô close
Flip for hiatus and a few grammatical contrasts.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Accented i or u can break a diphthong
REF: 1.7.6, 1.7.7, 1.7.8

Stressed i or u may take an acute accent to show that it begins a separate syllable rather than joining the neighboring vowel.
TL: saída · saúde · construído
EN: exit · health · built
[PAUSE:800]
The accent is omitted in several protected environments, including before nh and before certain consonants in the same syllable, as in rainha, cair, and ruim.
TL: Ele pôde vir, mas hoje não pode.
EN: He was able to come, but today he cannot.
[PAUSE:900]
Pôr contrasts with por; pôde with pode; and plural têm and vêm with singular tem and vem.
SHOW: hiatus i/u -> often acute · grammatical contrast -> preserve the written accent

[CARD]
TITLE: Hyphens link specific structures, not arbitrary compounds
REF: 1.8

A hyphen joins a verb to a following unstressed object pronoun and appears in defined types of compounds.
TL: Precisamos preencher o formulário e enviá-lo hoje.
EN: We need to fill in the form and send it today.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: A palavra-chave está no guarda-roupa.
EN: The key word is in the wardrobe.
[PAUSE:900]
Prefix rules depend on the prefix ending and the first letter of the next element. A shared vowel or initial h often triggers a hyphen, while other combinations may fuse and double initial r or s.
SHOW: verb + enclitic -> hyphen · lexical compound/prefix -> apply its rule
Flip from word formation to line breaking.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A line break must respect syllable structure
REF: 1.6, 1.8.5

Carry part of a word to the next line only at a legal syllable boundary. Do not leave a single letter alone at either end of the line.
TL: ca-mi-nho · trans-por-te · lau-do
EN: path · transport · report
[PAUSE:800]
If an already hyphenated word must break at its hyphen, repeat a hyphen at the beginning of the new line. The doubled mark tells the reader that one hyphen belongs to the word and the other to the line break.
SHOW: syllable boundary only · no isolated letter · existing hyphen -> repeat after break

[CARD]
TITLE: Portuguese capitalizes categories differently from English
REF: 1.9

Days, months, nationalities, religions, political affiliations, languages, and many adjectives derived from proper names normally begin with a lowercase letter in Portuguese.
TL: Na terça-feira, 3 de abril, a pesquisadora francesa falou russo.
EN: On Tuesday, April 3, the French researcher spoke Russian.
[PAUSE:800]
Academic disciplines take an initial capital in the usage described by the reference grammar.
TL: Ela estuda Medicina e ele faz um curso de História.
EN: She studies medicine and he is taking a history course.
[PAUSE:900]
Institutional words such as Estado, Igreja, País, and Prefeitura can also take a capital when they denote a particular institution.
SHOW: calendar/nationality/language -> lowercase · academic discipline/institution -> capital
Flip for punctuation conventions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Dialogue marks and numbers reveal local punctuation
REF: 1.10

Portuguese punctuation largely overlaps with English, but single quotation marks are not used as ordinary inverted commas. A dash can introduce each speaker in written dialogue and can reappear before a reporting verb.
TL: — Onde você vai? — perguntou Ana.
EN: “Where are you going?” Ana asked.
[PAUSE:800]
In numbers, a comma is used as the decimal separator and a full stop can separate thousands.
TL: O total foi de R$ 15.742,35.
EN: The total was R$15,742.35.
[PAUSE:900]
Portuguese also tends to mark phrase and clause boundaries with commas more often than English, but punctuation still follows syntax rather than every pause in speech.
SHOW: dialogue -> dash · decimal -> comma · thousands -> full stop
