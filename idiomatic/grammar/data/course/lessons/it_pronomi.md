## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A stressed pronoun is independent; a clitic belongs to its verb
REF: 6.1

Italian separates independent stressed pronouns from unstressed clitics. A stressed form can stand alone, carry contrast, or follow a preposition; a clitic sits immediately before or after its verb and cannot normally carry contrastive stress.
TL: Hanno invitato me, non te; poi mi hanno telefonato.
EN: They invited me, not you; then they phoned me.
[PAUSE:900]
Subject pronouns are stressed forms. Italian usually omits them because the verb ending identifies the subject, but states them for contrast, focus, or disambiguation.
TL: Io parto oggi; lei parte domani.
EN: I am leaving today; she is leaving tomorrow.
SHOW: independent, contrasted, or after a preposition -> stressed pronoun · attached to a verb -> clitic · neutral subject -> usually omitted

[SIDE]
TITLE: Io and tu contrast with oblique me and te
REF: 6.19, 6.21, 6.23

Use io and tu as ordinary subjects, but me and te as stressed objects and after prepositions. Noi, voi, lui, lei, and loro keep the same current form in both roles.
TL: Tu vieni con me; io resto con te.
EN: You are coming with me; I am staying with you.
[PAUSE:900]
With identificational essere, the verb agrees with the subject form: sono io, eri tu, saranno loro. After e in a coordinated subject, te is common, especially in io e te; isolated subject te is regional or colloquial, while subject me is non-standard.
TL: Siamo stati io e te; non sono stati loro.
EN: It was you and I; it was not them.
SHOW: subject -> io/tu · object or preposition -> me/te · identification -> sono io · coordinated subject -> io e te is current

[CARD]
TITLE: Direct, indirect, and reflexive roles select the clitic
REF: 6.2

First and second person use mi, ti, ci, and vi for direct objects, indirect objects, and reflexives. Third-person direct objects distinguish gender and number: lo, la, li, and le.
TL: Mi vedi? Sì, ti vedo; vedo anche loro e li saluto.
EN: Can you see me? Yes, I see you; I also see them and greet them.
[PAUSE:900]
Third-person indirect objects are gli for a man and le for a woman. Current Italian also widely uses gli for plural people; formal loro follows the verb and remains a separate word. Reflexive third person uses si in both numbers.
TL: Gli scrivo, le telefono e poi si incontrano.
EN: I write to him, phone her, and then they meet.
SHOW: direct 3rd person -> lo/la/li/le · indirect singular -> gli/le · indirect plural -> current gli or formal postverbal loro · reflexive -> mi/ti/si/ci/vi/si
Flip for elision and agreement clues.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Elision can hide gender, but agreement can reveal it
REF: 6.2

Singular lo and la commonly become l apostrophe before forms of avere, while plural li and le never elide. The spelling l'ho therefore does not show gender by itself.
TL: Il libro? L'ho letto. La lettera? L'ho letta.
EN: The book? I read it. The letter? I read it.
[PAUSE:900]
A preceding direct-object clitic often triggers participle agreement, especially with lo, la, li, and le. Do not confuse indirect le, to her, with direct feminine plural le, them.
TL: Le ho telefonato, ma non ho ancora letto le lettere; le leggerò stasera.
EN: I phoned her, but I have not read the letters yet; I will read them tonight.
SHOW: l'ho + participle agreement -> recover gender · le + intransitive/indirect-object verb -> to her · le + transitive verb -> feminine plural object

[CARD]
TITLE: A finite verb normally follows its clitic
REF: 6.3

With ordinary finite forms, place the clitic or complete clitic group immediately before the verb. Positive tu, noi, and voi imperatives instead attach it to the end; formal Lei imperatives keep it before the verb.
TL: Te lo spiego adesso; spiegamelo domani.
EN: I will explain it to you now; explain it to me tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Signora, me lo dica con calma.
EN: Madam, tell it to me calmly.
[PAUSE:1000]
Negative tu imperatives allow either position: non dirlo and non lo dire. In a public infinitival instruction, the clitic attaches to the infinitive.
SHOW: ordinary finite verb -> clitic before · positive tu/noi/voi command -> attached after · Lei command -> before · negative tu -> either position
Flip for infinitives, gerunds, and multi-verb phrases.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Non-finite forms take an attached clitic
REF: 6.3, 6.4

Attach clitics to an infinitive or gerund as one written word. The infinitive loses its final e: vedere plus lo becomes vederlo. A compound tense instead puts the clitic before the auxiliary.
TL: Dopo averlo salutato, sono uscita sorridendogli.
EN: After greeting him, I left while smiling at him.
[PAUSE:900]
With a modal or similar verb plus infinitive, the clitic can usually climb before the first verb or attach to the infinitive. Keep the meaning and the whole cluster together.
TL: Glielo devo dire; devo dirglielo.
EN: I have to tell it to her or him; I have to tell it to her or him.
SHOW: infinitive -> vederlo · gerund -> vedendolo · compound tense -> l'ho visto · modal + infinitive -> lo devo vedere / devo vederlo

[CARD]
TITLE: A clitic cluster has a fixed internal order
REF: 6.6

In the most useful two-clitic combinations, an indirect or reflexive clitic comes before lo, la, li, or le. The group stays in the same order whether it precedes the verb or attaches after it.
TL: Me lo presti? Sì, posso prestartelo domani.
EN: Will you lend it to me? Yes, I can lend it to you tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not split the group across two verbs. Say glielo voglio spiegare or voglio spiegarglielo, never gli voglio spiegarlo.
SHOW: recipient/reflexive + direct object -> one cluster · before first verb or attached to infinitive -> keep cluster intact
Flip for the special forms inside that cluster.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Before lo, la, li, le, or ne, i changes to e
REF: 6.2, 6.6

Mi, ti, gli, le, si, ci, and vi become me, te, glie, glie, se, ce, and ve before a third-person direct-object clitic or ne. Gli and indirect le therefore merge into the stem glie.
TL: Me la mandi? Gliene porto due e ce ne restano tre.
EN: Will you send it to me? I will take two to him or her, and we have three left.
[PAUSE:900]
Ne normally comes last in a cluster. Avoid inventing rare three-clitic strings: a stressed pronoun or an explicit place phrase is often clearer.
SHOW: mi/ti/gli-le/si/ci/vi -> me/te/glie/se/ce/ve before lo-la-li-le-ne · ne -> last

[CARD]
TITLE: Ecco accepts an attached pronoun; formal loro does not
REF: 6.5, 6.7

Attach a clitic to ecco to present a person or thing. Direct-object forms mean here or there it is; a first- or second-person form can present the participant.
TL: Dov'è la chiave? Eccola. Ed eccomi pronto.
EN: Where is the key? Here it is. And here I am, ready.
[PAUSE:900]
Formal plural indirect loro behaves unlike a true clitic: it normally follows the verb or participle and is written separately. Everyday Italian usually chooses gli instead.
TL: Ho dato loro le istruzioni; gli ho spiegato tutto.
EN: I gave them the instructions; I explained everything to them.
SHOW: ecco + clitic -> eccolo/eccola/eccomi · formal plural recipient -> verb + loro · neutral plural recipient -> gli before verb
Flip for two pronouns whose meaning is larger than a noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Propositional lo and idiomatic la must be learned by function
REF: 6.8, 6.9

Invariant lo can replace an adjective, predicate, or whole proposition. It is common after essere, sembrare, sapere, and verbs of understanding.
TL: Sono pronti? Sì, lo sono, ma non lo sapevo.
EN: Are they ready? Yes, they are, but I did not know it.
[PAUSE:900]
Feminine la appears without a recoverable feminine noun in fixed verbs such as farcela, sentirsela, prendersela, and cavarsela. Keep the entire lexical expression and conjugate its other clitic.
TL: Non me la sento, ma so che ce la farai.
EN: I do not feel up to it, but I know that you will manage.
SHOW: predicate or proposition -> invariant lo · fixed idiom -> learn la with the whole verb · farcela/cavarsela/sentirsela/prendersela
Thirteen current translation prompts follow; the source-open translation is excluded.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ci replaces an established place or directional complement
REF: 6.10, 6.11

Locative ci stands for a place or direction already mentioned, commonly one introduced by a. Formal vi can serve the same function, but neutral speech strongly prefers ci.
TL: Torni a Firenze? Sì, ci torno domani.
EN: Are you returning to Florence? Yes, I am going back there tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Ci is an anaphoric clitic, not the pointing word for every English there. When a gesture alone supplies the place, lì or là is normally clearer. With sopra, sotto, dentro, and similar words, ci replaces the noun while the locative word remains.
TL: La scatola? Ci ho messo sopra il quaderno.
EN: The box? I put the notebook on top of it.
SHOW: previously named place/direction -> ci · gesture or new deictic place -> lì/là · formal locative alternative -> vi
Flip for presentative and necessity constructions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Essere and volere give ci two high-frequency constructions
REF: 6.12

Presentative esserci agrees with what is presented: c'è un problema, ci sono due problemi. When another verb expresses arrival or appearance, Italian normally presents the subject after that verb without ci.
TL: C'è una soluzione, ma sono sorti altri problemi.
EN: There is a solution, but other problems have arisen.
[PAUSE:900]
Volerci means to be necessary or to take an amount of time, and it also agrees with its grammatical subject. In colloquial averci, ci reinforces possession but is not used when avere is merely an auxiliary.
TL: Ci vuole pazienza; ci vogliono tre ore.
EN: Patience is needed; it takes three hours.
SHOW: c'è singular / ci sono plural · ci vuole singular / ci vogliono plural · lexical ci belongs to the construction

[CARD]
TITLE: Ne replaces di plus a noun and makes quantities complete
REF: 6.13

Ne can replace a complement introduced by di, including a topic, source, or object of an adjective. It also replaces a partitive noun phrase meaning some or any.
TL: Parli del progetto? Sì, ne parlo spesso.
EN: Do you talk about the project? Yes, I often talk about it.
[PAUSE:900]
After a number or another quantifier, Italian normally keeps ne where English may omit of them. The quantifier stays in its ordinary position.
TL: Quante copie compri? Ne compro tre.
EN: How many copies are you buying? I am buying three.
SHOW: di + noun -> ne · some/any -> ne · number or quantifier without repeated noun -> ne + quantity
Flip for motion, agreement, and the limits of ne.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ne can mark motion away, but it cannot replace every embedded noun
REF: 6.13, 6.14

Ne can replace da plus a place when the subject moves away or out. In a compound tense with a quantified direct object, the participle commonly agrees with the quantity represented by ne.
TL: Sono entrata nella stanza e ne sono uscita con tre mappe; ne ho comprate due.
EN: I entered the room and left it with three maps; I bought two of them.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not use ne for a noun governed by a different preposition, as in a molti di loro. Learn lexical combinations such as andarsene and starsene as whole verbs.
TL: Me ne vado adesso; loro se ne stanno in silenzio.
EN: I am leaving now; they remain silent.
SHOW: motion away from a place -> ne · other governing preposition -> explicit pronoun phrase · lexical ne -> andarsene/starsene

[CARD]
TITLE: True reflexive and lexically reflexive verbs are different
REF: 6.15

With a true reflexive, the subject acts on itself and a stressed form can make that meaning explicit. With a lexically reflexive verb, the clitic is part of the verb and cannot be replaced by sé stesso.
TL: Marco si critica, cioè critica sé stesso; poi si alza.
EN: Marco criticizes himself, that is, he criticizes himself; then he gets up.
[PAUSE:900]
Many lexical reflexives contrast with a transitive verb: aprirsi versus aprire, spaventarsi versus spaventare, alzarsi versus alzare. Ask whether the subject changes state or causes a change in something else.
TL: Il vento apre la porta; la porta si apre.
EN: The wind opens the door; the door opens.
SHOW: action returns to subject -> true reflexive · clitic belongs to verb -> lexical reflexive · cause + object -> transitive · undergo change -> intransitive reflexive
Flip for a reflexive clitic that adds involvement rather than reflexivity.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An interested-subject clitic presents an action as one's own
REF: 6.16

With some transitive verbs, a reflexive clitic can show that the action concerns, benefits, or fully engages the subject. It often adds completion or personal involvement rather than a literal action on oneself.
TL: Mi bevo un caffè e poi mi leggo il rapporto.
EN: I will have myself a coffee and then read the report through.
[PAUSE:900]
This use is restricted to clitics; it cannot normally be expanded with a stressed reflexive. Keep the direct object because it is still what gets drunk, eaten, or read.
TL: Si è mangiata tutta la torta.
EN: She ate the whole cake up.
SHOW: reflexive clitic + transitive verb + object -> personal involvement or completion · object remains direct

[CARD]
TITLE: Plural reflexive clitics can express each other
REF: 6.27

With a plural subject, ci, vi, or si can mark a reciprocal relationship. Context often makes the each-other reading clear.
TL: Io e Marta ci scriviamo ogni settimana.
EN: Marta and I write to each other every week.
[PAUSE:900]
Some sentences remain ambiguous between a reflexive and a reciprocal reading. Add l'un l'altro, a vicenda, reciprocamente, or tra loro when the distinction matters.
TL: I due candidati si criticano a vicenda.
EN: The two candidates criticize each other.
SHOW: plural subject + reflexive clitic -> possible reciprocal · ambiguity -> add reciprocal expression
Flip to preserve prepositions and agreement in explicit reciprocals.

[SIDE]
TITLE: L'un l'altro changes form and keeps the verb's preposition
REF: 6.27, 6.28

The parts of l'un l'altro agree with their referents. When the underlying verb requires a preposition, put it before the second element.
TL: Le sorelle parlano l'una dell'altra e si aiutano l'una con l'altra.
EN: The sisters talk about each other and help one another.
[PAUSE:900]
Tra sé or tra loro can mean among themselves; tra me e me and tra sé e sé commonly accompany private thought or speech.
TL: Ne discutevano tra loro, mentre io riflettevo tra me e me.
EN: They discussed it among themselves while I reflected to myself.
SHOW: direct reciprocal -> l'un l'altro · governed relation -> l'uno + preposition + l'altro · among themselves -> tra sé/tra loro

[CARD]
TITLE: Indefinite si presents an unspecified human participant
REF: 6.29

Indefinite personal si corresponds to people, one, or generic you. It occurs with a finite third-person singular verb when no overt noun controls agreement.
TL: In questa città si vive bene e si lavora molto.
EN: In this city people live well and work a lot.
[PAUSE:900]
It belongs to neutral speech as well as formal writing and is especially frequent with dire, pensare, credere, dovere, potere, andare, and common generalizations.
TL: Si dice che il museo sia chiuso.
EN: People say that the museum is closed.
SHOW: unspecified human subject + finite verb -> si + third-person singular · named or non-human subject -> no indefinite si
Flip for non-finite clauses and competing readings.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A non-finite generic action normally has no indefinite si
REF: 6.29

Indefinite si itself appears only with finite verbs. An infinitive, gerund, or absolute participle can carry an understood generic human participant without adding si.
TL: Viaggiando da soli si incontrano persone nuove.
EN: When travelling alone, one meets new people.
[PAUSE:900]
With a transitive verb and a following noun, a si construction can overlap with the passive. Check which expression controls verb agreement before deciding what the structure means.
TL: In estate si affittano molte biciclette.
EN: In summer many bicycles are rented, or people rent many bicycles.
SHOW: infinitive/gerund/absolute participle -> generic participant can stay implicit · transitive si + noun -> inspect agreement and word order

[CARD]
TITLE: Passive si agrees with the promoted object
REF: 6.30

With passive si, the direct object of the active sentence becomes the grammatical subject. The verb agrees with that noun, which usually follows the verb in an unmarked presentation.
TL: Qui si vende il pane e si vendono i giornali.
EN: Bread is sold here and newspapers are sold here.
[PAUSE:900]
Indefinite personal si is clearest with intransitive verbs and normally keeps third-person singular. Singular non-agreement with a plural object is marked or advertising-like in modern neutral usage, so prefer the agreeing passive pattern where possible.
TL: In ufficio si lavora molto; alla mensa si servono pasti caldi.
EN: People work a lot in the office; hot meals are served in the canteen.
SHOW: transitive + promoted noun -> passive si and agreement · no object or human activity -> indefinite si singular · neutral plural object -> prefer plural agreement
Flip to combine indefinite si with another clitic.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reflexive si becomes ci beside indefinite si
REF: 6.31

Italian does not allow si si. When indefinite si combines with a reflexive verb, the first form becomes ci: ci si alza, ci si lava, ci si diverte.
TL: Durante le vacanze ci si alza tardi.
EN: During holidays people get up late.
[PAUSE:900]
An object clitic precedes indefinite si: lo si prepara, le si compra. With a modal, indefinite si may stay before the modal while another clitic attaches to the infinitive, although one full preverbal cluster is also possible.
TL: Un modulo? Si deve compilarlo con cura, oppure lo si deve compilare con cura.
EN: One must fill in the form carefully.
SHOW: indefinite si + reflexive si -> ci si · direct object + indefinite si -> lo/la/li/le si · modal exception -> si deve farlo

[CARD]
TITLE: Compound indefinite si always takes singular essere
REF: 6.32

In a compound tense, indefinite si selects singular essere. If the underlying intransitive verb normally takes avere, its participle remains masculine singular; if it normally takes essere, the participle is usually masculine plural.
TL: Si è lavorato molto, ma si è partiti presto.
EN: People worked a lot, but left early.
[PAUSE:900]
With an independently reflexive verb, use ci si and plural agreement. A passive-si subject instead controls both auxiliary number and participle agreement.
TL: Ci si è alzati presto; si sono aperte tutte le finestre.
EN: People got up early; all the windows were opened.
SHOW: underlying avere verb -> si è lavorato · underlying essere verb -> si è partiti · reflexive -> ci si è alzati · passive plural subject -> si sono aperte
Flip for object clitics and predicate adjectives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Object clitics and predicates carry their own agreement clues
REF: 6.32, 6.33

With indefinite si and a preceding direct-object clitic, the verb stays singular and the participle agrees with the clitic. With passive si and an overt subject, agreement instead follows that subject.
TL: Le lettere? Le si è spedite ieri; le copie si sono spedite stamattina.
EN: The letters? People sent them yesterday; the copies were sent this morning.
[PAUSE:900]
Predicate adjectives with indefinite si are normally masculine plural, even though essere remains singular. A feminine plural is possible when the generic group is explicitly all female.
TL: Quando si è stanchi, si è meno prudenti.
EN: When people are tired, they are less careful.
SHOW: object clitic -> la si è vista / le si è viste · passive subject -> si è aperta / si sono aperte · predicate adjective -> si è stanchi
