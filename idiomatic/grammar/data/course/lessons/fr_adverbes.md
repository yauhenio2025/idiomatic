## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: An adverb is defined by its scope, not by its ending
REF: 5.1, 5.6

French adverbs are invariable words or phrases that can modify one word, a whole verb phrase, or the speaker's presentation of a sentence. First find that scope; only then interpret the adverb.
TL: Le comité a répondu étonnamment vite, mais, franchement, sa réponse ne résout rien.
EN: The committee responded surprisingly quickly, but frankly, its answer solves nothing.
[PAUSE:900]
Vite tells us how the committee answered. Étonnamment grades vite. Franchement stands outside that event and comments on the speaker's act of saying it.
SHOW: word · phrase · whole sentence → identify the adverb's scope
Flip for the productive route from an adjective to an adverb.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Position can expose which constituent the adverb selects
REF: 5.1, 5.7.1

An adverb placed beside an adjective or another adverb normally selects that nearby expression. At sentence level, a different position can change what is restricted or evaluated.
TL: Seule Lina a relu le rapport; Lina a relu seulement l'annexe.
EN: Only Lina reread the report; Lina reread only the appendix.
[PAUSE:900]
The adjective seule agrees with Lina and restricts the participant. The invariable adverb seulement restricts the object. Similar surface meanings do not make the two structures interchangeable.
TL: Heureusement, le résultat est étonnamment clair.
EN: Fortunately, the result is surprisingly clear.
SHOW: adjacent target → local scope · detached adverb → sentence-level scope

[CARD]
TITLE: Most -ment adverbs start from the feminine adjective
REF: 5.2.1

For the broad productive pattern, take the feminine singular adjective and add ment. This accounts for pairs such as douce and doucement, naturelle and naturellement, or heureuse and heureusement.
TL: Elle a doucement refermé la porte, puis a naturellement baissé la voix.
EN: She gently closed the door again, then naturally lowered her voice.
[PAUSE:900]
The written feminine matters even when its final e is not pronounced. Treat adjective formation and adverb formation as two ordered steps.
TL: Une réponse brève peut être formulée brièvement sans paraître sèche.
EN: A brief answer can be phrased briefly without seeming curt.
SHOW: adjective → feminine base → add -ment · check lexical exceptions
Flip for the families that choose a different base or ending.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Vowel-final adjectives often use the masculine base
REF: 5.2.2, 5.2.5

Adjectives ending in i, é, or u normally attach ment directly to the masculine form: poli gives poliment, aisé gives aisément, and absolu gives absolument.
TL: Elle a poliment mais résolument écarté cette solution trop coûteuse.
EN: She politely but resolutely rejected that overly expensive solution.
[PAUSE:900]
A small inherited set adds an accent or has an exceptional stem. The pattern helps you predict, but a dictionary remains the authority for a form you have not met before.
TL: Le juge a dûment motivé une décision communément admise.
EN: The judge duly justified a commonly accepted decision.
SHOW: final -i/-é/-u → masculine base + ment · inherited forms → lexical check

[CARD]
TITLE: Special endings form families, but translation stays lexical
REF: 5.2.2, 5.2.3, 5.2.4, 5.2.5, 5.5

Adjectives in i, é, or u usually add ment to the masculine form. Those in ant and ent normally give amment and emment; despite the spelling contrast, both endings have the same nasal vowel.
TL: Le témoin a répondu prudemment, puis a décrit précisément la scène.
EN: The witness answered cautiously, then described the scene precisely.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: L'équipe a vaillamment défendu une proposition absolument cohérente.
EN: The team valiantly defended an absolutely coherent proposal.
[PAUSE:1000]
Past participles and a few lexical families add further patterns, so verify unfamiliar forms. More importantly, English ly is more productive than French ment: an idiomatic phrase may be the real translation.
TL: Il a rejeté l'offre avec colère, mais il a exposé ses raisons avec beaucoup de tact.
EN: He rejected the offer angrily, but he explained his reasons very tactfully.
SHOW: -i/-é/-u + ment · -ant → -amment · -ent → -emment · English -ly may require a phrase

[SIDE]
TITLE: English manner often becomes a French prepositional phrase
REF: 5.4, 5.5

When French lacks a natural ment adverb, use a noun phrase introduced by avec, d'une manière, d'une façon, or sur un ton. The choice expresses the intended manner rather than copying the English morphology.
TL: La présidente a répondu avec calme, puis a présenté l'objection d'une manière concise.
EN: The chair responded calmly, then presented the objection concisely.
[PAUSE:900]
Colours used adverbially normally follow en. Shapes are recast with a noun such as forme instead of being treated as bare adverbs.
TL: Peignez la bordure en noir et donnez au panneau une forme ovale.
EN: Paint the border black and give the panel an oval shape.
SHOW: no idiomatic -ment form → phrase · colour → en + colour · shape → noun frame

[CARD]
TITLE: A small verb-bound set uses an adjective as an adverb
REF: 5.3, 4.6, 5.4

Certain masculine adjective forms act as invariable adverbs with particular verbs. Learn the whole combination: travailler dur, coûter cher, sentir bon, parler bas, or tourner court.
TL: Ces techniciennes ont travaillé dur, mais la négociation a tourné court.
EN: These technicians worked hard, but the negotiation came to an abrupt end.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Les bougies sentent bon et coûtent étonnamment cher.
EN: The candles smell nice and cost surprisingly much.
[PAUSE:1000]
The same adjective may have a ment form with a different use, while many longer adverbial meanings are carried by fixed prepositional phrases. Store both as lexical constructions.
TL: Il a tout bonnement refusé, puis il est parti sans détour.
EN: He simply refused, then he left without beating around the bush.
SHOW: verb + fixed masculine form → invariable · phrase adverbs stay intact
Nine clean adjective-or-adverb retrieval drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The bare form and the -ment form are separate lexical choices
REF: 5.3, 4.6

Do not freely replace a fixed bare adjective with its ment relative. The construction and meaning may change: parler bas describes volume, while agir bassement judges conduct.
TL: Le témoin a parlé bas, mais l'accusateur s'est comporté bassement.
EN: The witness spoke quietly, but the accuser behaved despicably.
[PAUSE:900]
Likewise, cher belongs with verbs such as coûter and payer, while chèrement survives in narrower expressions. Memorize the verb-adverb pairing as one unit.
TL: Ils ont payé cher une erreur qu'ils avaient longtemps sous-estimée.
EN: They paid dearly for an error they had long underestimated.
SHOW: fixed bare form ≠ automatic -ment synonym · retrieve the whole pairing

[CARD]
TITLE: Degree adverbs impose different frames on their targets
REF: 5.6.2

Degree adverbs can measure a verb, adjective, adverb, noun phrase, or even a preposition, but each form has its own permissions. Très modifies an adjective or adverb; beaucoup modifies a verb; a noun normally needs de after its quantifier.
TL: Le train avançait tellement lentement que nous avons beaucoup discuté.
EN: The train was moving so slowly that we talked a great deal.
[PAUSE:900]
Si and tellement can grade an adjective or adverb. Tellement and tant can measure a verb, and tellement de or tant de measures a noun. Davantage can likewise stand with a verb or introduce a noun with de.
TL: Nous avons reçu tellement de dossiers qu'il faudra davantage de temps.
EN: We received so many applications that more time will be needed.
[PAUSE:900]
Au moins supplies a concrete lower bound; du moins adds the speaker's reservation. Même means even before its target, but after a noun it can mean that very noun.
TL: Il faut au moins trois signatures; du moins, c'est ce que prévoit le règlement même.
EN: At least three signatures are required; at any rate, that is what the regulation itself provides.
SHOW: très + quality · beaucoup + verb · tant/tellement de + noun · au moins ≠ du moins
Five clean degree-and-scope drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: So, much and more must be rebuilt around the French target
REF: 5.6.2

English uses a small set of degree words across many structures. French makes the target explicit: si or tellement before a quality, tant or tellement with a verb, and tant de, tellement de, or davantage de before a noun.
TL: Il parle si vite qu'il commet tellement d'erreurs que personne ne veut l'écouter davantage.
EN: He speaks so fast and makes so many mistakes that nobody wants to listen to him any longer.
[PAUSE:900]
Plus can compare several categories, but davantage is especially useful with verbs and nouns. Neither licenses a mechanical word-for-word mapping in every context.
TL: Cette réforme exige davantage de patience et coûtera beaucoup plus cher.
EN: This reform requires more patience and will cost much more.
SHOW: identify target category → choose si/tellement/tant/plus/davantage frame

[CARD]
TITLE: Adverbial comparison is regular except where the lexicon intervenes
REF: 5.6.3, 5.6.4, 5.6.5, 5.6.6

Place plus, moins, or aussi before most adverbs. For the superlative, use le plus or le moins; the article stays masculine singular because it belongs to the adverbial construction.
TL: Maya travaille plus méthodiquement que Paul, mais Paul réagit le plus vite.
EN: Maya works more methodically than Paul, but Paul reacts the fastest.
[PAUSE:900]
Bien has the special comparative mieux and superlative le mieux. Beaucoup compares as plus, while peu compares as moins. Modern French normally compares mal with plus mal; pis survives mainly in fixed expressions.
TL: Ce logiciel fonctionne mieux, mais son concurrent offre une meilleure interface.
EN: This software works better, but its competitor offers a better interface.
[PAUSE:900]
Mieux modifies the way something functions and never agrees. Meilleur is the agreeing adjective that evaluates a noun.
SHOW: plus/moins/aussi + adverb · le plus/le moins · bien → mieux → le mieux · meilleur modifies a noun
Eight clean comparative and superlative drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Equality and ranking preserve adverbial invariability
REF: 5.6.3, 5.6.4, 5.6.5

Aussi forms an equality comparison and introduces its standard with que. In a superlative, le is fixed even when the people being compared are feminine or plural.
TL: Les deux sœurs négocient aussi habilement, mais Inès improvise le plus aisément.
EN: The two sisters negotiate equally skillfully, but Inès improvises the most easily.
[PAUSE:900]
Beaucoup and peu have suppletive comparison frames: plus and moins. Fixed expressions preserve pis, but productive modern comparison normally prefers plus mal.
TL: La situation évolue de mal en pis, et notre équipe réagit de plus en plus lentement.
EN: The situation is going from bad to worse, and our team is reacting increasingly slowly.
SHOW: equality → aussi ... que · ranking → fixed le · beaucoup → plus · peu → moins

[CARD]
TITLE: Tout changes shape only after you identify its grammatical role
REF: 5.6.7

As a determiner or a noun-linked quantifier, tout agrees. As an indefinite pronoun meaning everything, it is singular; when it retrieves plural people or things, it takes an agreeing plural form.
TL: Tous les stagiaires sont arrivés; je les ai tous salués.
EN: All the trainees arrived; I greeted them all.
[PAUSE:900]
As an adverb meaning completely or very, tout is normally invariable before another adverb, a preposition, or a masculine adjective. Before a feminine adjective beginning with a consonant, however, it agrees.
TL: Elles ont parlé tout bas dans une salle toute silencieuse.
EN: They spoke very quietly in a completely silent room.
[PAUSE:900]
Before a feminine adjective beginning with a vowel or silent h, both agreeing and invariable usage occur. Parse the target instead of choosing the form by sound alone.
SHOW: noun-linked tout → agree · adverbial tout → normally fixed · feminine consonant → agreement
Seven clean role-and-agreement drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Feminine initials create the visible adverbial agreement trap
REF: 5.6.7

Before a feminine adjective beginning with a consonant, adverbial tout agrees even though it means completely. Before a vowel or silent h, both the invariable and agreeing forms are accepted.
TL: Les rues étaient toutes blanches, mais les places restaient tout illuminées.
EN: The streets were completely white, but the squares remained completely illuminated.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not confuse this exceptional adverb agreement with a plural pronoun. A pronoun retrieves actual participants or objects and agrees for that reason.
TL: Ces lettres, je les ai toutes lues; elles étaient tout aussi longues.
EN: These letters, I read them all; they were just as long.
SHOW: feminine consonant → toutes · feminine vowel/silent h → tout or agreeing form · pronoun → referential agreement

[CARD]
TITLE: Time adverbs encode both event structure and discourse function
REF: 5.6.8, 5.6.9, 5.6.10, 5.6.11, 5.6.12

Alors can locate an event at a past reference time, or introduce a conversational consequence meaning so. Its position and intonation reveal which job it is doing.
TL: Nora dirigeait alors l'agence; alors, pourquoi est-elle partie?
EN: Nora was managing the agency at the time; so why did she leave?
[PAUSE:900]
Encore and toujours overlap for an ongoing state. With a completed event, encore means again; with an added amount, it means more or another. Around pas, word order separates still, always, and yet.
TL: Le serveur est encore en panne; il a encore supprimé nos réglages.
EN: The server is still down; it deleted our settings again.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il n'est toujours pas prêt, mais il n'est pas toujours aussi lent.
EN: He is still not ready, but he is not always this slow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Ensuite is an adverb and can sit inside a clause; puis coordinates from the front. Jamais can mean ever without ne in questions, si clauses, and after sans.
SHOW: alors: time or discourse · encore: still/again/more · toujours + pas ≠ pas + toujours
Flip for time expressions whose meaning moves with the reference point.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Then and ever split according to syntax
REF: 5.6.11, 5.6.12

Ensuite is a time adverb, so it can follow the finite verb. Puis is a coordinator and introduces the next clause from its left edge.
TL: Le comité a d'abord délibéré, puis il a voté; il a ensuite publié sa décision.
EN: The committee first deliberated, then voted; afterwards it published its decision.
[PAUSE:900]
Jamais normally combines with ne for never. Without ne, it can mean ever in a question, a si clause, or after sans.
TL: Si jamais vous changez d'avis, appelez-moi sans jamais hésiter.
EN: If you ever change your mind, call me without ever hesitating.
SHOW: ensuite = mobile time adverb · puis = clause-initial coordinator · jamais without ne = ever in licensed frames

[CARD]
TITLE: Speech time and narrative time use different coordinates
REF: 5.6.13, 5.6.14, 5.6.15

Hier and demain point directly from the moment of speaking. In reported or narrated time, la veille and le lendemain point backward or forward from a past reference point; ce jour-là names that anchored day.
TL: Elle a promis hier: « Je viendrai demain. » Le lendemain, elle n'était toujours pas là.
EN: She promised yesterday, "I will come tomorrow." The next day, she still was not there.
[PAUSE:900]
Tout à l'heure is interpreted through the tense: with a past event it means just now or a short while ago; with a present or future event it points to later today.
TL: Je l'ai vu tout à l'heure, et je le rappellerai tout à l'heure.
EN: I saw him a short while ago, and I will call him back later today.
[PAUSE:900]
Tard describes lateness in the day or process. En retard specifically means behind schedule.
TL: Nous sommes arrivés tard, mais pas en retard: la séance commençait à minuit.
EN: We arrived late, but not behind schedule: the session started at midnight.
SHOW: speech now → hier/demain · past anchor → la veille/le lendemain · tard ≠ en retard
Thirteen clean time-reference drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build a time grid around the active reference point
REF: 5.6.13, 5.6.14, 5.6.15

Around speech time, avant-hier, hier, aujourd'hui, demain, and après-demain form a direct grid. Around a past anchor, switch to l'avant-veille, la veille, ce jour-là, le lendemain, and le surlendemain.
TL: Le lundi, elle a annoncé qu'elle partirait le surlendemain et reviendrait la semaine suivante.
EN: On Monday, she announced that she would leave two days later and return the following week.
[PAUSE:900]
Tense disambiguates tout à l'heure, while schedule determines whether late is tard or en retard. These are viewpoint choices, not interchangeable vocabulary pairs.
TL: Le conférencier parlera tout à l'heure; il arrivera tard, mais exactement à l'heure prévue.
EN: The speaker will talk later today; he will arrive late, but exactly at the scheduled time.
SHOW: speech grid · narrative grid · tense resolves tout à l'heure · schedule resolves en retard

[CARD]
TITLE: Place, linkage and speaker stance occupy different layers
REF: 5.6.16, 5.6.17

Place adverbs locate an event or orient an object: dehors, ailleurs, derrière, dessus, dessous, and the ci forms used in documents. Prepositional variants can add viewpoint or motion, so learn the whole spatial expression.
TL: Dehors, les équipes attendaient; le matériel était déjà rangé dedans.
EN: Outside, the teams were waiting; the equipment was already stored inside.
[PAUSE:900]
Sentence adverbs either link the current claim to prior discourse, or express the speaker's assessment of its likelihood or desirability. That is why pourtant and donc behave differently from probablement and malheureusement.
TL: La salle était ouverte; pourtant, personne n'est entré, probablement à cause de l'alarme.
EN: The room was open; yet nobody entered, probably because of the alarm.
[PAUSE:900]
Concrete au moins measures a lower bound, while sentence-level du moins qualifies the speaker's commitment. A familiar translation can therefore conceal a difference in scope.
TL: Nous avons sauvé au moins deux fichiers; du moins, leurs noms figurent encore dans l'index.
EN: We saved at least two files; at any rate, their names still appear in the index.
SHOW: place → event location · linker → discourse relation · stance → speaker judgment
Eleven clean place and sentence-adverb drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Linking adverbs relate claims; stance adverbs evaluate them
REF: 5.6.17

A linker such as cependant, pourtant, donc, or en revanche tells the listener how one claim follows another. A stance adverb such as probablement, heureusement, or sans doute reports the speaker's judgment.
TL: Le premier devis est moins cher; en revanche, il est probablement trop optimiste.
EN: The first estimate is cheaper; on the other hand, it is probably too optimistic.
[PAUSE:900]
The same broad English gloss may conceal different roles. Concrete au moins counts; du moins retreats to a qualified claim.
TL: Au moins cinq témoins ont répondu; du moins, cinq réponses figurent dans le dossier.
EN: At least five witnesses responded; at any rate, five responses appear in the file.
SHOW: relation between claims → linker · assessment of claim → stance · count → au moins · reservation → du moins

[CARD]
TITLE: Position follows the target, weight and register
REF: 5.7.1, 5.7.2, 5.7.3, 5.7.4

An adverb modifying an adjective, preposition, noun phrase, or another adverb stands immediately before that target. A one-word modifier of a verb phrase normally follows the conjugated verb, placing it between an auxiliary and a participle.
TL: Elle a soigneusement vérifié les chiffres et a très vite repéré l'erreur.
EN: She carefully checked the figures and spotted the error very quickly.
[PAUSE:900]
Specific times, multiword adverbials, and place adverbs usually prefer the beginning or end of the clause. A heavy object can make a medial place adverb more natural, while sentence adverbs are comparatively mobile.
TL: Demain, nous classerons ailleurs les dossiers jugés trop sensibles.
EN: Tomorrow, we will file the documents considered too sensitive elsewhere.
[PAUSE:900]
In formal writing, a small group of initial adverbs can trigger subject-verb inversion. With peut-être and sans doute, everyday French can instead use que and ordinary order.
TL: À peine la réunion avait-elle commencé qu'une alarme a retenti.
EN: The meeting had barely begun when an alarm sounded.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Peut-être répondra-t-il ce soir; peut-être qu'il répondra demain.
EN: Perhaps he will answer tonight; perhaps he will answer tomorrow.
SHOW: before a nonverb target · after the finite verb · heavy edge phrase · formal initial adverb → inversion
Ten clean placement and inversion judgments follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use a placement diagnostic instead of memorizing one slot
REF: 5.7.1, 5.7.2, 5.7.3, 5.7.4

Ask four questions in order: what does the adverb modify, is it one word or a heavier phrase, where is the finite verb, and is the register formal enough to license inversion?
TL: Le directeur a probablement rangé dans son bureau les contrats signés hier.
EN: The director probably filed the contracts signed yesterday in his office.
[PAUSE:900]
A long direct object can follow a medial place phrase, but detached insertion needs audible pauses. Formal inversion after an initial trigger is a construction, not a general license to invert after any adverb.
TL: Sans doute le directeur a-t-il déjà transmis ailleurs les documents les plus sensibles.
EN: No doubt the director has already sent the most sensitive documents elsewhere.
[PAUSE:900]
In conversation, move the adverb or use peut-être que or sans doute que when inversion would sound too written.
SHOW: scope → weight → finite verb → register · inversion only with licensed initial triggers
