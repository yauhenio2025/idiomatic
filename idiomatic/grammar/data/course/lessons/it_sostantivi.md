## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Three endings organize most Italian plurals
REF: 3.1, 3.3

Start with the final sound of the singular. A feminine noun or adjective in unstressed a normally changes a to e. Most other words ending in an unstressed vowel replace it with i.
TL: La ragazza italiana saluta le ragazze italiane.
EN: The Italian girl greets the Italian girls.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Il fiore rosso diventa due fiori rossi.
EN: The red flower becomes two red flowers.
[PAUSE:1000]
Words ending in a consonant or a stressed vowel are normally invariant. Agreement elsewhere in the phrase still reveals number.
TL: Il film francese; i film francesi.
EN: The French film; the French films.
SHOW: feminine unstressed -a → -e · other unstressed vowel → -i · consonant or stressed vowel → invariant
Flip for spelling changes and lexical exceptions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Preserve the sound when a plural ending changes the spelling
REF: 3.2, 3.3

Before e or i, spelling must continue to represent the intended consonant. Feminines in ca and ga therefore take che and ghe. Masculine words in co and go are less predictable: many preserve a hard sound with h, while others have a soft consonant and no h.
TL: Una giacca bianca; due giacche bianche.
EN: A white jacket; two white jackets.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Un amico greco; due amici greci.
EN: A Greek friend; two Greek friends.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn frequent exceptions as singular-plural pairs. Abbreviations such as foto and moto, color nouns used adjectivally, surnames, and citation forms can also remain unchanged.
SHOW: sound first → choose c/ch or g/gh · lexical exception → store the pair
Thirty-two hygiene-gated number transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Some masculine nouns switch to feminine plural -a
REF: 3.4

A small but important group has a masculine singular in o and a feminine plural in a. Several are ordinary fixed pairs, including uovo and paio.
TL: Ho comprato un uovo; ho usato due uova.
EN: I bought an egg; I used two eggs.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Mi serve un altro paio di guanti; queste paia sono troppo grandi.
EN: I need another pair of gloves; these pairs are too large.
[PAUSE:1000]
The gender switch affects the article, demonstrative, and every adjective that agrees with the plural noun.
SHOW: il paio → le paia · l'uovo → le uova · plural gender controls agreement
Flip for nouns that offer two plurals.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Double plurals often contrast a set with separate objects
REF: 3.4

Several body-part and collective nouns have both a feminine plural in a and a masculine plural in i. The feminine form often presents an anatomical pair or an organized whole; the masculine form often presents separate pieces or a different technical sense.
TL: Mi fanno male le braccia, ma la poltrona ha due bracci di legno.
EN: My arms hurt, but the armchair has two wooden arms.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Le mura circondano la città; i muri delle case sono bianchi.
EN: The walls surround the city; the walls of the houses are white.
[PAUSE:1000]
This is a lexical tendency, not a free rule. Learn the contrast with its noun: ossa versus ossi, dita versus diti, and corna versus corni.
SHOW: body or collective set → often feminine -a · separate or specialized objects → often masculine -i
Nine clean plural choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Masculine roots may change as well as endings
REF: 3.6

Most plural formation changes only the ending, but some masculine nouns alter the root. Words in co or go may preserve the hard consonant with h or shift to a soft consonant without it.
TL: Un dialogo antico; due dialoghi antichi.
EN: An ancient dialogue; two ancient dialogues.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Un amico simpatico; due amici simpatici.
EN: A friendly friend; two friendly friends.
[PAUSE:1000]
Stress and learned suffixes provide tendencies, but they do not remove the lexical choice. High-frequency irregular pairs should be retrieved whole.
SHOW: hard root → -chi/-ghi · soft root → -ci/-gi · frequent pair → memorize together
Flip for the most irregular roots and compounds.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A compound plural follows its internal head—or lexical usage
REF: 3.6, 3.7

Uomo, dio, and bue have the irregular plurals uomini, dei, and buoi. Possessives mio, tuo, and suo likewise have the masculine plurals miei, tuoi, and suoi.
TL: I miei uomini hanno visto due buoi nel campo.
EN: My men saw two oxen in the field.
[PAUSE:900]
In a transparent compound, the head noun often bears number and agreeing modifiers follow it. Other compounds pluralize only at the end or remain invariant, so analyze the structure but confirm the lexical pattern.
TL: Il capogruppo parla con gli altri capigruppo.
EN: The group leader speaks with the other group leaders.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not mechanically inflect every visible element.
SHOW: identify head → apply number there · non-head element → usually invariant · lexicalized compound → verify
Nine hygiene-gated root transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Italian and English can count the same thing differently
REF: 3.8

Grammatical number is language-specific. Italian uses plural capelli, soldi, and spaghetti where English commonly uses a mass-like singular, but singular gente, uva, and pigiama where English may suggest a plural.
TL: La gente aspetta e gli spaghetti sono pronti.
EN: People are waiting and the spaghetti is ready.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ho i capelli corti e pochi soldi in tasca.
EN: I have short hair and little money in my pocket.
[PAUSE:1000]
Agreement follows the Italian noun, not its English gloss.
SHOW: learn noun + number + agreement · never copy the English surface form
Flip for one-per-person expressions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A distributed possession is often singular
REF: 3.8

When each member of a plural group has one body part or one item of clothing, Italian often uses a singular noun. The possessor is usually already clear from the subject or indirect object.
TL: I bambini hanno aperto la bocca.
EN: The children opened their mouths.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Gli atleti si sono tolti la maglia.
EN: The athletes took off their shirts.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use a plural when the objects themselves are the topic or when each person has more than one. Ask whether the sentence naturally means each one has one.
SHOW: plural possessors + one each → often singular possessed noun · objects foregrounded → ordinary plural

[CARD]
TITLE: Predict noun gender from meaning and ending
REF: 3.9, 3.10

Every Italian noun is grammatically masculine or feminine. Nouns denoting people or animals often align with sex; otherwise, final o strongly favors masculine and final a strongly favors feminine.
TL: Il ragazzo cerca la ragazza davanti alla scuola.
EN: The boy is looking for the girl in front of the school.
[PAUSE:900]
Nouns in e, a stressed vowel, i, or a consonant usually require lexical learning. Store an article with the noun so gender becomes part of the word.
TL: Il mese finisce, ma la luce rimane.
EN: The month ends, but the light remains.
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat the generalizations as a fast first guess, never as permission to override evidence.
SHOW: male/female meaning · -o mostly M · -a mostly F · other endings → learn with article
Flip for productive suffix clues and exceptions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Suffixes improve the guess, but lexical gender wins
REF: 3.11, 3.12

Greek-derived nouns in ma with international-looking relatives are often masculine, while nouns in zione and tà are normally feminine. Action and abstract suffixes can therefore be more useful than the last letter alone.
TL: Il problema richiede una soluzione immediata.
EN: The problem requires an immediate solution.
[PAUSE:900]
Important exceptions include la mano, la radio, la moto, il poeta, and il tema. Nouns for living beings can also have grammatical gender independent of an individual's sex, as with la persona or la guida.
TL: La guida esperta si chiama Marco.
EN: The expert guide is called Marco.
[PAUSE:1000]
Agreement always follows grammatical gender.
SHOW: -ma often M · -zione/-tà normally F · exception → article + noun · agreement follows grammar

[CARD]
TITLE: Compounds and names inherit gender from a hidden head
REF: 3.13, 3.14, 3.16

The gender of a compound usually comes from its semantic head. Acronyms normally inherit the gender of the full expression, even when the head noun is not spoken.
TL: La Fiat ha presentato un nuovo modello.
EN: Fiat has presented a new model.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: L'ONU è stata fondata nel 1945.
EN: The United Nations was founded in 1945.
[PAUSE:1000]
Geographical names follow useful tendencies rather than a single rule: cities are normally feminine, rivers are usually masculine, and an expressed head such as monte, isola, or valle can determine gender.
SHOW: compound → semantic head · acronym → full-form head · place name → category plus usage
Flip for gender when a non-noun is nominalized.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An article can turn almost any expression into a noun
REF: 3.15, 3.16

Infinitives, quoted words, letters, and whole expressions can function as nouns. Infinitives and cited expressions are normally masculine; letter names are usually feminine.
TL: Il sapere non basta senza il fare.
EN: Knowing is not enough without doing.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: La erre italiana richiede pratica.
EN: The Italian letter r requires practice.
[PAUSE:1000]
These conventions also explain apparent exceptions among proper names. When usage is uncertain, a dictionary example is stronger evidence than analogy.
SHOW: infinitive or cited expression → usually M · letter name → usually F · established name → learn usage

[CARD]
TITLE: Gender can distinguish two different nouns
REF: 3.17, 3.18

Changing the article may select a different lexical meaning, not merely a male or female version. The tree-fruit pattern is common: the masculine can name the tree and the feminine its fruit.
TL: Il melo produce una mela ogni giorno.
EN: The apple tree produces an apple every day.
[PAUSE:900]
Other pairs must be learned directly: il capitale is financial capital, while la capitale is a capital city; il fine is a purpose, while la fine is an ending.
TL: Roma è la capitale, ma il capitale manca.
EN: Rome is the capital city, but the financial capital is lacking.
[PAUSE:1000]
The article is part of the meaning cue.
SHOW: article + noun → lexical meaning · tree M / fruit F is a pattern, not a universal law
Flip for gender and living beings.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Grammatical gender and a person's identity are separate questions
REF: 3.19, 3.20, 3.21

Some nouns have one grammatical gender for any referent, such as la persona and la vittima. Others distinguish forms, while professional titles vary with current usage, register, and the preference of the person concerned.
TL: La vittima era un uomo; la sindaca ha aperto la riunione.
EN: The victim was a man; the mayor opened the meeting.
[PAUSE:900]
Masculine plurals for relatives can denote a mixed group: fratelli may mean brothers or brothers and sisters, and nonni may mean grandparents.
TL: I miei nonni arrivano domani.
EN: My grandparents arrive tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose the noun first, then make its dependents agree with its grammatical features.
SHOW: referent identity ≠ noun gender · title choice follows living usage · mixed relatives may use masculine plural
Ten clean meaning choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Adjectives agree both beside the noun and after a verb
REF: 3.22, 3.24

An adjective takes gender and number from the noun it describes, whether it directly modifies that noun or forms a predicate after essere, sembrare, or diventare.
TL: La donna furba sembra stanca.
EN: The clever woman seems tired.
[PAUSE:900]
Four-ending adjectives use o, a, i, and e. Adjectives in e use a common singular and plural i; adjectives ending in a consonant or a stressed vowel can remain invariant.
TL: Le donne cortesi indossano gonne blu.
EN: The polite women wear blue skirts.
[PAUSE:1000]
Never replace the gender of an understood noun with a generic masculine.
SHOW: identify licensed noun → choose gender → choose number · attributive and predicate agreement match
Flip for one adjective shared by several nouns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mixed coordination needs a deliberate agreement strategy
REF: 3.23, 3.24

An adjective shared by masculine and feminine nouns commonly takes masculine plural, especially when a masculine noun is closest. With several feminine items and one masculine, natural speakers may reorder or recast the phrase rather than force an awkward agreement.
TL: Ho comprato penne e quaderni rossi.
EN: I bought red pens and red notebooks.
[PAUSE:900]
For mixed groups of people, a paraphrase can be clearer and more inclusive than a bare masculine plural adjective.
TL: Carla, Lucia ed Enrico sono tutte persone simpatiche.
EN: Carla, Lucia, and Enrico are all pleasant people.
[PAUSE:1000]
Agreement is grammatical, but good phrasing also manages clarity and emphasis.
SHOW: shared adjective → inspect every noun · awkward mixed list → reorder or recast
Nine clean agreement completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Post-nominal is the broad default for descriptive adjectives
REF: 3.25, 3.26

Italian allows many adjectives on either side of a noun, but post-nominal position is extremely common. It can classify, identify, or contrast the referent with other members of the noun's class.
TL: Apri la scatola grande, non quella piccola.
EN: Open the large box, not the small one.
[PAUSE:900]
An adjective normally stays next to the noun it modifies. Long intervening material can otherwise create a false attachment.
TL: Ho letto un rapporto dettagliato sulla crisi energetica.
EN: I read a detailed report on the energy crisis.
[PAUSE:1000]
Ask what set the adjective is narrowing and place it where that relation remains obvious.
SHOW: description or contrast → usually after noun · modifier stays beside its noun
Flip for adjective stacks.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Italian adjective order builds outward from the noun
REF: 3.27

Specificational words such as demonstratives and quantifiers precede the noun. Several descriptive adjectives after the noun typically build outward, so the wider modifier follows the tighter noun-adjective unit.
TL: Questi moderni impianti nucleari sono sicuri.
EN: These modern nuclear plants are safe.
[PAUSE:900]
When two adjectives independently describe the noun, Italian often joins them with e. An adjective carrying its own adverbial modifier normally comes last.
TL: Cerchiamo tavole rotonde ed eleganti.
EN: We are looking for round, elegant tables.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not reproduce an English adjective stack word for word.
SHOW: determiner/quantifier → preposed adjective → noun → tighter class → wider description
Eleven clean placement choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Preposed bello and buono borrow article-like forms
REF: 3.28

Before a noun, bello patterns like the definite article: bel giorno, bello studente, bell'amico, bei giorni, and begli amici. After the noun it has ordinary adjective endings.
TL: È un bell'amico e ha dei bei progetti.
EN: He is a good friend and has some fine plans.
[PAUSE:900]
Preposed buono patterns like the indefinite article in the singular: buon pane, buon amico, but buono studente. Its plurals are regular buoni and buone.
TL: È una buona idea per un buon progetto.
EN: It is a good idea for a good project.
[PAUSE:1000]
The following sound determines the shortened form.
SHOW: bel/bei before ordinary consonant · bello/begli before special clusters · bell' before vowel · buon/buono like un/uno
Flip for grande and santo.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Gran and san are licensed pre-nominal reductions
REF: 3.28

Grande may reduce to invariant gran before a noun, especially in set expressions and names. Full grande or grandi remains widely available and is often preferred before vowels, special clusters, and in the plural.
TL: Ha una gran voglia di visitare la Grande Isola.
EN: She greatly wants to visit the Big Island.
[PAUSE:900]
Santo normally becomes san before a consonant in a saint's name and sant' before a vowel, though established names preserve lexical exceptions.
TL: Visiteremo San Giorgio e Sant'Anna.
EN: We will visit Saint George and Saint Anne.
[PAUSE:1000]
These are position-sensitive forms, not general deletions of the final vowel.
SHOW: preposed grande → grande/gran · saint title + consonant → san · + vowel → sant'

[CARD]
TITLE: Quantifiers and identifiers come before the noun
REF: 3.29

Words such as molti, pochi, troppi, altri, stessi, and ordinal numbers identify or quantify rather than simply describe. They therefore precede the noun and any ordinary descriptive adjective.
TL: Abbiamo molte belle cose da raccontare.
EN: We have many beautiful things to tell.
[PAUSE:900]
Several adjectives change function with position. Diversi libri means several books, while libri diversi means different books; una leggera differenza is slight, while una valigia leggera is light in weight.
TL: Ho trovato nuovi documenti e documenti nuovi.
EN: I found further documents and newly made documents.
[PAUSE:1000]
Position can change scope and meaning, not merely style.
SHOW: quantifier/identifier → before noun · ordinary property → usually after noun · position may select sense
Flip for evaluative and predicate uses.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Preposing can turn description into evaluation
REF: 3.30, 3.31

Some adjectives acquire an evaluative reading before the noun. Una povera donna is pitiable; una donna povera lacks money. Un grand'uomo is admirable; un uomo grande is physically large.
TL: La povera donna vive in una casa povera.
EN: The poor unfortunate woman lives in a poor house.
[PAUSE:900]
With verbs such as trovare or considerare, an adjective can follow the object or precede it. Moving the adjective before the object avoids reading it as part of the noun phrase.
TL: Trovo interessante questo libro.
EN: I find this book interesting.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use position to reveal whether the adjective identifies a noun or predicates a judgment about it.
SHOW: preposed evaluation · postposed literal contrast · verb + adjective + object → clear predicate
Twenty hygiene-gated form and position choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Many forms in -nte are ordinary adjectives
REF: 3.32

Italian has adjectives historically related to present participles. They often resemble a gerund without do: parlando corresponds to parlante, and occupando to occupante. As adjectives they end in e and take plural i.
TL: Una notizia rassicurante ha calmato gli abitanti.
EN: Reassuring news calmed the inhabitants.
[PAUSE:900]
Their meanings are lexical. Vincente can mean winning, sorridente smiling, and carente deficient; not every verb freely produces a normal adjective in this way.
TL: Le squadre vincenti salutano il pubblico sorridente.
EN: The winning teams greet the smiling audience.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn each useful form with its natural complements.
SHOW: gerund -ndo ↔ lexical adjective -nte · singular -e · plural -i
Flip when the same form is a noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Forms in -nte often name people, roles, or things
REF: 3.32, 3.33

Many nte forms function chiefly as nouns: insegnante, dirigente, conducente, rappresentante, and conoscente. Their article and agreeing words reveal gender when the ending does not.
TL: La dirigente parla con il rappresentante e con l'insegnante.
EN: The manager speaks with the representative and the teacher.
[PAUSE:900]
English readily creates expressions such as slow eater from a verb and an adverb. Italian often prefers a relative clause when no established agent noun fits naturally.
TL: Sono uno che mangia lentamente.
EN: I am someone who eats slowly.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use a lexicalized noun where Italian has one; otherwise keep the action as a verb.
SHOW: established -nte noun → use it · improvised English agent noun → prefer uno che + verb
