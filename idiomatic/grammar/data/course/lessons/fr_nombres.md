## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Build French cardinal numbers in layers
REF: 6.1, 6.1.1

French builds most compound cardinals by placing the larger units first. Between twenty-one and seventy-one, a final one is normally linked with et; eighty-one and ninety-one do not use et.
TL: Le centre a reçu soixante et une demandes, puis quatre-vingt-un dossiers complets.
EN: The centre received sixty-one applications, then eighty-one complete files.
[PAUSE:900]
The form un agrees with a following feminine noun, even inside a compound. It stays masculine when a number identifies a page, route, or numbered item.
TL: Nous avons contrôlé trente et une factures et corrigé la facture numéro un.
EN: We checked thirty-one invoices and corrected invoice number one.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: 61 demandes → soixante et une · 81 dossiers → quatre-vingt-un · numéro un
Flip for the spelling boundaries inside compound numbers.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Hyphens and decimal punctuation mark the structure
REF: 6.1.2, 6.1.4, 6.1.5

In the spelling convention used by the course source, compound numbers below one hundred take hyphens except around et. Numbers at one hundred and above separate the larger layers with spaces.
TL: Le total exact est de cent quatre-vingt-douze unités.
EN: The exact total is one hundred and ninety-two units.
[PAUSE:900]
French normally groups thousands with spaces and writes the decimal separator as a comma. Scientific values, prices, dates, and measurements are commonly written in figures.
TL: La distance mesurée est de 12 450,8 mètres.
EN: The measured distance is 12,450.8 metres.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: trente-deux · quatre-vingt-onze · cent quatre-vingt-douze · 12 450,8

[CARD]
TITLE: Quatre-vingts and cents lose s before another numeral
REF: 6.1.3

Quatre-vingts takes s when eighty ends the number or comes directly before a noun. It loses s when another numeral follows. A multiplied cent follows the same contrast.
TL: Quatre-vingts candidats ont répondu; quatre-vingt-deux ont été retenus.
EN: Eighty candidates responded; eighty-two were retained.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Le musée a reçu trois cents visiteurs et vendu trois cent dix billets.
EN: The museum received three hundred visitors and sold three hundred and ten tickets.
[PAUSE:900]
Mille is always invariable. In page numbers and similar labels, quatre-vingt and cent normally have no final s because they act like ordinal identifiers.
TL: L'exemple se trouve à la page trois cent.
EN: The example is on page three hundred.
SHOW: 80 → quatre-vingts · 82 → quatre-vingt-deux · 300 → trois cents · mille never takes s
Three clean cardinal-number drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Million and milliard are counting nouns
REF: 6.1, 6.4.7, 6.4.8

Mille connects directly to the counted noun, but million and milliard are nouns. They take plural marking and introduce what is counted with de.
TL: Le programme concerne mille élèves et deux millions de familles.
EN: The programme concerns one thousand pupils and two million families.
[PAUSE:900]
Approximate plural nouns behave the same way: des centaines de, des milliers de, and des milliards de. Keep these distinct from exact cardinals.
TL: Des milliers de personnes ont consulté les trois mille pages du dossier.
EN: Thousands of people consulted the three thousand pages of the file.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: mille personnes · deux millions de personnes · des milliers de personnes

[CARD]
TITLE: Nombre, chiffre and numéro answer different questions
REF: 6.1.6

Nombre refers to a number as an abstract value or to the amount of things in a group. Chiffre is a digit, a written figure, or a statistical figure.
TL: Le nombre de réponses augmente, mais les derniers chiffres restent provisoires.
EN: The number of responses is rising, but the latest figures remain provisional.
[PAUSE:900]
Numéro identifies one member of an ordered or labelled series: a telephone number, an address number, a route, or a numbered object.
TL: Notez le numéro du dossier en chiffres, pas en lettres.
EN: Write down the file number in figures, not in words.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: amount/concept → nombre · digit/statistic → chiffre · identifier → numéro
Eight lexical-choice drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Pronunciation changes at the edge of the numeral phrase
REF: 6.1

The final consonants of six and dix sound different when the numeral stands alone, precedes a vowel, or precedes a consonant. Huit likewise pronounces final t alone and before a vowel, but normally not before a consonant.
TL: J'en ai vu six; six équipes ont gagné; six joueurs sont partis.
EN: I saw six; six teams won; six players left.
[PAUSE:900]
Vingt normally has no final consonant sound, but t is heard before a vowel and in twenty-one through twenty-nine. Neuf has the special voiced consonant in neuf ans and neuf heures.
TL: Le projet durera vingt et un mois et commencera à neuf heures.
EN: The project will last twenty-one months and begin at nine o'clock.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: position controls the final sound · learn numeral + following word as one phrase

[CARD]
TITLE: A bare direct-object number needs en
REF: 6.1.7

When a number replaces a counted noun as the direct object, put en before the verb. The number stays in its normal position after a simple verb or past participle.
TL: Il faut quatre formulaires; j'en ai déjà rempli deux.
EN: Four forms are needed; I have already filled in two.
[PAUSE:900]
A number used as the subject does not require en. The same object pattern extends to quantifiers such as plusieurs and certains.
TL: Plusieurs sont arrivés, mais nous n'en avons encore reçu que deux.
EN: Several have arrived, but we have received only two so far.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: subject: deux suffisent · object replacement: j'en prends deux
Flip for saying calculations aloud.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Arithmetic uses a compact set of connectors
REF: 6.1.9

Addition uses et or plus, subtraction moins, multiplication fois or multiplié par, and division divisé par. The result can be linked with faire or égaler.
TL: Douze plus huit font vingt; vingt divisé par quatre égale cinq.
EN: Twelve plus eight makes twenty; twenty divided by four equals five.
[PAUSE:900]
Both singular and plural verb forms occur with a compound calculation. The important retrieval target is the complete spoken equation, including the correctly formed result.
TL: Neuf fois sept font soixante-trois.
EN: Nine times seven makes sixty-three.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: plus/et · moins · fois/multiplié par · divisé par · fait/font/égale
Sixteen clean en-and-arithmetic drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ordinals agree and preserve the cardinal spelling pattern
REF: 6.2

Premier has the feminine première. Other ordinals normally add ième to an adjusted cardinal base and mark plural with s when needed.
TL: La première équipe occupe la vingt et unième place.
EN: The first team occupies twenty-first place.
[PAUSE:900]
Watch the stem changes in cinquième, neuvième, and vingtième. In compound ordinals, only the final element carries the ordinal ending.
TL: Nous comparons les cinquième et quatre-vingt-unième éditions.
EN: We are comparing the fifth and eighty-first editions.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: premier/première · cinq → cinquième · neuf → neuvième · vingt → vingtième
Flip for fractions built from ordinals.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Fractions combine special nouns with ordinal forms
REF: 6.3.1, 6.3.2, 6.3.3

French uses moitié for the noun half, tiers for third, and quart for quarter. Most other denominator nouns come from the masculine ordinal.
TL: La moitié du budget et les deux tiers des réserves sont déjà engagés.
EN: Half the budget and two-thirds of the reserves are already committed.
[PAUSE:900]
Demi is invariable before a noun in a hyphenated compound, but after et it agrees with the preceding noun. Verb agreement follows the quantity actually referred to.
TL: Une demi-heure suffit, mais deux heures et demie seront plus confortables.
EN: Half an hour is enough, but two and a half hours will be more comfortable.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: la moitié de · un tiers · trois quarts · une demi-heure · deux heures et demie
Sixteen clean ordinal-and-fraction drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: French often uses cardinals where English uses ordinals
REF: 6.4.1, 6.4.2, 6.4.5

Dates use a cardinal after le except for the first of a month, which is premier. Names of monarchs and popes follow the same cardinal pattern after the first.
TL: Le premier juin précède le deux juin; Louis XIV se lit Louis quatorze.
EN: The first of June precedes the second of June; Louis XIV is read Louis the Fourteenth.
[PAUSE:900]
Pages, platforms, buses, and other numbered entities also take a following cardinal. The noun normally keeps its definite article.
TL: Prenez le bus trente-deux et descendez au quai vingt.
EN: Take bus thirty-two and get off at platform twenty.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: le premier mai · le deux mai · Louis quatorze · la page un
Flip for adjective order and addresses.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Numbers precede adjective groups and address suffixes are lexical
REF: 6.4.4, 6.4.6

When English places a cardinal after an ordering adjective, French normally places the number first: the last five pages becomes les cinq dernières pages.
TL: Les trois premiers candidats ont lu les dix derniers chapitres.
EN: The first three candidates read the last ten chapters.
[PAUSE:900]
French addresses use bis, ter, and quater for letter-like extensions. Street-type nouns such as rue and boulevard normally begin with lower case in the address line.
TL: Le bureau se trouve au 18bis, rue des Écoles.
EN: The office is at 18A, rue des Écoles.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: les quatre autres options · 12bis · 12ter · 12quater
Sixteen clean names, addresses, and large-quantity drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Measurements choose among three grammatical frames
REF: 6.5.1

With être, put de before the measure after an adjective, or use a measurement noun followed by est de.
TL: La passerelle est longue de trente mètres; sa largeur est de deux mètres.
EN: The footbridge is thirty metres long; its width is two metres.
[PAUSE:900]
With faire or avoir, long, haut, and large can follow the measure after de and remain invariable. For depth and thickness, use the nouns profondeur and épaisseur in this frame.
TL: Le bassin fait dix mètres de long et trois mètres de profondeur.
EN: The pool is ten metres long and three metres deep.
[PAUSE:900]
People commonly take mesurer or faire for height, while dimensions can use mesurer or faire plus X sur Y.
SHOW: est long de X · longueur est de X · fait X de long · fait X de profondeur
Flip for comparisons around a benchmark.

[SIDE]
TITLE: De marks a threshold; que compares two terms
REF: 6.5.2

Use plus de or moins de before a numerical benchmark. Use plus que or moins que when one person, object, or activity is compared with another.
TL: Elle gagne plus de trois mille euros, mais moins que sa collègue.
EN: She earns more than three thousand euros, but less than her colleague.
[PAUSE:900]
A difference between measurements can also appear with de, or with avoir plus or moins followed by que.
TL: Cette étagère est plus large de cinq centimètres et a deux niveaux de plus que l'autre.
EN: This shelf is five centimetres wider and has two more levels than the other one.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: threshold: plus de 3 000 · comparison: plus que Paul · difference: de cinq centimètres
Thirteen clean measurement-and-comparison drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Numeral nouns present a quantity as approximate
REF: 6.5.3

Une dizaine, une vingtaine, une centaine, and related forms mean roughly the named quantity and introduce the counted noun with de. Une douzaine, by contrast, normally means exactly twelve.
TL: Une vingtaine de personnes attendaient avec une douzaine de dossiers.
EN: About twenty people were waiting with twelve files.
[PAUSE:900]
Other frames include environ, à peu près, près de, vers, and dans les. With age, ans must remain present.
TL: Elle a dans les quarante ans et dirige environ trois cents salariés.
EN: She is around forty and manages about three hundred employees.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: une trentaine de · environ 300 · dans les 300 · vers onze heures · quarante ans et quelques
Two clean approximation drills follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Exact large quantities and approximate groups are not interchangeable
REF: 6.4.7, 6.5.3

An exact cardinal directly states a count. A numeral noun packages the same scale as an approximate group and therefore requires de before the following noun.
TL: Cent personnes étaient inscrites, mais une centaine de personnes se sont présentées.
EN: One hundred people were registered, but around a hundred people turned up.
[PAUSE:900]
The same contrast separates mille participants from des milliers de participants. Decide first whether the speaker is counting or estimating.
TL: Le rapport recense deux mille cas parmi des milliers de signalements.
EN: The report records two thousand cases among thousands of reports.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: exact count → cardinal + noun · approximate group → numeral noun + de

[CARD]
TITLE: Dates and recurring days use different determiners
REF: 6.4.1, 6.6.1, 6.6.2

A calendar date begins with le and uses lower-case names for days and months. Le does not elide before huit or onze.
TL: La réunion aura lieu le lundi 8 septembre.
EN: The meeting will take place on Monday, 8 September.
[PAUSE:900]
A bare weekday normally points to one particular day. The definite article normally turns it into a recurring day.
TL: Je viendrai lundi, mais le bureau est fermé le mardi.
EN: I will come on Monday, but the office is closed on Tuesdays.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: date: le lundi 8 septembre · one Monday: lundi · every Tuesday: le mardi
Flip for years and clock time.

[SIDE]
TITLE: French reads years and clock time as complete numeral phrases
REF: 6.6.3, 6.7

Years in dates retain cent when read in the traditional hundred-based pattern, or use a full mille-based cardinal. Decades use années.
TL: Le bâtiment date de mille neuf cent quarante-cinq et a été rénové dans les années quatre-vingt.
EN: The building dates from 1945 and was renovated in the 1980s.
[PAUSE:900]
Clock time uses il est and retains heures. Et quart and et demie express time past the hour; moins le quart expresses a quarter to.
TL: Il est onze heures et demie; le train partira à midi moins le quart.
EN: It is half past eleven; the train will leave at a quarter to twelve.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: il est deux heures vingt · onze heures et demie · midi moins le quart
Two clean date-and-day drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: A quantifier determines the shape of its complement
REF: 6.9.1, 6.9.3

Quantifiers such as beaucoup, assez, peu, and trop normally take de before an indefinite noun. Do not restore an indefinite or partitive article after them.
TL: Beaucoup de clients demandent peu d'informations supplémentaires.
EN: Many clients ask for little additional information.
[PAUSE:900]
The definite article remains when the meaning is a subset of a known group. Other quantifiers, including la plupart, une partie, and le reste, normally select a contracted definite complement.
TL: Beaucoup des clients interrogés ont lu la plupart du rapport.
EN: Many of the clients interviewed read most of the report.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: beaucoup de clients · beaucoup des clients interrogés · la plupart du rapport
Flip for pronoun replacement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: En, d'entre and stressed pronouns prevent a missing complement
REF: 6.9.2, 6.9.4

When a quantifier stands alone as a direct object, insert en before the verb. Quelques becomes quelques-uns or quelques-unes when it is used without its noun.
TL: Plusieurs dossiers sont prêts; j'en ai vérifié quelques-uns.
EN: Several files are ready; I checked a few of them.
[PAUSE:900]
Before a stressed personal pronoun, quantifiers commonly use d'entre. Chacun de is also possible in its established frame.
TL: Beaucoup d'entre nous participeront, et chacun de vous recevra une réponse.
EN: Many of us will take part, and each of you will receive an answer.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: j'en ai plusieurs · quelques-uns · beaucoup d'entre eux · chacun de nous
Four clean quantifier drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Tout and chaque change when the noun disappears
REF: 6.9.5

Tous and toutes agree with the noun or group they quantify. With a direct-object pronoun, the quantifier normally appears around the conjugated verb or past participle.
TL: Toutes les pièces sont prêtes; je les ai toutes vérifiées.
EN: All the parts are ready; I checked them all.
[PAUSE:900]
Chaque must accompany a singular noun. When the noun is omitted, use the agreeing pronoun chacun or chacune.
TL: Chaque candidate a reçu un dossier; chacune doit le signer.
EN: Each candidate received a file; each one must sign it.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: tous/toutes + group · chaque + noun · chacun/chacune stands alone
Flip for agreement with quantified subjects.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Subject agreement follows the type of quantity
REF: 6.3.3, 6.9.6

With beaucoup de, peu de, la plupart de, and similar quantifiers, the verb normally follows the number of the complement noun.
TL: La plupart des demandes sont complètes, mais peu de réponses sont définitives.
EN: Most applications are complete, but few answers are final.
[PAUSE:900]
Collective expressions such as la majorité de, une partie de, and numeral nouns can support either agreement with the collective or with the following plural, according to how the quantity is construed. Fractions likewise follow the singular or plural entity meant.
TL: La moitié de l'année est passée, mais la moitié des candidats sont encore présents.
EN: Half the year has passed, but half the candidates are still present.
[PAUSE:900]
SHOW: identify the quantified entity → choose singular or plural agreement
