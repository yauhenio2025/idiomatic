## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Que links an antecedent to a defining clause
REF: 10, 10.1.1

A relative clause adds information about an earlier noun, its antecedent. Que is the broad default: it can point back to a person or a thing and can be the subject or object inside the relative clause.
TL: A pesquisadora que ligou deixou uma mensagem.
EN: The researcher who called left a message.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: O relatório que você enviou chegou ontem.
EN: The report that you sent arrived yesterday.
[PAUSE:950]
English can omit an object relative, but Portuguese keeps que. Identify the missing role inside the relative clause even when English says nothing there.
SHOW: person or thing · subject or object -> que · never omit the relative
Flip for the preposition that may stand before que.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A simple preposition comes before que
REF: 10.1.2

If the verb or expression inside the relative clause requires a, com, de, or em, put that preposition directly before que.
TL: Esta é a proposta de que falamos.
EN: This is the proposal that we talked about.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Conheci a colega com que você trabalha.
EN: I met the colleague you work with.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not strand the preposition at the end as English often does, and do not drop que. Recover the governing expression first: falar de, trabalhar com, acreditar em, acostumar-se a.
SHOW: falar de -> de que · trabalhar com -> com que · acostumar-se a -> a que
The first source block follows after quem and o que complete the core contrast.

[CARD]
TITLE: Quem follows a preposition when the antecedent is a person
REF: 10.2.1

After a simple preposition, quem can replace que when the antecedent is a person. The form does not change for gender or number.
TL: A médica com quem conversei explicou o resultado.
EN: The doctor I spoke with explained the result.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Os vizinhos para quem enviamos o aviso já responderam.
EN: The neighbors to whom we sent the notice have already replied.
[PAUSE:950]
The preposition still comes first. Use quem here because the antecedent denotes people; use que or an agreeing form for things.
SHOW: person + a/com/de/em/para/por -> preposition + quem · form stays quem
Flip for quem without an expressed antecedent.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quem can mean the person or anyone who
REF: 10.2.2, 10.2.3

Quem can supply its own human antecedent. In that construction it means the person who, anyone who, or those who rather than referring back to an overt noun.
TL: Quem chegar primeiro pode abrir a sala.
EN: Whoever arrives first may open the room.
[PAUSE:850]
It also supports a focused pattern in which the person is identified after the relative clause.
TL: Quem encontrou a chave foi a Lúcia.
EN: It was Lúcia who found the key.
[PAUSE:950]
Distinguish this fused use from preposition plus quem: one creates the human referent, while the other resumes a person already named.
SHOW: no antecedent -> quem = whoever/the one who · quem + clause + foi X -> focus on X

[CARD]
TITLE: O que can follow tudo or nada
REF: 10.3.1

After broad words such as tudo and nada, o que can introduce the relative clause. Que alone is also common in the same position.
TL: Anotei tudo o que precisamos revisar.
EN: I wrote down everything that we need to review.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Não aceitei nada que contrariasse o acordo.
EN: I accepted nothing that conflicted with the agreement.
[PAUSE:950]
Here tudo or nada is the antecedent. The optional o belongs to the relative expression; it is not an agreeing article chosen from a noun's gender.
SHOW: tudo/nada + (o) que · both forms are used
Flip for o que when no noun supplies the antecedent.

[SIDE]
TITLE: O que can point to an idea or mean what
REF: 10.3.2, 10.3.3

After a complete statement, o que can refer back to the whole preceding idea rather than to one noun.
TL: A reunião terminou cedo, o que surpreendeu a equipe.
EN: The meeting ended early, which surprised the team.
[PAUSE:850]
Without an expressed antecedent, o que means what or the thing that. A required preposition goes before the whole expression.
TL: Não entendi o que mudou. Você sabe do que precisamos?
EN: I did not understand what changed. Do you know what we need?
[PAUSE:950]
Ask whether the relative resumes a noun, an entire statement, or no stated antecedent at all.
SHOW: whole idea -> , o que · no antecedent -> o que = what · de + o que -> do que
The first source block now contrasts que, quem, and o que.

[CARD]
TITLE: O qual makes agreement and reference visible
REF: 10.4.1

O qual changes to a qual, os quais, or as quais according to the gender and number of its antecedent. This visible agreement can remove ambiguity when two possible antecedents are nearby.
TL: Encontrei o contrato e a carta, a qual estava sem assinatura.
EN: I found the contract and the letter, which was unsigned.
[PAUSE:850]
It is also useful when the relative does not immediately follow the noun it resumes.
TL: O prazo foi prorrogado por duas semanas, as quais serão decisivas.
EN: The deadline was extended by two weeks, which will be decisive.
[PAUSE:950]
Choose the form from the antecedent, not from a noun inside the relative clause.
SHOW: M SG o qual · F SG a qual · M PL os quais · F PL as quais
Flip for prepositions that favor this explicit form.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Compound prepositions select o qual
REF: 10.4.2, 10.4.3

After a compound preposition, use an agreeing qual form. This keeps the full prepositional relation visible.
TL: A regra segundo a qual trabalhamos mudou.
EN: The rule according to which we work changed.
[PAUSE:850]
The same family can follow simple prepositions. Contract a or de with its article where required.
TL: Estas são as normas às quais o projeto obedece.
EN: These are the rules that the project follows.
[PAUSE:950]
TL: A empresa da qual ela é diretora abriu uma filial.
EN: The company of which she is a director opened a branch.
SHOW: compound preposition + qual form · a + as quais -> às quais · de + a qual -> da qual

[CARD]
TITLE: Cujo agrees with the possession that follows
REF: 10.5.1

Cujo expresses whose or of which and behaves like an adjective. Its ending agrees with the noun immediately after it, the thing possessed, not with the antecedent or owner.
TL: Conhecemos a autora cujo romance recebeu o prêmio.
EN: We met the author whose novel received the prize.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Visitei o arquivo cujas janelas foram restauradas.
EN: I visited the archive whose windows were restored.
[PAUSE:950]
Read the relation as the author's novel or the archive's windows, then agree cujo with romance or janelas. Do not insert an article between cujo and that noun.
SHOW: cujo + M SG possession · cuja + F SG · cujos/cujas + plural · no following article
Flip for the spoken alternative.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Cujo belongs mainly to written style
REF: 10.5.2

Cujo is characteristic of writing and careful formal speech. Everyday Brazilian Portuguese normally avoids it by reshaping the sentence or by using que with a possessive expression inside the clause.
TL: A professora cuja pesquisa venceu o concurso...
EN: The professor whose research won the competition... Written style.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: A professora que teve a pesquisa dela premiada...
EN: The professor whose research received an award... Conversational reshaping.
[PAUSE:950]
The spoken construction does not make cujo wrong; it marks a different register. Preserve the clear ownership relation whichever structure you choose.
SHOW: writing -> cujo + possession · conversation -> que + explicit possessive or rephrasing
The next source block tests o qual and cujo together.

[CARD]
TITLE: Quanto mirrors tanto in amount and agreement
REF: 10.6.1

Relative quanto follows tanto to express as much as. In countable plural phrases, both words can agree with the noun.
TL: Recebemos tanto apoio quanto esperávamos.
EN: We received as much support as we expected.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Faremos tantas verificações quantas forem necessárias.
EN: We will make as many checks as are necessary.
[PAUSE:950]
The singular amount pattern is tanto quanto. With plural count nouns, copy gender and number across tantas quantas or tantos quantos.
SHOW: tanto quanto · tantos quantos · tantas quantas
Flip for fixed-looking expressions built with quanto.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Tudo quanto and todos quantos broaden the reference
REF: 10.6.2

Tudo quanto can mean everything that or, in a common emphatic pattern, every kind of. Todos quantos refers formally to all those who.
TL: Ela consultou tudo quanto era documento antigo.
EN: She consulted every kind of old document.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: O serviço atende todos quantos precisam de orientação.
EN: The service assists all those who need guidance.
[PAUSE:950]
These forms are less neutral in conversation than ordinary tudo o que and todos os que, but recognizing them prevents quanto from being mistaken for a question word.
SHOW: tudo quanto = everything that/every kind of · todos quantos = all those who

[CARD]
TITLE: Onde resumes a place or source location
REF: 10.7.1

Onde means where and points back to a place. Use it for location; add de when the relative clause expresses movement or origin from that place.
TL: Este é o bairro onde trabalho.
EN: This is the neighborhood where I work.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Voltamos à cidade de onde o trem havia partido.
EN: We returned to the city from which the train had departed.
[PAUSE:950]
The larger relation decides the preposition: estar or trabalhar locates, while sair or partir creates de onde.
SHOW: location -> onde · origin -> de onde
Flip for movement toward a destination.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Aonde and para onde express destination
REF: 10.7.2

When the clause describes movement to a place, careful usage distinguishes aonde or para onde from stationary onde.
TL: O prédio aonde fomos fica perto da estação.
EN: The building we went to is near the station.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Não conheço a cidade para onde eles vão se mudar.
EN: I do not know the city they are going to move to.
[PAUSE:950]
Conversation often uses onde more broadly, even for destination. In writing, keep the relation explicit: onde for location, de onde for origin, and aonde or para onde for destination.
SHOW: stay/work -> onde · leave/from -> de onde · go/to -> aonde/para onde
The translation source block follows.

[CARD]
TITLE: Relative when becomes em que
REF: 10.8

When English when links a clause to a time noun, Portuguese normally uses em que rather than quando.
TL: Lembro do dia em que recebemos a notícia.
EN: I remember the day when we received the news.
[PAUSE:850]
TL: Na época em que morávamos no interior, viajávamos pouco.
EN: At the time when we lived in the countryside, we traveled little.
[PAUSE:950]
The antecedent is a named time such as dia, momento, or época, and em que resumes it inside the relative clause.
SHOW: time noun + when-clause -> tempo + em que
Flip for a compact choice sequence across the whole unit.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Let antecedent, preposition, and register choose the form
REF: 10.1, 10.2, 10.3, 10.4, 10.5, 10.7, 10.8

Start with the antecedent. For a named person or thing, que is the default. A person after a preposition can take quem; a place takes onde; and a time noun followed by relative when takes em que.
[PAUSE:850]
If there is no noun antecedent, quem can mean whoever and o que can mean what. If agreement must disambiguate the antecedent or a compound preposition leads the clause, choose an agreeing qual form.
[PAUSE:850]
For possession, use written cujo and agree it with the possessed noun that follows. Then check every governing preposition before finalizing the clause.
TL: A pessoa com quem falei mostrou o arquivo no qual está o documento de que precisamos.
EN: The person I spoke with showed the archive in which the document we need is kept.
SHOW: antecedent -> role/preposition -> register -> relative form -> agreement
