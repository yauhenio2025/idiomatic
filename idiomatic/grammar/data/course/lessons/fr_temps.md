## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Start with a viewpoint, not an English tense name
REF: 10.1, 10.6

French tense choice locates an event relative to a viewpoint. First decide whether that viewpoint is present, past, or future; then ask whether the event overlaps it, precedes it, or follows it.
TL: Aujourd'hui, Nora prépare le dossier; hier, elle a vérifié les chiffres.
EN: Today Nora is preparing the file; yesterday she checked the figures.
[PAUSE:900]
The present overlaps now. The compound past places a completed check before now. The time expressions support the forms, but the relationship between the events does the real work.
SHOW: choose the viewpoint → order the events → choose the tense
Flip for the compound forms that look backward from another point.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A compound tense marks an event as already complete
REF: 10.5.1, 10.5.4, 10.5.5

A compound form places completion before its reference point. The pluperfect looks back from a past point, the future perfect from a future point, and the conditional perfect from a hypothetical or reported point.
TL: Elle avait envoyé le dossier quand la réunion a commencé.
EN: She had sent the file when the meeting began.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Demain à midi, elle aura reçu la réponse.
EN: By noon tomorrow, she will have received the answer.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle aurait accepté si les conditions avaient changé.
EN: She would have accepted if the conditions had changed.
SHOW: avait envoyé → before past · aura reçu → before future · aurait accepté → unreal or reported completion

[CARD]
TITLE: One present form serves several time readings
REF: 10.2

The French present can describe what is true now, a timeless fact, or a repeated habit. Context supplies the reading; the verb does not change form.
TL: Le musée ferme à dix-huit heures; aujourd'hui, la guide ferme déjà les volets.
EN: The museum closes at six; today the guide is already closing the shutters.
[PAUSE:900]
The schedule gives a habitual reading, while aujourd'hui and déjà make the second action current. A future time phrase can also let the present refer forward.
TL: Demain, nous partons avant l'aube.
EN: Tomorrow we are leaving before dawn.
SHOW: present form + context → current fact · general truth · habit · planned future
Flip for ongoing and historical readings.

[SIDE]
TITLE: French usually needs no progressive form
REF: 10.2.1

A simple present normally covers both English writes and is writing. Use être en train de only when the unfolding process or its duration needs special emphasis.
TL: Je rédige le compte rendu; je suis en train d'en corriger la conclusion.
EN: I am writing the report; I am in the middle of correcting its conclusion.
[PAUSE:900]
Writers can also use a present against a clear past setting to make history or narration feel immediate.
TL: En 1912, l'expédition atteint enfin le pôle.
EN: In 1912, the expedition finally reaches the pole.
SHOW: neutral ongoing action → present · foregrounded process → être en train de · vivid past → historical present

[CARD]
TITLE: The imperfect opens a past scene
REF: 10.3.1

Use the imperfect for a state or action viewed as ongoing at a past reference point. It supplies setting, circumstances, and background without presenting an endpoint.
TL: Il faisait nuit, la pluie tombait et les voyageurs attendaient sous l'auvent.
EN: It was night, rain was falling, and the travellers were waiting under the awning.
[PAUSE:900]
The same form describes a past habit. If English would can be replaced by used to, an imperfect reading is likely.
TL: Chaque vendredi, nous dînions chez nos voisins.
EN: Every Friday, we used to have dinner at our neighbours' home.
SHOW: background in progress · state · repeated past habit → imperfect
Flip for the bounded event that advances the story.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A completed event enters the imperfect background
REF: 10.3.1, 10.3.3

Against an imperfect background, a compound-past event is viewed as a whole and moves a spoken or personal narrative forward.
TL: Nous attendions depuis une heure quand le dernier train est arrivé.
EN: We had been waiting for an hour when the last train arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
Attendre is open around the reference point; arriver reaches its boundary. Do not choose by verb meaning alone: the speaker's chosen view can present the same activity as ongoing or completed.
TL: Je lisais quand Léa a appelé; ensuite, j'ai terminé le chapitre.
EN: I was reading when Léa called; afterwards, I finished the chapter.
SHOW: open background → imperfect · bounded event → compound past

[CARD]
TITLE: The compound past is the ordinary completed past
REF: 10.3.3, 10.3.4

The compound past reports a completed event in both speech and writing. It is also natural when the past result matters to the speaker's present situation.
TL: J'ai perdu mes clés, alors je ne peux pas ouvrir le bureau.
EN: I have lost my keys, so I cannot open the office.
[PAUSE:900]
It is not limited to English present-perfect contexts. With an explicit past-time phrase, it can translate an ordinary English past.
TL: Nous avons signé le contrat mardi dernier.
EN: We signed the contract last Tuesday.
SHOW: completed past · normal in speech · may retain present relevance → passé composé
Flip for the literary simple past.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The simple past belongs mainly to written narration
REF: 10.3.2, 10.3.4, 10.3.5

The simple past presents completed, foregrounded events in formal written narrative. Modern conversation normally uses the compound past instead.
TL: Le messager entra, posa la lettre sur la table et repartit aussitôt.
EN: The messenger entered, put the letter on the table, and left at once.
[PAUSE:900]
An English simple past does not decide the French form by itself. A past habit or background calls for the imperfect; a completed spoken event calls for the compound past; a literary event may take the simple past.
SHOW: English past → inspect event shape and register → imparfait / passé composé / passé simple

[CARD]
TITLE: With depuis, continuing time stays non-compound
REF: 10.4.4

When a situation began earlier and still holds at the viewpoint, French uses the present for a viewpoint now and the imperfect for a viewpoint in the past. English often uses a perfect form instead.
TL: Elle travaille ici depuis trois ans.
EN: She has been working here for three years.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle travaillait ici depuis trois ans quand le service a fermé.
EN: She had been working here for three years when the department closed.
SHOW: still true now → present + depuis · still true then → imperfect + depuis
Flip when the event itself is complete.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Separate since, ago, duration, and intended stay
REF: 10.4.4

A completed event with depuis takes an appropriate past form. Il y a counts backward from now and means ago. Pendant measures an event's duration, while pour commonly announces an intended future stay with movement verbs.
TL: Je ne l'ai pas revue depuis juin; elle est partie il y a six mois.
EN: I have not seen her since June; she left six months ago.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nous avons travaillé pendant deux heures; il part pour une semaine.
EN: We worked for two hours; he is leaving for a week.
SHOW: continuing interval → depuis · past distance → il y a · measured duration → pendant · intended trip → pour

[CARD]
TITLE: Future time clauses use future forms in French
REF: 10.4.3, 17.3.2

English often uses a present after when, as soon as, once, or after even though the event lies in the future. French normally marks that future relation in clauses introduced by quand, lorsque, dès que, après que, and similar conjunctions.
TL: Je te préviendrai quand le directeur arrivera.
EN: I will let you know when the director arrives.
[PAUSE:900]
The main clause is a useful diagnostic: if its event is future, the linked time clause usually needs a future form too.
SHOW: future main event + future time clause → future in both French clauses
Flip when one future event must be complete first.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use the future perfect for the earlier future event
REF: 10.4.3, 10.5.4

If the subordinate event will be finished before the main future event, use the future perfect. English may hide this relationship behind a present perfect.
TL: Dès que nous aurons validé le budget, nous lancerons le projet.
EN: As soon as we have approved the budget, we will launch the project.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not apply this mechanically to clauses that actually refer to the past. Locate both events first, then choose the simple or compound form that expresses their order.
TL: Il est parti quand la réunion a fini.
EN: He left when the meeting ended.
SHOW: earlier future completion → futur antérieur · genuinely past relation → past tense

[CARD]
TITLE: The future can add more than future time
REF: 10.4.1

The future normally places an event ahead of now, but it can also soften an instruction or present a cautious inference about a present situation.
TL: Vous déposerez le formulaire à l'accueil, s'il vous plaît.
EN: Please leave the form at reception.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Paul sera encore au laboratoire.
EN: Paul may still be at the laboratory.
[PAUSE:800]
For a planned or visibly imminent event, aller plus infinitive presents it as already developing; the simple future views it more neutrally from now.
TL: Regarde ces nuages: il va pleuvoir; demain, il pleuvra peut-être.
EN: Look at those clouds: it is going to rain; tomorrow, it may rain.
SHOW: future fact · softened direction · inference · imminent aller + infinitive
Flip for the conditional's distinct jobs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The conditional marks distance from actuality
REF: 10.4.2

The conditional can present a result dependent on a condition, a future viewed from a past point, an allegation the speaker does not endorse, or a polite request.
TL: Avec plus de temps, nous vérifierions chaque dossier.
EN: With more time, we would check every file.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Elle a promis qu'elle reviendrait; selon la presse, elle serait déjà en route.
EN: She promised that she would return; according to the press, she is reportedly already on her way.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Je voudrais reporter le rendez-vous.
EN: I would like to postpone the appointment.
SHOW: condition · future-in-the-past · reported allegation · politeness → conditional

[CARD]
TITLE: The pluperfect marks an earlier past
REF: 10.5.1, 10.6.2

When one past event was already complete before another past event, French normally uses the pluperfect for the earlier one. English sometimes leaves that ordering implicit, but French commonly makes it explicit.
TL: Quand nous sommes arrivés, le technicien avait déjà réparé la machine.
EN: When we arrived, the technician had already repaired the machine.
[PAUSE:900]
The pluperfect does not mean remote past. It means before the selected past reference point, even if only a few minutes separate the events.
SHOW: event A complete → later past event B → A in pluperfect
Flip for immediate succession in literary and spoken styles.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Immediate succession has rare marked forms
REF: 10.5.2, 10.5.3

In a simple-past literary narrative, the past anterior can mark an event completed immediately before the next event. Spoken French can use a double compound past in a similar slot, but ordinary compound past and pluperfect forms are much more usual.
TL: Dès qu'elle eut signé, le greffier emporta le document.
EN: As soon as she had signed, the clerk took away the document.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Quand j'ai eu terminé, tout le monde a applaudi.
EN: When I had finished, everyone applauded.
[PAUSE:800]
Recognize the register before producing the marked form. The safe everyday baseline remains an ordinary past sequence.
SHOW: literary passé simple frame → passé antérieur · marked spoken succession → passé surcomposé

[CARD]
TITLE: The future perfect looks back from the future
REF: 10.5.4, 10.6.3

Use the future perfect when an event will already be complete at a future checkpoint or before another future event.
TL: À dix heures, nous aurons publié les résultats.
EN: By ten o'clock, we will have published the results.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle appellera après qu'elle aura lu le rapport.
EN: She will call after she has read the report.
[PAUSE:800]
The same form can present an inference about a completed past event when the speaker lacks direct confirmation.
TL: Il aura oublié notre rendez-vous.
EN: He may have forgotten our appointment.
SHOW: completed before future checkpoint · or inferred past → futur antérieur
Flip for its conditional counterpart.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The conditional perfect keeps completion at a distance
REF: 10.5.5

The conditional perfect presents an unreal past result, a future-perfect event reported from a past viewpoint, or an alleged completed event.
TL: Nous serions partis plus tôt si le train n'avait pas été annulé.
EN: We would have left earlier if the train had not been cancelled.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Elle disait qu'elle aurait fini avant midi.
EN: She said that she would have finished before noon.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Le ministre aurait remis sa démission.
EN: The minister has reportedly submitted his resignation.
SHOW: unreal result · reported future completion · unconfirmed past report → conditionnel passé

[CARD]
TITLE: A past reporting verb shifts three tense families
REF: 10.7

When a past-tense verb introduces reported speech or thought, a present normally shifts to the imperfect, a future to the conditional, and a completed past to the pluperfect.
TL: « Je travaille. » → Elle a dit qu'elle travaillait.
EN: “I am working.” → She said that she was working.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: « Je viendrai. » → Elle a dit qu'elle viendrait.
EN: “I will come.” → She said that she would come.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: « J'ai fini. » → Elle a dit qu'elle avait fini.
EN: “I have finished.” → She said that she had finished.
SHOW: present → imperfect · future → conditional · compound/simple past → pluperfect
Flip for forms that usually remain stable.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Keep stable tenses stable and move the time anchor
REF: 10.7

An imperfect, pluperfect, or conditional normally stays in the same tense after a past reporting verb. With a non-past reporting verb, the original tense normally remains unchanged.
TL: Elle explique qu'elle viendra demain.
EN: She explains that she will come tomorrow.
[PAUSE:800]
When the report moves into a past frame, time words may move too: demain can become le lendemain and hier can become la veille.
TL: Elle a dit: « Je viendrai demain. » → Elle a dit qu'elle viendrait le lendemain.
EN: She said, “I will come tomorrow.” → She said that she would come the following day.
SHOW: shift the tense only when the reporting frame requires it · relocate time expressions with the viewpoint

[CARD]
TITLE: First decide which si you have
REF: 10.8, 17.3.6

Si can introduce an indirect yes-or-no question, where it means whether. In that use, its tense follows the ordinary reported-speech sequence.
TL: Je ne sais pas s'il viendra; je me demandais s'il viendrait.
EN: I do not know whether he will come; I wondered whether he would come.
[PAUSE:900]
Test whether whether is possible in English. If it is, si is interrogative and a future or conditional after si may be exactly right.
SHOW: si = whether → reported-question tense rules
Flip for a genuine condition.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A hypothetical si clause rejects future and conditional forms
REF: 10.8, 17.3.6

When si means on condition that, its own clause does not take a future, future perfect, conditional, or conditional perfect. Use a present with a present or future result, an imperfect with a conditional result, and a pluperfect with a conditional-perfect result.
TL: Si tu viens, nous commencerons.
EN: If you come, we will begin.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Si tu venais, nous commencerions.
EN: If you came, we would begin.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Si tu étais venu, nous aurions commencé.
EN: If you had come, we would have begun.
SHOW: si + present → future · si + imperfect → conditional · si + pluperfect → conditional perfect
