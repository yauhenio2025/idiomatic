## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose the relationship before the preposition
REF: 11.1, 11.5, 11.13, 11.18, 11.32

Italian prepositions encode relationships such as location, direction, source, recipient, cause, manner, and time. An English preposition is therefore a clue to the relationship, not a word to replace mechanically.
TL: La commissione ha rinviato la decisione per mancanza di dati, non per prudenza.
EN: The committee postponed the decision for lack of data, not out of caution.
[PAUSE:900]
A, di, da, in, su, and per each cover several relationships. Read the noun phrase and the verb together before choosing one.
TL: Ho scritto al direttore del museo per chiedere informazioni sulla mostra.
EN: I wrote to the museum director to ask for information about the exhibition.
SHOW: identify the relation -> choose the Italian construction · do not translate the English preposition in isolation
Flip for the contrast built into many simple and compound forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A small change in the complement can change the relation
REF: 11.1, 11.29

Simple prepositions may govern a noun phrase directly, while compound prepositions often end in a fixed a, di, da, or con. Learn that final link as part of the construction.
TL: Le biciclette sono fuori del garage; portale fuori dal cortile.
EN: The bicycles are outside the garage; take them out of the courtyard.
[PAUSE:900]
The first clause describes position with fuori di. The second describes motion out with fuori da. Oltre and oltre a make a similar lexical contrast.
TL: Oltre il ponte comincia il sentiero; oltre al sentiero, la mappa indica un rifugio.
EN: Beyond the bridge the path begins; in addition to the path, the map shows a refuge.
SHOW: location outside -> fuori di · motion out -> fuori da · beyond -> oltre · in addition to -> oltre a

[CARD]
TITLE: Learn a compound preposition with its final link
REF: 11.1

Forms such as davanti a, accanto a, per via di, in mezzo a, and riguardo a are units. The following article contracts with that final preposition where ordinary Italian requires it.
TL: Il laboratorio si trova di fronte alla biblioteca, in mezzo a due cortili.
EN: The laboratory is opposite the library, between two courtyards.
[PAUSE:900]
A spatial word can be direct before a noun in one meaning and take a before another. Store the contrast, not just the first word.
TL: Il tecnico è rimasto dietro la porta; poi è corso dietro al responsabile.
EN: The technician stayed behind the door; then he ran after the manager.
SHOW: compound preposition -> fixed final link + complement · stationary behind -> dietro + noun · following -> dietro a + noun

[SIDE]
TITLE: Personal pronouns may require a linking di
REF: 11.1

Several prepositions take di before an independent personal pronoun: su di me, contro di loro, verso di lei. With dopo, senza, and tra or fra, di is often optional.
TL: Il ricorso contro di noi è già stato depositato.
EN: The appeal against us has already been filed.
[PAUSE:900]
The optional di normally disappears when the pronoun is modified or coordinated with another noun phrase.
TL: Marco è arrivato dopo di me, ma ripartirà senza te e Giulia.
EN: Marco arrived after me, but he will leave again without you and Giulia.
SHOW: su/contro/verso + personal pronoun -> di · dopo/senza/tra + pronoun -> di often optional · modified or coordinated pronoun -> usually no di

[CARD]
TITLE: Italian normally places the preposition before its complement
REF: 11.2

English can leave a preposition at the end of a question or relative clause. Italian normally moves it before chi, che cosa, or the relative form cui.
TL: Con chi hai discusso la proposta? È la collega con cui lavoro.
EN: Who did you discuss the proposal with? She is the colleague I work with.
[PAUSE:900]
Keep the preposition even when English makes it easy to overlook.
TL: Di quale norma si occupa il rapporto che stai leggendo?
EN: Which rule is the report you are reading concerned with?
SHOW: question -> preposition + chi/che cosa · relative -> preposition + cui · no ordinary right-edge stranding

[SIDE]
TITLE: English in it often becomes no Italian pronoun at all
REF: 11.2, 11.4

In descriptions such as a folder with documents in it, Italian normally omits the redundant pronoun. A locative word can stand immediately after con or after the noun it locates.
TL: Ho scelto una cartella con dentro tutti i documenti.
EN: I chose a folder with all the documents in it.
[PAUSE:900]
Locative adverbs such as davanti, dietro, dentro, and sotto can also remain at the end when the reference point is already established.
TL: C'è una terrazza con il giardino sotto e le montagne davanti.
EN: There is a terrace with the garden below it and the mountains in front of it.
SHOW: English with noun + in/on it -> omit redundant pronoun · con + locative + noun / con + noun + locative · established reference point -> locative may stand alone

[CARD]
TITLE: Di is Italian's main noun-to-noun linker
REF: 11.5, 11.23

Di can mark possession, origin, subject matter, content, material, quantity, and many lexical complements. It often corresponds to an English noun used attributively.
TL: La responsabile del progetto ha ordinato una scatola di cavi e un tavolo di noce.
EN: The project manager ordered a box of cables and a walnut table.
[PAUSE:900]
The same link covers smell, taste, and composition. Let the head noun determine the relation.
TL: Il profumo di agrumi copriva l'odore di vernice.
EN: The scent of citrus covered the smell of paint.
SHOW: owner/origin/topic/content/material/quantity -> noun + di + complement

[SIDE]
TITLE: About has several Italian frames; a, di, and da classify differently
REF: 11.26, 11.29

Su and a proposito di introduce a topic. Nei confronti di describes an attitude or relation toward a person; riguardo a is a broad concerning frame.
TL: Abbiamo discusso del bilancio e, a proposito della scadenza, ho scritto ai fornitori.
EN: We discussed the budget and, regarding the deadline, I wrote to the suppliers.
[PAUSE:900]
Di describes content or material, da describes intended use, and a can describe pattern or style.
TL: Ho comprato un vaso di vetro, un vaso da fiori e un piatto a righe.
EN: I bought a glass vase, a flower vase, and a striped plate.
SHOW: topic -> su/a proposito di/riguardo a · attitude toward person -> nei confronti di · material -> di · intended use -> da · pattern/style -> a

[CARD]
TITLE: A sees a point; in sees an area
REF: 11.6

Use a when a place or time is viewed as a point, and in when it is viewed as an area or interior. The contrast resembles at versus in, but conventional place names matter too.
TL: Ci vediamo alle nove al municipio; i documenti sono nell'ufficio al secondo piano.
EN: We will meet at nine at the town hall; the documents are in the office on the second floor.
[PAUSE:900]
Cities normally take a, while regions and countries normally take in. Dentro makes containment explicit.
TL: Abita a Perugia, lavora in Umbria e conserva i fascicoli dentro un armadio blindato.
EN: She lives in Perugia, works in Umbria, and keeps the files inside a secure cabinet.
SHOW: point/city/clock time -> a · area/region/country/interior -> in · explicit containment -> dentro
Eleven clean location choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Da opens a person's domain; spatial frames locate a reference point
REF: 11.7, 11.9, 11.11, 11.12

Use da for movement to or presence at the place where a person lives, works, or is found. Use vicino a, accanto a, and in mezzo a for learned spatial relations.
TL: Stasera ceniamo da Lucia, vicino alla stazione.
EN: Tonight we are having dinner at Lucia's place, near the station.
[PAUSE:900]
Tra and fra both mean between or among. Choice between them is mainly phonetic: speakers often avoid tra before tr and fra before fr.
TL: Il tavolo è in mezzo alla sala, tra la finestra e la libreria.
EN: The table is in the middle of the room, between the window and the bookcase.
SHOW: at/to a person's domain -> da · near/next to/in the middle -> fixed spatial frame · between/among -> tra or fra

[CARD]
TITLE: Destination splits into point, area, person, and intended route
REF: 11.9, 11.13

Use a for motion to a point or city, in for entry into an area or resulting state, and da for movement to the place where a person is.
TL: Domani andiamo a Bologna; poi entreremo in Svizzera e passeremo da Luca.
EN: Tomorrow we are going to Bologna; then we will enter Switzerland and stop by Luca's place.
[PAUSE:900]
A person as recipient still takes a, not da: da identifies the person's place or domain.
TL: Porta questo fascicolo al direttore e poi corri da Elisa.
EN: Take this file to the director and then run to Elisa's place.
SHOW: destination point/city -> a · area or resulting state -> in · person's place -> da · recipient person -> a

[SIDE]
TITLE: Verso aims, fino a reaches a limit, and per labels a destination
REF: 11.13

Verso gives orientation toward a goal without asserting arrival. Fino a presents the endpoint reached, while per marks an intended destination on departures and transport labels.
TL: Cammineremo verso il porto, ma il taxi ci porterà fino al molo.
EN: We will walk toward the port, but the taxi will take us as far as the pier.
[PAUSE:900]
In can also mark conversion into a resulting form or currency.
TL: Il treno per Trieste parte alle sette; prima del viaggio cambierò gli euro in franchi.
EN: The train for Trieste leaves at seven; before the trip I will change euros into francs.
SHOW: orientation -> verso · reached limit -> fino a · intended destination -> per · transformation -> in

[CARD]
TITLE: A route, an opening, and a source use different lenses
REF: 11.14, 11.15

Attraverso and per trace motion across or through an area. Lungo follows a lengthwise route, while via names an intermediate point.
TL: Abbiamo attraversato il parco per un sentiero che corre lungo il fiume.
EN: We crossed the park by a path that runs along the river.
[PAUSE:900]
Da can mark an opening used as a route as well as motion out of it. Context separates through from out of.
TL: Il vento entrava dalla finestra mentre uscivamo dall'ingresso laterale.
EN: The wind was coming in through the window while we were leaving through the side entrance.
SHOW: across/through area -> attraverso/per · along -> lungo · intermediate point -> via · route through opening or motion out -> da

[SIDE]
TITLE: Da marks departure; di identifies origin after essere
REF: 11.15

Use da for movement from a place and for transitions from one state or role to another. After essere, di commonly identifies where a person is from.
TL: Sono appena tornati da Bari, ma lei è di Lecce.
EN: They have just returned from Bari, but she is from Lecce.
[PAUSE:900]
Repeated movement from one member of a series to another commonly uses di ... in. Asking a person for something uses chiedere a, not a literal from construction.
TL: Il progetto cambia di settimana in settimana, quindi ho chiesto chiarimenti al tecnico.
EN: The project changes from week to week, so I asked the technician for clarification.
SHOW: movement/source -> da · origin after essere -> di · from one to another -> di ... in · ask somebody -> chiedere a

[CARD]
TITLE: A marks a recipient; per marks an external beneficiary
REF: 11.18

Use a for the person to whom something is said, given, sent, or granted. The same indirect-object relation can show whose sphere is directly affected.
TL: Ho consegnato il dossier alla direttrice e le ho aggiornato il calendario.
EN: I delivered the file to the director and updated her calendar for her.
[PAUSE:900]
Per presents a beneficiary or intended user without necessarily making that person an argument of the verb.
TL: Ne ho preparato una copia per il consulente esterno.
EN: I prepared a copy of it for the external consultant.
SHOW: recipient or directly affected participant -> a / indirect-object clitic · external beneficiary -> per

[SIDE]
TITLE: Cause, purpose, price, role, and style have distinct frames
REF: 11.21, 11.24, 11.25, 11.29

Per via di and a causa di mark causes; grazie a presents an enabling cause. Per also marks purpose, price, and the role someone is taken for.
TL: Il volo è stato cancellato per via della nebbia; grazie a un collega, siamo partiti in treno.
EN: The flight was cancelled because of the fog; thanks to a colleague, we left by train.
[PAUSE:900]
A commonly classifies manner, power source, decoration, or culinary style. Da instead classifies intended purpose.
TL: Abbiamo pagato cinquanta euro per una tovaglia a quadri e una lampada da tavolo.
EN: We paid fifty euros for a checked tablecloth and a table lamp.
SHOW: adverse cause -> per via di/a causa di · enabling cause -> grazie a · purpose/price/as-role -> per · manner/style -> a · intended use -> da

[CARD]
TITLE: Exclusion removes a member; concession preserves the event
REF: 11.19, 11.20

Tranne, salvo, eccetto, fuorché, and a parte remove a person or thing from the set under discussion. The rest of the statement applies normally.
TL: Erano presenti tutti tranne la presidente; a parte quel ritardo, la seduta è andata bene.
EN: Everyone was present except the chair; apart from that delay, the meeting went well.
[PAUSE:900]
Al di fuori di can also mark exclusion, but its spatial meaning makes context important.
TL: Al di fuori di due allegati ancora da verificare, il fascicolo è completo.
EN: Apart from two attachments still to be checked, the file is complete.
SHOW: remove an exception from a set -> tranne/salvo/eccetto/fuorché/a parte/al di fuori di
Four hygiene-gated exclusion and concession choices follow; two structurally duplicated solutions are omitted.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Malgrado and nonostante acknowledge an obstacle
REF: 11.20

Malgrado and nonostante introduce a circumstance that might have prevented the main event but did not.
TL: Nonostante la pioggia, hanno completato il sopralluogo.
EN: Despite the rain, they completed the inspection.
[PAUSE:900]
A possessive before malgrado creates the fixed personal construction mio, tuo, suo, or nostro malgrado: despite my, your, their, or our wishes.
TL: Ha accettato, suo malgrado, di presiedere la commissione.
EN: He reluctantly agreed to chair the committee.
SHOW: acknowledged obstacle -> malgrado/nonostante + noun phrase · against someone's wishes -> possessive + malgrado

[CARD]
TITLE: Time prepositions distinguish duration, start, completion, delay, and deadline
REF: 11.32

Per can measure a bounded duration, though inherently durative verbs may omit it. Da connects a starting point or elapsed span to a situation that still holds.
TL: Studio qui da tre mesi; ieri ho lavorato per sei ore.
EN: I have been studying here for three months; yesterday I worked for six hours.
[PAUSE:900]
In measures the time taken to complete something. Fra or tra places an event after a delay, while entro sets the latest permitted endpoint.
TL: Il rapporto sarà pronto fra due giorni e dovrà essere consegnato entro venerdì.
EN: The report will be ready in two days and must be submitted by Friday.
SHOW: bounded duration -> per or zero · continuing since/for -> da · completion time -> in · after a delay -> fra/tra · deadline -> entro

[SIDE]
TITLE: Fino a reaches an endpoint; prima di and dopo order events
REF: 11.32

Fino a marks an endpoint in time. Prima di introduces an earlier noun or same-subject infinitive; dopo introduces a later noun or a same-subject compound infinitive for a completed event.
TL: Ha risolto il problema in mezz'ora ed è rimasta fino alla fine.
EN: She solved the problem in half an hour and stayed until the end.
[PAUSE:900]
Use the infinitival construction only when its understood subject matches the subject of the main clause.
TL: Prima di uscire ho chiuso le finestre; dopo aver controllato la porta, sono sceso.
EN: Before leaving I closed the windows; after checking the door, I went downstairs.
SHOW: endpoint -> fino a · before + same-subject action -> prima di + infinitive · after + completed same-subject action -> dopo + auxiliary infinitive + participle
