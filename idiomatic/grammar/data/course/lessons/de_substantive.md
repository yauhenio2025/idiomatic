## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Every noun has three coordinates
SVG: de_substantive_c01f.svg
REF: 1, 1.1, 1.2, 1.3

A German noun arrives with one coordinate already stored: its gender. In use, the speaker selects a second coordinate, number, and the sentence assigns a third, case. Those three choices control the whole noun phrase.
TL: Unsere seit Monaten verschobene Entscheidung überrascht die Fachleute.
EN: Our decision, which has been postponed for months, surprises the experts.
[PAUSE:1200]
The head noun is feminine and singular. Its role makes the entire opening phrase nominative. The determiner and the adjective make those coordinates visible.
SHOW: stored gender · selected number · assigned case
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The noun phrase is a small machine
REF: 1

A noun phrase can contain a determiner, one adjective or a whole adjectival phrase, and the noun itself. The head noun supplies the identity; the material around it packages that identity for the sentence.
TL: Selbst dieses überraschend klare Urteil überzeugt nicht alle Beteiligten.
EN: Even this surprisingly clear judgment does not convince everyone involved.
[PAUSE:1200]
The modifiers may grow, but the structure stays stable. Read from the head outward: identify the noun, recover its gender and number, then read the endings that expose its case.
SHOW: determiner + modifiers + head noun
Gender comes first, because every later ending depends on it.

[CARD]
TITLE: Reliable suffixes compress gender
SVG: de_substantive_c02f.svg
REF: 1.1.1

Many derivational suffixes assign gender as part of building the noun. This is the highest-yield alternative to memorizing every article in isolation. The feminine set includes the common abstract-noun endings for quality, state, collective, and process.
TL: Die Wirksamkeit der Maßnahme ist inzwischen unbestritten.
EN: The effectiveness of the measure is now undisputed.
[PAUSE:900]
Neuter endings dominate diminutives and several learned formations.
TL: Das Eigentum an den Daten bleibt beim Institut.
EN: Ownership of the data remains with the institute.
[PAUSE:900]
Masculine endings include several labels for agents and systems.
TL: Der Realismus des Entwurfs überzeugt die Kommission.
EN: The realism of the proposal convinces the committee.
SHOW: suffixes can assign gender during word formation
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A strong rule still has a lexicon
REF: 1.1.1

An invariant suffix outranks the real-world sex or meaning of the referent. That is why a diminutive referring to a girl is grammatically neuter.
TL: Das Mädchen beobachtet einen Schmetterling auf der Wiese.
EN: The girl is watching a butterfly in the meadow.
[PAUSE:1100]
But a learner still needs an exception list. A familiar ending may resemble a productive suffix without actually behaving like it.
TL: Trotz seiner Endung ist das Labor ein Neutrum.
EN: Despite its ending, the word for laboratory is neuter.
[PAUSE:900]
Another frequent exception belongs with the article from day one.
TL: Das Abitur beendet für viele Jugendliche die Schulzeit.
EN: The school-leaving examination ends secondary education for many young people.
SHOW: strong suffix rule first · lexical exceptions stored with the article
Ten prefix-based gender decisions follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Form gives probabilities too
SVG: de_substantive_c03f.svg
REF: 1.1.2

Not every clue is absolute. Some shapes merely shift the odds. The unstressed prefix associated with collective or result nouns points strongly toward neuter, but common masculine and feminine exceptions remain.
TL: Das Gespräch verlief sachlicher als erwartet.
EN: The conversation was more objective than expected.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Gefahr wurde zunächst unterschätzt.
EN: The danger was initially underestimated.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Gedanke lässt mich seit gestern nicht los.
EN: The thought has been preoccupying me since yesterday.
The ending used for many result and state nouns behaves similarly: mostly neuter, sometimes feminine. Treat such clues as weighted evidence, not as permission to guess blindly.
SHOW: productive suffix = rule · broader word shape = probability
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Meaning helps after form
REF: 1.1.3, 1.1.4, 1.1.5, 1.1.6

Semantic fields supply a second layer of clues. Days, months, seasons and weather terms tend masculine; native river names tend feminine; metals, young creatures and substantivized words tend neuter.
TL: Der Mittwoch bleibt für die Sitzung reserviert.
EN: Wednesday remains reserved for the meeting.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Die Donau führt nach dem Unwetter Hochwasser.
EN: The Danube is carrying floodwater after the storm.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Das Kupfer wurde vollständig recycelt.
EN: The copper was completely recycled.
[PAUSE:1000]
When form and meaning conflict, derivational form normally wins.
TL: Das Mädchen leitet inzwischen das Forschungsteam.
EN: The girl now leads the research team.
SHOW: form usually outranks meaning · unresolved nouns stay lexical
Ten ending-based gender drills come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: In compounds, the head stands last
SVG: de_substantive_c04f.svg
REF: 1.1.8

German compounds are right-headed. The final component says what kind of thing the whole compound is, and it normally supplies the gender as well. Everything before it narrows the meaning.
TL: Die Datenschutzbehörde veröffentlicht ihren Jahresbericht.
EN: The data protection authority is publishing its annual report.
[PAUSE:1100]
The first compound ends in a feminine word for an authority; the second ends in a masculine word for a report. The material on the left changes the concept, not the gender controller.
TL: Das Kundenportal braucht einen neuen Sicherheitscode.
EN: The customer portal needs a new security code.
SHOW: rightmost component = semantic head + gender controller
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Abbreviations inherit; loans negotiate
REF: 1.1.8, 1.1.9

An abbreviation normally inherits the gender of its full base expression.
TL: Die EU hat ihre Richtlinie grundlegend überarbeitet.
EN: The EU has fundamentally revised its directive.
[PAUSE:900]
Loanwords find gender by analogy with a close German equivalent, by their ending, or by a broad semantic class.
TL: Das Team hat seinen Bericht fristgerecht eingereicht.
EN: The team submitted its report on time.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Die Crew überprüft vor dem Start sämtliche Anzeigen.
EN: The crew checks all the displays before take-off.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Der Deal birgt erhebliche rechtliche Risiken.
EN: The deal entails considerable legal risks.
When those pressures compete, regional or register variation can persist. Record the variety you actually use, but recognize the alternatives.
SHOW: abbreviation → base word · loan → analogy + form + usage
Ten foreign-suffix decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Grammatical gender governs agreement
REF: 1.1.7, 1.1.12

Words for humans have natural reference, but the noun phrase still obeys grammatical gender. Productive feminine forms matter whenever a specifically female person is meant.
TL: Die erfahrene Ingenieurin übernimmt morgen die Projektleitung.
EN: The experienced female engineer will take over management of the project tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
When a group explicitly includes women and men, formal and public German commonly names both forms.
TL: Zur Tagung sind alle Mitarbeiterinnen und Mitarbeiter eingeladen.
EN: All female and male employees are invited to the conference.
SHOW: human reference does not cancel grammatical agreement
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Agreement can shift with distance
REF: 1.1.12

When grammatical and natural gender conflict, close agreement remains strongly grammatical. A relative pronoun next to its neuter antecedent follows the noun, and so does a nearby possessive.
TL: Das Mädchen, das den Wettbewerb gewonnen hat, erhält morgen seinen Preis.
EN: The girl who won the competition will receive her prize tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1200]
Farther away, especially in a new sentence, a personal pronoun often follows natural gender instead.
TL: Das Mädchen gewann den Wettbewerb im Mai. Inzwischen bereitet sie sich auf das Finale vor.
EN: The girl won the competition in May. She is now preparing for the final.
This is not free variation everywhere. Determiners and adjectives stay grammatical; distance mainly affects personal reference.
SHOW: close modifiers: grammatical · distant personal pronoun: often natural
Nine mixed gender-in-context drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Gender can encode a lexical choice
SVG: de_substantive_c06f.svg
REF: 1.1.10, 1.1.11

Some nouns genuinely vary by region or register. More revealing are spellings that cover separate lexical entries. Gender then distinguishes meanings rather than offering two interchangeable articles.
TL: Für meinen Teil ist die Debatte damit beendet.
EN: As far as I am concerned, that concludes the debate.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Das defekte Teil muss vollständig ausgetauscht werden.
EN: The defective component has to be replaced completely.
The masculine reading names a share or portion; the neuter reading often names a detached component. The article is part of the meaning.
SHOW: same spelling + new gender can mean a new lexical entry
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: One shell, three nouns
REF: 1.1.11

Listen to one spelling branch three ways.
TL: Der Band enthält bislang unveröffentlichte Briefe.
EN: The volume contains previously unpublished letters.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Das Band um das Paket hat sich gelöst.
EN: The ribbon around the parcel has come loose.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Die Band tritt erst nach Mitternacht auf.
EN: The band does not perform until after midnight.
[PAUSE:1000]
The gender selects book volume, ribbon, or musical group, and each entry brings its own plural. Learn such words as separate lexemes, not as exceptions to one noun.
TL: Vom Ufer aus wirkt der See vollkommen ruhig.
EN: From the shore, the lake looks completely calm.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Auf hoher See änderte der Wind plötzlich die Richtung.
EN: On the open sea, the wind suddenly changed direction.
SHOW: article + meaning + plural belong in one lexical record
Fourteen varying- and double-gender decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The plural system has strong defaults
SVG: de_substantive_c07f.svg
REF: 1.2, 1.2.1, 1.2.2, 1.2.3

German has several plural shapes, but gender and word form make the system far less random than a flat list suggests. Most feminine nouns add an ending with n. Masculine and neuter nouns ending in the unstressed syllables -el, -en, or -er often add nothing.
TL: die Besprechung – die Besprechungen
EN: the meeting – the meetings
[PAUSE:700]
TL: der Rechner – die Rechner
EN: the computer – the computers
[PAUSE:700]
Other masculine nouns commonly add e, often with a stem vowel change; many neuters use e or er.
TL: der Antrag – die Anträge
EN: the application – the applications
[PAUSE:700]
TL: das Fach – die Fächer
EN: the subject – the subjects
SHOW: feminine → -(e)n · unstressed -el/-en/-er → often zero · elsewhere -e/-er
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Store a stem change and an ending
REF: 1.2

A plural form has two independent moving parts: the stem vowel may change, and an ending may appear. Either part can also stay still.
TL: der Schlüssel – die Schlüssel
EN: the key – the keys
[PAUSE:700]
No stem change and no ending.
TL: der Zugang – die Zugänge
EN: the entrance – the entrances
[PAUSE:700]
A stem change plus an e ending.
TL: das Archiv – die Archive
EN: the archive – the archives
[PAUSE:700]
No stem change, but an e ending. Do not memorize an abstract plural class alone; store the actual plural beside the noun and its article.
SHOW: plural = stem operation + ending · either may be zero
Ten plural forms in full sentences follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Newer words often take -s
REF: 1.2.4, 1.2.5

The s-plural occupies identifiable niches: many recent loans, many words ending in a full vowel, abbreviations, shortened words, and names.
TL: das Start-up – die Start-ups
EN: the start-up – the start-ups
[PAUSE:700]
TL: die WG – die WGs
EN: the shared flat – the shared flats
[PAUSE:700]
Learned loans may instead replace a classical ending or preserve a specialized plural.
TL: das Kriterium – die Kriterien
EN: the criterion – the criteria
[PAUSE:700]
TL: das Paradigma – die Paradigmen
EN: the paradigm – the paradigms
Register matters: a nativized plural may compete with a learned one, and the balance can change over time.
SHOW: -s has niches · learned endings may be replaced, not merely extended
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Competing plurals can split meaning
REF: 1.2.6

Two plural forms are sometimes a semantic contrast, not stylistic decoration. One noun for a word distinguishes isolated lexical items from connected utterances.
TL: Sie notierte drei unbekannte Wörter am Rand.
EN: She wrote three unfamiliar words in the margin.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Seine letzten Worte waren kaum hörbar.
EN: His final words were barely audible.
[PAUSE:1000]
Other spellings hide different nouns and therefore different plurals.
TL: Die Bank finanziert mehrere kommunale Bauprojekte.
EN: The bank is financing several municipal construction projects.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Im Park stehen zwei frisch gestrichene Bänke.
EN: Two freshly painted benches stand in the park.
SHOW: alternate plural may signal alternate meaning
Next, number itself stops matching English.

[CARD]
TITLE: Number is not translated word for word
SVG: de_substantive_c09f.svg
REF: 1.2.7

The nearest English and German nouns may package reality with different numbers. English spectacles correspond to a German singular; English furniture corresponds to a German plural.
TL: Meine neue Brille liegt noch im Büro.
EN: My new glasses are still at the office.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Möbel werden morgen geliefert.
EN: The furniture will be delivered tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
Agreement follows the German noun, never the translated idea. Festival names that look plural are normally neuter singular when they govern a verb.
TL: Weihnachten fällt dieses Jahr auf einen Mittwoch.
EN: Christmas falls on a Wednesday this year.
SHOW: choose German number first · then make German agreement follow it
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Counting does not always create a plural
REF: 1.2.7e, 1.2.8

After a numeral, masculine and neuter units of weight, measure, and value normally keep the singular form. They function as calibrated units, not as individually counted containers.
TL: Die Werkstatt bestellte zwanzig Kilogramm Stahl.
EN: The workshop ordered twenty kilograms of steel.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Für das Seminar wurden drei Dutzend Hefte geliefert.
EN: Three dozen notebooks were delivered for the seminar.
[PAUSE:900]
Feminine measure nouns use their ordinary plural.
TL: Auf jedem Tisch standen zwei Flaschen Mineralwasser.
EN: Two bottles of mineral water stood on each table.
[PAUSE:1000]
German can also use a singular body part or item of clothing distributively when each person has one.
TL: Die Gäste drehten gleichzeitig den Kopf zur Bühne.
EN: The guests turned their heads toward the stage at the same time.
SHOW: m/n unit after numeral: singular form · feminine unit: plural form

[CARD]
TITLE: Nouns carry only two core case signals
SVG: de_substantive_c10f.svg
REF: 1.3, 1.3.1

Determiners and adjectives do most of the visible case work. Regular nouns themselves add only two productive signals. Masculine and neuter nouns mark the genitive singular; any plural that can accept another n marks the dative plural.
TL: Der Entwurf des Vertrags liegt den Ausschüssen seit Montag vor.
EN: The draft of the treaty has been available to the committees since Monday.
[PAUSE:1200]
The genitive singular carries its s ending. The dative plural carries its n ending. A plural already ending in n or s cannot add another one.
TL: Die Folgen des Beschlusses wurden in mehreren Sitzungen erörtert.
EN: The consequences of the decision were discussed in several meetings.
SHOW: genitive singular m/n → -(e)s · dative plural → extra -n if possible
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Choose -s or -es by shape
REF: 1.3.5

The longer genitive ending is required after sibilant sounds and is common when a short base would otherwise be awkward. The shorter ending is usual after vowels, with names, and with many polysyllabic nouns.
TL: die Folgen des Sturms
EN: the consequences of the storm
[PAUSE:700]
TL: das Protokoll des Ausschusses
EN: the minutes of the committee
[PAUSE:700]
TL: die Auswertung des Ereignisses
EN: the evaluation of the event
[PAUSE:1000]
With many ordinary nouns both lengths are grammatical; frequency and phonology decide what sounds natural. Learn fixed preferences where usage is clear.
SHOW: sibilant ending → -es · vowel/name/polysyllable → usually -s
Weak nouns now reveal a different declension pattern.

[CARD]
TITLE: Weak masculines mark everything but one cell
REF: 1.3.2

A large class of masculine nouns adds n or en in every form except the nominative singular. Many denote male humans or animals; several stressed foreign suffixes belong here too.
TL: Der Journalist befragt den Zeugen.
EN: The journalist is interviewing the witness.
[PAUSE:1000]
The subject is nominative and bare. The direct object has its weak ending. Change the roles and the endings follow the new cases.
TL: Die Aussage des Zeugen widerspricht dem Journalisten.
EN: The witness's statement contradicts the journalist.
[PAUSE:1100]
The same ending also runs through the entire plural. Think of one exceptional bare cell, not a collection of unrelated forms.
SHOW: nominative singular: bare · every other cell: -(e)n
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Name and heart have their own paradigms
REF: 1.3.3, 1.3.4

A small group of masculine nouns resembles the weak class but adds an extra s in the genitive singular. One frequent neuter noun has a parallel irregular pattern.
TL: Der Name des neuen Kollegen ist mir entfallen.
EN: I have forgotten the new colleague's name.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Entscheidung liegt mir am Herzen.
EN: The decision is close to my heart.
[PAUSE:1000]
The old dative e has otherwise retreated from productive grammar, but it remains alive inside set phrases.
TL: Im Laufe des Abends änderte sie ihre Einschätzung.
EN: Over the course of the evening, she changed her assessment.
SHOW: Name: Namen/Namens · Herz: Herzen/Herzens · dative -e: fossilized
Ten weak and irregular declension drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Proper names build the genitive directly
REF: 1.3.6

A proper name without an article normally takes s directly. The construction can stand before or after the noun it determines.
TL: Leas Dissertation untersucht Kafkas späte Erzählungen.
EN: Lea's dissertation examines Kafka's late stories.
[PAUSE:1000]
A name already ending in an s-sound takes an apostrophe in writing instead of another ending.
TL: Max’ Vortrag beginnt erst am Nachmittag.
EN: Max's lecture does not begin until the afternoon.
[PAUSE:900]
With a geographical name ending in that sound, a phrase with a preposition is the available solution.
TL: Die Straßen von Paris waren ungewöhnlich leer.
EN: The streets of Paris were unusually empty.
SHOW: plain name → -s · final s-sound → apostrophe · geography → von
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Titles decide where declension lands
REF: 1.3.6

Without an article, a title and name form one unit, and the genitive ending lands at the end of the name.
TL: Bundespräsidentin Webers Rede wurde live übertragen.
EN: Federal President Weber's speech was broadcast live.
[PAUSE:1000]
With an article, the title declines and the following name remains bare.
TL: Die Erlasse des Herzogs Leopold veränderten die Verwaltung grundlegend.
EN: Duke Leopold's decrees fundamentally changed the administration.
[PAUSE:1000]
After a descriptive noun, a full quoted title remains in the nominative rather than copying the surrounding case.
TL: Im Hotel „Goldene Linde“ fand eine internationale Tagung statt.
EN: An international conference took place at the Hotel “Golden Lime Tree.”
SHOW: no article: ending on name · article: title declines · quoted title stays nominative
