## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose the architecture before the linker
REF: 17.1, 17.2, 17.3

French links ideas through three main architectures. Coordination joins elements of equal rank. Subordination makes one clause depend on another and may control its mood. A non-finite link compresses the dependent event into an infinitive or participle.
TL: Ce dossier avance, mais il reste fragile.
EN: This file is moving forward, but it remains fragile.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Bien que le dossier avance, son financement reste fragile.
EN: Although the file is moving forward, its funding remains fragile.
[PAUSE:900]
The first linker coordinates two assertions. The second makes a concessive clause subordinate and selects the subjunctive.
SHOW: equal rank -> coordination · dependent clause -> subordination
Flip for the compressed alternatives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Non-finite forms package a second event
REF: 17.6, 17.7, 17.8, 17.9

When the same participant controls two events, French often avoids a second finite clause. An infinitive can express purpose or sequence; a participle can express prior, simultaneous, or accompanying action.
TL: Après avoir relu le contrat, Léa a signalé deux incohérences.
EN: After rereading the contract, Léa pointed out two inconsistencies.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: En comparant les versions, nous avons retrouvé la clause supprimée.
EN: By comparing the versions, we found the deleted clause.
[PAUSE:900]
This compression is not merely stylistic. Auxiliary choice, agreement, and subject attachment still encode who did what and when.
SHOW: infinitive -> compact relation · participle -> compact event

[CARD]
TITLE: Coordinators keep both sides at equal rank
REF: 17.2

Et adds, ou offers an alternative, mais opposes, and puis advances the sequence. Paired forms make the balance explicit: et ... et means both ... and, while ou ... ou and soit ... soit present alternatives.
TL: Le comité a validé le budget, mais il a reporté le lancement.
EN: The committee approved the budget, but postponed the launch.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Soit vous révisez le calendrier, soit nous réduisons le périmètre.
EN: Either you revise the schedule, or we reduce the scope.
[PAUSE:900]
A coordinator does not make its clause subordinate, and it does not trigger a subjunctive by itself.
SHOW: et = addition · ou = choice · mais = contrast · puis = next
Flip from sequence to reasoning.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Car explains; or advances an argument
REF: 17.2, 17.2.1

Car supplies a reason after the statement it supports. It is chiefly a written coordinator, so do not front it like a mobile parce que clause. Or is not the ordinary time adverb now; in formal narrative or argument it introduces the next decisive fact.
TL: Nous reporterons le vote, car deux pièces manquent au dossier.
EN: We will postpone the vote, because two documents are missing from the file.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Deux pièces manquent ; or, le règlement exige un dossier complet.
EN: Two documents are missing; now, the rules require a complete file.
[PAUSE:900]
Use maintenant for present-time now and alors for then in a past frame. Reserve or for a logical turn.
SHOW: reason after claim -> car · next premise -> or

[CARD]
TITLE: Time conjunctions locate boundaries and spans
REF: 17.3.1

Dès que, aussitôt que, and sitôt que mark an immediate boundary. Pendant que presents overlapping duration; tant que and aussi longtemps que measure the whole span. Chaque fois que repeats an event, while une fois que treats a threshold as complete.
TL: Dès que la sauvegarde se termine, le serveur redémarre.
EN: As soon as the backup finishes, the server restarts.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Pendant que Nora vérifiait les comptes, Malik préparait l'annexe.
EN: While Nora was checking the accounts, Malik was preparing the appendix.
[PAUSE:900]
Choose the temporal relation first: boundary, overlap, duration, repetition, or completed prerequisite.
SHOW: boundary · overlap · span · repetition · completion
Flip for mood and tense.

[SIDE]
TITLE: These time clauses normally take the indicative
REF: 17.3.1, 17.3.2

Après que reports an event as realized and normally takes the indicative. Do not import the subjunctive of avant que merely because both expressions contain que. Future time clauses also remain indicative.
TL: Nous publierons le rapport après que le conseil l'aura approuvé.
EN: We will publish the report after the council has approved it.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Chaque fois que ce voyant s'allume, le système enregistre une alerte.
EN: Every time this indicator lights up, the system records an alert.
[PAUSE:900]
French treats these clauses as placed on the timeline, not as desired or merely envisaged.
SHOW: realized time relation -> indicative · après que, not avant que

[CARD]
TITLE: French keeps future viewpoint inside time clauses
REF: 17.3.2

English often uses a present form after when or as soon as even when the event is future. French normally keeps the future viewpoint in both clauses. Use the future perfect when the dependent event must be complete first.
TL: Je vous enverrai les chiffres dès que l'équipe les aura validés.
EN: I will send you the figures as soon as the team has validated them.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Le chantier commencerait lorsque la mairie aurait délivré le permis.
EN: The work would begin when the town hall had issued the permit.
[PAUSE:900]
The tense pairing follows the French timeline rather than the English surface form.
SHOW: future main clause -> future time clause · prior completion -> compound future
Flip from future boundaries to duration already under way.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ongoing duration stays present or imperfect
REF: 17.3.3, 17.3.4

With depuis que, voici or voilà ... que, and il y a ... que, an activity still in progress normally uses the present in a present frame and the imperfect in a past frame. A completed event instead keeps a compound past or pluperfect.
TL: Voilà trois ans qu'elle dirige ce laboratoire.
EN: She has been running this laboratory for three years now.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Cela faisait six mois qu'ils négociaient lorsque l'accord a échoué.
EN: They had been negotiating for six months when the agreement fell through.
[PAUSE:900]
For prior events in literary narrative, the past anterior can pair with the simple past. In ordinary usage, the pluperfect is the safer unmarked choice.
SHOW: still ongoing -> present/imperfect · already completed -> compound tense

[CARD]
TITLE: Indicative links distinguish kinds of evidence
REF: 17.3.5

Parce que gives a neutral cause. Comme commonly places background cause first. Puisque presents the reason as already known or accepted. Vu que and étant donné que frame it as evidence or a given premise.
TL: Comme les données sont incomplètes, nous ajournerons l'analyse.
EN: Since the data are incomplete, we will postpone the analysis.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Vous connaissez déjà le protocole, puisque vous l'avez validé.
EN: You already know the protocol, since you approved it.
[PAUSE:900]
All four normally take the indicative. The difference lies in discourse status, not truth versus uncertainty.
SHOW: neutral cause · background · shared premise · given evidence
Flip from cause to progression and contrast.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Link the relation, not an English keyword
REF: 17.3.5

À mesure que and au fur et à mesure que track gradual change. Alors que and tandis que can mark simultaneity, but they often sharpen a contrast. Comme si introduces an apparent comparison and follows its own tense logic.
TL: À mesure que l'enquête avançait, les témoignages se contredisaient.
EN: As the investigation progressed, the accounts contradicted one another.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle promet la transparence, alors que son équipe refuse de publier les chiffres.
EN: She promises transparency, whereas her team refuses to publish the figures.
[PAUSE:900]
Ask whether the relation is progressive, simultaneous, contrastive, comparative, causal, or exceptive before choosing the phrase.
SHOW: meaning first -> connector second

[CARD]
TITLE: First decide whether si means whether
REF: 17.3.6

After verbs of asking, knowing, or wondering, si introduces an indirect yes-or-no question. In that use it means whether and may contain whatever tense the question requires, including future and conditional forms.
TL: Je ne sais pas si le serveur répondra avant midi.
EN: I do not know whether the server will respond before noon.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Le comité se demande si cette mesure serait applicable partout.
EN: The committee wonders whether this measure would be applicable everywhere.
[PAUSE:900]
Test the meaning by replacing if with whether in English. If it still works, this is interrogative si, not a condition.
SHOW: si = whether -> indirect question -> full tense range
Flip for hypothetical si.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Hypothetical si excludes future and conditional forms
REF: 10.8, 17.3.6

In a real or open condition, use the present after si even when the result is future. For a present counterfactual, use si plus imperfect and a conditional result. For an unreal past, use si plus pluperfect and a compound conditional result.
TL: Si vous confirmez aujourd'hui, nous partirons demain.
EN: If you confirm today, we will leave tomorrow.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Si nous avions reçu l'avis plus tôt, nous aurions modifié le contrat.
EN: If we had received the opinion earlier, we would have amended the contract.
[PAUSE:900]
The future or conditional belongs in the result clause, never in the hypothetical si clause itself.
SHOW: si + present -> future result · si + pluperfect -> past conditional result

[CARD]
TITLE: Some subordinate relations select the subjunctive
REF: 17.3.8

Avant que, jusqu'à ce que, and en attendant que place an event before a boundary not yet realized. Bien que and quoique concede a fact without making it the main assertion. Pour que and afin que express intended purpose.
TL: Nous attendrons jusqu'à ce que l'auditeur rende son avis.
EN: We will wait until the auditor gives an opinion.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Bien qu'elle connaisse les risques, elle maintient sa décision.
EN: Although she knows the risks, she is maintaining her decision.
[PAUSE:900]
Learn the relation together with its mood. Translating que word by word will not predict the choice.
SHOW: pending boundary · concession · purpose -> subjunctive
Flip for the difference between result and intention.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mood can distinguish achieved result from intended result
REF: 17.3.8

De sorte que, de façon que, and de manière que may report an actual consequence with the indicative or express a desired outcome with the subjunctive. À moins que, pourvu que, and à condition que present conditions and normally take the subjunctive.
TL: Il a parlé distinctement, de sorte que chacun l'a compris.
EN: He spoke clearly, so everyone understood him.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Il a reformulé la consigne de sorte que chacun puisse l'appliquer.
EN: He rephrased the instruction so that everyone could apply it.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nous partirons à moins qu'une alerte ne soit émise.
EN: We will leave unless an alert is issued.
[PAUSE:900]
The ne after à moins que is expletive: it adds no negation and is more common in careful style.
SHOW: achieved consequence -> indicative · intended outcome -> subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Translate since, while, as, and when by function
REF: 17.4.1, 17.4.2, 17.4.3, 17.4.4

English since may mean from a starting point or because: choose depuis que for the first and puisque, comme, or vu que for the second. While may express overlap, duration, or contrast: choose pendant que, tant que, or alors que and tandis que accordingly.
TL: Depuis qu'elle travaille à distance, elle voyage moins souvent.
EN: Since she has been working remotely, she travels less often.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Tandis que le prix baisse, la demande reste faible.
EN: While the price is falling, demand remains weak.
[PAUSE:900]
The same method separates progressive à mesure que, simultaneous comme, temporal quand, and contrastive alors que.
SHOW: identify the relation -> choose the French linker
Flip when two dependent clauses share one linker.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Repeat a complex conjunction with que
REF: 17.4.4

When coordinated dependent clauses share the same conjunction, French normally replaces the repeated conjunction with que. The second clause keeps the mood selected by the first link. After hypothetical si, the replacement que takes the subjunctive.
TL: Quand le débat s'échauffe et que chacun parle à la fois, la présidente suspend la séance.
EN: When the debate heats up and everyone speaks at once, the chair suspends the meeting.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: S'il accepte le principe et que nous puissions fixer une date, nous signerons vendredi.
EN: If he accepts the principle and we can set a date, we will sign on Friday.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not repeat the whole phrase mechanically. Let que carry the shared dependency.
SHOW: conjunction + clause · et que + parallel clause

[CARD]
TITLE: Shared subjects favor an infinitive link
REF: 17.6

When one subject controls both events, French commonly reduces the dependent clause to an infinitive. Many linkers then take de, some take à, and pour or sans take the infinitive directly.
TL: Avant de répondre, relisez la dernière version.
EN: Before replying, reread the latest version.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle a chuchoté de peur de réveiller les enfants.
EN: She whispered for fear of waking the children.
[PAUSE:900]
Afin de, avant de, à condition de, à moins de, and de peur de belong to the de group. De manière à expresses intended result. Plutôt que exceptionally becomes plutôt que de before an infinitive.
SHOW: same subject -> linker + infinitive
Flip when the subjects differ.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Different subjects restore a finite clause
REF: 17.3.8, 17.6

If the subordinate event has its own subject, keep que and use the mood required by the finite construction. Compare purpose with pour que and a prior boundary with avant que.
TL: Avant que vous ne répondiez, relisez la dernière version.
EN: Before you reply, reread the latest version.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Elle a fermé la porte pour que les enfants puissent dormir.
EN: She closed the door so that the children could sleep.
[PAUSE:900]
Again, the ne after avant que is optional and expletive. The subject contrast, not formality alone, decides whether compression is possible.
SHOW: same subject -> infinitive · different subject -> que + finite verb

[CARD]
TITLE: Après plus a compound infinitive marks anteriority
REF: 17.7

To express after doing or having done, combine après with the compound infinitive. Most verbs use avoir; verbs in the être family use être and show the agreement required by that auxiliary.
TL: Après avoir vérifié les chiffres, nous avons corrigé le tableau.
EN: After checking the figures, we corrected the table.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Après être descendues du train, elles ont appelé un taxi.
EN: After getting off the train, they called a taxi.
[PAUSE:900]
The understood subject of the compound infinitive is normally the subject of the main clause.
SHOW: après avoir + participle · après être + agreeing participle
Flip for reflexive and passive-looking shapes.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Preserve the verb's auxiliary and pronouns
REF: 17.7

Reflexive verbs use après s'être plus the participle. A preceding object pronoun stays inside the infinitive group, and passive structure may require avoir été plus a participle.
TL: Après s'être excusé, le directeur a repris la parole.
EN: After apologizing, the director resumed speaking.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Après avoir été avertie, la juriste a demandé un délai.
EN: After being warned, the lawyer asked for an extension.
[PAUSE:900]
Build the full compound verb first, then place it after après. That prevents auxiliary choice and agreement from being guessed locally.
SHOW: après + complete compound infinitive

[CARD]
TITLE: Detached past participles behave like agreeing predicates
REF: 17.8

With verbs that use être, a past participle can stand without après and link a prior or accompanying state to the main clause. It agrees with the participant it describes. Posture words such as assis, debout, couché, and appuyé work the same way.
TL: Arrivées avant l'aube, les techniciennes ont relancé le système.
EN: Having arrived before dawn, the technicians restarted the system.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Assis près de la fenêtre, Paul relisait ses notes.
EN: Sitting near the window, Paul was rereading his notes.
[PAUSE:900]
Place the phrase so its controller is unmistakable. Agreement confirms that attachment.
SHOW: detached past participle -> agree with its controller
Flip from past participles to forms in -ant.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Adjectival -ant agrees; verbal -ant does not
REF: 17.9.1, 17.9.2

An -ant form used as an adjective names a property and agrees with its noun. A present participle still behaves like a verb: it can take an object or adverbial complement and remains invariable.
TL: Une réforme convaincante doit produire des résultats mesurables.
EN: A convincing reform must produce measurable results.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Convainquant les derniers indécis, son intervention a débloqué le vote.
EN: By convincing the last undecided members, her intervention unlocked the vote.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ayant identifié l'erreur, l'équipe a relancé le calcul.
EN: Having identified the error, the team reran the calculation.
[PAUSE:900]
If the form carries verbal complements or expresses an action, resist adjective agreement. Anterior action uses ayant or étant plus the past participle.
SHOW: property -> adjective + agreement · action -> invariable participle

[CARD]
TITLE: En plus present participle makes a gerund
REF: 17.9.2, 17.9.4

The gerund presents an event as simultaneous with the main event or as its means. Its understood subject must be the subject of the main clause.
TL: En relisant le contrat, Léa a repéré une contradiction.
EN: While rereading the contract, Léa spotted a contradiction.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Léa a rassuré le client en détaillant chaque étape.
EN: Léa reassured the client by explaining each step in detail.
[PAUSE:900]
The first gerund supplies the setting; the second explains how the result was achieved.
SHOW: en + participle -> while doing / by doing
Flip for tout en and subject attachment.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Tout en highlights an accompanying contrast
REF: 17.9.4

Tout en emphasizes that one event continues alongside another and often adds concession. Because en fixes the gerund to the main-clause subject, adding or removing it can change who performs the action.
TL: Tout en reconnaissant l'incertitude, nous maintenons notre recommandation.
EN: While acknowledging the uncertainty, we are maintaining our recommendation.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: J'ai aperçu Nora en traversant la place.
EN: I caught sight of Nora while I was crossing the square.
[PAUSE:900]
Without en, a bare present participle can attach more freely and may become ambiguous. Use the gerund when the main subject is the intended controller and the relation is simultaneous, causal, or concessive.
SHOW: tout en -> sustained contrast · en -> main-subject attachment
