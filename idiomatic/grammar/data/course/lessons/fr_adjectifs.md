## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A French adjective carries position and agreement
REF: 4, 4.1

An adjective can modify a noun directly or describe it through a verb. In either role, it normally agrees with the noun in gender and number.
TL: Une analyse détaillée reste nécessaire.
EN: A detailed analysis remains necessary.
[PAUSE:900]
Both détaillée beside the noun and nécessaire after rester describe the feminine singular analyse. Their positions differ; their agreement does not.
SHOW: identify modified noun → choose position → apply gender and number
Flip for the default position beside a noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The productive default is after the noun
REF: 4.1.1

Most French adjectives follow the noun. Colours, national or geographic origin, shape, affiliation, and participial descriptions strongly favor that position.
TL: Ils ont restauré une façade italienne couverte de mosaïques bleues.
EN: They restored an Italian façade covered with blue mosaics.
[PAUSE:900]
Nationality adjectives are lower-case when they remain adjectives. A corresponding noun denoting a person takes a capital letter.
TL: Cette architecte est italienne; c'est une Italienne de Turin.
EN: This architect is Italian; she is an Italian woman from Turin.
SHOW: ordinary default · colour · origin · shape · participle → after noun

[CARD]
TITLE: Learn the small prenominal set as lexical behavior
REF: 4.1.2

Frequent, often short adjectives such as beau, bon, grand, gros, jeune, joli, mauvais, nouveau, petit and vieux commonly precede the noun. Their placement is part of the lexical pattern, not a translation of English order.
TL: Une jeune chercheuse a proposé une nouvelle méthode.
EN: A young researcher proposed a new method.
[PAUSE:900]
A cardinal number comes before other prenominal adjectives.
TL: Les deux premiers résultats sont encourageants.
EN: The first two results are encouraging.
SHOW: number → ordinary prenominal adjective → noun
Flip for adjectives whose position changes the message.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Position can select a different sense
REF: 4.1.3, 4.1.4

Some adjectives contribute a classifying or figurative meaning before the noun and a literal description after it. Store the contrast with a phrase, not as two interchangeable placements.
TL: Une ancienne collègue habite dans un quartier ancien.
EN: A former colleague lives in an old neighborhood.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ce pauvre homme vit dans un village pauvre.
EN: This unfortunate man lives in a poor village.
[PAUSE:1000]
Other normally postnominal adjectives may move forward for rhythm, emphasis, or style without a stable change of meaning.
SHOW: fixed sense contrast → memorize phrase · stylistic movement → read context

[CARD]
TITLE: Several adjectives form a French order, not an English stack
REF: 4.1.5

With several adjectives before the noun, cardinal numbers normally come first. Long strings are eased by coordination or by placing suitable adjectives after the noun.
TL: Les trois premières grandes salles donnent sur la cour.
EN: The first three large rooms overlook the courtyard.
[PAUSE:900]
Several postnominal classifiers often unfold outward from the noun in the reverse of their English order.
TL: Une politique économique européenne exige un accord commun.
EN: A European economic policy requires a common agreement.
SHOW: number first · avoid crowded strings · noun → nearer class → wider class
Flip for adjectives made heavier by their own modifiers.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A long modifier can pull a short adjective after the noun
REF: 4.1.6, 4.1.7

An adjective that normally precedes can follow when an adverb or prepositional phrase makes the adjective group heavy. The shift keeps the noun phrase easier to parse.
TL: Ils ont recruté un ingénieur jeune pour un poste aussi exigeant.
EN: They recruited an engineer young for such a demanding position.
[PAUSE:900]
After a numbered noun, an informal de may introduce the adjective and can imply that the numbered items form only part of a larger set.
TL: Nous avons retenu deux candidatures de solides.
EN: We retained two solid applications, among a larger group.
SHOW: heavier adjective group → later position · numbered subset → optional de
Fourteen clean placement and combination drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Predicate adjectives agree with the participant they describe
REF: 4.2

After être, sembler, paraître, devenir, rester and related verbs, the adjective agrees with the subject. With opinion or result verbs, it may instead agree with the direct object.
TL: Les consignes semblent claires, mais le comité les juge trop longues.
EN: The instructions seem clear, but the committee considers them too long.
[PAUSE:900]
Claires describes the subject les consignes. Longues describes the object les, which retrieves the same feminine plural noun.
TL: Cette mise à jour a rendu les données accessibles.
EN: This update made the data accessible.
SHOW: subject-oriented verb → subject agreement · object-oriented result → object agreement
Flip for complements licensed by an adjective.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The adjective determines whether its complement uses à or de
REF: 4.3, 4.4

Learn an adjective together with its complement pattern: prêt à, facile à, fier de, responsable de. English wording cannot reliably choose the French link.
TL: Léa est prête à répondre et fière de son équipe.
EN: Léa is ready to answer and proud of her team.
[PAUSE:900]
After quelque chose, quelqu'un, rien, personne, quoi and similar indefinite or negative expressions, de links an invariable masculine-singular adjective.
TL: Nous cherchons quelqu'un de disponible, mais personne de nouveau n'a répondu.
EN: We are looking for someone available, but no one new has replied.
SHOW: store adjective + complement · indefinite expression + de + invariant adjective

[CARD]
TITLE: An article can turn an adjective into a noun phrase
REF: 4.5, 3.2.25

French freely uses an adjective as a noun when the missing noun is recoverable. Its article and ending still mark the intended gender and number.
TL: Parmi ces dossiers, gardez les urgents et archivez les anciens.
EN: Among these files, keep the urgent ones and archive the old ones.
[PAUSE:900]
An adjectival one does not trigger en as a direct object. A remaining number or quantifier does.
TL: Je prends les rouges, mais je n'en prends que deux.
EN: I am taking the red ones, but I am taking only two of them.
SHOW: article + adjective → noun phrase · surviving quantity → en
Flip for adjectives that behave as adverbs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A small adverbial adjective set remains invariable
REF: 4.6, 6.1.7

In expressions such as parler bas, coûter cher, voir clair and travailler dur, the adjective modifies the verb and remains invariable.
TL: Ces réparations coûtent cher, mais les techniciennes travaillent dur.
EN: These repairs cost a lot, but the technicians work hard.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not infer agreement merely because a feminine or plural subject is nearby. First decide whether the word describes a participant or the manner of the event.
TL: Après l'annonce, elles ont parlé bas.
EN: After the announcement, they spoke quietly.
SHOW: describes noun → agree · modifies event → fixed adverbial form
Six clean substantivized-adjective and adverbial-form drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Feminine forms range from silent spelling to audible reshaping
REF: 4.7, 4.7.1, 4.7.2, 4.7.3

The default feminine adds written e, but the spoken result depends on the final sound. Some families double a consonant, add a grave accent, or turn a nasal vowel into an oral vowel plus consonant.
TL: Un rapport complet accompagne une note complète.
EN: A complete report accompanies a complete note.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Un débat ancien a révélé une inquiétude ancienne.
EN: An old debate revealed an old concern.
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat productive families as spelling-and-sound mappings rather than as a single silent-e rule.
SHOW: base form → family change → feminine agreement
Flip for the most visible lexical alternations.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Irregular families still reveal recurring patterns
REF: 4.7.4, 4.7.5

Final consonants and syllables may change: doux becomes douce, blanc becomes blanche, public becomes publique, and heureux becomes heureuse. Adjectives already ending in e normally show no gender change.
TL: Le débat public a provoqué une réaction publique très vive.
EN: The public debate caused a very strong public reaction.
[PAUSE:900]
Beau, nouveau and vieux have special masculine forms before a vowel or silent h: bel, nouvel and vieil. These are masculine liaison forms, not feminine forms.
TL: Un vieil immeuble jouxte une vieille usine.
EN: An old apartment building stands next to an old factory.
SHOW: lexical family · final sound · vowel-facing masculine form

[CARD]
TITLE: Plural spelling begins with s, then checks the ending
REF: 4.8

Most adjectives add s in the plural. Forms already ending in s or x do not change; adjectives in eau normally add x, and many in al change to aux.
TL: Des rapports précis décrivent de nouveaux partenariats internationaux.
EN: Precise reports describe new international partnerships.
[PAUSE:900]
Some frequent al adjectives, including banal and fatal, take ordinary s instead. Agreement is therefore both a productive rule and a lexical checkpoint.
TL: Ils ont corrigé plusieurs détails banals mais potentiellement fatals.
EN: They corrected several ordinary but potentially fatal details.
SHOW: default +s · final s/x unchanged · -eau → -eaux · many -al → -aux
Flip for agreement with more than one noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Coordination makes meaning choose the agreement target
REF: 4.9

An adjective can agree with the closest coordinated noun or with the coordinated group. Group agreement is plural and traditionally masculine if any member is masculine.
TL: Une veste et un pantalon noirs étaient posés sur la chaise.
EN: A black jacket and trousers were lying on the chair.
[PAUSE:900]
With de, meaning determines which noun the adjective modifies. Gens is exceptional: a preceding adjective can be feminine plural, while a following or predicate adjective is masculine plural.
TL: Ces bonnes gens sont restés patients.
EN: These good people remained patient.
SHOW: determine semantic target · group → plural · gens changes by position

[CARD]
TITLE: Invariable and compound forms require an internal parse
REF: 4.10, 4.11

Some noun-derived colour terms and other fixed adjectives remain unchanged. In adjective compounds, coordinated adjectives may both agree, while an adverbial first element remains invariable.
TL: Des vestes orange côtoyaient des solutions techniquement complexes.
EN: Orange jackets were displayed beside technically complex solutions.
[PAUSE:900]
Compound colour descriptions normally remain invariable as a unit.
TL: Elle portait une veste vert pomme et avait les yeux bleu-vert.
EN: She wore an apple-green jacket and had blue-green eyes.
SHOW: parse each component → decide which component can agree
Flip for demi, nu and mi.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Position controls agreement in several prefix-like compounds
REF: 4.11.4

Before a noun, demi and nu are invariable and hyphenated. After the noun, they agree; demi then marks gender but not plural number.
TL: La réunion a duré deux heures et demie après une demi-heure d'attente.
EN: The meeting lasted two and a half hours after a half-hour wait.
[PAUSE:900]
Mi appears only before the noun, remains invariable, and is linked by a hyphen.
TL: À mi-parcours, les coureurs avaient les jambes à demi engourdies.
EN: Halfway through, the runners had half-numb legs.
SHOW: before noun → demi-/nu-/mi- invariant · after noun → demi or nu agrees
Eight clean agreement and compound-form translations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Comparatives wrap the ordinary adjective in plus, moins or aussi
REF: 4.12.1

Plus and moins make unequal comparisons; aussi makes equality and often becomes si after negation. The adjective keeps its normal position and continues to agree.
TL: Nous cherchons une solution plus simple que la précédente.
EN: We are looking for a simpler solution than the previous one.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Cette option n'est pas si coûteuse qu'on le craignait.
EN: This option is not as expensive as people feared.
[PAUSE:1000]
Que introduces the comparison target. Do not move an adjective merely because English uses an -er form.
SHOW: plus/moins/aussi + adjective + que · after negation, aussi often → si
Flip for bon, mauvais and the adjective-adverb boundary.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Meilleur is an adjective; mieux is an adverb
REF: 4.12.1, 5.6.6

Meilleur replaces comparative bon and agrees with a noun. Mieux compares how an action is performed and does not agree.
TL: Cette équipe propose de meilleures solutions et travaille mieux sous pression.
EN: This team proposes better solutions and works better under pressure.
[PAUSE:900]
Both pire and plus mauvais can compare mauvais. Pire is especially natural when both alternatives are already bad or when an abstract judgment is at stake.
TL: Leur second compromis serait pire que le premier.
EN: Their second compromise would be worse than the first.
SHOW: better noun quality → meilleur · better action → mieux · worse → pire or plus mauvais
Ten clean comparative transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: A superlative adds an agreeing definite article
REF: 4.12.2

French forms the superlative with le, la or les plus or moins. If the adjective follows the noun, both the noun and the superlative adjective carry a definite article.
TL: Nous avons réservé la plus petite salle du bâtiment.
EN: We booked the smallest room in the building.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ils ont choisi la route la moins longue de la région.
EN: They chose the least long route in the region.
[PAUSE:1000]
The comparison group usually follows de, not dans. A lengthened superlative may follow even when the bare adjective usually precedes.
SHOW: le/la/les + plus/moins + adjective · postnominal → repeat article · group → de
Flip for irregular and absolute superlatives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Irregular superlatives follow the same agreement frame
REF: 4.12.2, 4.14

Le meilleur and le pire supply the superlatives of bon and mauvais. Le moindre is favored in fixed expressions and with abstract nouns, while a concrete smallest item normally uses le plus petit.
TL: C'est la meilleure option au moindre coût.
EN: It is the best option at the lowest cost.
[PAUSE:900]
An absolute judgment can use des plus plus an agreeing adjective. With a singular noun, modern usage often favors a plural adjective.
TL: Leur proposition est d'une clarté des plus remarquables.
EN: Their proposal is exceptionally clear.
SHOW: meilleur · pire · moindre for abstractions · des plus + adjective

[CARD]
TITLE: A superlative can also frame a claim about uniqueness
REF: 4.13, 4.14

A relative clause after a superlative commonly takes the subjunctive when the speaker presents the referent as uniquely unsurpassed.
TL: C'est le dossier le plus complet que nous ayons reçu.
EN: It is the most complete file we have received.
[PAUSE:900]
The indicative is available when the clause merely states a practical limit and makes no claim that nothing better exists.
TL: Nous avons choisi le plus grand bureau que nous avons pu faire entrer par la porte.
EN: We chose the largest desk that we were able to get through the door.
[PAUSE:1000]
Read the intended claim: unique ranking invites the subjunctive; a factual constraint supports the indicative.
SHOW: unsurpassed claim → subjunctive · practical factual limit → indicative
Flip for compact absolute-superlative alternatives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: French has several ways to say as emphatic as possible
REF: 4.14

Alongside des plus, French can use tout ce qu'il y a de plus, on ne peut plus, or a superlative followed by possible. In the last construction, possible remains invariable.
TL: Le raisonnement est on ne peut plus clair.
EN: The reasoning could not be clearer.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Nous avons retenu les formulations les plus simples possible.
EN: We retained the simplest possible wordings.
[PAUSE:1000]
These are lexical frames for an absolute degree, not ordinary comparisons with an explicit second term.
SHOW: des plus · tout ce qu'il y a de plus · on ne peut plus · superlative + possible
Seven clean superlative transformations follow.
