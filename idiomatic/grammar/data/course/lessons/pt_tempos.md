## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: The present covers current facts, habits, and general truths
REF: 18.1.1

Use the simple present for a state that holds now, a repeated routine, or a fact presented as generally valid. Time expressions reveal which reading is intended.
TL: Moro em Recife. · Ela corre todas as manhãs.
EN: I live in Recife. · She runs every morning.
[PAUSE:900]
The same form can report what normally happens even when the surrounding conversation is about a particular day.
TL: Normalmente saímos às seis. · O banco abre às dez.
EN: We normally leave at six. · The bank opens at ten.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: state now · repeated habit · general fact -> simple present
Flip for two important present-tense meanings that English often expresses differently.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Portuguese keeps the present with duration and some future reference
REF: 18.1.1

When an action began earlier and still holds, Portuguese commonly uses the present with há, faz, or desde. English often uses a present perfect instead.
TL: Estudo português há dois anos. · Faz meses que não a vejo.
EN: I have studied Portuguese for two years. · I have not seen her for months.
[PAUSE:900]
The present can also point ahead for a timetable or a spontaneous decision.
TL: O voo sai amanhã às oito. · Mais tarde eu te ligo.
EN: The flight leaves tomorrow at eight. · I will call you later.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: continuing duration -> present · schedule/spontaneous future -> present

[CARD]
TITLE: The preterite presents a past event as bounded and complete
REF: 18.1.3

Choose the preterite for a one-off event, a stated number of repetitions, or a period viewed as finished. The length of the event does not decide the tense; its boundary does.
TL: Paulo caiu. · Fui lá três vezes. · Moraram vinte anos no exterior.
EN: Paulo fell. · I went there three times. · They lived abroad for twenty years.
[PAUSE:900]
Portuguese also uses the preterite where English may use a present perfect for a completed experience, especially with já, nunca, or a finished result.
TL: Você já almoçou? · Nunca provei isso.
EN: Have you eaten lunch yet? · I have never tried that.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: one event · finite count · completed period/experience -> preterite
Flip before choosing a compound perfect merely because English has have.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The Portuguese present perfect signals recurrence up to now
REF: 18.2.1, 18.2.2

Ter in the present plus a participle normally presents repeated or recurring activity over a recent span. It is not the default form for one completed event.
TL: Tenho lido muito ultimamente. · Eles têm discutido com frequência.
EN: I have been reading a lot lately. · They have been arguing frequently.
[PAUSE:900]
For an event already completed, use the preterite. For an event completed before another past reference point, use tinha or havia plus the participle.
TL: Já li o relatório. · Ela disse que tinha enviado a mensagem.
EN: I have read the report. · She said she had sent the message.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: recurring up to now -> tenho feito · completed event -> fiz · earlier past -> tinha feito

[CARD]
TITLE: Compound forms order one event relative to another time point
REF: 18.2.2, 18.2.3, 18.2.4

Use tinha plus a participle for an event already complete before another past event. Havia is a more formal alternative in this pluperfect construction.
TL: Quando cheguei, eles já tinham saído. · O jornal informou que o voo havia sido cancelado.
EN: When I arrived, they had already left. · The newspaper reported that the flight had been cancelled.
[PAUSE:900]
In speech, the preterite sometimes replaces a pluperfect when the order is already obvious, but tinha feito states the earlier-past relation explicitly.
TL: Foi então que percebi que tinha esquecido a chave.
EN: It was then that I realized I had forgotten the key.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: earlier completed event + later past reference -> tinha/havia + participle
Flip for compound forms that point forward or imagine an unreal result.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Future and conditional perfects belong mainly to careful writing
REF: 18.2.3, 18.2.4

Ter in the future plus a participle means will have done. Ter in the conditional plus a participle means would have done.
TL: Até amanhã, terão terminado. · Sem a sua ajuda, eu teria desistido.
EN: By tomorrow, they will have finished. · Without your help, I would have given up.
[PAUSE:900]
Both are more characteristic of formal writing. Conversation often replaces a perfect conditional with a pluperfect when the hypothetical relation remains clear.
TL: Se soubesse, tinha avisado você.
EN: If I had known, I would have warned you.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: terá feito = will have done · teria feito = would have done · speech may use tinha feito

[CARD]
TITLE: The imperfect supplies past habits, states, and background
REF: 18.1.2

Use the imperfect for an old routine or an action repeated an indefinite number of times. It often corresponds to English used to.
TL: Quando criança, eu nadava todo dia. · Meus avós falavam italiano.
EN: As a child, I swam every day. · My grandparents used to speak Italian.
[PAUSE:900]
It also sets the scene with descriptions, time, weather, age, and ongoing states rather than advancing the sequence of events.
TL: Era verão, fazia calor e a rua estava vazia.
EN: It was summer, it was hot, and the street was empty.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: past habit/state/background -> imperfect
Flip to put background and completed events into the same narrative.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Imperfect frames the scene; preterite advances the event line
REF: 18.1.2, 18.1.3

In a narrative, the imperfect presents what was already in progress or describes the circumstances. The preterite marks the bounded event that occurred within that frame.
TL: Eu esperava o ônibus quando começou a chover.
EN: I was waiting for the bus when it began to rain.
[PAUSE:900]
Habit versus finite count gives the same contrast. A duration can still take the preterite when the speaker presents it as complete.
TL: Ela ia sempre à praia. · Naquele mês, foi três vezes. · Moramos lá por dez anos.
EN: She always went to the beach. · That month, she went three times. · We lived there for ten years.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: ongoing frame/indefinite repetition -> imperfect · bounded foreground/finite repetition -> preterite

[CARD]
TITLE: Estar plus a gerund makes an action explicitly continuous
REF: 18.3.1, 18.3.2, 18.3.3

The tense of estar locates an action in progress. The present continuous describes activity underway now; the imperfect continuous gives an ongoing past backdrop.
TL: Estou procurando emprego. · Estávamos dormindo quando o telefone tocou.
EN: I am looking for work. · We were sleeping when the phone rang.
[PAUSE:900]
Esteve plus a gerund can present a finite stretch spent doing something, while the simple preterite may suggest the action itself was completed.
TL: Ontem estive lendo o livro. · Ontem li o livro.
EN: Yesterday I spent time reading the book. · Yesterday I read the book.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: está fazendo = underway now · estava fazendo = ongoing past frame · esteve fazendo = bounded stretch
Flip to choose a natural future construction by register.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Continuous and simple forms give different viewpoints
REF: 18.1.2, 18.1.3, 18.3.2, 18.3.3

An imperfect can itself present an unfolding background, especially in writing. Estava plus a gerund makes that progressive viewpoint explicit.
TL: Eles jantavam quando cheguei. · Eles estavam jantando quando cheguei.
EN: They were having dinner when I arrived. · They were having dinner when I arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
With a bounded past stretch, esteve fazendo highlights time spent in the activity; fez presents the event as a completed whole.
TL: Esteve trabalhando no projeto por um ano. · Fez o projeto em um ano.
EN: He spent a year working on the project. · He completed the project in a year.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: imperfect = unfolding background · estava fazendo = explicit progress · esteve fazendo = bounded stretch

[CARD]
TITLE: Conversation normally avoids the synthetic future
REF: 18.1.4, 18.4.1

The inflected future is formal and common in reporting or official prose. Everyday Brazilian Portuguese usually chooses the present of ir plus the infinitive.
TL: O governo anunciará a decisão. · O governo vai anunciar a decisão.
EN: The government will announce the decision. · The government is going to announce the decision.
[PAUSE:900]
A simple present is also natural for a scheduled event or when context already makes the future clear.
TL: Ela vai viajar na segunda. · Ela viaja na segunda.
EN: She is going to travel on Monday. · She travels on Monday.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: formal/news -> anunciará · conversation -> vai anunciar · schedule -> anuncia
Flip to distinguish a genuine future action in progress from an inflated service formula.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A future continuous needs an action genuinely in progress
REF: 18.3.4

Ir plus estar plus a gerund is the conversational future continuous when an action will be underway at a future time. The inflected future of estar is more formal.
TL: Ao meio-dia, ele vai estar almoçando. · Nesse horário, estaremos viajando.
EN: At midday, he will be having lunch. · At that time, we will be travelling.
[PAUSE:900]
Customer-service language often extends this pattern to a single non-continuous act. That established register-specific usage is known as gerundismo and need not be copied into neutral conversation.
TL: Vou transferir a sua ligação. · Vou estar transferindo a sua ligação.
EN: I will transfer your call. · I will be transferring your call.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: future action underway -> vai estar fazendo · single act -> normally vai fazer

[CARD]
TITLE: The conditional is precise but often formal in conversation
REF: 18.1.5, 18.4.2

The synthetic conditional expresses would and remains useful when the conditional meaning needs to be unmistakable, especially with ser and ter or in careful writing.
TL: Se fosse possível, eu aceitaria. · Isso seria melhor.
EN: If it were possible, I would accept. · That would be better.
[PAUSE:900]
Speech commonly replaces it with the imperfect of the main verb or with ia plus the infinitive. The same alternatives appear in reported future-in-the-past contexts.
TL: Se fosse possível, eu aceitava. · Ela disse que ia voltar.
EN: If it were possible, I would accept. · She said she would return.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: formal/explicit -> faria · conversational -> fazia / ia fazer
Flip for the conditional's special evidential use in reporting.

[SIDE]
TITLE: News-report conditionals can distance the speaker from a claim
REF: 18.1.5, 18.2.4

In reporting, a conditional can mark information as alleged or unconfirmed rather than hypothetical.
TL: O suspeito teria várias contas no exterior.
EN: The suspect allegedly has several accounts abroad.
[PAUSE:900]
The perfect conditional similarly presents an alleged earlier event. Outside that reporting use, teria feito normally means would have done and is more characteristic of careful writing than casual speech.
TL: Segundo a polícia, ele teria deixado o país.
EN: According to the police, he reportedly left the country.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: report + conditional -> unconfirmed claim · teria feito -> alleged past or would have done

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose a tense by boundary, repetition, viewpoint, and register
REF: 18.1, 18.2, 18.3, 18.4

First locate the event in present, past, or future time. Then ask whether it is a state or habit, an ongoing frame, a bounded event, or recurrence extending to now.
[PAUSE:850]
For past narration, use the imperfect for background and indefinite repetition, and the preterite for completed foreground events. Reserve tenho feito for recurrence up to now.
TL: fazia · estava fazendo · fez · tem feito
EN: used to do/was doing · was doing · did · has been doing repeatedly
[PAUSE:900]
Finally, check register: conversation favors vou fazer and often ia fazer; formal reporting more readily uses farei, faria, and the evidential conditional.
TL: vou fazer · farei · ia fazer · faria
EN: I will do, conversational · formal future · conversational would · formal conditional
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: time -> boundary/repetition/viewpoint -> conversational or formal register
Flip only after every choice has a time cue and a meaning.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A five-question check resolves the common tense traps
REF: 18.1, 18.2, 18.3, 18.4

One: is the event still true now? Two: if it is past, is it background or bounded? Three: does a stated duration continue into the present?
[PAUSE:850]
Four: does the speaker mean one completed experience or repeated activity up to now? Five: does the future or conditional need a formal written form?
[PAUSE:900]
Read the time expression last. Há, desde, já, ontem, sempre, três vezes, and amanhã often confirm the viewpoint but do not replace the meaning test.
TL: há dois anos · já fiz · fazia sempre · fiz três vezes · vou fazer amanhã
EN: for two years · I have done · I used to do · I did three times · I will do tomorrow
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: continuing now? · background or bounded? · one event or recurrence? · which register?
