## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Spelling preserves the word family
SVG: de_rechtschreibung_c01f.svg
REF: 21.1.1, 21.1.2, 21.1.3

German spelling is systematic, but it does not copy every sound on the surface. At the end of a word or syllable, written b, d and g are normally heard as p, t and k. The spelling keeps the consonant that reappears before a vowel:
TL: Der Dieb trug einen schweren Koffer.
EN: The thief carried a heavy suitcase.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Zwei Diebe warteten vor dem Bahnhof.
EN: Two thieves were waiting outside the station.
[PAUSE:1100]
Position controls other consonants too. The two sounds written with c plus h depend mainly on what comes before; initial s before a vowel is voiced, while initial s before p or t begins with a sh-sound. And n plus g is one nasal sound, never a nasal followed by a separate g.
TL: Sonja spielt sonntags Schach.
EN: Sonja plays chess on Sundays.
SHOW: spelling preserves families · position controls sound
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Vowels leave length clues
SVG: de_rechtschreibung_c01b.svg
REF: 21.1.4

Vowel length is less perfectly predictable, but the spelling gives you a strong map. A vowel before a doubled consonant is short. Before one consonant it is usually long. A silent h, a doubled vowel, and i plus e are further length signals. The sharp-s letter counts as one consonant; c plus k does the work of a doubled k.
TL: Der Rat tagte mitten im Sommer.
EN: The council met in the middle of summer.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Sie riet mir, die Mitte des Saals zu meiden.
EN: She advised me to avoid the middle of the hall.
[PAUSE:1100]
Clusters are less reliable, and a vowel before c plus h can be long or short. That contrast belongs to the individual word:
TL: Das Dach des Turms ist ungewöhnlich hoch.
EN: The roof of the tower is unusually high.
SHOW: doubled consonant = short · h / double vowel / ie = long
Next, hear how stress exposes a word's internal architecture.

[CARD]
TITLE: Stress reveals the architecture
REF: 21.1.6

In native vocabulary, stress normally begins on the first syllable and stays on the same root when endings are added. Inseparable prefixes yield the stress to the root; separable prefixes normally claim it. That difference can separate two meanings that otherwise use the same letters.
TL: Der Fahrer umfährt die Baustelle.
EN: The driver goes around the construction site.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Fahrer fährt den Poller um.
EN: The driver knocks over the bollard.
[PAUSE:1100]
Foreign words often place stress later. Some endings have their own patterns, and a plural ending can even pull the stress one syllable to the right:
TL: Der Professor begrüßt mehrere Professoren.
EN: The professor greets several professors.
SHOW: root, prefix, origin and ending all guide stress
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Let the vowel choose ss or ß
SVG: de_rechtschreibung_c02b.svg
REF: 21.4.1, 21.1.4

The choice between double s and the sharp-s letter is not visual decoration. It records vowel length. After a short vowel, write double s. After a long vowel or a diphthong, write the sharp-s letter.
TL: Sie wusste, dass der Fluss eiskalt war.
EN: She knew that the river was ice-cold.
[PAUSE:1100]
TL: Draußen grüßte er die große Reisegruppe.
EN: Outside, he greeted the large tour group.
[PAUSE:1100]
This distinction is standard in Germany and Austria. Swiss standard spelling uses double s in both environments. In all-capital writing, the capital sharp-s letter is available, although double S remains common.
SHOW: short vowel → ss · long vowel or diphthong → ß
Now turn from sound to the grammatical work of capital letters.

[CARD]
TITLE: Capitalize the noun function
REF: 21.2.1

German capitalization follows grammatical function. Ordinary nouns begin with capitals, and any other part of speech takes a capital when the sentence turns it into a noun. Articles, prepositions with hidden articles, adjective endings and noun-like modifiers are your diagnostics.
TL: Nach langem Zögern entschied sie sich fürs Bleiben.
EN: After hesitating for a long time, she decided to stay.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wir erwarten wenig Erfreuliches, aber nichts Beunruhigendes.
EN: We expect little that is pleasing, but nothing alarming.
[PAUSE:1000]
Even a whole coordination can become one noun phrase:
TL: Das ständige Hin und Her erschöpft das ganze Team.
EN: The constant back-and-forth exhausts the whole team.
SHOW: noun job → capital · look for article and adjective endings
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A noun can lose its capital
REF: 21.2.2

The reverse process also happens. An old noun can settle into the language as a preposition, an adverb, an indefinite quantity, or part of a fixed predicate. Then it may lose both noun force and its capital.
TL: Die Vereinbarung bleibt kraft des Vertrags gültig.
EN: The agreement remains valid by virtue of the contract.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Morgens arbeite ich konzentriert, doch heute Abend mache ich früher Schluss.
EN: I work with focus in the mornings, but this evening I am finishing earlier.
[PAUSE:1100]
Time-of-day nouns after words meaning today, yesterday or tomorrow keep their capitals. A small special group of predicate words after copular verbs goes lowercase, while an ordinary noun use stays capitalized:
TL: Die Werkstatt ist pleite, doch die Leitung trägt keine Schuld.
EN: The workshop is bankrupt, but the management bears no blame.
SHOW: lexicalized preposition or adverb → small · true noun → capital
The next card handles the contrasts that syntax alone cannot settle.

[CARD]
TITLE: An omitted noun is still present
REF: 21.2.1

An inflected adjective does not automatically become a noun. If a nearby noun is understood, the adjective stays lowercase. But if the adjective names an abstract person, thing or quality in its own right, it takes a capital.
TL: Der graue Mantel ist teurer als der schwarze.
EN: The grey coat is more expensive than the black one.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wir brauchen etwas Dauerhaftes, nicht bloß das Billigste.
EN: We need something durable, not merely the cheapest thing.
[PAUSE:1100]
Superlatives with the contracted preposition and article are lowercase; noun-like superlatives after a definite article are capitalized:
TL: Von allen Vorschlägen war dieser am überzeugendsten, doch die Jury wählte nicht das Beste aus.
EN: Of all the proposals, this one was the most convincing, yet the jury did not choose the best one.
SHOW: understood noun → small · independent noun meaning → capital
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Names, languages and pronouns
REF: 21.2.1, 21.2.3

Proper-name adjectives divide into types. Indeclinable forms derived from place names keep a capital. Ordinary nationality adjectives and most forms with the adjective-forming suffix stay lowercase. A language used as a noun takes a capital.
TL: Die Berliner Galeristin erklärt italienische Malerei auf Englisch.
EN: The Berlin gallery owner explains Italian painting in English.
[PAUSE:1100]
The formal second-person pronoun and all its forms always take capitals:
TL: Haben Sie Ihren Ausweis dabei, oder soll ich Ihnen helfen?
EN: Do you have your identity card with you, or shall I help you?
[PAUSE:1100]
In letters, informal second-person forms may begin either way. A capital can mark the personal address without turning the pronoun into the formal form:
TL: Im Brief schrieb Lea: „Kannst Du meinen Vorschlag prüfen?“
EN: In the letter, Lea wrote, “Can you review my proposal?”
SHOW: place-name -er → capital · language noun → capital · adjective → small
Three vetted capitalization exercises follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: One concept pulls words together
SVG: de_rechtschreibung_c05f.svg
REF: 21.3, 21.3.1

The central spelling question is semantic: do the parts still carry independent meanings, or have they formed one lexical concept? Stress often reveals the answer. Separate words keep independent stress; a true compound has one main stress.
TL: Auch unter Zeitdruck kann sie gut schreiben.
EN: She can write well even under time pressure.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Bank wird uns den Betrag morgen gutschreiben.
EN: The bank will credit the amount to us tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1100]
A modifier test helps with adjective-plus-verb combinations. If the adjective keeps its ordinary meaning and can be intensified or compared, separation is likely. If the whole expression has an idiomatic meaning and resists that modification, write it together.
TL: Der lange Regen hat den Boden sehr weich gemacht.
EN: The prolonged rain made the ground very soft.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Ärztin hat den Patienten für zwei Wochen krankgeschrieben.
EN: The doctor signed the patient off sick for two weeks.
SHOW: independent meanings → separate · lexical concept → together
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Noun plus verb: test the noun
REF: 21.3.1

Most noun-plus-verb combinations remain two words. The noun keeps its capital and behaves like a normal object. A small closed group has lost that independence and acts as a separable prefix, so the infinitive is one lowercase word.
TL: Nach Feierabend spielt sie Klavier.
EN: After work, she plays the piano.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Tagung findet trotz des Streiks statt.
EN: The conference is taking place despite the strike.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir werden an der Abstimmung teilnehmen.
EN: We will take part in the vote.
[PAUSE:1100]
A handful of combinations permit both analyses. And combinations with the copular verb are always separate, even when the first element is a familiar prefix elsewhere:
TL: Nach Mitternacht wird im Büro niemand mehr da sein.
EN: After midnight, no one will be in the office anymore.
SHOW: live noun = separate and capital · fossilized prefix = together and small
Next, apply the boundary test to larger verbal and adjectival structures.

[CARD]
TITLE: Result, construction, or two verbs?
REF: 21.3.1

When an adjective names the result produced by an action, current spelling often permits either one word or two, with the separate spelling generally preferred. The writer is choosing whether to foreground the result or the lexical unit.
TL: Die Technikerin wird den Sensor sauber machen.
EN: The technician will clean the sensor.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Technikerin wird den Sensor saubermachen.
EN: The technician will clean the sensor.
[PAUSE:1100]
Verb-plus-verb and participle-plus-verb combinations normally stay separate. A few established combinations allow both spellings, but separation is always the safe structural default.
TL: Das Kind möchte allein laufen lernen.
EN: The child would like to learn to walk on its own.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Schlüssel könnte im Gedränge verloren gehen.
EN: The key could get lost in the crowd.
SHOW: result adjective = often variable · verb plus verb = usually separate
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Phrases can fuse at several levels
REF: 21.3.2, 21.3.3, 21.3.4

Some preposition-plus-noun expressions have become single adverbs or prepositions. Others remain transparent phrases, and a useful middle group permits both spellings:
TL: Aufgrund des Nebels blieb der Hafen geschlossen.
EN: Because of the fog, the harbour remained closed.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Auf Grund des Nebels blieb der Hafen geschlossen.
EN: Because of the fog, the harbour remained closed.
[PAUSE:1100]
Participles fuse with a noun or adverb when the compound compresses an underlying phrase. Adjective combinations also fuse when both qualities form one concept or the first one grades the second.
TL: Die handgefertigten Teile lagen neben den staubbedeckten Geräten.
EN: The handmade parts lay beside the dust-covered devices.
[PAUSE:1000]
If one adjective merely defines another more closely, variation is possible:
TL: Der Bericht enthält schwer verständliche Details.
EN: The report contains details that are difficult to understand.
SHOW: fixed unit → fuse · transparent phrase → separate · middle zone → variation
Meaning-sensitive pairs come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Spacing can change the meaning
SVG: de_rechtschreibung_c07f.svg
REF: 21.3.5

With compounds built on words meaning so, how and where, spacing can switch the grammatical relationship. One word may be a conjunction or a settled adverb; two words let each part keep its own force.
TL: Sobald der Bericht vorliegt, rufe ich an.
EN: As soon as the report is available, I will call.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: So bald wird der Bericht nicht vorliegen.
EN: The report will not be available that soon.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Woanders finden wir sicher einen ruhigen Tisch.
EN: We will surely find a quiet table elsewhere.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wo anders als hier sollen wir suchen?
EN: Where else but here are we supposed to look?
[PAUSE:1100]
The conjunction meaning so that permits both one-word and two-word spellings. Most combinations with quantity words stay separate; the fused form is reserved for a special conjunction meaning as far as.
SHOW: one word = grammatical unit · two words = two live meanings
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Do not simplify the seam
REF: 21.4.2

German protects the structure of a word even when the result looks crowded. At a compound boundary, all letters survive. Three identical consonant letters in a row are not an error.
TL: Die Schifffahrtsgesellschaft schloss die dritte Messstelle.
EN: The shipping company closed the third measuring station.
[PAUSE:1100]
Elsewhere, spelling does simplify. Doubled vowel letters collapse under an umlaut, and nouns ending in two written vowels do not add yet another letter for the plural. The same economy appears in a few verb forms.
TL: Der Saal ist kleiner als die beiden Säle im Westflügel.
EN: The hall is smaller than the two halls in the west wing.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Am See knien drei Fotografen, obwohl ihre Knie schmerzen.
EN: Three photographers are kneeling by the lake although their knees hurt.
SHOW: compound seam → keep every letter · umlaut and vowel endings → no extra e
Now punctuation: German commas follow syntax, not breathing.

[CARD]
TITLE: The comma maps clause structure
SVG: de_rechtschreibung_c08f.svg
REF: 21.5, 21.5.1

A German comma marks a grammatical boundary. It is not a written pause. That is why an adverb inside a clause does not get English-style commas around it:
TL: Gestern hat sie den Vertrag überraschend doch noch unterschrieben.
EN: Yesterday, surprisingly, she signed the contract after all.
[PAUSE:1100]
Coordinated clauses or phrases linked by the plain conjunctions meaning and or or normally need no comma. A comma is required before the contrasting and explanatory coordinators.
TL: Er blieb im Büro und sie fuhr allein nach Hause.
EN: He stayed at the office and she drove home alone.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Zug fiel aus, aber wir erreichten den Termin trotzdem.
EN: The train was cancelled, but we still made the appointment.
SHOW: adverb inside clause = no comma · contrast coordinator = comma
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Subordinate clauses get bookends
SVG: de_rechtschreibung_c08b.svg
REF: 21.5.2

Every subordinate clause is separated from the surrounding sentence by commas. Put it first and the comma closes it; put it last and the comma opens it. Embed it, and you need both edges.
TL: Obwohl die Frist abgelaufen war, nahm die Behörde den Antrag an.
EN: Although the deadline had passed, the authority accepted the application.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Behörde nahm den Antrag an, obwohl die Frist abgelaufen war.
EN: The authority accepted the application although the deadline had passed.
[PAUSE:1100]
The rule also covers unintroduced reported speech and conditional clauses without a conjunction:
TL: Die Gutachterin sagte, sie habe keine Einwände.
EN: The assessor said that she had no objections.
SHOW: subordinate clause = comma at every outer edge
Infinitive groups require a more selective decision.

[CARD]
TITLE: An optional comma can carry meaning
SVG: de_rechtschreibung_c09f.svg
REF: 21.5.3

With an ordinary infinitive or participial group, the comma is generally optional. Short groups usually go without one. Use the comma when it exposes the structure or prevents a modifier from attaching to the wrong action.
TL: Mara versprach heute, den Bericht zu senden.
EN: It was today that Mara promised to send the report.
[PAUSE:1100]
Here, the time word belongs to the promise. Move the comma left, and the same time word belongs to the sending:
TL: Mara versprach, heute den Bericht zu senden.
EN: Mara promised that she would send the report today.
[PAUSE:1100]
A detached participial group can likewise be framed when the separation helps the reader:
TL: Von der Nachricht überrascht, schwieg sie zunächst.
EN: Surprised by the news, she remained silent at first.
SHOW: optional comma = choose the intended attachment
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Three anchors normally require the comma
REF: 21.5.3

With infinitive clauses, three anchors normally require the comma. First, one of the special introducers meaning in order to, without, instead of or except. Second, a governing noun. Third, an anticipatory pronoun or a prepositional adverb that points forward to the infinitive.
TL: Er ging früher, um den letzten Bus zu erreichen.
EN: He left earlier in order to catch the last bus.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sie hatte den festen Vorsatz, niemanden zu unterbrechen.
EN: She had the firm intention not to interrupt anyone.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir freuen uns darauf, das Ergebnis gemeinsam auszuwerten.
EN: We are looking forward to evaluating the result together.
[PAUSE:1100]
If a noun-dependent or pointer-dependent group shrinks to just to plus an infinitive, that comma can again be omitted. There is also one opposite pattern. An infinitive enclosed inside a chain of semi-auxiliary verbs never takes a comma:
TL: Sie wird den Konflikt diplomatisch zu lösen versuchen.
EN: She will try to resolve the conflict diplomatically.
SHOW: introducer · noun · pointer → comma · enclosed infinitive → none
One final card covers the remaining comma boundaries and marks.

[CARD]
TITLE: Commas also frame detached material
REF: 21.5.4, 21.5.5

Interjections, afterthoughts, explanations, appositions and parenthetical comments take commas when the writer presents them as separate units. A full apposition embedded in the sentence needs a comma on both sides.
TL: Frau Lehmann, die neue Leiterin, eröffnet die Sitzung.
EN: Ms Lehmann, the new director, is opening the meeting.
[PAUSE:1100]
Adjectives take a comma when they have equal weight and could be joined with and. No comma appears when the nearer adjective forms one unit with the noun.
TL: Wir brauchen einen kühlen, trockenen Raum.
EN: We need a cool, dry room.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Dort lagern empfindliche medizinische Geräte.
EN: Sensitive medical devices are stored there.
[PAUSE:1000]
Plain comparisons are not detached units:
TL: Der neue Raum ist heller als der alte.
EN: The new room is brighter than the old one.
SHOW: detached unit = commas · equal adjectives = comma · comparison = none
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Colons open speech; German quotes sit low
REF: 21.6.1, 21.6.2, 21.6.3, 21.6.4

After a reporting expression, German introduces direct speech with a colon. The opening quotation marks sit on the line; the closing marks sit high. Double quotation marks are the normal choice.
TL: Die Vorsitzende sagte: „Wir vertagen die Abstimmung.“
EN: The chairwoman said, “We are postponing the vote.”
[PAUSE:1100]
The semicolon exists, but German often chooses a comma or a full stop where English might reach for one:
TL: Der Himmel wurde dunkel, wir kehrten um.
EN: The sky grew dark; we turned back.
[PAUSE:1000]
Commands and exclamations traditionally take an exclamation mark, though a full stop can soften an ordinary command. A letter greeting usually ends in a comma, and the letter proper then begins lowercase:
TL: Liebe Nora, heute sende ich dir endlich die Unterlagen.
EN: Dear Nora, today I am finally sending you the documents.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Bitte schließen Sie die Tür!
EN: Please close the door!
SHOW: speech → colon + „…“ · command → ! · letter greeting, then lowercase
You now have one system: spelling preserves structure, and punctuation reveals it.
