## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Não goes directly before the verb it negates
REF: 14.1.1

Portuguese forms an ordinary verbal negative with não before the verb. It does not insert an equivalent of English do or did.
TL: A loja não abre hoje. Eu não encontrei a chave.
EN: The store is not opening today. I did not find the key.
[PAUSE:900]
Keep não with the verb even when a subject or time expression comes first.
TL: Amanhã nós não vamos trabalhar.
EN: Tomorrow we are not going to work.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: subject/time + não + verb · no extra auxiliary
Flip for the system that lets several negative words agree in one clause.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Portuguese negative concord is grammatical
REF: 14

Two or even three negative expressions can belong to one semantic negative. Portuguese does not cancel them into a positive.
TL: Eu não vi nada. Nós nunca contamos nada a ninguém.
EN: I did not see anything. We never told anyone anything.
[PAUSE:900]
When nada, ninguém, nunca, jamais, nem, or nenhum follows the verb, an earlier não or another negative word normally licenses it.
SHOW: não + verb + negative word · negative + verb + another negative
The position of the first negative decides whether não is needed.

[CARD]
TITLE: A negative word before the verb can replace não
REF: 14, 14.2.1, 14.3.1, 14.4.1

If nada, ninguém, nunca, or a nenhum phrase comes before the verb, that word itself makes the clause negative. Do not add não after it.
TL: Nada mudou. Ninguém telefonou. Nunca viajei sozinho.
EN: Nothing changed. No one called. I have never traveled alone.
[PAUSE:900]
After the verb, the same ideas normally require an earlier negative.
TL: Não mudou nada. Não telefonei para ninguém. Eu não viajei nunca.
EN: Nothing changed. I did not call anyone. I never traveled.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: negative + verb -> no não · verb + negative -> earlier não/negative required
Flip for the useful expressions built specifically with não.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Não can stand alone, form a tag, or combine with mais
REF: 14.1.2, 14.1.3, 14.1.4

When the verb is understood, não can answer or contrast on its own. The general confirmation tag is não é?, often reduced to né? in conversation.
TL: A Júlia vai; eu não. Você já enviou o arquivo, não é?
EN: Júlia is going; I am not. You have already sent the file, haven't you?
[PAUSE:900]
Não ... mais means not any more. Já não expresses no longer and often sounds slightly more formal.
TL: Ele não mora mais aqui. Ele já não mora aqui.
EN: He does not live here any more. He no longer lives here.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: understood verb -> não · confirmation -> não é?/né? · no longer -> não ... mais / já não

[CARD]
TITLE: Nada refers to things and can intensify a description
REF: 14.2.1, 14.2.2, 14.2.3, 14.2.4

Nada means nothing before the verb and anything after a negative. With an adjective, optional de can introduce the description.
TL: Nada aconteceu. Não aconteceu nada. Não há nada de estranho aqui.
EN: Nothing happened. Nothing happened. There is nothing strange here.
[PAUSE:900]
Mais nada and nada mais both mean nothing else. Nada can also make an adjective or adverb emphatically negative.
TL: Não preciso de mais nada. A prova não foi nada fácil.
EN: I do not need anything else. The test was not at all easy.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: thing -> nada · nothing else -> (mais) nada (mais) · not at all -> não ... nada + adjective/adverb
Flip from things to people.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ninguém is the negative word for people
REF: 14.3.1, 14.3.2

Ninguém means nobody before the verb and anybody after a negative. It is invariable and refers to people.
TL: Ninguém respondeu. Eu não conheço ninguém nesta cidade.
EN: Nobody answered. I do not know anybody in this city.
[PAUSE:900]
For nobody else, place mais before or after ninguém.
TL: Mais ninguém reclamou. Não convidamos ninguém mais.
EN: Nobody else complained. We did not invite anybody else.
[PAUSE:950]
Do not use ninguém for an absent thing; use nada or an agreeing nenhum phrase instead.
SHOW: person -> ninguém · nobody else -> mais ninguém / ninguém mais

[CARD]
TITLE: Nunca is neutral never; jamais is more emphatic
REF: 14.4.1, 14.4.2, 14.4.3, 14.5

Nunca commonly means never. In the normal pre-verbal position it already negates the clause.
TL: Eu nunca esqueci aquela conversa. Ela quase nunca se atrasa.
EN: I never forgot that conversation. She hardly ever arrives late.
[PAUSE:900]
Nunca mais means never again. Jamais is a stronger or more emphatic alternative to nunca.
TL: Nunca mais farei isso. Jamais aceitaremos essa condição.
EN: I will never do that again. We will never ever accept that condition.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: never -> nunca · hardly ever -> quase nunca · never again -> nunca mais · emphatic -> jamais

[SIDE]
TITLE: Nem means not even; nem sequer adds emphasis
REF: 14.6.1

Before the verb, nem itself can mean not even and needs no separate não.
TL: Eu nem percebi o erro. Ela nem respondeu.
EN: I did not even notice the error. She did not even answer.
[PAUSE:900]
After a negative clause, nem can add an excluded action or item. Nem sequer makes not even more emphatic.
TL: Não tive tempo nem de almoçar. Ele nem sequer pediu desculpas.
EN: I did not even have time for lunch. He did not even apologize.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: pre-verbal not even -> nem + verb · after a negative -> não ... nem · emphatic -> nem sequer

[CARD]
TITLE: Nem ... nem joins two rejected alternatives
REF: 14.6.2, 14.6.3, 14.6.4

Repeated nem means neither ... nor. When the pair comes before the verb, it supplies the clause's negation.
TL: Nem a Ana nem o Bruno vieram. Não trouxe nem café nem chá.
EN: Neither Ana nor Bruno came. I brought neither coffee nor tea.
[PAUSE:900]
Nem eu expresses negative agreement. Before sempre or todos, nem narrows the scope: not always or not all, rather than never or none.
TL: Eu não gostei. Nem eu. Nem sempre concordamos. Nem todos responderam.
EN: I did not like it. Neither did I. We do not always agree. Not everyone answered.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: neither A nor B -> nem A nem B · me neither -> nem eu · partial scope -> nem sempre/nem todos

[SIDE]
TITLE: Nenhum agrees, but its negated noun stays singular
REF: 14.7.1, 14.7.2, 14.7.3

Nenhum or nenhuma before a singular noun means no or neither. Even when English uses a plural noun, Portuguese normally keeps that noun singular.
TL: Nenhuma proposta foi aprovada. Não tivemos nenhum problema.
EN: No proposal was approved. We did not have any problems.
[PAUSE:900]
Placed after its noun, nenhum adds emphasis and still requires an earlier negative. Used alone, an agreeing form means none or neither.
TL: Não houve falha nenhuma. Quantas mensagens chegaram? Nenhuma.
EN: There was no failure at all. How many messages arrived? None.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: nenhum + M SG · nenhuma + F SG · emphatic -> não + verb + noun + nenhum/a

[CARD]
TITLE: Sem means without and does not itself negate a verb
REF: 14.8.1, 14.8.2, 14.8.3

Sem introduces what is absent. Other negative words can follow it without an added não because there is no negated verb inside the phrase.
TL: Ela saiu sem dizer nada. Trabalhamos sem nenhuma interrupção.
EN: She left without saying anything. We worked without any interruption.
[PAUSE:900]
In verbless labels, sem can correspond to English no. Estar sem means to have none available, while ficar sem describes losing access or going without.
TL: Sem comentários. Estou sem dinheiro. Ficamos sem internet.
EN: No comment. I have no money. We lost our internet connection.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: without -> sem · have none available -> estar sem · lose/go without -> ficar sem

[SIDE]
TITLE: Let position and referent choose the negative structure
REF: 14, 14.1, 14.2, 14.3, 14.4, 14.6, 14.7, 14.8

First identify the referent: nada for a thing, ninguém for a person, nenhum or nenhuma with a singular noun, and nunca or jamais for time.
[PAUSE:850]
Next locate that negative relative to the verb. Before the verb it normally replaces não; after the verb it normally needs an earlier não or negative word.
TL: Ninguém trouxe nada. Eu não trouxe nada para ninguém.
EN: Nobody brought anything. I did not bring anything for anyone.
[PAUSE:900]
Finally check special scope: nem for not even or neither, não ... mais for no longer, and sem for without.
SHOW: referent -> position -> licensing negative -> agreement/scope
Flip for a final contrast between English wording and Portuguese structure.

[CARD]
TITLE: Não ... mais and nunca mais describe different endpoints
REF: 14.1.4, 14.4.2

Não ... mais says that an earlier situation no longer holds. Nunca mais says that an event did not or will not happen again after a reference point.
TL: Ela não trabalha mais aqui. Depois da discussão, ela nunca mais voltou.
EN: She no longer works here. After the argument, she never came back.
[PAUSE:900]
Já não is a slightly more formal alternative for no longer; it does not by itself mean never again.
TL: Já não usamos aquele sistema.
EN: We no longer use that system.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: no longer -> não ... mais / já não · never again -> nunca mais
Flip for partial negatives that leave room for exceptions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Nem sempre and nem todos do not mean never or nobody
REF: 14.6.4

Nem before sempre, todo, or tudo narrows the negative scope. It rejects an absolute claim without denying every case.
TL: Nem sempre chove aqui. Nem todos os alunos faltaram.
EN: It does not always rain here. Not all the students were absent.
[PAUSE:900]
Contrast the stronger total negatives.
TL: Nunca chove aqui. Nenhum aluno faltou.
EN: It never rains here. No student was absent.
[PAUSE:950]
Choose partial scope when exceptions remain; choose nunca, ninguém, nada, or nenhum when the whole set is excluded.
SHOW: not always -> nem sempre · not all -> nem todos · never -> nunca · none -> nenhum/a

[CARD]
TITLE: English any often becomes a Portuguese negative word
REF: 14, 14.2.1, 14.3.1, 14.7.1

After an English negative, anything, anyone, and any plus a noun normally map to nada, ninguém, and nenhum or nenhuma in Portuguese.
TL: Não comprei nada. Não falei com ninguém. Não recebi nenhuma resposta.
EN: I did not buy anything. I did not speak to anyone. I did not receive any answer.
[PAUSE:900]
This is negative concord, not a word-for-word series of independent negatives. Preserve the one negative meaning while making each Portuguese element agree with it.
SHOW: not ... anything -> não ... nada · not ... anyone -> não ... ninguém · not ... any noun -> não ... nenhum/a + SG

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build a negative clause in four checks
REF: 14, 14.1, 14.2, 14.3, 14.4, 14.5, 14.6, 14.7, 14.8

Choose the main negative meaning. Select its word by referent and scope. Put the first negative in its natural position, then license every post-verbal negative with não or an earlier negative.
TL: Nunca mais contei nada a ninguém.
EN: I never told anyone anything again.
[PAUSE:900]
Finish by checking agreement for nenhum or nenhuma, the singular negated noun, and fixed expressions such as nem ... nem, quase nunca, and estar sem.
TL: Nenhum dos dois trouxe documento nenhum.
EN: Neither of the two brought any document at all.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: meaning -> negative word -> verb position -> concord -> agreement/fixed phrase
The source exercises close the unit by contrasting these decisions in short answers.
