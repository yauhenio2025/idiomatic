## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Do not translate se before you classify it
REF: 30.1.1, 30.1.2

A pronominal verb carries a pronoun that matches its subject: me, te, se, nos, os, or se. The form alone does not say what the pronoun means.
TL: Me lavo. Nos escribimos. Se rompió el vaso. Se venden entradas.
EN: I wash myself. We write to each other. The glass broke. Tickets are sold.
[PAUSE:1000]
The same small word can mark a reflexive or reciprocal object, an intransitive change, a lexical verb, a passive, or an impersonal construction.
SHOW: match the pronoun → identify the participants → classify the construction
Flip for the first diagnostic split.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ask whether the subject acts on itself
REF: 30.1.2, 30.2.1, 30.4

A true reflexive starts from a transitive action: the subject is also its object. If there is no self-directed action, se is doing another job.
TL: Ana se peina. La puerta se abrió.
EN: Ana combs her own hair. The door opened.
[PAUSE:900]
Ana can act on Ana, so the first is reflexive. A door does not normally act on itself; the second presents a change without naming a causer.
SHOW: living agent acts on itself → reflexive · event happens to subject → intransitive change
Flip for reflexive and reciprocal pronouns.

[CARD]
TITLE: Reflexive pronouns match the subject
REF: 30.2.1, 30.2.2

Use the pronoun of the same person and number as the subject. It may be the direct object or an indirect object beside an expressed body part or possession.
TL: Me visto. Nos lavamos las manos.
EN: I get dressed. We wash our hands.
[PAUSE:900]
Spanish normally uses the definite article with a body part because the pronoun already identifies the possessor.
TL: Clara se puso el abrigo.
EN: Clara put on her coat.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: yo me · tú te · él/ella/usted se · nosotros nos · vosotros os · ellos/ustedes se
Flip when a plural subject acts mutually.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reciprocal meaning needs more than one participant
REF: 30.3

With a plural subject, nos, os, or se can mean each other rather than ourselves. Context often decides the reading.
TL: Marta y Luis se miraron.
EN: Marta and Luis looked at each other—or at themselves.
[PAUSE:900]
Add el uno al otro, entre sí, or mutuamente when the reciprocal reading must be explicit.
TL: Los dos equipos se felicitaron mutuamente.
EN: The two teams congratulated each other.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: plural subject + mutual action → reciprocal · clarify if ambiguity matters

[CARD]
TITLE: Mismo strengthens identity; it does not replace the pronoun
REF: 10.11, 13.3, 30.2

Use a mí mismo, a ti misma, a sí mismos, and similar forms for emphasis or contrast. The adjective agrees with the person it describes.
TL: Ella se escribió una nota a sí misma.
EN: She wrote a note to herself.
[PAUSE:900]
After con, the third-person form is consigo; when emphasis is useful, add mismo.
TL: Hay que estar en paz consigo mismo.
EN: One must be at peace with oneself.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: se + a sí mismo · consigo mismo · agreement follows the referent
Flip for two common English-transfer traps.

[SIDE]
TITLE: English self can be emphatic, reflexive, or lexical
REF: 10.11, 13.3, 30.2

Do not add a reflexive pronoun merely because English contains self. Emphatic myself is usually yo mismo; by myself is often solo; the reflexive object remains me.
TL: Lo hice yo misma. Vivía sola. Me vi en el espejo.
EN: I did it myself. I lived by myself. I saw myself in the mirror.
[PAUSE:1000]
First decide the role of self, then choose the Spanish structure.
SHOW: emphasis → mismo · alone → solo · subject = object → reflexive pronoun

[CARD]
TITLE: Separate a caused event from an uncaused change
REF: 30.4

Many transitive verbs have a pronominal partner that presents the affected thing as undergoing a change. The causer disappears from the grammar.
TL: El viento abrió la puerta. La puerta se abrió.
EN: The wind opened the door. The door opened.
[PAUSE:900]
This se does not mean itself. It marks the intransitive view of the event.
SHOW: causer + verb + object · affected subject + se + verb
Flip when the event affects someone unexpectedly.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An extra pronoun marks the affected person
REF: 30.4, 11.9.3

Add an indirect-object pronoun to show who is affected, often in an accidental or unwanted event.
TL: Rompí el vaso. Se rompió el vaso. Se me rompió el vaso.
EN: I broke the glass. The glass broke. The glass broke on me.
[PAUSE:900]
The verb agrees with the thing that changed, not with the affected person.
TL: Se nos perdieron las llaves.
EN: The keys got lost on us.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: se + affected-person pronoun + verb agreeing with the changed thing

[CARD]
TITLE: Learn a pronominal verb with its complement
REF: 30.4, 30.5

Adding the pronoun can create a lexical meaning and a new preposition. Store the whole pattern rather than treating se as a detachable translation.
TL: Acordamos la fecha. Nos acordamos de la fecha.
EN: We agreed on the date. We remembered the date.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Niega el rumor. Se niega a contestar.
EN: She denies the rumor. She refuses to answer.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: acordar algo / acordarse de · negar algo / negarse a
Flip for more meaning-changing pairs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The pronoun can change valency and viewpoint
REF: 30.4, 30.5, 30.7

Compare ocupar algo with ocuparse de algo, despedir a alguien with despedirse de alguien, and dormir with dormirse. Each pronominal entry has its own argument pattern and aspect.
TL: El gerente despidió a Pablo. Pablo se despidió del gerente.
EN: The manager dismissed Pablo. Pablo said goodbye to the manager.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: El niño duerme. El niño se durmió.
EN: The child is asleep. The child fell asleep.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: memorize verb + se + required preposition + meaning

[CARD]
TITLE: Se can add a boundary or an involved viewpoint
REF: 30.5, 30.6, 30.7, 30.9

With some motion and activity verbs, the pronominal form highlights departure, completion, entry into a state, or the subject's involvement.
TL: Fue al banco. Se fue del banco. Durmió. Se durmió.
EN: She went to the bank. She left the bank. She slept. She fell asleep.
[PAUSE:900]
With a bounded quantity, a consumption verb often uses se to present the whole amount as completed.
TL: Se bebió toda la botella de agua.
EN: He drank the whole bottle of water.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: unmarked activity · se-form = departure, onset, completion, or involvement
Flip for quedar and quedarse.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quedar and quedarse organize different outcomes
REF: 30.7.33

Quedar can mean to arrange, be located, fit, or remain to be done. Quedarse commonly means to stay behind, keep something, or enter and remain in a resulting state.
TL: Quedamos a las ocho. El museo queda cerca. Me quedé en casa.
EN: We arranged to meet at eight. The museum is nearby. I stayed at home.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: La noticia lo dejó sorprendido; se quedó sin palabras.
EN: The news left him surprised; he was left speechless.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: quedar = arrangement/location/result · quedarse = stay/keep/be left

[CARD]
TITLE: Becoming verbs classify the kind of change
REF: 31.2, 31.3

Spanish has no single all-purpose equivalent of become. Choose the verb from the nature of the new state.
TL: Se puso nerviosa. Se hizo médica. Se volvió desconfiado.
EN: She became nervous. She became a doctor. He became distrustful.
[PAUSE:900]
Ponerse often marks a temporary condition; hacerse a chosen or gradual identity; volverse a lasting change in character.
SHOW: temporary state → ponerse · adopted role/belief → hacerse · character change → volverse
Flip for results, transformations, and achievements.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quedarse, convertirse en, and llegar a ser focus different endpoints
REF: 31.2, 31.3

Quedarse presents a resulting state, often after loss or surprise. Convertirse en marks transformation into a new kind of thing. Llegar a ser presents an achieved status after a process.
TL: Se quedó sordo. El almacén se convirtió en teatro. Llegó a ser directora.
EN: He became deaf. The warehouse became a theater. She eventually became a director.
[PAUSE:1000]
The complement helps: adjective with ponerse, volverse, or quedarse; noun or role often with hacerse, convertirse en, or llegar a ser.
SHOW: result → quedarse · transformation → convertirse en · attained status → llegar a ser
Flip for event passive versus resulting state.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ser presents a passive event; estar presents its result
REF: 32.2.1, 32.2.3, 32.2.5

Ser plus a participle presents what was done to a patient and can name the agent with por. The participle agrees with the patient.
TL: Las solicitudes fueron revisadas por el comité.
EN: The applications were reviewed by the committee.
[PAUSE:900]
Estar plus a participle normally describes the state left by an earlier event.
TL: Las solicitudes ya están revisadas.
EN: The applications have already been reviewed and are in that state.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: ser + participle = event · estar + participle = resulting state
Flip for the more usual alternatives in everyday Spanish.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Prefer an active or se construction when it is more natural
REF: 32.2.7, 32.3, 32.9

Spanish uses the ser passive less freely than English, especially in ordinary conversation. Preserve the information structure, not the English syntax.
TL: Aprobaron la propuesta ayer. La propuesta se aprobó ayer.
EN: They approved the proposal yesterday. The proposal was approved yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
Use ser when the event and agent matter; use passive se when the patient matters but the agent does not; use an active clause when it is clearest.
SHOW: named agent → ser passive may fit · unnamed agent → active plural or passive se
Flip to test passive se agreement.

[CARD]
TITLE: Passive se makes the patient the grammatical subject
REF: 32.3, 32.4

With a non-personal patient, passive se agrees with the noun phrase in singular or plural.
TL: Se vende una entrada. Se venden dos entradas.
EN: One ticket is sold. Two tickets are sold.
[PAUSE:900]
The agent is normally absent. A transitive verb and an affected noun are the clues that a passive analysis is possible.
SHOW: se + verb agreeing with patient · no expressed agent
Flip when the affected participant is a specific person.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Personal a points toward impersonal se
REF: 32.4, 32.5, 32.6

When a human direct object is introduced by personal a, it does not control agreement. Use a singular impersonal verb.
TL: Se entrevistó a los candidatos.
EN: The candidates were interviewed.
[PAUSE:900]
Contrast a non-personal plural patient, which does control agreement.
TL: Se revisaron los documentos.
EN: The documents were reviewed.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: se entrevistó a personas · se revisaron cosas

[CARD]
TITLE: Impersonal se keeps the verb singular
REF: 30.10, 30.11, 32.6

Impersonal se means people in general, one, or an unspecified human agent. There is no grammatical subject, so the verb stays third-person singular.
TL: Aquí se trabaja mucho. Se habló de varios problemas.
EN: People work hard here. Several problems were discussed.
[PAUSE:900]
It works naturally with intransitive verbs and with prepositional complements, where no patient noun can become the subject.
SHOW: no subject noun → se + singular verb
Flip for pronominal verbs and the uno alternative.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use uno when se would collide with another se
REF: 30.10, 30.11, 32.6, 32.7.1

A verb that is already pronominal can make an impersonal se sequence unclear or impossible. Uno or una keeps the indefinite subject explicit.
TL: Uno se acostumbra pronto. Una se da cuenta del cambio.
EN: One gets used to it quickly. One notices the change.
[PAUSE:900]
The choice between passive and impersonal se depends on syntax, not on an English passive translation. Find a patient noun, check personal a, and then test agreement.
SHOW: patient subject → passive agreement · no subject / personal object → singular impersonal · pronominal verb → uno/una
