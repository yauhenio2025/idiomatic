## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Passive changes the camera angle
SVG: de_passiv_c01f.svg
REF: 13

Voice is a choice of perspective. The active sentence begins with the participant who acts:
TL: Die Sachverständigen prüfen derzeit die Unterlagen.
EN: The experts are currently examining the documents.
[PAUSE:1000]
The passive begins with what the action affects:
TL: Die Unterlagen werden derzeit geprüft.
EN: The documents are currently being examined.
[PAUSE:1200]
The old accusative object is now the nominative subject. The original agent can disappear because the event, not the actor, is the point.
SHOW: active object → passive subject · agent optional
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Topic shift is not always passive
REF: 13

German can move an accusative object to the first position without changing it into a subject:
TL: Diesen Vorschlag hat die Kommission nach langer Debatte angenommen.
EN: This proposal was adopted by the committee after a long debate.
[PAUSE:1200]
The ending still marks the proposal as accusative, and the committee still controls the verb. A genuine passive suppresses that actor:
TL: Dieser Vorschlag wurde nach langer Debatte angenommen.
EN: This proposal was adopted after a long debate.
[PAUSE:1200]
Choose the passive when the actor is unknown, obvious, or deliberately backgrounded. Choose fronting when you merely want a different topic.
SHOW: fronted object = same roles · passive = new subject and backgrounded agent
Next, build the core mechanism.

[CARD]
TITLE: The process passive has two moving parts
SVG: de_passiv_c02f.svg
REF: 13.1.2

Start with a transitive clause:
TL: Die Redaktion kürzt den Artikel.
EN: The editorial team is shortening the article.
[PAUSE:900]
Promote the accusative object, remove or demote the agent, and combine the process auxiliary with the past participle:
TL: Der Artikel wird von der Redaktion gekürzt.
EN: The article is being shortened by the editorial team.
[PAUSE:1200]
Only the auxiliary agrees with the new subject. The participle does not change.
SHOW: accusative → nominative · werden carries tense · participle stays fixed
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Transitive does not mean automatic
REF: 13.1.2

Most transitive verbs allow this conversion, but a lexical group resists it: verbs of possession, knowledge, contents, and receipt. German recasts the thought instead:
TL: Dem Museum gehören drei seltene Handschriften.
EN: Three rare manuscripts belong to the museum.
[PAUSE:1000]
Perception plus a bare infinitive also keeps an active frame:
TL: Eine Nachbarin sah den Verdächtigen über den Zaun klettern.
EN: A neighbor saw the suspect climb over the fence.
[PAUSE:1200]
So test the verb's valency before applying the mechanical conversion.
SHOW: productive pattern, lexical limits · possession / knowledge / receipt resist
Ten basic process-passive transformations come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: One participle, several tense frames
SVG: de_passiv_c03f.svg
REF: 13.1.1

Keep your eye on the finite auxiliary. It alone locates the process in time:
TL: Der Antrag wird geprüft.
EN: The application is being examined.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Antrag wurde geprüft.
EN: The application was examined.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Antrag ist geprüft worden.
EN: The application has been examined.
[PAUSE:1200]
The perfect uses a special shortened participle. That form distinguishes a completed passive process from the ordinary lexical participle.
SHOW: present: wird geprüft · past: wurde geprüft · perfect: ist geprüft worden
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Future meaning usually needs no future form
REF: 13.1.1

A time phrase normally lets the present form point forward:
TL: Der Antrag wird nächste Woche geprüft.
EN: The application will be examined next week.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use the explicit future when context would otherwise make the present reading too strong:
TL: Der Antrag wird noch geprüft werden.
EN: The application will still be examined.
[PAUSE:1000]
The pluperfect adds one more layer when a completed process precedes another past event:
TL: Der Antrag war bereits geprüft worden, bevor die Sitzung begann.
EN: The application had already been examined before the meeting began.
SHOW: time phrase often carries futurity · explicit future only when needed

[CARD]
TITLE: Modal verbs take the finite slot
REF: 13.1.1, 13.1.4

With a modal, the modal verb becomes finite and the passive infinitive moves to the end:
TL: Der Vertrag muss heute unterschrieben werden.
EN: The contract must be signed today.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Daten dürfen erst morgen veröffentlicht werden.
EN: The data may not be published until tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1200]
The subject agrees with the modal. At the right edge, the participle comes before the passive infinitive.
SHOW: finite modal + … + past participle + werden
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The perfect becomes a three-verb cluster
REF: 13.1.1

The simple past is often the cleanest narrative choice:
TL: Der Vertrag musste noch am selben Tag unterschrieben werden.
EN: The contract had to be signed that same day.
[PAUSE:1000]
The perfect uses an infinitive cluster rather than an ordinary participle:
TL: Der Vertrag hat noch am selben Tag unterschrieben werden müssen.
EN: The contract had to be signed that same day.
[PAUSE:1200]
In a subordinate clause, the finite perfect auxiliary steps in front of that cluster:
TL: Sie erklärte, dass der Vertrag noch am selben Tag hat unterschrieben werden müssen.
EN: She explained that the contract had to be signed that same day.
SHOW: perfect modal passive: hat + participle + werden + modal infinitive

[CARD]
TITLE: Only the accusative can be promoted
SVG: de_passiv_c05f.svg
REF: 13.1.3

With two objects, the accusative becomes the subject and the dative stays dative:
TL: Die Leitung schickt den Mitgliedern die Unterlagen.
EN: Management sends the members the documents.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Unterlagen werden den Mitgliedern geschickt.
EN: The documents are sent to the members.
[PAUSE:1200]
German cannot promote the receiver in the way English often can. Case outranks position.
SHOW: accusative rises to subject · dative remains dative
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Other objects survive unchanged
REF: 13.1.3

A verb with only a dative object produces a subjectless passive:
TL: Dem Gutachter wurde entschieden widersprochen.
EN: The expert was firmly contradicted.
[PAUSE:1000]
Prepositional and genitive objects keep their original form too:
TL: Über den Vorschlag wurde ausführlich beraten.
EN: The proposal was discussed at length.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Opfer wurde in einer stillen Zeremonie gedacht.
EN: The victims were commemorated in a quiet ceremony.
[PAUSE:1200]
With no nominative subject, the finite verb stays singular.
SHOW: dative / genitive / prepositional object stays put · verb = singular
Sixteen object-and-eligibility transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: An activity can be passive without a subject
SVG: de_passiv_c06f.svg
REF: 13.1.4

German can present an ongoing activity without naming either actor or affected object:
TL: Im Innenhof wird gefeiert.
EN: There is a celebration going on in the courtyard.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Heute wird länger gearbeitet.
EN: People are working longer today.
[PAUSE:1200]
This works with intransitive verbs as long as they express an activity. The finite verb remains third-person singular.
SHOW: activity alone → subjectless passive · always third-person singular
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Es only holds the first position
REF: 13.1.4

If nothing else precedes the finite verb in a main clause, a placeholder fills the opening slot:
TL: Es wird im Innenhof gefeiert.
EN: There is a celebration going on in the courtyard.
[PAUSE:1000]
Move another constituent forward and the placeholder disappears:
TL: Im Innenhof wird bis Mitternacht gefeiert.
EN: People are celebrating in the courtyard until midnight.
[PAUSE:1000]
The same pattern can deliver a brisk command:
TL: Jetzt wird aufgeräumt!
EN: Time to tidy up now!
SHOW: first position empty → es · another opener → no es
Eight subjectless-passive responses come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Process and state answer different questions
SVG: de_passiv_c07f.svg
REF: 13.2.2

The process passive reports an event:
TL: Um acht Uhr wurde das Labor abgeschlossen.
EN: The laboratory was locked at eight o'clock.
[PAUSE:1000]
The state passive describes the resulting condition:
TL: Als ich ankam, war das Labor abgeschlossen.
EN: When I arrived, the laboratory was locked.
[PAUSE:1200]
Ask whether the clause locates a change or inspects the state left behind. English often uses the same form for both; German makes you choose.
SHOW: werden = event in progress or occurrence · sein = resulting state
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Duration points to state; event time points to process
REF: 13.2.1, 13.2.2

A continuing duration naturally selects the state:
TL: Seit drei Tagen ist der Zugang gesperrt.
EN: Access has been blocked for three days.
[PAUSE:1000]
A point in time selects the completed event:
TL: Der Zugang ist am Freitag gesperrt worden.
EN: Access was blocked on Friday.
[PAUSE:1000]
Only verbs whose action leaves a recognizable result form a natural state passive. Birth also follows a useful convention:
TL: Sie ist in Graz geboren.
EN: She was born in Graz.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Sie wurde am 14. Mai in Graz geboren.
EN: She was born in Graz on May 14.
SHOW: continuing result → sein · dated occurrence → werden
Fourteen process-or-state choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Agent, cause, and instrument are three roles
SVG: de_passiv_c08f.svg
REF: 13.3

Use the agent phrase for whoever deliberately carries out the action:
TL: Der Bericht wurde von einer externen Kanzlei verfasst.
EN: The report was prepared by an external law firm.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use the cause phrase for the means or involuntary force that brings it about:
TL: Der Betrieb wurde durch einen Stromausfall lahmgelegt.
EN: Operations were brought to a standstill by a power outage.
[PAUSE:1200]
The familiar person-versus-thing shortcut helps, but semantic role is the stronger test.
SHOW: von = agent or source · durch = cause, channel, intermediary
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mit names the tool, not the actor
REF: 13.3

The instrument is neither the source nor the intermediary:
TL: Das beschädigte Schloss wurde mit einem Spezialwerkzeug geöffnet.
EN: The damaged lock was opened with a special tool.
[PAUSE:1000]
All three relations can be kept distinct in one mental model:
TL: Der Entwurf wurde von der Architektin mit einem neuen Programm erstellt.
EN: The design was created by the architect using a new program.
[PAUSE:1000]
And a person may still be a channel rather than the true source:
TL: Die Entscheidung wurde uns durch einen Kurier übermittelt.
EN: The decision was conveyed to us through a courier.
SHOW: actor → von + dative · cause/channel → durch + accusative · tool → mit + dative
Next, widen the system beyond the two core passives.

[CARD]
TITLE: German can restore a human viewpoint
REF: 13.4.1, 13.4.2

An indefinite human subject is often more natural than a formal passive:
TL: Man geht davon aus, dass die Nachfrage sinkt.
EN: It is assumed that demand is falling.
[PAUSE:1000]
The recipient passive promotes a person who would otherwise remain dative:
TL: Die Preisträgerin bekam die Urkunde feierlich überreicht.
EN: The prizewinner was ceremonially presented with the certificate.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wir haben die Änderungen ausführlich erklärt bekommen.
EN: We had the changes explained to us in detail.
[PAUSE:1200]
This construction is standard with the neutral recipient auxiliary; its colloquial counterpart is less formal.
SHOW: man = unnamed people · bekommen + participle = recipient becomes subject
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reflexive form can background the actor
REF: 13.4.3

With an accomplishment, a reflexive form can report a passive-like result:
TL: Meine Befürchtung hat sich bestätigt.
EN: My fear has been confirmed.
[PAUSE:900]
With an activity, a manner phrase usually completes the thought:
TL: Die neue Ausgabe verkauft sich überraschend gut.
EN: The new edition is selling surprisingly well.
[PAUSE:900]
An intransitive reflexive pattern can evaluate an experience without naming the experiencer:
TL: In diesem Sessel sitzt es sich erstaunlich bequem.
EN: This armchair is surprisingly comfortable to sit in.
SHOW: reflexive alternative = passive-like event, often with a manner phrase
Six paired passive-and-reflexive translations come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Passive meaning can carry modality
SVG: de_passiv_c10f.svg
REF: 13.4.5, 13.4.6

The reflexive construction with an infinitive expresses possibility:
TL: Der Fehler lässt sich leicht beheben.
EN: The error can easily be corrected.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Der Fehler kann leicht behoben werden.
EN: The error can easily be corrected.
[PAUSE:1200]
The two versions are close in meaning. The reflexive alternative is frequent in every register and often sounds lighter.
SHOW: sich lassen + infinitive = können + passive infinitive
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Infinitive frames distribute possibility and duty
REF: 13.4.5, 13.4.7

The state auxiliary plus an infinitive can express possibility, obligation, or an instruction; context decides:
TL: Die Unterlagen sind bis Freitag einzureichen.
EN: The documents must be submitted by Friday.
[PAUSE:900]
Other frames specialize further:
TL: Vieles bleibt noch zu klären.
EN: Much remains to be clarified.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Es gibt mehrere Risiken zu berücksichtigen.
EN: There are several risks to be considered.
[PAUSE:800]
A further obligation pattern is regional and not accepted as standard everywhere:
TL: Dieser Vorschlag gehört gründlich überarbeitet.
EN: This proposal needs to be thoroughly revised.
SHOW: sein + zu = can / must · bleiben + zu = still must · gehören + participle = should
Ten rewrites across these alternatives follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: A suffix can compress the whole construction
REF: 13.4.8

An adjective in the productive ability suffix turns a passive possibility into a property:
TL: Das Gerät ist reparierbar.
EN: The device can be repaired.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Die Handschrift ist kaum lesbar.
EN: The handwriting is barely legible.
[PAUSE:1000]
Related adjective families express intelligibility or capacity:
TL: Seine Begründung war gut verständlich.
EN: His reasoning was easy to understand.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Das Konzept ist unter diesen Bedingungen nicht entwicklungsfähig.
EN: Under these conditions, the concept cannot be developed further.
SHOW: verb + -bar · selected -lich / -fähig forms = passive possibility as property
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Formal prose also packages events as nouns
REF: 13.4.4

Written non-literary German often combines an event noun with a light verb:
TL: Der neue Standard kommt ab Januar zur Anwendung.
EN: The new standard will be applied from January.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Vorschlag fand im Ausschuss breite Zustimmung.
EN: The proposal received broad approval in the committee.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Das frühere Verfahren geriet allmählich in Vergessenheit.
EN: The former procedure was gradually forgotten.
[PAUSE:1200]
These are not empty ornaments. They can signal inception, result, or institutional register more precisely than a plain passive.
SHOW: event noun + light verb = formal passive-like packaging

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose the construction by meaning
REF: 13, 13.4

Do not begin by translating an English auxiliary. Begin with the communicative job:
TL: Die Datei wird verschlüsselt.
EN: The file is being encrypted.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Die Datei ist verschlüsselt.
EN: The file is encrypted.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Die Datei lässt sich verschlüsseln.
EN: The file can be encrypted.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Man verschlüsselt die Datei vor dem Versand.
EN: The file is encrypted before dispatch.
[PAUSE:1200]
One proposition yields an event, a state, an ability, or an unnamed human routine. Meaning chooses the form.
SHOW: event · state · possibility · human routine
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: English passive cues can be false friends
REF: 13.1.2, 13.1.3, 13.4.1

Permission is often simply an active modal in German:
TL: Sie dürfen den Sicherheitsbereich nicht betreten.
EN: You are not allowed to enter the secure area.
[PAUSE:900]
An English personal passive may require a German dative and no subject:
TL: Mir wurde geraten, den Termin zu verschieben.
EN: I was advised to postpone the appointment.
[PAUSE:900]
And a perception verb with a bare infinitive stays active:
TL: Eine Zeugin sah den Mann die Garage verlassen.
EN: The man was seen leaving the garage by a witness.
[PAUSE:1200]
The final test is therefore structural: identify roles, valency, aspect, and modality before selecting a German construction.
SHOW: translate the grammar job, not the English surface form
