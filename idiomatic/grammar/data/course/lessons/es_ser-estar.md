## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Classify the relationship before choosing a verb
REF: 33.1, 34.2.1, 34.3

English to be covers three different questions in Spanish. Ser identifies or classifies, estar locates or describes a condition, and haber introduces what exists.
TL: Lucía es médica. Lucía está en la clínica. Hay una médica en la clínica.
EN: Lucía is a doctor. Lucía is at the clinic. There is a doctor at the clinic.
[PAUSE:1000]
The subject may be the same, but the information structure changes with the verb.
SHOW: identity or class → ser · location or condition → estar · existence → haber
Flip to replace the misleading permanence rule.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Permanent versus temporary is not the rule
REF: 33.1, 33.3.1

A permanent condition can take estar, and a changeable classification can take ser. Ask how the speaker presents the property now.
TL: El hierro es un elemento. El hierro está oxidado.
EN: Iron is an element. The iron is rusty.
[PAUSE:900]
Rust may last indefinitely, but it is still a condition. A profession can change, but it still classifies a person.
TL: Antes era camarera; ahora es abogada.
EN: She used to be a server; now she is a lawyer.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: nature, identity, category → ser · state, condition, location → estar
Flip for identification and classification with ser.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ser links an identity, class, or equation
REF: 33.2.1, 33.2.2

Use ser when one expression identifies or classifies another. A noun complement normally requires ser, not estar.
TL: Mi vecino es profesor. Dos y dos son cuatro.
EN: My neighbor is a teacher. Two plus two is four.
[PAUSE:900]
With an adjective, ser presents a characteristic as part of the description or type.
TL: Este metal es ligero y resistente.
EN: This metal is light and strong.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: A = B · member of a class · characteristic description → ser
Flip when the identified expression is a pronoun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ser agrees with the identified person
REF: 33.2.1

In an identification sentence, Spanish keeps agreement with yo, tú, nosotros, or vosotros even when that expression comes after the verb.
TL: La responsable soy yo. Los ganadores sois vosotros.
EN: I am the person in charge. You are the winners.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not copy the English third-person frame and say es yo or son vosotros.
TL: ¿Quién es? Soy yo.
EN: Who is it? It is me.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: predicate first does not erase person agreement
Nineteen clean mixed choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Estar presents a condition or current appearance
REF: 33.3.1, 33.4.3

Use estar for mood, health, physical condition, or an appearance viewed at a particular moment.
TL: Marta está cansada. El café está frío.
EN: Marta is tired. The coffee is cold.
[PAUSE:900]
Estar often signals an observed change or a comparison with what the speaker expected.
TL: ¡Qué alta está tu hija!
EN: Your daughter has grown so tall!
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: how is it now? · what condition is it in? → estar
Flip when the same adjective supports two viewpoints.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The adjective does not choose the verb by itself
REF: 33.4.1, 33.4.3, 33.4.4

Ser presents a characteristic; estar presents a state, result, impression, or a conventional change of meaning.
TL: Daniel es callado. Daniel está callado.
EN: Daniel is the quiet type. Daniel is keeping quiet.
[PAUSE:900]
Some pairs must be learned as distinct expressions.
TL: Ana es lista. Ana está lista.
EN: Ana is clever. Ana is ready.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: same adjective + different viewpoint may produce a different meaning
Seven hygiene-gated change-of-condition prompts follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Locate people and things with estar
REF: 33.3.2

When the question is where an existing person or object is, use estar.
TL: Elena está en el teatro. El abrigo está sobre la silla.
EN: Elena is at the theater. The coat is on the chair.
[PAUSE:900]
The same rule applies to permanent locations; duration does not turn location into identity.
TL: Bogotá está en Colombia.
EN: Bogotá is in Colombia.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: person, place, or thing + physical location → estar
Flip when the located subject is an event.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Events take ser for place and time
REF: 33.2.5, 33.3.2

If be means take place or happen, use ser. The event is not an object sitting at a location.
TL: La reunión es en la sala tres. El concierto fue el sábado.
EN: The meeting is in room three. The concert was on Saturday.
[PAUSE:900]
Find the grammatical subject before choosing: the guests are at the venue, but the wedding takes place there.
TL: Los invitados están en el hotel; la boda es allí.
EN: The guests are at the hotel; the wedding is there.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: event location or time → ser · participant or object location → estar
Five clean location contrasts follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ser de describes origin, material, or affiliation
REF: 33.2.4

Ser de plus a noun can say where someone comes from, what something is made of, or what group or owner it belongs to.
TL: Soy de Quito. El anillo es de plata. Este libro es de Inés.
EN: I am from Quito. The ring is made of silver. This book belongs to Inés.
[PAUSE:1000]
These phrases identify or classify rather than describe a temporary situation.
SHOW: origin · material · affiliation or possession → ser de
Flip for temporary estar de expressions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Estar de frames a temporary role or situation
REF: 33.3.3

Estar de plus a noun can present a temporary activity, duty, mood, or situation.
TL: Marta está de vacaciones. Hoy estoy de guardia.
EN: Marta is on vacation. I am on duty today.
[PAUSE:900]
It can contrast with a profession classified by ser.
TL: Es músico, pero este verano está de camarero.
EN: He is a musician, but this summer he is working as a server.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: defining origin or material → ser de · temporary role or situation → estar de
Eleven clean ser-de versus estar-de choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Estar combines naturally with adverbs and current evaluations
REF: 33.3.1, 33.3.5

Use estar with bien, mal, mejor, peor, cerca, lejos, and similar adverbs when they describe a condition or situation.
TL: Mi padre está bien. Las oficinas están lejos.
EN: My father is well. The offices are far away.
[PAUSE:900]
Adverbs do not agree for gender or number.
TL: Ellas están mejor; nosotros estamos peor.
EN: They are better; we are worse.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: estar + adverb · bien, mal, mejor, peor stay invariable
Flip for prices and quantities.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Fixed and current prices use different frames
REF: 33.4.2

Ser presents a fixed price or quantity as an equation. Estar is common for a current, fluctuating market price.
TL: La entrada es de veinte euros. Los tomates están a dos euros el kilo.
EN: The ticket costs twenty euros. Tomatoes are at two euros a kilo.
[PAUSE:900]
In a shop, ask the price with a cuánto and answer in the same current-price frame.
TL: ¿A cuánto están las naranjas? Están a tres euros.
EN: How much are the oranges? They are three euros.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: fixed amount → ser · current quoted price → estar a
Twenty clean contextual shop-dialogue items follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Haber introduces existence; estar locates the known item
REF: 34.2.1, 34.3, 34.3.1

Use hay when a person or thing enters the conversation as something that exists or is available. Use estar when the listener can identify it and you give its location.
TL: Hay una farmacia en la plaza. La farmacia está junto al banco.
EN: There is a pharmacy in the square. The pharmacy is next to the bank.
[PAUSE:900]
An indefinite noun naturally follows hay; a definite, possessive, or demonstrative noun normally takes estar for location.
SHOW: introduce an entity → hay · locate an identified entity → estar
Flip for agreement and tense.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Existential haber stays third-person singular
REF: 34.2.1, 34.3.3

In standard formal Spanish, existential haber does not agree with the following plural noun.
TL: Había tres opciones. Hubo muchos cambios.
EN: There were three options. There were many changes.
[PAUSE:900]
Use hay only in the present; use the ordinary third-person singular forms había, hubo, habrá, and habría in other tenses.
TL: Mañana habrá otra reunión.
EN: Tomorrow there will be another meeting.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: hay · había · hubo · habrá + singular existential verb
Five clean haber-versus-estar dialogues follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Resume a countable noun before haber
REF: 34.2.2

When a countable noun has been mentioned and is omitted, use the matching direct-object pronoun before existential haber.
TL: ¿Hay entradas? Sí, las hay.
EN: Are there tickets? Yes, there are.
[PAUSE:900]
With an uncountable substance, the pronoun is normally omitted.
TL: ¿Hay café? Sí, hay.
EN: Is there any coffee? Yes, there is.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: countable noun omitted → lo, la, los, las + haber · mass noun → no pronoun
Flip for the neutral pronoun with ser and estar.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Resume a property with neutral lo
REF: 8.4.2, 34.2.2

When ser or estar links the subject to a property or description, neutral lo can stand for the whole complement. It does not agree with the subject.
TL: ¿Es urgente? Lo es. ¿Están listas? Sí, lo están.
EN: Is it urgent? It is. Are they ready? Yes, they are.
[PAUSE:900]
Keep the two patterns apart: gendered direct objects before haber, but invariable lo for a predicative description.
SHOW: las hay · los había · lo es · lo están
Eight clean pronoun replacements follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Context decides whether a description is general or current
REF: 33.4.1, 33.4.3

Ser presents a general characterization; estar can restrict the same description to the current situation or to the speaker's impression.
TL: La vida es difícil. La vida está difícil.
EN: Life is difficult in general. Life is difficult at the moment.
[PAUSE:900]
With appearance, estar often means look rather than be by nature.
TL: Julia es elegante. Julia está muy elegante hoy.
EN: Julia is elegant. Julia looks very elegant today.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: general characterization → ser · current impression or behavior → estar
Flip for taste and conventional adjective pairs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Learn frequent adjective pairs as complete meanings
REF: 33.4.3, 33.4.4

The contrast may be predictable from viewpoint, or it may be lexical and deserve its own memory entry.
TL: El cocinero es bueno. La sopa está buena.
EN: The cook is good. The soup tastes good.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: La camiseta es verde. La fruta está verde.
EN: The shirt is green. The fruit is unripe.
[PAUSE:1000]
Ask whether the adjective classifies, describes a state, reports an impression, or creates an established idiom.
SHOW: es bueno = good by quality · está bueno = tastes good · está verde = unripe
Eight clean bien, mal, and current-state choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Ser plus a participle presents an event
REF: 32.2.1, 32.2.5, 33.1

Ser plus a past participle can form an eventive passive: it says what happened to the patient and may name the agent with por.
TL: La puerta fue cerrada por Ana a las nueve.
EN: The door was closed by Ana at nine.
[PAUSE:900]
The participle agrees with the patient in gender and number.
TL: Las cartas fueron enviadas ayer.
EN: The letters were sent yesterday.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: ser + participle = passive event · agent may follow por
Flip for the resulting state with estar.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Estar plus a participle presents the result
REF: 32.2.5, 33.1

Estar plus a past participle normally describes the condition left by an earlier event, without narrating that event.
TL: La puerta estaba cerrada. Las cartas ya están enviadas.
EN: The door was closed. The letters are already sent.
[PAUSE:900]
Use time and context to decide whether the sentence reports an action or inspects its result.
TL: El informe fue preparado ayer; ahora está terminado.
EN: The report was prepared yesterday; now it is finished.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: what happened? → ser passive · what state is it in? → estar result
Flip for the final three-verb diagnostic.

[CARD]
TITLE: Run the noun phrase through a three-way diagnostic
REF: 33.1, 33.2, 33.3, 34.3

First ask whether the sentence identifies, classifies, or locates an event. If so, choose ser. If it locates an identified person or thing or describes its condition, choose estar.
TL: El festival es mañana. El escenario está en la plaza.
EN: The festival is tomorrow. The stage is in the square.
[PAUSE:900]
If the sentence introduces what exists or is available, choose existential haber.
TL: Hay dos escenarios y habrá varios conciertos.
EN: There are two stages, and there will be several concerts.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: identity, class, event → ser · known location, condition → estar · existence → haber
Flip for a final check against English transfer.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Translate the relationship, not the word be
REF: 33.1, 34.2.1, 34.3

Do not begin with a memorized English phrase. Identify the subject, the complement, and the kind of information being added.
TL: La charla es en línea. La profesora está en casa. Hay cien participantes.
EN: The talk is online. The professor is at home. There are one hundred participants.
[PAUSE:900]
Then check special frames: ser de or estar de, current prices, event versus result passives, and resumptive pronouns.
SHOW: classify → locate or assess → introduce existence → check the special frame
Twenty clean mixed-context items follow.
