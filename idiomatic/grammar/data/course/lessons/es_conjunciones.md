## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Pero contrasts; sino replaces
REF: 37.1

Use pero when the second idea adds a contrast without cancelling the first one.
TL: El piso es pequeño, pero tiene mucha luz.
EN: The flat is small, but it has plenty of light.
[PAUSE:900]
After a negation, use sino when the second element corrects or replaces the rejected one.
TL: No vive en Madrid, sino en Toledo.
EN: She does not live in Madrid, but rather in Toledo.
[PAUSE:900]
The diagnostic is meaning, not the English word but: is the speaker adding a contrast or replacing the rejected alternative?
SHOW: contrast added → pero · rejected A replaced by B → sino
Flip when the replacement is a full clause.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A finite replacement needs sino que
REF: 37.1

Before a finite verb, expand sino to sino que.
TL: No cancelaron la reunión, sino que la aplazaron.
EN: They did not cancel the meeting; instead, they postponed it.
[PAUSE:900]
Keep si no as two words when it means if not.
TL: Sal ahora; si no, perderás el tren.
EN: Leave now; if not, you will miss the train.
[PAUSE:900]
Mas without an accent is a rare literary equivalent of pero, not the ordinary production choice.
SHOW: sino + phrase · sino que + finite clause · si no = if not
Twenty-four hygiene-gated contrasts and corrections follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Y and o change form before the same opening sound
REF: 37.2, 37.3

Y becomes e before a word that begins with the vowel sound i. O becomes u before a word that begins with the vowel sound o.
TL: padres e hijos · siete u ocho
EN: parents and children · seven or eight
[PAUSE:900]
The sound controls the change, not the written first letter alone.
TL: cobre y hierro · agua o hielo
EN: copper and iron · water or ice
[PAUSE:1000]
Hierro and hielo begin with a consonantal glide, so ordinary y and o remain.
SHOW: /i/ → e · /o/ → u · consonantal y-sound → y or o
Flip for paired coordination and ni.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Coordinate complete grammatical units
REF: 37.2, 37.3

Paired o presents alternatives; ni coordinates negative alternatives.
TL: O lo aceptas o propones otra solución.
EN: Either you accept it or you propose another solution.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: No llamó ni escribió.
EN: She neither called nor wrote.
[PAUSE:900]
When coordinated pronouns require a preposition, repeat it where Spanish grammar requires it.
TL: Es para ella y para mí.
EN: It is for her and for me.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not write an accent on o merely because it stands beside a digit.
SHOW: o ... o · ni ... ni · para ella y para mí
Eight sound-conditioned choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Let the governing expression choose que or de que
REF: 37.4.1, 37.4.2, 37.4.3

The conjunction que introduces a clause after a verb such as decir, creer, or pensar. Spanish normally keeps it even where English omits that.
TL: Creo que tienen razón.
EN: I think they are right.
[PAUSE:900]
Use de que when the governing noun, adjective, verb, or phrase requires de.
TL: Me alegro de que estés aquí.
EN: I am glad that you are here.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Existe la posibilidad de que cambien la fecha.
EN: There is a possibility that they will change the date.
[PAUSE:1000]
Test the frame with eso: creo eso, but me alegro de eso.
SHOW: creo eso → creo que · me alegro de eso → me alegro de que
Flip to avoid both opposite errors.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Avoid automatic de—and do not drop required de
REF: 37.4, 37.4.3

Inserting de after a verb that does not govern it is dequeísmo and is rejected in careful standard usage.
TL: Dijo que llegaría tarde.
EN: She said that she would arrive late.
[PAUSE:900]
Dropping a required de is the opposite tendency, often called queísmo.
TL: Se acordó de que tenía una cita.
EN: He remembered that he had an appointment.
[PAUSE:900]
Qué with an accent is a different form used in questions and exclamations. Que also serves as a relative pronoun and in comparisons, so identify its job before editing the preposition.
TL: No sé qué quiere ni de qué se queja.
EN: I do not know what she wants or what she is complaining about.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: government test first · que ≠ qué · no automatic de
Sixteen unflagged government and accent choices follow; the implicit-key dequeísmo set is omitted.

[CARD]
TITLE: Por qué asks; porque answers
REF: 37.5.1

Write por qué as two words when it asks for a cause, in a direct or indirect question.
TL: ¿Por qué se fueron? No entiendo por qué se fueron.
EN: Why did they leave? I do not understand why they left.
[PAUSE:900]
Write porque as one unstressed word when it introduces the cause.
TL: Se fueron porque estaban cansados.
EN: They left because they were tired.
[PAUSE:1000]
Stress and syntactic role travel together: a question requests the reason; a causal clause supplies it.
SHOW: question → por qué · cause → porque
Flip for the noun and the purpose question.

[SIDE]
TITLE: El porqué names the reason itself
REF: 37.5.1

The noun porqué takes an accent and normally appears with a determiner. It can also be plural.
TL: Nadie explicó el porqué de la decisión.
EN: Nobody explained the reason for the decision.
[PAUSE:900]
Para qué asks about an intended purpose, which is not always the same as asking about a cause.
TL: ¿Para qué necesitas esa llave?
EN: What do you need that key for?
[PAUSE:900]
Do not use porque to mean that is why. Use a result expression such as por eso.
TL: Estaba enfermo; por eso no vino.
EN: He was ill; that is why he did not come.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: por qué · porque · el porqué · para qué · por eso
Eight spelling-and-role choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Place the cause according to its discourse job
REF: 37.5.1, 37.5.2, 37.5.3

Porque commonly follows the main statement and presents its explanation.
TL: Cerramos las ventanas porque hacía frío.
EN: We closed the windows because it was cold.
[PAUSE:900]
Como meaning since or seeing that normally places the shared background cause first.
TL: Como hacía frío, cerramos las ventanas.
EN: Since it was cold, we closed the windows.
[PAUSE:900]
Ya que and puesto que also present a cause as given or already accessible to the listener.
TL: Ya que estás aquí, podemos empezar.
EN: Since you are here, we can begin.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: explanation after → porque · background first → como · given premise → ya que
Flip to keep causal como distinct from its other uses.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Como changes meaning with position and mood
REF: 37.5.2, 37.7

At the front with an indicative cause, como can mean since. With the subjunctive, it can introduce a forceful condition or warning.
TL: Como no había entradas, volvimos a casa.
EN: Since there were no tickets, we went home.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Como llegues tarde otra vez, empezaremos sin ti.
EN: If you arrive late again, we will start without you.
[PAUSE:900]
Como can also mean in the way that, and como que has separate colloquial uses. Read the clause relation before assigning a translation.
TL: Hazlo como te enseñaron.
EN: Do it the way they taught you.
[PAUSE:1000]
The workbook's open-ended causal transformations are all judgment-flagged, so no book exercise block is attached here.
SHOW: como + fact first = since · como + subjunctive = warning · manner = the way

[CARD]
TITLE: Result reports an effect; purpose names an aim
REF: 37.9

De modo que, de manera que, and de forma que can introduce an actual result with the indicative.
TL: Entraron en silencio, de modo que nadie los oyó.
EN: They entered quietly, so nobody heard them.
[PAUSE:900]
With the subjunctive, the same family can present an intended effect.
TL: Entraron en silencio, de modo que nadie los oyera.
EN: They entered quietly so that nobody would hear them.
[PAUSE:1000]
Ask whether the second clause happened as an effect or was pursued as a goal.
SHOW: actual effect → indicative · intended effect → subjunctive
Flip for unambiguous result connectors.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use result markers at the scale you mean
REF: 37.9, 37.11.6, 37.11.11

Así que and conque introduce a consequence or conclusion. Por eso and por lo tanto more clearly link one statement to the next.
TL: Perdimos el último autobús, así que volvimos a pie.
EN: We missed the last bus, so we walked back.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: No había pruebas; por lo tanto, archivaron el caso.
EN: There was no evidence; therefore, they closed the case.
[PAUSE:900]
De tal modo que, de tal manera que, and de tal forma que express result, not purpose.
TL: Gritó de tal manera que todos salieron al pasillo.
EN: She shouted in such a way that everyone came out into the corridor.
[PAUSE:1000]
The workbook's free completions are judgment-flagged and remain outside the deck.
SHOW: así que = immediate consequence · por lo tanto = explicit inference · de tal modo que = result

[CARD]
TITLE: Concession preserves the main claim despite an obstacle
REF: 37.6.1

Aunque introduces information that might have been expected to change the outcome but does not.
TL: Aunque estaba cansada, terminó el informe.
EN: Although she was tired, she finished the report.
[PAUSE:900]
The indicative presents the obstacle as a fact. The subjunctive can leave it hypothetical, anticipated, disputed, or irrelevant to the outcome.
TL: Aunque esté cansada, terminará el informe.
EN: Even if she is tired, she will finish the report.
[PAUSE:1000]
A pesar de que follows the same broad fact-versus-contingency contrast.
SHOW: established obstacle → indicative · hypothetical or immaterial obstacle → subjunctive
Flip for y eso que and si bien.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Y eso que follows the surprising main statement
REF: 37.6.2

Y eso que points back to an established fact and therefore takes the indicative. It comes after the main statement, not before it.
TL: No la reconocí, y eso que la había visto ayer.
EN: I did not recognize her, even though I had seen her yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
Si bien also concedes an established fact, often in a more written register.
TL: Si bien el plan es caro, ofrece una solución estable.
EN: Although the plan is expensive, it offers a stable solution.
[PAUSE:900]
Por mucho que and related degree patterns say that no amount or degree changes the result.
TL: Por mucho que insistas, no cambiaré de opinión.
EN: However much you insist, I will not change my mind.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: aunque = general concession · y eso que = fact after main clause · si bien = written concession
Seven unambiguous concessive rewrites follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Stronger conditions are not neutral substitutes for si
REF: 37.7

Con tal de que, a condición de que, and siempre y cuando mean provided that or on condition that. They take the subjunctive in this use.
TL: Te ayudaré con tal de que me digas la verdad.
EN: I will help you provided that you tell me the truth.
[PAUSE:900]
Siempre que can also mean whenever with an indicative habitual event, so the intended relation matters.
TL: Siempre que voy a Sevilla, visito a Ana.
EN: Whenever I go to Seville, I visit Ana.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not replace every si mechanically: the new connector can add insistence, restriction, or a warning.
SHOW: neutral condition → si · explicit requirement → con tal de que + subjunctive
Flip for exception and warning patterns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Exception connectors exclude the blocking case
REF: 37.7

A menos que, salvo que, and excepto que mean unless and normally take the subjunctive.
TL: Saldremos a las seis, a menos que llueva.
EN: We will leave at six unless it rains.
[PAUSE:900]
Mientras can mean while, but it can also mean as long as when it imposes a condition.
TL: Puedes quedarte mientras no hagas ruido.
EN: You may stay as long as you do not make noise.
[PAUSE:900]
Como plus the subjunctive can turn a condition into a warning.
TL: Como siga aumentando la contaminación, tendremos que actuar.
EN: If pollution keeps increasing, we will have to act.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: unless → a menos que · as long as → mientras · warning → como + subjunctive
One hygiene-gated conditional transformation follows; nine judgment-call alternatives are excluded.

[CARD]
TITLE: Purpose looks forward to an intended outcome
REF: 37.8

Para que introduces a purpose clause with its own finite verb and takes the subjunctive.
TL: Cerré la puerta para que no entrara el ruido.
EN: I closed the door so that the noise would not come in.
[PAUSE:900]
When the understood subject is the same, para plus the infinitive is usually more compact.
TL: Cerré la puerta para trabajar tranquilo.
EN: I closed the door in order to work in peace.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not choose the mood from certainty. The subjunctive marks the intended, not-yet-asserted outcome.
SHOW: separate finite purpose clause → para que + subjunctive · same subject → para + infinitive
Flip for negative aims and formal variants.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Recast the aim, not merely the words
REF: 37.8

Spanish often expresses a negative desired outcome directly with para que no.
TL: Habla despacio para que no se pierda ningún detalle.
EN: Speak slowly so that no detail is missed.
[PAUSE:900]
A fin de que and con el fin de que are more formal purpose connectors and also take the subjunctive.
TL: Revisaron el acuerdo a fin de que quedaran claras las condiciones.
EN: They revised the agreement so that the conditions would be clear.
[PAUSE:900]
When rewriting a cause, warning, or alternative, preserve who acts and what outcome is intended.
SHOW: desired result · explicit subject · subjunctive · no accidental change of agency
Nine hygiene-gated purpose rewrites follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Time clauses contrast realized time with anticipated time
REF: 37.10

Use the indicative for a habitual event or a completed event presented as fact.
TL: Cuando llega a casa, siempre llama a su madre.
EN: When he gets home, he always calls his mother.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Cuando llegó a casa, llamó a su madre.
EN: When he got home, he called his mother.
[PAUSE:900]
Use the subjunctive when the time event is still anticipated.
TL: Cuando llegue a casa, llamará a su madre.
EN: When he gets home, he will call his mother.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: habit or completed fact → indicative · anticipated event → subjunctive
Flip for conjunction-specific constraints.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Choose the time relation before the mood
REF: 37.10

Antes de que always takes the subjunctive because its reference event is viewed from before it happens.
TL: Salieron antes de que terminara la película.
EN: They left before the film ended.
[PAUSE:900]
Hasta que, en cuanto, una vez que, mientras, and después de que then combine their time relation with the realized-versus-anticipated contrast.
TL: Esperaré hasta que vuelvas. Esperé hasta que volviste.
EN: I will wait until you return. I waited until you returned.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not put a future indicative after cuando merely because English uses will in the main clause.
SHOW: relation first · timeline second · mood last
Six unambiguous time-connector choices follow; four alternative-key items are excluded.

[CARD]
TITLE: Discourse markers organize turns, not clause grammar
REF: 37.11.1, 37.11.3, 37.11.4, 37.11.6

A conjunction links grammatical units. A discourse marker tells the listener how the next move relates to the conversation or text.
TL: Por cierto, vi a tu hermana ayer.
EN: By the way, I saw your sister yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sin embargo, todavía falta una prueba.
EN: However, one piece of evidence is still missing.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: De todos modos, tenemos que decidir hoy.
EN: In any case, we have to decide today.
[PAUSE:1000]
Punctuation and intonation often set the marker off from the proposition it frames.
SHOW: afterthought · reservation · dismissal · resumption
Flip to calibrate register.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Match the marker to register and conversational force
REF: 37.11.3, 37.11.5, 37.11.8, 37.11.11

Sin embargo works in speech and writing; no obstante is more formal. Bueno and pues often manage a spoken turn rather than translate one fixed English word.
TL: Bueno, si todos están de acuerdo, seguimos.
EN: Right, if everyone agrees, we will continue.
[PAUSE:900]
Por lo tanto states a formal inference, while por eso is the everyday way to say that is why.
TL: No recibió el aviso; por eso llegó tarde.
EN: She did not receive the notice; that is why she arrived late.
[PAUSE:900]
En resumen is written and explicit; total can sum up a colloquial story. Avoid treating register variants as freely interchangeable synonyms.
SHOW: neutral · formal · colloquial · turn-management force

[CARD]
TITLE: Build the relation before choosing the connector
REF: 37

First decide whether the units are equal or one clause depends on another. Then name the relation: addition, alternative, correction, cause, result, concession, condition, purpose, or time.
TL: No rechazó la propuesta, sino que pidió tiempo porque faltaban datos.
EN: She did not reject the proposal; instead, she asked for time because data were missing.
[PAUSE:1000]
For a subordinate clause, check whether the connector requires a preposition and whether the clause is asserted, anticipated, or intended.
TL: Aunque falten datos, decidirán cuando termine la reunión.
EN: Even if data are missing, they will decide when the meeting ends.
[PAUSE:1000]
Finally choose a discourse marker only if you need to frame the relation between complete statements or conversational moves.
SHOW: grammar level · logical relation · government · timeline · register
Flip for the final production check.

TL: No fue un error, sino una decisión; sin embargo, nadie explicó el porqué.
EN: It was not a mistake but a decision; however, nobody explained the reason.
[PAUSE:1000]

TL: Aunque parezca difícil, lo entenderás en cuanto veas el ejemplo.
EN: Even if it seems difficult, you will understand it as soon as you see the example.
[PAUSE:1000]
Translate the relation between ideas, not the nearest isolated English word.
