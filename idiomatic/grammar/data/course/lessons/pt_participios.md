## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Form every gerund by replacing infinitive r with -ndo
REF: 17.1.1

Portuguese gerunds are strikingly regular. Remove the final r of the infinitive and add -ndo. This yields -ando, -endo, and -indo across the three conjugations.
TL: falar -> falando · fazer -> fazendo · decidir -> decidindo
EN: to speak -> speaking · to do -> doing · to decide -> deciding
[PAUSE:900]
Ir and pôr follow the same operation; pôr drops its written accent when the added ending makes it unnecessary.
TL: ir -> indo · pôr -> pondo
EN: to go -> going · to put -> putting
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: infinitive minus final r + ndo -> gerund
Flip to put this form inside a continuous tense.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Estar plus the gerund presents an action in progress
REF: 17.1.2

Conjugate estar for the time frame and leave the gerund unchanged. The present and imperfect of estar create the common present and past continuous patterns.
TL: Estou esperando a resposta. · Ele estava vendo televisão.
EN: I am waiting for the answer. · He was watching television.
[PAUSE:900]
Other auxiliaries can profile duration or development in their own way, but the gerund itself never agrees with person, gender, or number.
TL: Ela fica repetindo a pergunta. · A situação vem melhorando.
EN: She keeps repeating the question. · The situation has been improving.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: conjugated auxiliary + invariable gerund

[CARD]
TITLE: An adverbial gerund can express manner or a concurrent action
REF: 17.1.2

Outside a continuous tense, a gerund can describe what is happening at the same time as the main event, or how that event is carried out.
TL: Ela passou o fim de semana pintando a casa. · Voltamos pedindo carona.
EN: She spent the weekend painting the house. · We got back by hitching a ride.
[PAUSE:900]
With movement, Portuguese often puts direction in the finite verb and manner in the gerund. English often packages those meanings in the opposite way.
TL: O ladrão saiu correndo. · Ele atravessou o rio remando.
EN: The thief ran off. · He rowed across the river.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: finite verb = event/direction · gerund = concurrent action/manner
Flip for the subjects and pronouns that can accompany this form.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The gerund may have a logical subject and its own pronoun pattern
REF: 17.1.2, 17.1.3

An adverbial gerund may share the main-clause subject or carry a separately stated one. Mesmo and nem commonly introduce concessive uses.
TL: Conhecendo a Paula, ela não vai desistir. · Mesmo você não indo, eu vou.
EN: Knowing Paula, she will not give up. · Even if you do not go, I will.
[PAUSE:900]
With an auxiliary, neutral Brazilian usage places me, te, se, nos, and conversational lhe between the auxiliary and gerund. A formal written pronoun can attach to a free gerund.
TL: Estou te devendo cinquenta reais. · empurrando-me delicadamente
EN: I owe you fifty reais. · gently pushing me
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: auxiliary + pronoun + gerund · formal free gerund + hyphenated pronoun

[CARD]
TITLE: Regular past participles end in -ado or -ido
REF: 17.2.1

Replace -ar with -ado. Both -er and -ir verbs take -ido. These are participles, not finite past-tense forms.
TL: falar -> falado · comer -> comido · decidir -> decidido
EN: spoken · eaten · decided
[PAUSE:900]
Eight high-frequency families have core irregular forms. Their prefixed derivatives normally preserve the irregular participle.
TL: abrir aberto · cobrir coberto · dizer dito · escrever escrito
EN: opened · covered · said · written
[PAUSE:900]
TL: fazer feito · pôr posto · ver visto · vir vindo
EN: done · put · seen · come
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: -ar -> -ado · -er/-ir -> -ido · memorize eight irregular families
Flip to decide whether a participle agrees.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The auxiliary determines whether a participle agrees
REF: 17.2, 17.3, 17.4

After ter or haver in an active compound tense, the participle is invariable and ends in masculine singular -o, whatever the subject or object.
TL: Ela tinha escrito as cartas. · Nós havíamos vendido a casa.
EN: She had written the letters. · We had sold the house.
[PAUSE:900]
With ser in a passive, or with estar or ficar in a resultant-state expression, the participle agrees with the grammatical subject.
TL: As cartas foram escritas. · As janelas ficam abertas.
EN: The letters were written. · The windows stay open.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: ter/haver + fixed -o · ser/estar/ficar + agreement with subject

[CARD]
TITLE: Some verbs have both a long and a short participle
REF: 17.2.3

A useful starting rule chooses the regular, longer form after ter or haver and the shorter form with ser, estar, ficar, or as an adjective.
TL: Ela tinha acendido as luzes. · As luzes estavam acesas.
EN: She had turned on the lights. · The lights were on.
[PAUSE:900]
Likewise, matado belongs naturally to the active perfect while morto belongs naturally to the passive or a state.
TL: Diziam que ele havia matado um homem. · O homem foi morto.
EN: They said he had killed a man. · The man was killed.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: ter/haver -> often regular long form · state/passive -> often short form
Flip before treating that tendency as an absolute rule.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Dual participles follow usage, not a perfectly rigid formula
REF: 17.2.3

Several short participles are frequent even after ter and haver, especially in writing and educated speech. Both variants may be correct.
TL: tinha pagado / tinha pago · haviam entregado / haviam entregue
EN: had paid · had delivered
[PAUSE:900]
Conversely, some regular forms remain more common even with ser, estar, or ficar. Learn frequent constructions as combinations rather than forcing every verb into one rule.
TL: O assunto foi concluído. · Ele estava envolvido no projeto.
EN: The matter was concluded. · He was involved in the project.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: start with the auxiliary rule · then learn established verb-by-verb preferences

[CARD]
TITLE: Ter plus an invariable participle builds the active perfect system
REF: 17.3, 17.3.1

The Brazilian Portuguese present perfect usually describes an activity repeated or continuing over a recent period. It does not normally equal a one-off English have done.
TL: Tenho lido muito ultimamente. · Tem sido difícil dormir.
EN: I have been reading a lot lately. · It has been difficult to sleep.
[PAUSE:900]
For a single completed event, Portuguese commonly selects the preterite instead.
TL: Já li o livro. · Ela chegou ontem.
EN: I have already read the book. · She arrived yesterday.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: tenho feito -> repeated/continuing up to now · one completed event -> often preterite
Flip to move the same compound pattern through time and mood.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Change the auxiliary to locate a compound perfect
REF: 17.3.2, 17.3.3

The pluperfect uses tinha plus the participle in speech; havia is more formal and literary. Future and conditional forms use terá and teria.
TL: tinha esquecido · havia terminado · terá chegado · teria vindo
EN: had forgotten · had finished · will have arrived · would have come
[PAUSE:900]
Subjunctive and infinitive forms of ter extend the same system into subordinate and non-finite contexts.
TL: Espero que tenham gostado. · se tivesse acontecido · poderia ter ligado
EN: I hope they liked it. · if it had happened · could have called
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: inflect ter for tense/mood + keep the active participle invariable

[CARD]
TITLE: A ser-passive preserves tense and makes the patient the subject
REF: 17.4.1

Move the active object into subject position, conjugate ser in the original tense, and use a participle that agrees with the new subject.
TL: O Congresso aceitará a proposta. -> A proposta será aceita pelo Congresso.
EN: Congress will accept the proposal. -> The proposal will be accepted by Congress.
[PAUSE:900]
The agent, when expressed, is introduced by por. A progressive passive inserts sendo after the finite form of estar.
TL: As garrafas serão recicladas. · O dicionário está sendo atualizado.
EN: The bottles will be recycled. · The dictionary is being updated.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: patient + conjugated ser + agreeing participle + optional por-agent
Flip to separate an event from its resulting state.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ser presents a passive event; estar presents its resultant state
REF: 17.4.1, 17.4.3

Ser plus a participle tells what happens or happened. Estar plus a participle describes the state that holds after an event.
TL: As janelas foram abertas. · As janelas estão abertas.
EN: The windows were opened. · The windows are open.
[PAUSE:900]
Both patterns require agreement, but their meanings differ: one is an eventive passive and the other is stative.
TL: Os impostos foram incluídos. · Os impostos estão incluídos.
EN: The taxes were included. · The taxes are included.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: ser + participle = event · estar + participle = resulting state

[CARD]
TITLE: Ficar plus a participle emphasizes entering an involuntary state
REF: 17.4.2, 17.4.3

Ficar is common when an event leaves someone or something in a condition, especially an unwanted or involuntary one.
TL: Cinco pessoas ficaram feridas. · Tenho medo de ficar preso no elevador.
EN: Five people were injured. · I am afraid of getting stuck in the elevator.
[PAUSE:900]
Compare the three auxiliaries by asking whether the message foregrounds an event, an existing state, or transition into a state.
TL: foi ferida · está ferida · ficou ferida
EN: was injured · is injured · became/was left injured
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: ser = passive event · estar = state · ficar = entry/result
Flip for participles that work without a finite auxiliary.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A participle can modify a noun or frame a completed circumstance
REF: 17.5.1, 17.5.2, 17.5.3

As an adjective, a participle agrees directly with its noun. In an absolute construction, it can set a completed circumstance for the main clause.
TL: casas alugadas · Dadas as circunstâncias, o governo recuou.
EN: rented houses · Given the circumstances, the government backed down.
[PAUSE:900]
Depois de or formal após can introduce the state reached before the following instruction or event.
TL: Depois de dourado o frango, junte os ingredientes. · Após aberto, mantenha sob refrigeração.
EN: Once the chicken is browned, add the ingredients. · Once opened, keep refrigerated.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: noun + agreeing participle · completed participial frame + main clause

[CARD]
TITLE: Diagnose the construction before choosing the participle form
REF: 17.1, 17.2, 17.3, 17.4

First identify the job: an ongoing or adverbial action calls for a gerund; an active compound tense calls for ter or haver plus an invariable participle.
TL: está escrevendo · tinha escrito
EN: is writing · had written
[PAUSE:900]
For a passive event, choose ser and agreement. For an existing or resulting condition, choose estar or ficar and agreement.
TL: a carta foi escrita · a carta está escrita · a carta ficou pronta
EN: the letter was written · the letter is written · the letter ended up ready
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: gerund? -> -ndo · active perfect? -> ter + fixed participle · passive/state? -> agreement
Flip for a final four-check decision path.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Build the construction in four checks
REF: 17.1, 17.2, 17.3, 17.4

One: decide between action in progress and a completed/resulting relation. Two: for a participle, choose active perfect, passive event, or state.
[PAUSE:850]
Three: choose the auxiliary: ter or haver, ser, estar, or ficar. Four: make the participle invariable only after ter or haver; otherwise agree it with the subject.
[PAUSE:900]
Finally, check whether the verb has an irregular or dual participle and whether the intended construction favors one variant.
TL: falando · tinha feito · foram feitas · estão feitas
EN: speaking · had done · were done · are done
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: meaning -> auxiliary -> agreement -> irregular/dual form
