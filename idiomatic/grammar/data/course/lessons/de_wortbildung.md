## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: One root, several routes
SVG: de_wortbildung_c01f.svg
REF: 20, 20.1.1

German word formation is not a bag of long words. It is a small set of operations applied to roots. An affix can change meaning or word class, a vowel can change, or two full words can lock together. Listen to one family:
TL: Aus „klar“ entstehen „unklar“, „Klarheit“ und „klären“.
EN: “Unclear,” “clarity,” and “to clarify” are formed from “clear.”
[PAUSE:1200]
One root has fed three different operations. Once you see those operations, an unfamiliar word becomes a structure you can test instead of an indivisible block.
SHOW: affixation · vowel change · compounding
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Productive does not mean automatic
REF: 20.1.2

A productive pattern is still available for new coinages. A stored pattern may remain common even though speakers no longer use it to build new words. The first example uses a productive possibility:
TL: Die neue Schnittstelle ist problemlos erweiterbar.
EN: The new interface can be expanded without difficulty.
[PAUSE:1000]
The next abstract noun is perfectly current, but its older formation pattern is not a general invitation to coin parallel forms:
TL: Die Güte des Materials ließ sich nicht am Preis erkennen.
EN: The quality of the material could not be inferred from its price.
[PAUSE:1200]
So learn two things separately: what an existing word means, and whether its pattern can still generate new words.
SHOW: productive = build · unproductive = recognize
Noun-building machines come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Noun suffixes assign a job
REF: 20.2.1d, 20.2.1e, 20.2.1k

Noun suffixes often assign both a semantic job and a gender. From one verb, an agent noun and an action noun can emerge:
TL: planen → der Planer · die Planung
EN: to plan → the planner · the planning
[PAUSE:1000]
From an adjective, an abstract quality appears:
TL: zuverlässig → die Zuverlässigkeit
EN: reliable → reliability
[PAUSE:1000]
The agent pattern is masculine by default, the action and abstract patterns here are feminine, and the feminine person counterpart adds its own suffix:
TL: Die Planerin dokumentiert jede Änderung sorgfältig.
EN: The female planner documents every change carefully.
SHOW: suffix = semantic slot + frequent gender signal
Flip for the smaller specialist machines.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Small forms, attitudes and collectives
REF: 20.2.1a, 20.2.1c, 20.2.1g, 20.2.1i, 20.2.1j

Some suffixes carry much more than word class. One makes a neuter diminutive and often triggers a vowel change; another presents repeated activity as irritating; a third labels the person affected by an action:
TL: das Häuschen · die Fragerei · der Schützling
EN: the little house · the endless questioning · the protégé
[PAUSE:1200]
Other suffixes package groups, institutions or states:
TL: Die Belegschaft wählte eine neue Vertretung.
EN: The workforce elected a new representative body.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not reduce these endings to translations. Each one opens a preferred semantic slot, and many also predict gender.
SHOW: diminutive · attitude · affected person · collective
Now build nouns from whole words.

[CARD]
TITLE: In compounds, the head stands last
SVG: de_wortbildung_c03f.svg
REF: 20.2.4a, 20.2.4c

The final member is the head of a German compound. It supplies the core meaning, the gender and normally the plural pattern; everything before it narrows the type. Read from the right:
TL: die Forschungsdatenbank = die Forschung + die Datenbank
EN: the research database = research + the database
[PAUSE:1200]
The whole expression is feminine because its head is feminine, and it denotes a kind of database, not a kind of research:
TL: Die Forschungsdatenbank wurde vollständig neu aufgebaut.
EN: The research database was completely rebuilt.
SHOW: modifier + head · the head controls the compound
Flip for the seam between the parts.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Linking elements are lexical
REF: 20.2.4b

About a third of noun compounds insert a linking element. The element belongs to the first member's compound behavior, but it is not reliably its plural or genitive ending. Compare three established joins:
TL: der Jahresbericht · die Sonnenbrille · der Kinderwagen
EN: the annual report · the sunglasses · the pram
[PAUSE:1200]
The join has to be learned with the compound family. Even related compounds can choose different seams, and regional usage can differ:
TL: Der Jahresbericht enthält eine ausführliche Risikoanalyse.
EN: The annual report contains a detailed risk analysis.
[PAUSE:1000]
German makes compounds freely, but not mechanically. Check that the result names a recognized type and that its linking element is established.
SHOW: right-headed, productive—yet lexically constrained
Adjective building is next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Adjective suffixes encode relations
REF: 20.3.1a, 20.3.1d, 20.3.1g

Adjective suffixes do not merely decorate a root. They encode recurring relations. One says that an action is possible, one says that a quality or substance is present, and one says that something is absent:
TL: prüfen → prüfbar · Staub → staubig · Hoffnung → hoffnungslos
EN: to test → testable · dust → dusty · hope → hopeless
[PAUSE:1200]
Those relations can combine naturally inside a judgment:
TL: Die These ist überprüfbar, aber keineswegs fehlerlos.
EN: The thesis is verifiable, but by no means flawless.
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat the suffix as an instruction for construing the root: possible, possessing, resembling, lacking, recurring, or belonging.
SHOW: root + relation → adjective
Flip for contrasts that translation hides.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Near-neighbours divide the meaning
SVG: de_wortbildung_c04b.svg
REF: 20.3.1b, 20.3.1d, 20.3.1f

Two adjectives from one noun can divide the semantic space. The first pair distinguishes actual material from mere resemblance:
TL: ein hölzerner Rahmen · ein holziger Geschmack
EN: a wooden frame · a woody taste
[PAUSE:1200]
The next pair distinguishes duration from frequency:
TL: eine zweistündige Sitzung · eine zweistündliche Messung
EN: a two-hour meeting · a measurement taken every two hours
[PAUSE:1200]
This is why suffix choice cannot be guessed from one English equivalent. It selects a relation, and that relation selects the noun it can naturally modify.
SHOW: material ≠ resemblance · duration ≠ frequency
One more adjective system, then verbs.

[CARD]
TITLE: Adjective compounds form semantic families
REF: 20.3.3c

Many frequent final elements now behave almost like suffixes. They sort adjectives into reusable families: low in something, rich in something, free of something, protected against something, capable, worthy, or in need:
TL: Die neue Anlage ist wartungsarm, ausfallsicher und hitzefest.
EN: The new installation is low-maintenance, fail-safe and heat-resistant.
[PAUSE:1200]
TL: Der Bericht ist lesenswert, aber noch korrekturbedürftig.
EN: The report is worth reading, but it still needs correcting.
[PAUSE:1000]
These families are highly useful for decoding technical and formal prose because the final element states the relation explicitly.
SHOW: recurring final elements package recurring relations
Flip for prefixes and additive compounds.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Negation has scope
REF: 20.3.2, 20.3.3a

A negative prefix does not always create the same meaning as the ordinary lexical opposite. It can simply withhold the positive judgment:
TL: Die Entscheidung war unklug, aber keineswegs dumm.
EN: The decision was unwise, but by no means stupid.
[PAUSE:1200]
Two adjective roots can also form an additive compound in which both qualities remain active:
TL: Der Raum wirkte nasskalt und trostlos.
EN: The room felt damp, cold and bleak.
[PAUSE:1000]
Other prefixes intensify rather than negate:
TL: Die beiden Entwürfe sind grundverschieden.
EN: The two drafts are fundamentally different.
SHOW: negative scope · additive qualities · intensification
Now prefix meaning starts moving sentence parts.

[CARD]
TITLE: Verb prefixes come in three classes
SVG: de_wortbildung_c06f.svg
REF: 20.4

Verb prefixes are a prosodic grammar system. In the core patterns, stress predicts whether the prefix separates and where participle and infinitive morphology goes. First, an unstressed inseparable pattern:
TL: beantworten → beantwortet → zu beantworten
EN: to answer → answered → to answer
[PAUSE:1000]
Now a stressed separable pattern:
TL: anfangen → angefangen → anzufangen
EN: to begin → begun → to begin
[PAUSE:1200]
The third class can follow either pattern. Its stress, syntax and meaning have to be learned together.
SHOW: stress links separation · participle · infinitive
Flip to hear why stress belongs in the lexical entry.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Stress is grammar
REF: 20.4, 20.7

With a variable prefix, the same written beginning can produce two verbs. The unstressed version stays attached and often carries a figurative, transitive meaning:
TL: Die Übersetzerin übersetzt den Bericht.
EN: The translator translates the report.
[PAUSE:1200]
The stressed version separates and keeps a more literal directional meaning:
TL: Die Wanderer setzen mit dem Boot ans andere Ufer über.
EN: The hikers cross to the opposite bank by boat.
[PAUSE:1200]
Accent is therefore not decorative pronunciation. It chooses a lexical entry and controls the forms that follow.
SHOW: one spelling · two stress patterns · two grammars
The inseparable meanings come first.

[CARD]
TITLE: Be- changes the frame
SVG: de_wortbildung_c07f.svg
REF: 20.5.1

One productive inseparable prefix repackages valency. A prepositional relation can become a direct object:
TL: Wir antworten auf die Beschwerde.
EN: We respond to the complaint.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir beantworten die Beschwerde.
EN: We answer the complaint.
[PAUSE:1200]
The same prefix can form verbs from nouns with the idea of supplying something, or from adjectives with the idea of giving a quality:
TL: Die Gärtner bewässern die jungen Bäume.
EN: The gardeners water the young trees.
SHOW: valency shift · provision · change of quality
Flip for the prefix that removes or escapes.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ent- points away
REF: 20.5.2

Another inseparable prefix often signals removal, reversal or motion away. The removal pattern remains productive:
TL: Die Fachleute entgiften den belasteten Boden.
EN: The specialists decontaminate the polluted soil.
[PAUSE:1200]
With motion away, the person losing control commonly appears in the dative:
TL: Dem Kind entglitt der Luftballon.
EN: The balloon slipped from the child's grasp.
[PAUSE:1200]
The shared image is separation from a source, but the exact frame belongs to the derived verb.
SHOW: remove · reverse · escape from a source
Three more inseparable meanings next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Er- reaches a result
REF: 20.5.3

This inseparable prefix often presents an action as achieving its result or conclusion. It can also create verbs of acquiring something through an activity:
TL: Sie erarbeitete sich eine tragfähige Lösung.
EN: She worked out a viable solution for herself.
[PAUSE:1200]
With adjective bases, it often marks a change of state, either becoming a quality or causing it:
TL: Er errötete, als man ihn öffentlich lobte.
EN: He blushed when he was praised in public.
[PAUSE:1000]
The common thread is arrival at a result, although individual verbs can specialize.
SHOW: achievement · acquisition · change of state
Flip for a broad prefix and a narrow one.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ver- branches; zer- stays focused
SVG: de_wortbildung_c08b.svg
REF: 20.5.4, 20.5.5

One inseparable prefix has several productive branches: completion, disappearance, error, excess, change of state and provision. Context and the whole verb decide which branch is active:
TL: Im Spätherbst verblühten die letzten Rosen.
EN: In late autumn, the last roses faded.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Koch versalzte die Suppe.
EN: The cook put too much salt in the soup.
[PAUSE:1200]
By contrast, the destructive prefix keeps a stable image of separation into pieces:
TL: Beim Öffnen des Fensters zerbrach die dünne Scheibe.
EN: The thin pane broke when the window was opened.
SHOW: broad semantic network · stable destructive image
Now let the stressed prefixes move.

[CARD]
TITLE: Separable prefixes occupy sentence slots
SVG: de_wortbildung_c09f.svg
REF: 20.6, 20.6.1

A stressed prefix separates in a finite main clause and lands in the right bracket:
TL: Das Team arbeitet nach der Pause weiter.
EN: The team continues working after the break.
[PAUSE:1000]
In the participle, the extra syllable sits between prefix and root:
TL: Das Team hat bis Mitternacht weitergearbeitet.
EN: The team continued working until midnight.
[PAUSE:1000]
And the infinitive marker goes into the same seam:
TL: Das Team beschloss, am nächsten Morgen weiterzuarbeiten.
EN: The team decided to continue working the next morning.
SHOW: finite: split · participle and infinitive: insert
Flip for the meanings these prefixes contribute.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Direction grows into aspect
REF: 20.6.1, 20.6.2

Many separable prefixes begin as spatial words, then extend into meanings such as starting, completing, continuing, accompanying or moving away:
TL: Die Gäste kamen früh an, blieben lange auf und gingen gemeinsam weg.
EN: The guests arrived early, stayed up late and left together.
[PAUSE:1200]
Compound adverbs can become prefixes too, especially for approach, return, agreement and addition:
TL: Die Vorsitzende nahm den Vorschlag entgegen.
EN: The chair received the proposal.
[PAUSE:1000]
The stress and syntax remain regular even when the meaning has become lexicalized.
SHOW: spatial particle → aspect and lexical meaning
The variable class now puts every diagnostic together.

[CARD]
TITLE: Variable prefixes fork by stress
SVG: de_wortbildung_c10f.svg
REF: 20.7.5, 20.7.6, 20.7.7

Variable prefixes demand a three-part lexical entry: stress, valency and meaning. An unstressed transitive verb often expresses surrounding or bypassing:
TL: Die Fahrerin umfährt die Baustelle.
EN: The driver goes around the roadworks.
[PAUSE:1200]
Stress the prefix and separate it, and the literal turning meaning appears:
TL: Die Fahrerin fährt den Absperrpfosten um.
EN: The driver knocks over the barrier post.
[PAUSE:1200]
The tendencies are useful, but the verb pair—not the prefix alone—is the unit to learn.
SHOW: stress + valency + meaning = one lexical entry
Flip for the decisive form tests.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Participle and infinitive expose the class
REF: 20.7

When the finite clause hides the accent, the participle exposes the class. The inseparable verb has no inserted syllable:
TL: Die Polizei hat das Gebäude umstellt.
EN: The police have surrounded the building.
[PAUSE:1000]
The separable verb inserts it:
TL: Das Team hat die Möbel umgestellt.
EN: The team has rearranged the furniture.
[PAUSE:1200]
The infinitive shows the same contrast:
TL: Der Lieferwagen soll die Baustelle umfahren.
EN: The delivery van is supposed to go around the roadworks.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Fahrer versucht, den Pfosten nicht umzufahren.
EN: The driver is trying not to knock over the post.
[PAUSE:1200]
Learn accent, syntax and sense together. Six clean mixed-prefix exercises now test that decision.
SHOW: no insertion = inseparable · insertion = separable
One final card covers verbs without prefixes.

[CARD]
TITLE: A noun or adjective can become a verb
REF: 20.8.1

German can convert a noun or adjective into a verb simply by adding verbal endings. A vowel change may accompany the conversion, especially when the verb gives something a quality:
TL: Das Wasser dampft, und die Techniker leeren den Tank.
EN: The water is steaming, and the technicians are emptying the tank.
[PAUSE:1200]
TL: Die Werkstatt kürzt das Kabel und schärft die Klinge.
EN: The workshop shortens the cable and sharpens the blade.
[PAUSE:1000]
This route is alive and readily absorbs new loanword roots.
SHOW: root + verbal endings · vowel change sometimes joins in
Flip for three smaller verb-building patterns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Causatives, attenuatives and loans
REF: 20.8.2

Some inherited weak verbs cause the event named by a related strong verb:
TL: Das Kind sitzt; die Mutter setzt das Kind auf den Stuhl.
EN: The child is sitting; the mother seats the child on the chair.
[PAUSE:1200]
A productive diminutive ending can weaken an action:
TL: Das Kind lächelt, während der Großvater leise hüstelt.
EN: The child smiles while the grandfather coughs lightly.
[PAUSE:1200]
And a foreign suffix supplies a major route for learned and borrowed verbs:
TL: Das Labor standardisiert und digitalisiert den gesamten Ablauf.
EN: The laboratory standardizes and digitizes the entire process.
[PAUSE:1200]
That closes the system: identify the root, identify the operation, then ask what meaning, word class, stress and syntax the operation contributes.
SHOW: root → operation → meaning, class, stress and syntax
