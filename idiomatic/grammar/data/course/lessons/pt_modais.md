## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A helper verb changes how an event is viewed
REF: 24.1

Portuguese auxiliary constructions combine an inflected helper with an infinitive or gerund. The helper adds time, progression, repetition, intention, or outcome to the lexical event.
TL: Vou viajar. · Ando procurando emprego. · Acabamos dormindo no chão.
EN: I am going to travel. · I have been looking for a job. · We ended up sleeping on the floor.
[PAUSE:950]
Identify the main event first. Then read the helper as the speaker's viewpoint on that event.
SHOW: finite helper + infinitive/gerund -> event plus viewpoint
Flip to contrast two constructions with acabar.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Acabar marks either an outcome or immediate recency
REF: 24.1.1, 24.1.2

Acabar plus a gerund means to end up doing something; the more formal alternative is acabar por plus an infinitive.
TL: A polícia acabou prendendo o ladrão. · O réu acabou por confessar o crime.
EN: The police eventually caught the thief. · The defendant eventually confessed to the crime.
[PAUSE:950]
Acabar de plus an infinitive most often means to have just done something. In everyday language, use the preterite for a recent result and the pluperfect for an event just before another past event.
TL: Eu acabei de chegar. · Ele tinha acabado de jantar.
EN: I have just arrived. · He had just had dinner.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: acabar + gerund = end up · acabar por + infinitive = formal end up · acabar de + infinitive = have just

[CARD]
TITLE: Andar and vir plus the gerund look backward over a developing activity
REF: 24.1.3, 24.1.9

Andar plus a gerund presents activity distributed across a recent period, often with a sense of repeated effort or concern.
TL: O que é que você anda fazendo? · Andei pensando no que você falou.
EN: What have you been doing lately? · I have been thinking about what you said.
[PAUSE:950]
Vir plus a gerund presents a development continuing up to the reference point.
TL: Os alunos vêm reclamando há algum tempo. · O setor vinha crescendo a cada mês.
EN: The students have been complaining for some time. · The sector had been growing every month.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: andar + gerund -> activity across a recent period · vir + gerund -> development up to now/then
Flip for progression, movement, and eventual result.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ir and vir select different paths through an event
REF: 24.1.5, 24.1.6, 24.1.7, 24.1.8

Ir plus an infinitive expresses future intent or actual movement toward an action. Ir plus a gerund presents gradual or continuing progress.
TL: Vou me encontrar com ela amanhã. · Pode ir fazendo as malas.
EN: I am meeting her tomorrow. · You can get on with packing.
[PAUSE:950]
Vir directly before an infinitive retains movement toward the action. Vir a plus an infinitive instead marks an eventual or possible result.
TL: Ele vem nos visitar. · Isso pode vir a comprometer a empresa.
EN: He comes to visit us. · This may turn out to harm the company.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: ir + infinitive = intention/movement · ir + gerund = gradual progress · vir + infinitive = come and do · vir a = turn out to

[CARD]
TITLE: Haver de and viver add expectation or habitual repetition
REF: 24.1.4, 24.1.10

Haver de plus an infinitive is a formal or literary emphatic future, often carrying hope, resolve, or confident expectation.
TL: Dias melhores hão de vir! · Haveremos de conseguir.
EN: Better days will surely come! · We shall succeed.
[PAUSE:950]
Viver plus a gerund means to be always doing something, often with an expressive sense of repetition. Viver can carry the same habitual meaning before an adjective or prepositional phrase.
TL: Ela vive perdendo as chaves. · Minha irmã vive de mau humor.
EN: She is always losing her keys. · My sister is always in a bad mood.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: haver de + infinitive = emphatic future · viver + gerund/adjective = persistent habit or state
Flip to organize the three verbs commonly translated by can.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Translate the construction, not the English helper
REF: 24.1

English helper verbs collapse several viewpoints that Portuguese keeps distinct. Build from the intended relationship instead of replacing one English word mechanically.
TL: Acabei de sair. · Ando estudando. · Vou estudar. · Vivo estudando.
EN: I have just left. · I have been studying. · I am going to study. · I am always studying.
[PAUSE:1000]
The infinitive names an intended or bounded event; the gerund lets a helper frame the event from inside its course.
SHOW: recency -> acabar de · recent activity -> andar + gerund · intention -> ir + infinitive · constant habit -> viver + gerund

[CARD]
TITLE: Poder covers possibility, availability, and permission
REF: 24.2.1

Poder plus an infinitive says that circumstances make an event possible, available, or permitted.
TL: O tempo pode mudar. · Posso sentar aqui? · Acho que não vou poder ir.
EN: The weather can change. · May I sit here? · I do not think I will be able to go.
[PAUSE:950]
In speech, third-person singular pode can be impersonal. Tense distinguishes a continuing past limitation from failure at one bounded event.
TL: Pode estacionar aqui? · Não podia jogar. · Não pôde terminar o jogo.
EN: Is it OK to park here? · He could not play. · He could not finish the match.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: circumstances/permission -> poder · ongoing past -> podia · bounded failure -> não pôde
Flip to separate achieved ability from learned skill.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Conseguir is achieved ability; saber is learned skill
REF: 24.2.2, 24.2.3

Conseguir plus an infinitive presents physical ability or success in overcoming a difficulty.
TL: Não consigo abrir a porta. · Finalmente conseguimos convencê-la.
EN: I cannot get the door open. · We finally managed to persuade her.
[PAUSE:950]
Saber plus an infinitive presents knowledge of how to perform a learned skill.
TL: Ele não sabe nadar. · Eu sei contar até dez em alemão.
EN: He cannot swim. · I know how to count to ten in German.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: circumstantial possibility/permission -> poder · managed result -> conseguir · learned know-how -> saber

[CARD]
TITLE: Querer expresses desire; desejar makes it more formal
REF: 24.2.4, 24.2.5

Querer plus an infinitive is the ordinary way to say that someone wants to do something. In the imperfect, it can soften a present request or present an unrealized wish.
TL: Você quer jantar com a gente? · Eu queria te pedir um favor.
EN: Do you want to have dinner with us? · I would like to ask you a favour.
[PAUSE:950]
Desejar plus an infinitive is a more formal or ceremonious synonym.
TL: Com quem deseja falar? · Eu sempre quis conhecer Salvador.
EN: Who do you wish to speak to? · I have always wanted to visit Salvador.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: ordinary desire -> querer · softened request -> queria · formal wish -> desejar
Flip for tense contrasts inside querer.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Queria and quis frame wanting differently
REF: 24.2.4

Queria presents an ongoing past desire, a softened current wish, or a wish whose realization remains in the background. Quis presents wanting as a bounded past stance.
TL: Eu não queria ir. · Eu não quis ir.
EN: I did not want to go. · I chose not to go.
[PAUSE:950]
Quiser is the future subjunctive used after expressions such as se and quanto when the wanting belongs to an open future situation.
TL: Se quiser, pode ficar. · Coma quanto quiser.
EN: If you want, you may stay. · Eat as much as you want.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: queria = ongoing/softened wish · quis = bounded past stance · quiser = open future condition

[CARD]
TITLE: Ter que and precisar present practical necessity
REF: 24.2.6, 24.2.8

Ter que plus an infinitive is the common neutral construction for an imposed requirement. Ter de is a more formal written alternative.
TL: Temos que estar lá às nove. · Tiveram que arrombar a porta.
EN: We have to be there at nine. · They had to break the door down.
[PAUSE:950]
Precisar plus an infinitive presents a need. Negated precisar removes the necessity rather than prohibiting the event.
TL: Preciso ir. · Vocês não precisam me esperar.
EN: I need to go. · You do not need to wait for me.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: external/practical requirement -> ter que · need -> precisar · não precisar = need not
Flip for the range of dever.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Dever spans obligation, recommendation, expectation, and deduction
REF: 24.2.7

Dever plus an infinitive can impose or recommend an action. Context can instead make it an expectation or inference.
TL: Você deve ir ao médico. · O presidente deve fazer uma declaração amanhã.
EN: You should see a doctor. · The president is due to make a statement tomorrow.
[PAUSE:950]
Negation does not erase the ambiguity: não deve can prohibit an action or judge it unlikely.
TL: Você não deve mexer no computador. · Eles não devem terminar hoje.
EN: You must not touch the computer. · They are not likely to finish today.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: dever + action -> should/must · dever + expected event -> be due/likely · context fixes force

[CARD]
TITLE: The perfect infinitive moves modal judgment into the past
REF: 24.2.1, 24.2.7

Poder plus ter and a participle presents a past possibility; in a counterfactual frame it can present an unrealized option or criticism.
TL: Ele pode ter ligado. · Você podia ter me avisado.
EN: He may have called. · You could have let me know.
[PAUSE:950]
Dever plus the perfect infinitive separates present deduction about a past event from unfulfilled past obligation.
TL: Ela deve ter esquecido. · Eu deveria ter telefonado.
EN: She must have forgotten. · I should have phoned.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: pode/deve ter feito = present judgment about past · podia/deveria ter feito = unrealized option or duty
Flip for impersonal necessity and register.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Third-person singular can leave the responsible person unstated
REF: 24.2.1, 24.2.6, 24.2.8

Spoken Portuguese often uses pode, tem que, and precisa without a named subject when the rule or practical question applies generally.
TL: Pode entrar? · Tem que pagar adiantado? · Precisa marcar hora?
EN: May one enter? · Do we have to pay in advance? · Is it necessary to make an appointment?
[PAUSE:950]
The unexpressed participant is recovered from the situation. Do not invent an overt ele or ela.
SHOW: pode/tem que/precisa + infinitive -> general permission, obligation, or need

[CARD]
TITLE: Impersonal haver and spoken ter present existence
REF: 24.3.1, 24.3.2

Impersonal haver means there is or there are in written and neutral formal language. Spoken Brazilian Portuguese commonly uses ter instead.
TL: Há várias soluções. · Tem leite na geladeira.
EN: There are several solutions. · There is milk in the fridge.
[PAUSE:950]
These constructions have no specific grammatical subject, so the verb remains third-person singular even when the following noun is plural.
TL: Havia muitas pessoas. · Tinha duas cadeiras na sala.
EN: There were many people. · There were two chairs in the room.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: formal/neutral existence -> há/havia · spoken existence -> tem/tinha · always singular
Flip for elapsed and continuing time.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Há and faz connect a duration to its time viewpoint
REF: 24.3.1, 24.3.3

With a present-tense event continuing now, há or the less formal faz expresses for. With a past event, há expresses ago.
TL: Moramos aqui há cinco anos. · Eles chegaram há dois dias.
EN: We have lived here for five years. · They arrived two days ago.
[PAUSE:950]
Fazer can also introduce a duration in a que construction. Formal past narration may shift the impersonal verb to havia or fazia.
TL: Faz anos que não a vejo. · Fazia seis meses que não se falavam.
EN: I have not seen her for years. · They had not spoken for six months.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: continuing duration -> há/faz + time · past event -> há + time = ago · past reference -> havia/fazia

[CARD]
TITLE: Fazer gives impersonal weather; dar tests possibility or sufficiency
REF: 24.3.3, 24.3.4

Fazer is impersonal in weather and temperature expressions.
TL: Fez sol ontem. · Amanhã vai fazer quarenta graus.
EN: It was sunny yesterday. · Tomorrow it will be forty degrees.
[PAUSE:950]
In speech, dar often means that something is possible, sufficient, or fits the available time.
TL: Dá para falar mais alto? · Você acha que vai dar tempo? · Cinquenta reais dá?
EN: Could you speak louder? · Do you think there will be time? · Is fifty reais enough?
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: weather/temperature -> fazer · practical possibility/enough/time -> dar
Flip to choose among the four impersonal verbs.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Meaning and register select haver, ter, fazer, or dar
REF: 24.3

Choose haver or spoken ter for existence, haver or fazer for duration, fazer for weather, and dar for practical possibility or sufficiency.
TL: Há um problema. · Tem café. · Faz frio. · Não dá para esperar.
EN: There is a problem. · There is coffee. · It is cold. · It is not possible to wait.
[PAUSE:1000]
All four remain singular in these impersonal uses. The following exercise preserves the source's formal and spoken alternatives where both are valid.
SHOW: existence -> haver/ter · duration -> haver/fazer · weather -> fazer · possibility/sufficiency -> dar

[CARD]
TITLE: Diagnose meaning before choosing the helper
REF: 24.1, 24.2, 24.3

First identify the lexical event. Then decide whether the added layer is viewpoint, modal force, or an impersonal setting.
TL: Acabei de sair. · Posso sair. · Preciso sair. · Dá para sair.
EN: I have just left. · I may leave. · I need to leave. · It is possible to leave.
[PAUSE:1000]
Finally choose tense and register: podia versus pôde, queria versus quis, há versus tem, or ter que versus formal ter de.
TL: Andei trabalhando. · Não consegui terminar. · Há muito trabalho.
EN: I have been working. · I did not manage to finish. · There is a lot of work.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: event -> viewpoint/force/setting -> tense -> register -> construction
Flip for the high-value contrasts.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Keep five contrasts separate under pressure
REF: 24.1, 24.2, 24.3

Do not insert a preposition between ir and an infinitive. Distinguish vir fazer, movement toward an action, from vir a fazer, an eventual result.
TL: Vou comprar pão. · Ele veio ajudar. · Isso veio a mudar tudo.
EN: I am going to buy bread. · He came to help. · This ended up changing everything.
[PAUSE:950]
Use conseguir for achieved physical ability and saber for learned skill. Use dever without a preposition, and remember the written/spoken pair há versus tem for existence.
TL: Consegui abrir. · Sei nadar. · Deve chegar cedo. · Há gente aqui. · Tem gente aqui.
EN: I managed to open it. · I can swim. · He should arrive early. · There are people here.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: ir + infinitive · vir vs vir a · conseguir vs saber · dever + infinitive · há written / tem spoken
