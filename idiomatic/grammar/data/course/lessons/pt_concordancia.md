## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Gender belongs to the noun and controls its partners
REF: 2.1, 2.2

Every Portuguese noun is grammatically masculine or feminine. Natural sex helps with many words for people and animals, but it cannot determine the gender of an object or an idea.
TL: O carro novo ficou ao lado da casa nova.
EN: The new car was left beside the new house.
[PAUSE:900]
The article is the clearest everyday signal. Learn a new noun with o or a, because adjectives, demonstratives, possessives, and pronouns must follow the noun's gender.
SHOW: noun + article as one memory · o carro novo · a casa nova
Flip to make a fast first prediction from the ending.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Endings predict gender, but they do not guarantee it
REF: 2.2.1, 2.2.2

An unstressed final o strongly favors masculine, while final a strongly favors feminine. The endings -dor, -sor, -tor, -eu, and many international nouns in -ma also favor masculine.
TL: O problema do cantor parece sério.
EN: The singer's problem seems serious.
[PAUSE:900]
The endings -ção, -são, -dade, -gem, -ice, -idão, -ie, -ite, -se, and -tude strongly favor feminine.
TL: A decisão sobre a viagem foi difícil.
EN: The decision about the trip was difficult.
[PAUSE:1000]
Store exceptions whole: a mão, a foto, o dia, o mapa, o planeta, and o tema.
SHOW: ending -> prediction · known article -> decision · exception -> store whole
Twenty-six article completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Meaning can override the visible ending
REF: 2.2.3, 2.2.4, 2.2.5

Many human nouns in -ante, -ente, -ista, and similar endings keep one noun form while the article shows the person's gender.
TL: O dentista chegou; a dentista já estava aqui.
EN: The male dentist arrived; the female dentist was already here.
[PAUSE:900]
Other nouns have a fixed grammatical gender even when they refer to a person of any sex: a pessoa, a testemunha, a vítima, but o cônjuge and o indivíduo.
TL: A vítima era um homem jovem.
EN: The victim was a young man.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not force grammatical gender to mirror identity. It belongs first to the noun.
SHOW: common form + article -> referent · fixed-gender noun -> agreement stays fixed
Flip for nouns whose article changes the meaning.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Sometimes gender selects a different lexical meaning
REF: 2.2.5, 2.2.6, 2.2.7

With a small set of nouns, o and a distinguish two meanings. O capital is financial capital; a capital is a capital city. O moral is morale; a moral is morality or the moral of a story.
TL: A capital precisa de capital para o projeto.
EN: The capital city needs capital for the project.
[PAUSE:900]
An implied category can also control gender. Rivers, seas, mountains, cars, hotels, and sports teams are normally masculine; cities, streets, networks, companies, shops, and airlines are normally feminine.
TL: O Amazonas é enorme, e toda Paris parece distante.
EN: The Amazon is enormous, and all of Paris seems distant.
SHOW: article change -> check meaning · unspoken category -> supplies gender

[CARD]
TITLE: Regular feminine forms change the agreeing phrase
REF: 2.3.1, 2.3.2, 2.3.3

For nouns and adjectives in o, replace o with a. In -ão forms that have a gender contrast, replace -ão with -ã. Words in -dor, -sor, or -tor normally add a to the final r.
TL: Um professor alemão; uma professora alemã.
EN: A German male teacher; a German female teacher.
[PAUSE:900]
Agreement is distributed: changing the referent may require a new article, a feminine noun, and a feminine adjective at the same time.
TL: O escritor trabalhador; a escritora trabalhadora.
EN: The hard-working male writer; the hard-working female writer.
SHOW: -o -> -a · -ão -> -ã · -or -> -ora · update every agreeing word
Flip for derived and invariant forms.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Some feminine forms are lexical, while many adjectives stay unchanged
REF: 2.3.4, 2.3.5, 2.3.6, 2.3.7

Productive changes include europeu to europeia and chinês to chinesa. Other frequent pairs must be retrieved whole: herói and heroína, príncipe and princesa, poeta and poetisa, deus and deusa, mau and má.
TL: A heroína europeia encontrou uma princesa chinesa.
EN: The European heroine met a Chinese princess.
[PAUSE:900]
Adjectives ending in a, e, l, m, r, s, or z usually have no distinct feminine form, apart from named classes such as -or and -ês and exceptions such as bom and boa.
TL: Um estudante feliz; uma estudante feliz.
EN: A happy male student; a happy female student.
[PAUSE:1000]
The unchanged adjective still agrees grammatically; it simply has one surface form.
SHOW: productive class -> transform · lexical pair -> retrieve · invariant ending -> no visible change
Twelve complete phrase transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Compounds inherit gender from structure and use
REF: 2.2.8, 2.3.9, 2.3.10

A hyphenated compound noun usually takes the gender of its first noun. A compound beginning with a verb stem is normally masculine.
TL: A meia-noite chegou; o guarda-chuva ficou no carro.
EN: Midnight arrived; the umbrella was left in the car.
[PAUSE:900]
In a compound adjective made from two adjectives, normally only the second element takes the feminine form. If the second element is a noun used as a color label, the compound stays unchanged.
TL: Uma blusa vermelho-escura; uma blusa amarelo-laranja.
EN: A dark-red blouse; an orange-yellow blouse.
[PAUSE:1000]
Most animal names have one grammatical gender for either sex; add macho or fêmea when the distinction matters.
SHOW: compound noun -> first noun or verb-stem rule · compound adjective -> inspect second element
Flip for Portuguese-Spanish gender traps.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Familiar Spanish cognates can carry different Portuguese gender
REF: 2.2, 2.3

For a Spanish speaker, resemblance is useful but not decisive. Portuguese nouns in -agem are feminine, as are a origem, a margem, a cor, a dor, a flor, a análise, a árvore, and a ponte.
TL: A origem da viagem fica clara no mapa.
EN: The origin of the trip is clear on the map.
[PAUSE:900]
Important masculine contrasts include o costume, o legume, o leite, o mel, o nariz, o sal, and o sangue.
TL: O costume inclui leite e mel.
EN: The custom includes milk and honey.
[PAUSE:1000]
The shared Portuguese ending -ão hides several histories, so it does not settle gender by itself: o alemão but a decisão.
SHOW: cognate -> verify Portuguese article · -agem normally F · -ão -> inspect the word
Twenty-six owner-relevant contrast decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The basic plural adds s and preserves agreement
REF: 3.1, 3.2

Most nouns and adjectives ending in a vowel add s. A feminine singular is formed first and then pluralized, so every agreeing word carries both gender and number where its form permits.
TL: A casa barata; as casas baratas.
EN: The cheap house; the cheap houses.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: O museu novo; os museus novos.
EN: The new museum; the new museums.
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat the noun phrase as a linked system, not as independent word endings.
SHOW: vowel + s · feminine singular + s · article + noun + adjective move together
Flip for the three lexical plurals of -ão.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Final -ão has three plural outcomes
REF: 3.2.1

Most words in -ão take -ões, including decisão and caminhão. A smaller group takes -ãos, including cidadão, cristão, irmão, mão, and paroxytones such as órgão.
TL: As decisões dos cidadãos chegaram.
EN: The citizens' decisions arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
A lexical group takes -ães, including alemão, cão, capitão, and pão.
TL: Os capitães deram pães aos cães.
EN: The captains gave bread rolls to the dogs.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn a frequent -ão noun with its plural; spelling alone does not choose among all three endings.
SHOW: default -ões · lexical -ãos · lexical -ães
Twenty-two broad plural transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Final consonants trigger a small set of plural repairs
REF: 3.2.2, 3.2.3, 3.2.4

Final l becomes is. Stress controls the written accent in important subclasses: papel to papéis, lençol to lençóis, fuzil to fuzis, but fóssil to fósseis.
TL: Os papéis azuis estão nos móveis.
EN: The blue papers are on the furniture.
[PAUSE:900]
Final m becomes ns. Final r and z normally add es.
TL: Um som ruim; dois sons ruins. Uma luz; duas luzes.
EN: One bad sound; two bad sounds. One light; two lights.
[PAUSE:1000]
High-frequency exceptions still win: gol commonly gives gols, and mal gives males.
SHOW: -l -> -is · -m -> -ns · -r/-z -> +es · re-evaluate stress and accents
Flip for final s and invariant words.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Final s responds to stress
REF: 3.2.5

A monosyllable or a word stressed on its final syllable normally adds es. A written accent can disappear when the new ending moves the stress pattern.
TL: Um mês; dois meses. Um país; dois países.
EN: One month; two months. One country; two countries.
[PAUSE:900]
Other nouns and adjectives already ending in s normally stay unchanged: o ônibus, os ônibus; uma pergunta simples, duas perguntas simples.
TL: Os ônibus simples chegaram cedo.
EN: The simple buses arrived early.
[PAUSE:1000]
Invariant form does not mean invariant agreement elsewhere in the phrase.
SHOW: final-stressed -s -> +es · other -s -> usually unchanged · partners still pluralize

[CARD]
TITLE: A compound plural follows its internal relationship
REF: 3.2.6

When a compound is adjective plus noun or noun plus adjective, both elements normally pluralize. With a verb stem plus noun, an invariable word plus noun, or repeated elements, normally only the second element pluralizes.
TL: A segunda-feira; as segundas-feiras.
EN: Monday; Mondays.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: O guarda-roupa; os guarda-roupas.
EN: The wardrobe; the wardrobes.
[PAUSE:1000]
In noun-plus-noun compounds, the second element may also remain unchanged when it limits the first; usage increasingly permits both elements to pluralize.
SHOW: adjective+noun -> both · verb/invariable+noun -> second · noun+noun -> usage may vary
Flip for compound adjectives and fixed compounds.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Not every visible element is eligible for plural marking
REF: 3.2.6, 3.2.7

Compounds beginning with sem are invariant, and a compound stays unchanged if neither element can pluralize. In compound adjectives made from two adjectives, normally only the second adjective takes the plural.
TL: Os sem-teto falaram com os guardas-costas.
EN: The homeless people spoke with the bodyguards.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Os vestidos verde-escuros ficaram prontos.
EN: The dark-green dresses were ready.
[PAUSE:1000]
Exceptions such as surdo-mudo pluralize both elements, while azul-marinho and azul-celeste remain invariant.
SHOW: classify elements first -> pluralize only licensed positions
Ten compound-noun transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Plural agreement reaches every licensed target
REF: 3.1, 3.2, 3.3

A plural noun controls its articles and adjectives. The masculine plural can refer to males alone or to a mixed group; the feminine plural refers only to females.
TL: Os professores brasileiros e as professoras brasileiras chegaram.
EN: The Brazilian male teachers and female teachers arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
Family plurals such as filhos, irmãos, netos, pais, primos, and tios can include both sexes unless the context narrows them.
TL: Ela tem dois irmãos: um homem e uma mulher.
EN: She has two siblings: a man and a woman.
[PAUSE:1000]
If male-only meaning matters, context or homens can make it explicit.
SHOW: plural controller -> plural partners · masculine plural -> male or mixed · feminine plural -> female only
Flip for a small family-word ambiguity.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Avôs and avós make both stress and reference visible
REF: 3.3

The spelling and pronunciation distinguish family meanings. Avôs, with the closed circumflex vowel, means grandfathers. Avós, with the open acute vowel, can mean grandparents or grandmothers.
TL: Meus avós incluem meu avô e minha avó.
EN: My grandparents include my grandfather and my grandmother.
[PAUSE:900]
Agreement follows the group meaning chosen in context, while the accent remains part of the lexical form.
TL: As avós estavam cansadas; os avôs estavam cansados.
EN: The grandmothers were tired; the grandfathers were tired.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not translate a masculine family plural as male-only without evidence.
SHOW: avôs = grandfathers · avós = grandparents or grandmothers
Twelve complete noun-and-adjective plural phrases follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Spoken Brazilian Portuguese can use singular with generic plural meaning
REF: 3.4.1, 3.4.2

In colloquial speech, a countable noun can appear as an uncountable singular for an unspecified quantity or a whole category.
TL: Hoje em dia tem muito carro na rua.
EN: Nowadays there are a lot of cars on the road.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Criança adora história de fantasma.
EN: Children love ghost stories.
[PAUSE:1000]
Formal writing normally makes the generic plurals explicit: muitos carros; as crianças adoram histórias de fantasmas. A numeral or quantifier such as alguns or vários also requires ordinary countable number.
SHOW: colloquial generic -> singular possible · specified/formal -> ordinary plural
Flip for paired objects and one-per-person expressions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A Portuguese singular can represent a pair or one item each
REF: 3.4.3, 3.4.4, 3.4.5

Brazilian Portuguese often uses a singular for paired body parts, clothing, and tools where English favors a plural expression.
TL: O Vinícius tem olho azul e comprou um sapato novo.
EN: Vinícius has blue eyes and bought a new pair of shoes.
[PAUSE:900]
With a plural subject, singular often expresses one item per person.
TL: Todos os homens tiraram o chapéu.
EN: All the men took off their hats.
[PAUSE:1000]
Use context to decide whether one physical object, a pair, or one-per-person distribution is intended.
SHOW: paired item -> singular often natural · plural owners + one each -> singular

[CARD]
TITLE: Countability is lexical, not a direct translation
REF: 3.5.1, 3.5.2, 3.5.3

Several Portuguese plurals correspond to English mass nouns: conselhos, dados, equipamentos, informações, móveis, notícias, provas, and talheres.
TL: Ela me deu dois bons conselhos.
EN: She gave me two pieces of good advice.
[PAUSE:900]
Conversely, a Portuguese mass noun can take an indefinite article for an amount, and an abstract noun can become countable for one act or instance.
TL: Ele herdou um dinheiro e depois falou várias idiotices.
EN: He inherited some money and later said several stupid things.
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose number from Portuguese meaning and usage, then translate the whole expression.
SHOW: Portuguese lexical countability -> number -> agreement -> natural translation
Flip for a compact agreement checklist.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Decide the noun first, then propagate its features
REF: 2.1, 3.1, 3.4, 3.5

For a new phrase, first retrieve the noun with its article and lexical plural. Then decide whether the intended meaning is singular, plural, generic, paired, or one-each.
TL: Essas informações importantes chegaram ontem.
EN: This important information arrived yesterday.
[PAUSE:900]
Finally, propagate gender and number to every article, demonstrative, possessive, adjective, pronoun, and verb that the construction requires.
TL: A maioria dos alunos é brasileira.
EN: Most of the students are Brazilian.
[PAUSE:1000]
If a form looks unchanged, verify that it is genuinely invariant rather than silently left uninflected.
SHOW: noun identity -> gender -> lexical plural -> intended count -> agreement chain
