## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Tense locates an event from a chosen viewpoint
REF: 15.1, 15.2, 15.11

An Italian tense does more than place an event on a calendar. It locates the event before, at, or after a reference point chosen by the speaker.
TL: Oggi lavoro; ieri avevo già finito; domani partirò.
EN: Today I am working; yesterday I had already finished; tomorrow I will leave.
[PAUSE:900]
The reference point can be the present, another past event, or a future deadline. Compound forms normally put one event before that reference point.
SHOW: event time + reference point -> tense choice
Flip to separate tense from mood.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mood presents the speaker's stance toward a clause
REF: 15.25, 15.26, 15.27

The indicative normally presents a clause as asserted. The conditional presents it through a condition, report, or softened stance. The subjunctive is selected by particular meanings and structures rather than by a simple rule of uncertainty.
TL: So che arriva; arriverebbe domani; voglio che arrivi.
EN: I know that he is arriving; he would arrive tomorrow; I want him to arrive.
[PAUSE:900]
First identify the time relationship. Then ask what the governing expression does with the embedded clause: assert it, imagine it, evaluate it, desire it, or leave it nonfactual.
SHOW: time relationship -> tense · clause status -> mood

[CARD]
TITLE: Future and future perfect use different future landmarks
REF: 15.1, 15.3

Use the future for an event later than now. Use the future perfect when that event must already be complete before a later future reference point.
TL: Domani finirò il rapporto; entro venerdì l'avrò consegnato.
EN: Tomorrow I will finish the report; by Friday I will have submitted it.
[PAUSE:900]
Unlike English, Italian normally keeps a future form after future-time conjunctions such as quando, appena, finché, and dopo che.
TL: Ti chiamerò appena sarò arrivata.
EN: I will call you as soon as I have arrived.
SHOW: later event -> future · completed before future landmark -> future perfect

[SIDE]
TITLE: The future can express inference; the past conditional looks forward from the past
REF: 15.2, 15.4, 15.5

A future form can mark a present conjecture, while a future perfect can infer a completed past event.
TL: Saranno le nove; avrà perso il treno.
EN: It must be nine; he probably missed the train.
[PAUSE:900]
For an event that was still future from a past viewpoint, standard Italian uses the past conditional, not the present conditional.
TL: Credevo che sarebbe arrivata presto.
EN: I thought she would arrive soon.
SHOW: guess now -> future · guess about completed past -> future perfect · future from past -> past conditional

[CARD]
TITLE: Da measures an activity still open at the reference time
REF: 15.10

When an activity began earlier and is still continuing now, Italian uses the present with da, not a compound past tense.
TL: Studio italiano da tre anni.
EN: I have been studying Italian for three years.
[PAUSE:900]
Move the reference point into the past and the imperfect takes over.
TL: Studiavo italiano da tre anni quando mi trasferii a Roma.
EN: I had been studying Italian for three years when I moved to Rome.
SHOW: still continuing now -> present + da · still continuing then -> imperfect + da
Twelve source duration and pluperfect decisions follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A pluperfect event is already complete at a past landmark
REF: 15.11

The trapassato prossimo places a completed event before another past situation. Its auxiliary is imperfect; its participle follows the ordinary avere or essere rules.
TL: Quando siamo arrivati, Marta era già partita.
EN: When we arrived, Marta had already left.
[PAUSE:900]
The trapassato remoto is far more restricted. It occurs chiefly in formal or literary sequences after conjunctions such as appena or dopo che when the main event is in the passato remoto.
TL: Dopo che ebbe firmato, uscì.
EN: After he had signed, he left.
SHOW: prior to ordinary past -> trapassato prossimo · prior in remote-past sequence -> trapassato remoto

[CARD]
TITLE: Imperfect opens the scene; a perfective past advances it
REF: 15.12, 15.16

Use the imperfect for an ongoing state, repeated habit, or background frame without presenting its endpoint. Use passato prossimo or passato remoto for a bounded event viewed as a whole.
TL: Pioveva quando il taxi è arrivato.
EN: It was raining when the taxi arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
The contrast is aspectual: the imperfect looks inside the event, while a perfective past presents its boundary.
TL: Da bambina leggeva ogni sera; ieri ha letto tutto il libro.
EN: As a child she read every evening; yesterday she read the whole book.
SHOW: background / state / habit -> imperfect · bounded event -> perfective past
Ten hygiene-gated past-tense choices follow; synthetic passages are omitted.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Passato prossimo and passato remoto divide discourse, not simply time
REF: 15.13, 15.14, 15.15, 15.16

In much everyday speech, passato prossimo reports completed events even in the distant past. Passato remoto remains productive in historical and literary narrative and in regional usage.
TL: Dante nacque a Firenze; stamattina ho incontrato Luca.
EN: Dante was born in Florence; this morning I met Luca.
[PAUSE:900]
The imperfect also has special discourse uses: softening a request, entering a child's play frame, or presenting an event as background-like narrative description.
TL: Volevo chiederle un favore.
EN: I wanted to ask you a favor.
SHOW: choose discourse viewpoint first · calendar distance alone is not enough

[CARD]
TITLE: Stare plus gerund zooms in on an event in progress
REF: 15.17

Stare plus gerund presents a dynamic event as unfolding around the reference time. It is useful when the ongoing phase matters, not as an automatic replacement for every English progressive.
TL: Sto preparando la cena; stavo guidando quando hai chiamato.
EN: I am preparing dinner; I was driving when you called.
[PAUSE:900]
State predicates and generic facts normally stay in a simple tense.
TL: Conosco la risposta; vivo a Bologna.
EN: I know the answer; I live in Bologna.
SHOW: dynamic event viewed from inside -> stare + gerund · state or general fact -> simple tense
Eleven progressive decisions follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Andare and venire plus gerund add direction through time
REF: 15.17, 15.18, 15.19

Andare plus gerund often presents gradual or repeated development; venire plus gerund can show a process building toward the reference point.
TL: La situazione va migliorando; il dubbio veniva crescendo.
EN: The situation is gradually improving; the doubt had been growing.
[PAUSE:900]
Physical posture is normally expressed with essere plus an adjective or posture phrase, not by copying English be plus -ing.
TL: Giulia è seduta; Paolo è in piedi davanti alla porta.
EN: Giulia is sitting; Paolo is standing in front of the door.
SHOW: stare -> event in progress · andare / venire -> developing process · posture -> essere + posture expression

[CARD]
TITLE: A gerund compresses a clause controlled by the main subject
REF: 15.20, 15.21

An Italian gerund can express time, manner, cause, condition, or concession. Its understood subject normally matches the subject of the finite clause.
TL: Uscendo di casa, Marta ha incontrato Leo.
EN: While leaving home, Marta met Leo.
[PAUSE:900]
If the subjects differ, the compressed version can attach the action to the wrong participant. Keep a finite clause instead.
TL: Mentre Leo usciva, Marta lo ha visto.
EN: While Leo was leaving, Marta saw him.
SHOW: same understood subject -> gerund possible · different subjects -> finite clause

[SIDE]
TITLE: Participles and infinitives compress different structures
REF: 15.22, 15.23, 15.24

An absolute past participle presents a prior completed event and agrees with its expressed noun. A perfect infinitive can mark anteriority when its subject is controlled by the main clause.
TL: Chiusa la porta, siamo usciti; dopo aver cenato, ho telefonato.
EN: Once the door was closed, we left; after having dinner, I called.
[PAUSE:900]
The infinitive can also function as a noun-like subject, usually without an article in ordinary modern use.
TL: Viaggiare da soli richiede pazienza.
EN: Traveling alone requires patience.
SHOW: gerund -> simultaneous frame · participle -> completed frame + agreement · infinitive -> controlled event or noun-like action

[CARD]
TITLE: The subjunctive is selected by a relationship between clauses
REF: 15.25, 15.26, 15.28

The subjunctive does not independently mean doubtful. It commonly appears when one clause presents another as wanted, required, feared, imagined, or evaluated rather than simply asserted.
TL: Voglio che tu venga; temo che sia tardi.
EN: I want you to come; I fear that it is late.
[PAUSE:900]
With one controller, Italian often prefers an infinitive; a changed subject normally requires che plus a finite subjunctive.
TL: Spero di partire; spero che Marta parta.
EN: I hope to leave; I hope Marta leaves.
SHOW: same controller -> often infinitive · different subject -> che + subjunctive
Thirty source clause transformations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Subjunctive tense is relative to the governing viewpoint
REF: 15.40, 15.41, 15.42, 15.47, 15.48

With a present or future governing frame, use present subjunctive for simultaneous or later time and perfect subjunctive for earlier time.
TL: Penso che arrivi oggi; penso che sia già arrivato.
EN: I think he is arriving today; I think he has already arrived.
[PAUSE:900]
With a past or conditional frame, imperfect subjunctive normally marks simultaneous or later time, and pluperfect subjunctive marks earlier time.
TL: Pensavo che arrivasse; pensavo che fosse già arrivato.
EN: I thought he would arrive; I thought he had already arrived.
SHOW: present frame -> present / perfect subjunctive · past frame -> imperfect / pluperfect subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Purpose, hypothesis, and concession create nonasserted frames
REF: 15.29, 15.30, 15.43

Purpose clauses with perché or affinché select the subjunctive. So do many hypothetical expressions such as purché and a meno che, and concessive expressions such as benché or sebbene.
TL: Parlo piano perché tutti capiscano; verrò purché tu mi avverta.
EN: I am speaking slowly so that everyone understands; I will come provided you warn me.
[PAUSE:900]
These forms preserve meaning: purpose is not cause, a condition is not a fact, and a concession acknowledges an obstacle without making it decisive.
SHOW: purpose / hypothesis / concession -> subjunctive frame
Twenty-two conjunction transformations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Evaluation and future time can also open a subjunctive clause
REF: 15.44, 15.45, 15.46

An evaluation built around a noun or adjective can select che plus subjunctive because the clause is being judged rather than newly asserted.
TL: È un peccato che il museo sia chiuso.
EN: It is a shame that the museum is closed.
[PAUSE:900]
Prima che selects the subjunctive; other time conjunctions can do so when their event is anticipated or nonfactual. Indirect questions allow a subjunctive when the question is presented as unresolved or distanced.
TL: Partiamo prima che inizi a piovere; mi chiedo dove sia Luca.
EN: Let us leave before it starts raining; I wonder where Luca is.
SHOW: evaluation / anticipated boundary / unresolved question -> subjunctive often selected

[CARD]
TITLE: A relative clause chooses mood from the noun it describes
REF: 15.33, 15.34, 15.36, 15.37

Use the indicative when the antecedent is specific and presented as real. The subjunctive is common when the antecedent is sought, denied, indefinite, or merely possible.
TL: Conosco una guida che parla arabo; cerco una guida che parli arabo.
EN: I know a guide who speaks Arabic; I am looking for a guide who speaks Arabic.
[PAUSE:900]
Negated existence, indefinite relatives, and superlative or exclusive descriptions also favor the subjunctive.
TL: Non c'è nessuno che possa aiutarci; è il film più bello che abbia visto.
EN: There is nobody who can help us; it is the best film I have seen.
SHOW: identified and asserted -> indicative · sought / denied / indefinite / exclusive -> subjunctive
Thirty-six hygiene-gated relative, impersonal, and indirect-question transformations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Impersonal expressions differ in whether they assert a fact
REF: 15.38, 15.39, 15.40, 15.41, 15.42, 15.45

Evaluations and possibilities such as è strano che, è importante che, and può darsi che normally take the subjunctive.
TL: È importante che voi siate pronti; può darsi che nevichi.
EN: It is important that you be ready; it may snow.
[PAUSE:900]
Expressions that straightforwardly report a fact can take the indicative. With opinion, seeming, reaction, negation, or an indirect question, identify whether the embedded content is asserted, evaluated, or left open.
TL: È chiaro che hanno capito; non so se abbiano capito.
EN: It is clear that they understood; I do not know whether they understood.
SHOW: factual assertion -> indicative · evaluation / possibility / nonassertion -> subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Translate English modal words by the job they perform
REF: 15.49, 15.50

English will and would may express future time, willingness, habit, inference, or a condition. Italian distributes those jobs among the future, volere, the imperfect, and the conditional.
TL: Domani partirà; non vuole ascoltare; da bambino veniva spesso; partirebbe con noi.
EN: He will leave tomorrow; he will not listen; as a child he would often come; he would leave with us.
[PAUSE:900]
Likewise, must can express obligation with dovere or inference with a conjectural future.
TL: Devi firmare; saranno già a casa.
EN: You must sign; they must already be home.
SHOW: identify function -> choose Italian construction · never translate the modal in isolation
Forty-seven source modal translations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Potere, sapere, and riuscire divide the territory of can
REF: 15.51, 15.52, 15.53, 15.54, 15.55, 15.56, 15.57

Potere expresses permission or possibility; sapere plus infinitive expresses learned ability; riuscire a expresses managing or succeeding. Perception and understanding verbs often need no modal at all.
TL: Puoi entrare; sa nuotare; riesco ad aprirlo; non ti sento.
EN: You may come in; she can swim; I can manage to open it; I cannot hear you.
[PAUSE:900]
For uncertain possibility, Italian can use forse, può darsi che plus subjunctive, or a conditional construction. Past forms distinguish actual ability, unrealized opportunity, and speculation.
TL: Può darsi che arrivino; avrebbero potuto avvertirci.
EN: They may arrive; they could have warned us.
SHOW: permission / possibility -> potere · learned skill -> sapere · achievement -> riuscire · perception -> simple verb
