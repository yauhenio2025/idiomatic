## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Mood tells the listener how to take the clause
SVG: de_konjunktiv_c01f.svg
REF: 14

Mood is the speaker's stance built into the verb. The indicative presents a proposition as part of the real-world conversation:
TL: Der Ausschuss billigt heute den Entwurf.
EN: The committee is approving the draft today.
[PAUSE:900]
The imperative turns a proposition into a directive:
TL: Billigen Sie den Entwurf noch heute!
EN: Approve the draft today!
[PAUSE:900]
And the subjunctive opens two kinds of distance. One marks a report as someone else's claim:
TL: Die Sprecherin erklärt, der Ausschuss billige heute den Entwurf.
EN: The spokesperson states that the committee is approving the draft today.
[PAUSE:900]
The other marks a situation as imagined or remote:
TL: Wenn der Ausschuss den Entwurf billigen würde, könnten wir beginnen.
EN: If the committee approved the draft, we could begin.
SHOW: fact · directive · reported claim · imagined world

[SIDE]
TITLE: The addressee determines the imperative form
SVG: de_konjunktiv_c01b.svg
REF: 14.1.1

German has dedicated command patterns for the three ways of saying you. Informal singular uses the verb stem and normally drops the pronoun:
TL: Prüf bitte noch einmal die Zahlen.
EN: Please check the figures once more.
[PAUSE:800]
An unstressed ending is optional after many stems but remains where the sound pattern requires it:
TL: Antworte bitte bis Freitag.
EN: Please reply by Friday.
[PAUSE:800]
Informal plural uses the ordinary second-person plural verb, again without the pronoun:
TL: Prüft bitte noch einmal die Zahlen.
EN: Please check the figures once more.
[PAUSE:800]
The formal command keeps its pronoun after the verb, which looks like the infinitive for almost every verb:
TL: Prüfen Sie bitte noch einmal die Zahlen.
EN: Please check the figures once more.
[PAUSE:1000]
The verb meaning to be is the exception:
TL: Seien Sie bitte vorsichtig.
EN: Please be careful.
[PAUSE:800]
Some strong verbs change their stem vowel in the singular command:
TL: Lies den Absatz bitte noch einmal.
EN: Please read the paragraph once more.
[PAUSE:800]
But a present-tense umlaut normally disappears:
TL: Lauf nicht so schnell!
EN: Don't run so fast!
SHOW: singular = stem · plural = present minus pronoun · formal = verb plus pronoun
Seven command transformations come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Tune a command before you strengthen it
SVG: de_konjunktiv_c02f.svg
REF: 14.1.1

A bare imperative can be efficient, but intonation may make it sound abrupt:
TL: Schick mir die Unterlagen.
EN: Send me the documents.
[PAUSE:800]
Small modal particles can recast the same act as a reminder, invitation or reassurance:
TL: Schick mir doch bitte die Unterlagen.
EN: Please do send me the documents.
[PAUSE:900]
One particle can explicitly grant permission:
TL: Nehmen Sie ruhig noch etwas Kuchen.
EN: Do feel free to have some more cake.
These particles are not decorative. They tell the listener how the social force of the command should land.
SHOW: bare command = direct · particles tune urgency, warmth and permission
Flip for commands without a personal addressee.

[SIDE]
TITLE: German has a whole command toolbox
REF: 14.1.2, 14.1.3

Public instructions often use an infinitive, making the directive impersonal:
TL: Bitte die Tür geschlossen halten.
EN: Please keep the door closed.
[PAUSE:800]
A subjectless passive sounds like a rule already in force:
TL: Hier wird nicht telefoniert.
EN: No phone calls are made here.
[PAUSE:800]
A standalone subordinate clause sounds emphatic and personally invested:
TL: Dass du mir ja pünktlich zurückkommst!
EN: Make sure you come back on time!
[PAUSE:900]
For a shared proposal, an imperative of letting can include the speaker:
TL: Lassen Sie uns mit dem schwierigsten Punkt beginnen.
EN: Let us begin with the most difficult point.
[PAUSE:900]
And a third-person instruction normally uses a modal of obligation:
TL: Die Teilnehmenden sollen ihre Telefone ausschalten.
EN: The participants are to switch off their phones.
SHOW: person addressed → imperative · public rule → infinitive or passive · third person → obligation

[CARD]
TITLE: The two subjunctives divide the work
SVG: de_konjunktiv_c03f.svg
REF: 14.2.1, 14.2.2

The traditional tense names are a trap. The first subjunctive and the second subjunctive are better understood as two systems with different jobs. The first is the reporting system:
TL: Die Ministerin erklärt, die Lage sei stabil.
EN: The minister states that the situation is stable.
[PAUSE:1000]
The second is the distance system. It builds imagined, unlikely or counterfactual worlds:
TL: Wenn die Lage stabil wäre, könnten wir investieren.
EN: If the situation were stable, we could invest.
[PAUSE:1000]
Both sentences concern the present. The contrast is not present versus past; it is reported claim versus distance from reality.
SHOW: first subjunctive = report · second subjunctive = remoteness
Flip for time inside each system.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Each system has a present and a past lane
REF: 14.2.1, 14.2.2

In the reporting system, a simple form places the reported event at the same time as the report:
TL: Er behauptet, der Zug komme pünktlich.
EN: He claims that the train is arriving on time.
[PAUSE:800]
An auxiliary plus participle moves the reported event back:
TL: Er behauptet, der Zug sei pünktlich angekommen.
EN: He claims that the train arrived on time.
[PAUSE:1000]
The distance system makes the same contrast with its own forms:
TL: Wenn der Zug pünktlich käme, würden wir unseren Anschluss noch erreichen.
EN: If the train arrived on time, we would still make our connection.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Wenn der Zug pünktlich gekommen wäre, hätten wir unseren Anschluss erreicht.
EN: If the train had arrived on time, we would have made our connection.
SHOW: report: simple / perfect · remote: simple / pluperfect
Next, choose between a one-word form and the would-form.

[CARD]
TITLE: Keep the common one-word forms
SVG: de_konjunktiv_c04f.svg
REF: 14.2.3

In the second subjunctive, the common auxiliaries and modal verbs strongly prefer their compact one-word forms:
TL: Das wäre riskant.
EN: That would be risky.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Wir hätten mehr Spielraum.
EN: We would have more room to manoeuvre.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Die Änderung könnte teuer werden.
EN: The change could become expensive.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Ich wüsste einen Ausweg.
EN: I would know a way out.
[PAUSE:900]
A small group of familiar strong verbs also sound comfortable in compact form, especially in writing:
TL: Unter diesen Umständen käme er vermutlich mit.
EN: Under those circumstances, he would probably come along.
SHOW: be · have · modals · a small familiar strong-verb core → compact forms
Flip for where the would-form wins.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Use the would-form where a compact form jars
REF: 14.2.3

With weak verbs, the compact second-subjunctive form looks identical to the simple past. With many rarer strong forms, it sounds theatrical. Modern German normally chooses the transparent would-form instead:
TL: Ich würde den Vorschlag noch einmal prüfen.
EN: I would examine the proposal once more.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wenn sie etwas später abreisen würde, könnten wir in Ruhe sprechen.
EN: If she left a little later, we could talk without rushing.
[PAUSE:1000]
Past counterfactuals do not need that workaround. They use the second-subjunctive auxiliary plus a participle:
TL: Ohne die Warnung hätte ich den Fehler übersehen.
EN: Without the warning, I would have overlooked the error.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ohne die Warnung wäre ich zu früh abgereist.
EN: Without the warning, I would have left too early.
[PAUSE:900]
With a modal, the two infinitives gather at the end:
TL: Ich hätte den Termin verschieben müssen.
EN: I would have had to postpone the appointment.
[PAUSE:900]
One last distinction: in a past narrative, the would-form can be a genuine future viewed from that past point:
TL: Damals ahnte niemand, dass die Reparatur Monate dauern würde.
EN: At the time, nobody suspected that the repair would take months.
SHOW: awkward compact form → would-form · past unreal → auxiliary plus participle

[CARD]
TITLE: Conditions are a reality scale
SVG: de_konjunktiv_c05f.svg
REF: 14.3, 14.3.1, 14.3.2

An open condition remains a live possibility, so German normally uses the indicative:
TL: Wenn Lea heute Zeit hat, übernimmt sie die Spätschicht.
EN: If Lea has time today, she will take the late shift.
[PAUSE:1100]
Move the same situation into an imagined or unlikely world, and both clauses move into the second subjunctive:
TL: Wenn Lea heute Zeit hätte, würde sie die Spätschicht übernehmen.
EN: If Lea had time today, she would take the late shift.
[PAUSE:1100]
The mood records the speaker's stance, not an objective probability score. Two speakers can frame the same event differently.
SHOW: live possibility → indicative · remote world → second subjunctive in both clauses
Flip for past and mixed-time conditions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Past unreality uses the pluperfect system
REF: 14.3.1

For a condition and consequence that both failed to happen, both clauses use an auxiliary in the second subjunctive plus a participle:
TL: Wenn der Server nicht ausgefallen wäre, hätten wir den Bericht rechtzeitig verschickt.
EN: If the server had not failed, we would have sent the report on time.
[PAUSE:1200]
Time can be mixed. A past event can have a present consequence:
TL: Wenn der Server nicht ausgefallen wäre, wären wir jetzt fertig.
EN: If the server had not failed, we would be finished now.
[PAUSE:1000]
Read each half for its own time: the condition is past; the result is present.
SHOW: past condition + past result · past condition + present result
Fifteen conditional rewrites come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Remove the conjunction and the verb takes the lead
REF: 14.3.3

A conditional clause does not need an explicit conjunction. Put the finite verb first and the condition remains clear:
TL: Sollten weitere Fragen auftreten, melden Sie sich bitte bei mir.
EN: Should further questions arise, please contact me.
[PAUSE:1000]
This pattern is at home in formal, administrative and legal prose, though the version with a modal of contingency is common more widely. A following main clause can be picked up with a correlating word:
TL: Hätte ich die Daten früher gesehen, so hätte ich widersprochen.
EN: Had I seen the data earlier, I would have objected.
[PAUSE:1000]
The correlating word is optional; inversion is the mechanism that replaces the conjunction.
SHOW: no conjunction → finite verb first · optional link into the result
Flip for conditions that are not clauses at all.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Conditions hide in several shapes
REF: 14.3.3

A more explicit conjunction can remove the ambiguity between if and whenever:
TL: Falls die Lieferung morgen eintrifft, beginnen wir sofort.
EN: If the delivery arrives tomorrow, we will begin immediately.
[PAUSE:800]
A proviso can be presented as assumed:
TL: Vorausgesetzt, alle stimmen zu, veröffentlichen wir den Bericht.
EN: Provided everyone agrees, we will publish the report.
[PAUSE:900]
A prepositional phrase can carry an implicit remote condition:
TL: Ohne deine genaue Prüfung wäre der Fehler unbemerkt geblieben.
EN: Without your careful review, the error would have gone unnoticed.
[PAUSE:900]
And the fixed unless expression normally follows the main clause:
TL: Wir veröffentlichen den Bericht am Freitag, es sei denn, der Kunde verlangt noch Änderungen.
EN: We will publish the report on Friday unless the client requests further changes.
SHOW: explicit condition · assumed proviso · implicit phrase · unless exception

[CARD]
TITLE: Formal reporting preserves perspective, not tense sequence
SVG: de_konjunktiv_c07f.svg
REF: 14.4.1, 14.4.2

English often shifts a reported verb backward. Formal German instead keeps the original time relationship and marks the report with the first subjunctive. Start with a present statement:
TL: Nora sagt: „Ich brauche mehr Zeit.“
EN: Nora says, “I need more time.”
[PAUSE:700]
Change the pronoun and mark the claim as reported:
TL: Nora sagt, sie brauche mehr Zeit.
EN: Nora says that she needs more time.
[PAUSE:1000]
A past or perfect statement becomes the perfect form of the first subjunctive:
TL: Nora sagt: „Ich habe den Entwurf bereits geschickt.“
EN: Nora says, “I have already sent the draft.”
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Nora sagt, sie habe den Entwurf bereits geschickt.
EN: Nora says that she has already sent the draft.
[PAUSE:900]
A future claim uses the first-subjunctive form of the future auxiliary:
TL: Nora sagt, sie werde die überarbeitete Fassung morgen schicken.
EN: Nora says that she will send the revised version tomorrow.
SHOW: preserve event time · shift viewpoint · mark the report
Flip for the replacement rule.

[SIDE]
TITLE: If the report marker disappears, replace it
REF: 14.4.2

For most verbs, the first subjunctive is visibly distinct only in the third-person singular. In the plural it often collides with the indicative. Formal German then replaces the ambiguous form with the second subjunctive:
TL: Die Zeugen sagen: „Wir kennen den Fahrer nicht.“
EN: The witnesses say, “We do not know the driver.”
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Die Zeugen sagen, sie kennten den Fahrer nicht.
EN: The witnesses say that they do not know the driver.
[PAUSE:1000]
The replacement applies in past reporting too:
TL: Die Zeugen sagen, sie hätten den Fahrer nicht erkannt.
EN: The witnesses say that they did not recognize the driver.
[PAUSE:900]
The verb meaning to be has distinct first-subjunctive forms even in the plural, so no replacement is needed:
TL: Die Zeugen sagen, sie seien während des Vorfalls im Hotel gewesen.
EN: The witnesses say that they were at the hotel during the incident.
Here the second subjunctive is a formal substitute, not a signal that the content is counterfactual.
SHOW: distinct first subjunctive → keep it · collision with indicative → second subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Reported speech changes with register
REF: 14.4.2, 14.4.3

In formal writing, omitting the conjunction makes the subjunctive carry the whole burden of marking a report:
TL: Die Behörde teilte mit, die Brücke sei wieder befahrbar.
EN: The authority announced that the bridge was open to traffic again.
[PAUSE:1100]
Everyday speech often keeps the conjunction and uses the indicative:
TL: Die Behörde hat gesagt, dass die Brücke wieder befahrbar ist.
EN: The authority said that the bridge is open to traffic again.
[PAUSE:1000]
Spoken German also uses the second subjunctive even where the first would be distinct:
TL: Tobias hat gesagt, er käme später nach.
EN: Tobias said that he would join us later.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not treat these three choices as a reliable truth meter. In practice, register and style usually explain the choice better than belief or disbelief.
SHOW: formal writing → first subjunctive · speech → indicative or second subjunctive
Flip for questions and commands inside reports.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Questions keep their interrogative frame
REF: 14.4.4

An indirect yes-or-no question keeps an interrogative conjunction and follows the same mood rules as a statement:
TL: Sie fragte, ob der Vertrag noch gelte.
EN: She asked whether the contract was still valid.
[PAUSE:900]
A question-word remains in place; an ambiguous first-subjunctive plural form triggers replacement:
TL: Der Redakteur wollte wissen, wann die endgültigen Zahlen vorlägen.
EN: The editor wanted to know when the final figures would be available.
[PAUSE:1000]
Reported commands use a modal. The usual one presents the instruction neutrally:
TL: Die Ärztin sagte, ich solle das Medikament absetzen.
EN: The doctor told me to stop taking the medication.
[PAUSE:900]
A stronger modal preserves greater urgency:
TL: Die Ärztin sagte, ich müsse sofort in die Klinik fahren.
EN: The doctor said that I had to go to the hospital immediately.
SHOW: indirect question → same mood rules · indirect command → modal infinitive
Hypothetical comparisons are next.

[CARD]
TITLE: An as-if clause carries its own timeline
SVG: de_konjunktiv_c09f.svg
REF: 14.5.1

The default formal pattern uses the second subjunctive. For a simultaneous comparison, choose its simple form:
TL: Er tut so, als ob er alles wüsste.
EN: He acts as if he knew everything.
[PAUSE:1000]
If the compared event is earlier, choose the pluperfect form:
TL: Sie sah aus, als ob sie die ganze Nacht gearbeitet hätte.
EN: She looked as if she had worked all night.
[PAUSE:1000]
If the compared event lies ahead, use the would-form:
TL: Es klang, als ob der Motor gleich ausfallen würde.
EN: It sounded as if the engine was about to fail.
SHOW: simultaneous → simple · earlier → pluperfect · later → would-form
Flip for word order and register.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Without ob, the finite verb follows als
REF: 14.5.1

In a compact written alternative, drop the second conjunction and place the finite verb immediately after the first:
TL: Er tut so, als wüsste er alles.
EN: He acts as if he knew everything.
[PAUSE:1000]
Formal writing sometimes uses the first subjunctive instead, without changing the basic meaning:
TL: Die Stellungnahme wirkt, als sei sie sorgfältig vorbereitet.
EN: The statement appears to have been carefully prepared.
[PAUSE:1000]
In informal speech, the full conjunction pattern often takes the indicative:
TL: Du redest, als ob du jedes Detail kennst.
EN: You talk as if you know every detail.
SHOW: compact writing: als + finite verb · full clause: als ob + verb final

[CARD]
TITLE: Distance can sound courteous rather than unreal
REF: 14.5.3

The second subjunctive can create social distance. That makes a request less abrupt:
TL: Könnten Sie mir bis morgen eine Rückmeldung geben?
EN: Could you give me a response by tomorrow?
[PAUSE:900]
It can also soften the arrival of a statement:
TL: Ich hätte dazu noch eine kurze Frage.
EN: I have one more brief question about that.
[PAUSE:900]
Or close an interaction without sounding too final:
TL: Das wäre fürs Erste alles.
EN: That would be all for now.
[PAUSE:900]
Nothing here is logically impossible. The mood is managing interpersonal pressure.
SHOW: grammatical distance → social tact
Flip from tact to desire.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Wishes reuse the conditional frame
REF: 14.5.6b

An unreal wish is shaped like a conditional clause, often strengthened by small particles. A present state takes the simple second subjunctive:
TL: Wenn ich doch mehr Zeit hätte!
EN: If only I had more time!
[PAUSE:800]
A desired change often uses the would-form:
TL: Wenn der Lärm doch endlich aufhören würde!
EN: If only the noise would finally stop!
[PAUSE:900]
For regret about the past, use the pluperfect system, with or without the conjunction:
TL: Wäre ich bloß früher losgefahren!
EN: If only I had set off earlier!
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Hätte sie doch nur angerufen!
EN: If only she had called!
SHOW: present wish → simple or would-form · past regret → pluperfect

[CARD]
TITLE: The first subjunctive survives beyond reporting
REF: 14.1.2, 14.5.6

Outside reported speech, the first subjunctive lives in a compact set of formal instructions, propositions and wishes. Recipes and academic demonstrations can use an impersonal third-person instruction:
TL: Man prüfe zunächst, ob die Messwerte plausibel sind.
EN: First, check whether the measurements are plausible.
[PAUSE:900]
A formal proposition often uses the passive:
TL: Es sei daran erinnert, dass die Frist morgen endet.
EN: Let it be recalled that the deadline expires tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Fixed wishes preserve the same system:
TL: Es lebe die Neugier!
EN: Long live curiosity!
[PAUSE:800]
And the first-person plural can frame a shared appeal, though only the verb meaning to be shows a visibly special form here:
TL: Seien wir ehrlich: Der Plan ist zu teuer.
EN: Let us be honest: the plan is too expensive.
SHOW: formal instruction · proposition · set wish · shared appeal

[SIDE]
TITLE: Recognize the formal and lexical edges
REF: 14.5.2, 14.5.4, 14.5.5

In elevated prose, a purpose clause can take a subjunctive, although the indicative is now more usual:
TL: Ich erkläre den Ablauf noch einmal, damit niemand behaupten könne, er sei unklar.
EN: I will explain the procedure once more so that nobody can claim it is unclear.
[PAUSE:1000]
A formal time clause may use the second subjunctive to keep the future event in doubt:
TL: Der Ausschuss vertagte die Abstimmung, bis weitere Gutachten vorlägen.
EN: The committee postponed the vote until further expert opinions were available.
[PAUSE:1000]
Negative contexts preserve a few highly idiomatic uses:
TL: Nicht, dass ich etwas dagegen hätte, aber der Termin ist ungünstig.
EN: Not that I have anything against it, but the timing is inconvenient.
[PAUSE:900]
And a pluperfect form with almost marks a near miss that did not happen:
TL: Fast hätte ich die vertrauliche Datei an alle geschickt.
EN: I almost sent the confidential file to everyone.
SHOW: productive core first · formal edge for reading · fixed phrases as whole patterns
