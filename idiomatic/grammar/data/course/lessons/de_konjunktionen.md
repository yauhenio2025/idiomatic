## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Two ways to link clauses
SVG: de_konjunktionen_c01f.svg
REF: 17

A conjunction does more than join pieces of meaning. It tells you whether the next clause stands beside the first clause or sits inside a larger sentence. Equal clauses are coordinated. Dependent clauses are subordinated. Listen first to two equal main clauses:
TL: Der Ausschuss tagt noch, aber die Presse wartet bereits.
EN: The committee is still in session, but the press is already waiting.
[PAUSE:1200]
Both finite verbs occupy the normal second-position frame of a main clause. Now make the second event dependent:
TL: Wir gehen, sobald die Abstimmung beendet ist.
EN: We will leave as soon as the vote is over.
[PAUSE:1200]
The finite verb of the dependent clause moves to its right edge.
SHOW: coordination = equal status · subordination = dependency
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The link sets the verb frame
REF: 17

Keep the propositions nearly constant and change only their relationship:
TL: Der Vorsitzende bleibt ruhig, aber die Sprecherin widerspricht.
EN: The chair remains calm, but the spokesperson objects.
[PAUSE:1100]
TL: Der Vorsitzende bleibt ruhig, obwohl die Sprecherin widerspricht.
EN: The chair remains calm although the spokesperson objects.
[PAUSE:1200]
The first link preserves a second independent clause. The second link embeds a concession, so its finite verb closes the clause. This is the master diagnostic for the whole unit: classify the relationship, then choose both the connector and the verb frame.
SHOW: equal clause → verb second · dependent clause → verb final
Coordinators come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Coordinators preserve independence
SVG: de_konjunktionen_c02f.svg
REF: 17.1, 17.1.2

A coordinator does not occupy the first constituent slot of the clause it introduces. The clause after it keeps its own main-clause architecture:
TL: Die Frist läuft morgen ab, und der Antrag ist noch unvollständig.
EN: The deadline expires tomorrow, and the application is still incomplete.
[PAUSE:1100]
The same applies when the second clause supplies an afterthought-like reason:
TL: Der Antrag ist noch unvollständig, denn zwei Nachweise fehlen.
EN: The application is still incomplete, because two supporting documents are missing.
[PAUSE:1200]
The finite verb remains second in both clauses. That makes the second sentence structurally different from a sentence with a subordinating causal link.
SHOW: und · aber · oder · denn · sondern → main-clause frame
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Contrast, correction — and clause adverbs
REF: 17.1.1, 17.1.2

Ordinary contrast and correction after a negative are not the same operation:
TL: Der Entwurf ist knapp, aber überzeugend.
EN: The draft is concise but convincing.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Entwurf ist nicht unbrauchbar, sondern nur zu ausführlich.
EN: The draft is not unusable, but merely too detailed.
[PAUSE:1100]
In the first sentence both descriptions remain true. In the second, the phrase after the link replaces the rejected description. Some contrast words can instead function inside the clause; then they count toward the clause's field structure:
TL: Die Daten wirken plausibel; jedoch fehlt ein entscheidender Beleg.
EN: The data seem plausible; however, one crucial piece of evidence is missing.
SHOW: aber = contrast · sondern = correction after negation · jedoch can sit inside its clause
Six clean coordinating-conjunction items follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Noun clauses occupy a sentence slot
SVG: de_konjunktionen_c03f.svg
REF: 17.2, 17.2.1

A dependent clause can do the work of a noun phrase. It can be the subject, object, or another complement selected by the larger sentence. Here the entire opening clause is the subject:
TL: Dass die Akten verschwunden sind, beunruhigt die Prüferin.
EN: The fact that the files have disappeared worries the auditor.
[PAUSE:1100]
Here the dependent clause fills the direct-object slot:
TL: Die Prüferin vermutet, dass jemand sie versehentlich archiviert hat.
EN: The auditor suspects that someone filed them by mistake.
[PAUSE:1200]
Do not treat these as decorative add-ons. The larger sentence is grammatically waiting for precisely that complement.
SHOW: noun clause = subject, object, or selected complement
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Questions, correlates, and omitted links
REF: 17.2.1, 17.2.2, 17.2.3

An indirect yes-or-no question selects one connector; an indirect content question keeps its question word:
TL: Ob der Einspruch Erfolg hat, lässt sich noch nicht sagen.
EN: It is not yet possible to say whether the appeal will succeed.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wir wissen noch nicht, warum die Frist verkürzt wurde.
EN: We do not yet know why the deadline was shortened.
[PAUSE:1000]
When a complement depends on a preposition, a pointing compound often announces it in the main clause:
TL: Wir warten darauf, dass die Behörde den Bescheid korrigiert.
EN: We are waiting for the authority to correct the decision.
[PAUSE:1000]
After reporting and thinking verbs, the connector may disappear and main-clause order returns, especially in indirect speech:
TL: Der Referent erklärte, er habe die Zahlen erneut geprüft.
EN: The speaker stated that he had checked the figures again.
SHOW: ob = yes/no question · w-word = content question · correlate points forward
Time clauses are next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Als, wenn, wann: event or question?
SVG: de_konjunktionen_c04f.svg
REF: 17.2.2, 17.3.1

English often offers one vague word where German makes you classify the time reference. A single completed occasion in the past takes one form:
TL: Als das Protokoll eintraf, war die Sitzung bereits beendet.
EN: When the minutes arrived, the meeting was already over.
[PAUSE:1100]
A present or future event takes another:
TL: Wenn das Protokoll eintrifft, leite ich es sofort weiter.
EN: When the minutes arrive, I will forward them immediately.
[PAUSE:1100]
An actual or indirect question about the time takes the interrogative form:
TL: Ich weiß nicht, wann das Protokoll eintrifft.
EN: I do not know when the minutes will arrive.
SHOW: als = one past occasion · wenn = present/future/repeated · wann = question
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Repeated past and future-in-the-past
REF: 17.3.1

Past tense alone does not force the single-occasion form. Repetition is the decisive feature:
TL: Immer wenn die Sachverständige sprach, hörten alle aufmerksam zu.
EN: Whenever the expert spoke, everyone listened attentively.
[PAUSE:1100]
The same connector also looks forward from a past viewpoint:
TL: Sie wollte im Büro sein, wenn der Kurier eintraf.
EN: She wanted to be in the office when the courier arrived.
[PAUSE:1100]
From the wanting, the arrival was still ahead. If the intended meaning might be conditional, add an explicit recurrence signal or choose an unambiguous conditional connector.
SHOW: repeated past → immer wenn · later from a past viewpoint → wenn
Twelve event-versus-question choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Build a temporal sequence
REF: 17.3.2, 17.3.4, 17.3.5

Temporal connectors encode the boundary between events. Before points forward from the subordinate event; after looks back from it:
TL: Bevor wir unterschreiben, prüft die Rechtsabteilung den Vertrag.
EN: Before we sign, the legal department reviews the contract.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Nachdem die Rechtsabteilung den Vertrag geprüft hat, unterschreiben wir.
EN: After the legal department has reviewed the contract, we sign.
[PAUSE:1000]
One connector marks an endpoint, another a starting point that still matters:
TL: Ich bleibe hier, bis die Verhandlung zu Ende ist.
EN: I will stay here until the hearing is over.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Seitdem die neue Regel gilt, sind die Anträge kürzer.
EN: Since the new rule came into force, the applications have been shorter.
SHOW: bevor/ehe = before · nachdem = after · bis = endpoint · seitdem = starting point
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Threshold, duration, simultaneity
REF: 17.3.3, 17.3.6, 17.3.7, 17.3.8

Three more relations are worth keeping sharply apart: the instant something becomes true, the whole span while it remains true, and simple overlap:
TL: Sobald der Beschluss vorliegt, informieren wir die Beteiligten.
EN: As soon as the decision is available, we will inform those involved.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Solange die Prüfung läuft, bleibt der Zugang gesperrt.
EN: As long as the review is in progress, access will remain blocked.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Während die Delegationen noch verhandelten, bereitete das Team bereits die Erklärung vor.
EN: While the delegations were still negotiating, the team was already preparing the statement.
[PAUSE:1100]
For a near-instant sequence, formal German can use two main clauses:
TL: Kaum war die Meldung veröffentlicht, da folgte schon das Dementi.
EN: Hardly had the report been published when the denial followed.
SHOW: sobald = threshold · solange = duration · während = overlap or contrast
Reasons come next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Package the reason
SVG: de_konjunktionen_c06f.svg
REF: 17.1.2, 17.4.1, 17.4.3

The neutral subordinating choice commonly introduces a newly relevant cause:
TL: Wir verschickten eine Erinnerung, weil sich der Termin geändert hatte.
EN: We sent a reminder because the date had changed.
[PAUSE:1100]
A reason treated as known background often comes first:
TL: Da der Termin allen bekannt war, verschickten wir keine weitere Erinnerung.
EN: Since everyone knew the date, we sent no further reminder.
[PAUSE:1100]
A coordinator instead adds the reason as a second independent assertion:
TL: Die Sitzung wurde vertagt, denn zwei Mitglieder fehlten.
EN: The meeting was postponed, because two members were absent.
SHOW: new cause → weil · known frame → often da · added assertion → denn
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Position reveals the causal grammar
REF: 17.1.2, 17.4.1, 17.4.3

One causal word is not a conjunction at the left edge at all. It sits inside an independent clause, normally after the finite verb:
TL: Die Sitzung wurde vertagt; zwei Mitglieder fehlten nämlich.
EN: The meeting was postponed; two members were absent, you see.
[PAUSE:1100]
Another strengthens a reason that is especially persuasive in the context:
TL: Wir sollten den Entwurf heute beschließen, zumal alle Einwände geklärt sind.
EN: We should approve the draft today, especially since all the objections have been resolved.
[PAUSE:1200]
In careful standard German, a subordinating causal connector still sends the finite verb to the end. The widespread spoken second-position pattern is not the written norm.
SHOW: denn + V2 · nämlich inside V2 clause · weil/da/zumal + verb-final
Ten gated causal choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Purpose is chosen; result happens
SVG: de_konjunktionen_c07f.svg
REF: 17.5.1, 17.5.2

Purpose belongs to the plan before the action. The subordinate clause names the intended outcome:
TL: Die Assistentin markiert die Änderungen, damit der Ausschuss sie sofort erkennt.
EN: The assistant marks the changes so that the committee can identify them immediately.
[PAUSE:1100]
When the acting person stays the same, an infinitive construction is often leaner:
TL: Die Assistentin markiert die Änderungen, um Missverständnisse zu vermeiden.
EN: The assistant marks the changes in order to avoid misunderstandings.
[PAUSE:1100]
Result is different. It reports what followed, intended or not:
TL: Der Scanner verzerrte die Farben, sodass mehrere Markierungen unleserlich wurden.
EN: The scanner distorted the colors, with the result that several markings became illegible.
SHOW: damit / um zu = intended goal · sodass = actual result
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Degree can trigger a result clause
REF: 17.5.2, 17.5.3

A degree word in the main clause can set up the result:
TL: Die Schrift war so klein, dass selbst die Gutachterin sie kaum lesen konnte.
EN: The print was so small that even the reviewer could barely read it.
[PAUSE:1100]
After too, not enough, or not so, a formal construction states the consequence that is ruled in or out. It often uses the second subjunctive:
TL: Der Bericht ist zu umfangreich, als dass wir ihn heute vollständig prüfen könnten.
EN: The report is too extensive for us to be able to review it fully today.
[PAUSE:1200]
Do not select by the English words alone. Ask whether the clause names an aim, an actual consequence, or a consequence made impossible by degree.
SHOW: so … dass = degree → result · zu … als dass = too … for
Concession comes next.

[CARD]
TITLE: Concession preserves the surprise
REF: 17.6.1

A concessive clause concedes a fact while the main clause denies the result you might normally expect from it:
TL: Obwohl der Vorschlag teuer ist, unterstützt ihn die Mehrheit.
EN: Although the proposal is expensive, the majority supports it.
[PAUSE:1100]
The same logic can be coordinated as two main-clause pieces:
TL: Der Vorschlag ist zwar teuer, aber die Mehrheit unterstützt ihn.
EN: The proposal is expensive, admittedly, but the majority supports it.
[PAUSE:1100]
Formal writing offers several less frequent subordinators for the same relation:
TL: Wenngleich die Methode umstritten ist, liefert sie brauchbare Ergebnisse.
EN: Although the method is controversial, it produces usable results.
SHOW: conceded fact + expectation-defying outcome
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Whoever, wherever, however
REF: 17.6.2

Open concession starts with an interrogative expression and adds a particle later in the dependent clause. The value may vary; the main claim survives every value:
TL: Wer auch die Leitung übernimmt, muss den Konflikt offen ansprechen.
EN: Whoever takes over the leadership must address the conflict openly.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wie sorgfältig der Plan auch ausgearbeitet sein mag, ein Restrisiko bleibt.
EN: However carefully the plan may have been worked out, a residual risk remains.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wohin die Debatte auch führt, wir brauchen am Ende einen Beschluss.
EN: Wherever the debate leads, we need a decision in the end.
[PAUSE:1100]
Notice the last main clause: it has ordinary second-position order, not inversion after the preceding concessive clause.
SHOW: w-word / wie + auch = every possible value, same conclusion
Now ask how an action is carried out.

[CARD]
TITLE: Indem answers how
SVG: de_konjunktionen_c09f.svg
REF: 17.3.1f, 17.7c

In current German, the instrumental construction names the means by which the main event is achieved. It answers by what method:
TL: Man senkt den Energieverbrauch, indem man die Anlage nachts abschaltet.
EN: Switching the system off at night reduces energy consumption.
[PAUSE:1100]
The expanded version makes the pointing relation explicit:
TL: Man senkt den Energieverbrauch dadurch, dass man die Anlage nachts abschaltet.
EN: Switching the system off at night is how energy consumption is reduced.
[PAUSE:1200]
The old use for merely simultaneous actions is no longer the normal modern pattern. Require a genuine means-to-result relation.
SHOW: indem / dadurch, dass = method that produces the result
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: English -ing has no single German form
REF: 17.3.8, 17.7c, 17.7h, 11.6.2, 11.6.3a

An English participial phrase may express method, simultaneity, accompaniment, or absence. German chooses by relation. Simple parallel actions can be coordinated:
TL: Sie trat ans Mikrofon und räusperte sich.
EN: She stepped up to the microphone, clearing her throat.
[PAUSE:1000]
Accompanying information can take a dependent clause of simultaneity:
TL: Die Moderatorin eröffnete die Fragerunde, wobei sie auf die knappe Zeit hinwies.
EN: The moderator opened the question period, pointing out the limited time.
[PAUSE:1000]
With different subjects, absence requires a full subordinate clause:
TL: Sie trat ans Mikrofon, ohne dass das Publikum verstummte.
EN: She stepped up to the microphone without the audience falling silent.
SHOW: method ≠ parallel action ≠ accompaniment ≠ without-event
Choose the semantic relation, not the English surface form.

[CARD]
TITLE: The so-family encodes a relation
SVG: de_konjunktionen_c10f.svg
REF: 17.3.6, 17.5.2

Do not learn the forms beginning with the same syllable as one interchangeable list. Their second halves point to different logical relations. One marks the instant a threshold is crossed:
TL: Sobald die Freigabe vorliegt, beginnt die Veröffentlichung.
EN: As soon as approval is available, publication begins.
[PAUSE:900]
One covers the duration before that threshold:
TL: Solange die Freigabe fehlt, bleibt der Text intern.
EN: As long as approval is lacking, the text remains internal.
[PAUSE:900]
One quantifies recurrence:
TL: Sooft ein Fehler auftritt, wird er protokolliert.
EN: Whenever an error occurs, it is logged.
[PAUSE:1100]
And the result form, which you already know, links an event to its consequence.
SHOW: sobald = threshold · solange = duration · sooft = recurrence · sodass = result
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Knowledge, extent, condition, qualification
REF: 17.7d

Now separate four easily blurred uses. A fixed knowledge formula takes the quantity form:
TL: Soviel ich weiß, ist die Finanzierung gesichert.
EN: As far as I know, the financing is secure.
[PAUSE:900]
Extent or the limits of a judgment take the range form:
TL: Soweit ich das anhand der Unterlagen beurteilen kann, fehlt nur eine Unterschrift.
EN: As far as I can judge from the documents, only one signature is missing.
[PAUSE:900]
A formal condition takes another form:
TL: Sofern der Vorstand zustimmt, kann das Projekt im September beginnen.
EN: Provided that the board agrees, the project can begin in September.
[PAUSE:900]
And a qualification can announce the precise respect in which a claim holds:
TL: Der Plan ist insofern riskant, als er keine Reserve vorsieht.
EN: The plan is risky insofar as it makes no provision for a reserve.
SHOW: soviel = knowledge formula · soweit = extent · sofern = condition · insofern, als = respect
Nine so-family items follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Comparison and exception clauses
REF: 17.7a, 17.7b

Comparison clauses select their link according to inequality or equality:
TL: Die Kosten steigen schneller, als wir erwartet hatten.
EN: The costs are rising faster than we had expected.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Umsetzung verlief nicht so reibungslos, wie der Bericht vermuten lässt.
EN: The implementation did not proceed as smoothly as the report suggests.
[PAUSE:1100]
Exception can also be clausal. A conditional exception uses a full dependent clause:
TL: Wir stimmen dem Plan zu, außer wenn die Haftungsfrage ungeklärt bleibt.
EN: We will agree to the plan unless the liability issue remains unresolved.
SHOW: comparative inequality → als · equality → wie · exception → außer wenn
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Expand a phrase into a clause
REF: 17.7e, 17.7g, 17.7h, Ch. 17

Advanced German constantly alternates between a compact phrase and an explicit clause. The clause makes participants, tense, and logical relation visible:
TL: Je nachdem, wie die Abstimmung ausgeht, tagt der Ausschuss morgen erneut.
EN: Depending on how the vote turns out, the committee will meet again tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Reform ist sinnvoll, nur dass ihre Finanzierung offenbleibt.
EN: The reform makes sense, except that its financing remains unresolved.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Direktor verließ den Saal, ohne dass jemand seine Abwesenheit bemerkte.
EN: The director left the hall without anyone noticing his absence.
[PAUSE:1100]
When you expand a prepositional phrase, do not translate the preposition mechanically. Recover the semantic relation first, then choose the connector and build a full verb-final clause.
SHOW: phrase → recover relation → connector + explicit clause

[CARD]
TITLE: Choose the relation before the word
REF: Ch. 17

The safest workflow is semantic, then syntactic, then lexical. First name the relation. Next decide whether the linked clause is equal or dependent. Only then select the connector. Compare three readings built from nearly the same facts:
TL: Weil die Daten unvollständig sind, verschieben wir die Entscheidung.
EN: Because the data are incomplete, we are postponing the decision.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir verschieben die Entscheidung, denn die Daten sind unvollständig.
EN: We are postponing the decision, because the data are incomplete.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Daten sind unvollständig, sodass wir die Entscheidung verschieben.
EN: The data are incomplete, with the result that we are postponing the decision.
[PAUSE:1200]
Cause as background, reason as a second assertion, and consequence are close in content but different in discourse structure.
SHOW: relation → clause status → verb frame → connector

[SIDE]
TITLE: The final three-question check
REF: 17, Ch. 17

Before you accept a complex sentence, ask three questions. What logical relation do I mean? Is the new clause independent or embedded? Where must its finite verb stand? A compact phrase and a clause can carry similar information, but the clause exposes the machinery:
TL: Vor der Abstimmung prüft die Juristin den letzten Absatz.
EN: Before the vote, the lawyer reviews the final paragraph.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Bevor abgestimmt wird, prüft die Juristin den letzten Absatz.
EN: Before the vote is held, the lawyer reviews the final paragraph.
[PAUSE:1000]
And purpose adds a different relation again:
TL: Die Juristin prüft den letzten Absatz, damit keine Zweideutigkeit bestehen bleibt.
EN: The lawyer reviews the final paragraph so that no ambiguity remains.
[PAUSE:1100]
The connector is not ornament. It is the control panel for status, order, and logic.
SHOW: meaning first · syntax second · connector third
