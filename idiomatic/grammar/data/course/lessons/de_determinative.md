## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: The noun phrase has a control slot
SVG: de_determinative_c01f.svg
REF: 5

A determiner occupies the first slot of a noun phrase. It locates the noun in the situation: this report, my report, every report. A pronoun goes one step further and replaces the whole noun phrase. Compare the same pointing word in its two jobs:
TL: Diese gründliche Analyse überzeugt das Gremium.
EN: This thorough analysis convinces the committee.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Diese überzeugt das Gremium.
EN: This one convinces the committee.
[PAUSE:1000]
In the first sentence a noun follows, so the pointing word is a determiner. In the second, it carries the noun phrase alone, so it is a pronoun.
SHOW: before a noun = determiner · instead of a noun phrase = pronoun
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: One ending system, one crucial split
REF: 5, 5.2.1, 5.5.4

Most words in this chapter borrow the same visible case endings as the basic pointing paradigm. Possessives and the one-word family split in two: before a noun they pattern with the indefinite article, but alone they must reveal gender and case themselves.
TL: Dein Vorschlag ist riskanter als meiner.
EN: Your proposal is riskier than mine.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first form can leave masculine nominative unmarked because the noun is present. The pronoun cannot lean on a noun, so its ending does the identifying.
TL: Keines dieser Argumente trägt.
EN: None of these arguments holds up.
SHOW: noun present → article endings · noun absent → full pronoun endings
Next, the demonstratives turn pointing into a discourse system.

[CARD]
TITLE: Spoken German points with der
SVG: de_determinative_c02f.svg
REF: 5.1.1

The ordinary definite article is unstressed. Stress that same shape, and spoken German gets its most frequent demonstrative: this one or that one. A following here or there makes the distance explicit.
TL: Der Ordner hier ist vollständig; der da nicht.
EN: This folder here is complete; that one there is not.
[PAUSE:1100]
An explicitly pointed-out object can also keep the demonstrative after a preposition, with the case demanded by that preposition:
TL: In dem da liegt die unterschriebene Fassung.
EN: The signed version is in that one there.
SHOW: stressed der-series = the spoken pointing pronoun
Flip the card for the written alternatives.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Near, selected, identical
REF: 5.1.2, 5.1.3, 5.1.4, 5.1.5

The standard written pointing form usually marks something present or recently mentioned. The distant counterpart belongs mainly to formal writing. Two compounds do more precise work: one selects the member defined by a relative clause; the other asserts strict identity, not mere similarity.
TL: Diejenige, die den Vertrag entworfen hat, sollte ihn erläutern.
EN: The person who drafted the contract should explain it.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wir teilen uns dasselbe Büro, haben aber den gleichen Schreibtischtyp.
EN: We share the very same office but have the same model of desk.
[PAUSE:1100]
Strict identity matters in careful writing: one physical office, merely matching desk models.
The spoken pronoun's genitive has possessive-looking forms. One points back before a noun; the other commonly points forward to a relative clause.
TL: Die Sachverständigen reichten Stellungnahmen ein; wir prüften deren Einwände und die Motive derer, die widersprochen hatten.
EN: The experts submitted statements; we examined their objections and the motives of those who had objected.
SHOW: dieser = near/recent · derjenige = selected · derselbe = identical
Twelve exercises now test the spoken demonstrative across case and reference.

[CARD]
TITLE: Possessives run two agreements
SVG: de_determinative_c03f.svg
REF: 5.2.1

A possessive carries two pieces of grammar at once. Its stem identifies the possessor. Its ending belongs to the possessed noun phrase and marks that phrase's gender, number and case.
TL: Wegen unseres verspäteten Antrags wurde die Sitzung vertagt.
EN: Because of our late application, the meeting was postponed.
[PAUSE:1100]
The stem points to us. The ending is genitive masculine because the application sits inside the prepositional phrase. Never choose the ending from the possessor.
TL: Seine neue Analyse widerspricht unserer.
EN: His new analysis contradicts ours.
SHOW: stem → possessor · ending → possessed noun phrase
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A pronoun must finish the signal
REF: 5.1.1f, 5.2.1, 5.2.2

Before a noun, the possessive uses indefinite-article endings. Alone, it takes the fuller pronoun endings. That is why the masculine nominative contrast is audible.
TL: Mein Entwurf liegt neben deinem.
EN: My draft is lying next to yours.
[PAUSE:1000]
Third-person possessives can leave the antecedent unclear. Formal German can use the genitive of the demonstrative to point back unambiguously.
TL: Der Bericht nennt den Ausschuss und dessen Empfehlung.
EN: The report names the committee and the latter's recommendation.
SHOW: mein Entwurf · meiner = mine · dessen/deren remove ambiguity
Nine possessive exercises follow; make the possessor and the ending answer different questions.

[CARD]
TITLE: Which one, or what kind?
SVG: de_determinative_c04f.svg
REF: 5.1.6, 5.3.1, 5.3.2

German separates selection from classification. The first interrogative asks you to choose an identifiable member of a set. The longer phrase asks what sort of thing is meant.
TL: Welchen Ansatz empfehlen Sie?
EN: Which approach do you recommend?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Was für einen Ansatz verfolgen Sie?
EN: What kind of approach are you pursuing?
[PAUSE:1100]
Inside the longer phrase, the word that looks like the indefinite article gets its case from the whole noun phrase, not from the fixed middle word. Here it is accusative because the approach is the object.
The classifying question naturally pairs with the family meaning such. Careful writing often uses an inflected form after the indefinite article; everyday speech favors the shorter so-plus-article pattern.
TL: Einen solchen Einwand kann man leicht entkräften.
EN: An objection of that kind is easy to refute.
SHOW: welcher = select one · was für ein = classify the kind
Flip for people, things and prepositions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: People decline; things compound
REF: 5.3.3

The personal interrogative has separate forms for subject, direct object, possessive and indirect object. The thing-interrogative has no usable dative. With most prepositions, German therefore builds a compound beginning with the question element.
TL: Mit wem haben Sie die Probe untersucht?
EN: Who did you examine the sample with?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Womit haben Sie die Probe untersucht?
EN: What did you examine the sample with?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Von wem stammt diese Unterschrift?
EN: Whose signature is this?
SHOW: person → mit wem · thing → womit
The compounds themselves are the next mechanism.

[CARD]
TITLE: Prepositions grow a pointer
SVG: de_determinative_c05f.svg
REF: 5.3.3

When a prepositional complement refers to a thing or proposition rather than a visibly selected noun, a preposition combines with one of two pointers. The question series asks for an unknown connection; the answer series resumes a known one. Before a vowel, a linking consonant appears.
TL: Worauf warten wir?
EN: What are we waiting for?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir warten darauf, dass die Freigabe erteilt wird.
EN: We are waiting for approval to be granted.
[PAUSE:1100]
The preposition belongs to the verb's frame. The first pointer asks for its complement; the second supplies that complement and can point forward to a clause.
SHOW: wo(r)- asks · da(r)- answers · r before a vowel
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: People refuse the compound
REF: 5.3.3, 5.4.4

Ordinary non-deictic reference to things, ideas and whole situations uses the compound system. A person keeps the ordinary preposition plus an inflected personal or relative pronoun.
TL: Woran erinnerst du dich?
EN: What do you remember?
[PAUSE:800]
TL: An wen erinnerst du dich?
EN: Who do you remember?
[PAUSE:1000]
This contrast becomes decisive in relative clauses: named people and named objects keep the preposition in front of the relative pronoun, while unbounded ideas often use the compound.
SHOW: thing or proposition → woran · person → an wen
Now relative clauses put all these distinctions to work.

[CARD]
TITLE: A relative pronoun has two governors
SVG: de_determinative_c06f.svg
REF: 5.4, 5.4.1

A relative pronoun answers two independent questions. Its antecedent supplies gender and number. Its job inside the relative clause supplies case. The main clause does not choose that case.
TL: Die Forscherin, der wir vertrauten, leitete die Studie.
EN: The researcher whom we trusted led the study.
[PAUSE:1100]
The antecedent is feminine singular. Inside the relative clause, the verb requires a dative object. Those two coordinates produce the form you hear.
TL: Der Bericht, den sie gestern vorlegte, überzeugte den Ausschuss.
EN: The report that she presented yesterday convinced the committee.
SHOW: antecedent → gender/number · clause role → case
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: German never drops the link
REF: 5.4, 5.4.1, 5.4.4

English can omit an object relative pronoun and strand a preposition at the end. Standard German does neither. The relative word is present, the preposition stands before it, and the finite verb closes the relative clause.
TL: Das ist der Einwand, auf den niemand eingegangen ist.
EN: That is the objection that no one addressed.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Daten, mit denen wir gerechnet hatten, fehlten.
EN: The data we had counted on were missing.
[PAUSE:1000]
The genitive link has a compact two-way contrast: one form follows masculine and neuter antecedents, the other feminine and plural antecedents. Neither takes another ending.
TL: Das Institut, dessen Daten wir ausgewertet haben, liegt in Bonn.
EN: The institute whose data we analyzed is in Bonn.
[PAUSE:1000]
Read from the verb outward: recover the required preposition and case, then take gender and number from the antecedent.
SHOW: preposition + relative pronoun · finite verb at the end
Ten exercises now isolate that two-governor calculation.

[CARD]
TITLE: Named anchor or unbounded idea?
SVG: de_determinative_c07f.svg
REF: 5.4.3, 5.4.4

After a named noun, a preposition normally takes the inflected relative pronoun. After a neuter indefinite or when the antecedent is a whole proposition, the prepositional compound is the natural link.
TL: Der Beschluss, über den wir beraten, betrifft alle Standorte.
EN: The decision we are discussing affects every site.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Alles, worüber wir beraten, bleibt vertraulich.
EN: Everything we discuss remains confidential.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sie zog den Antrag zurück, worüber niemand überrascht war.
EN: She withdrew the application, which surprised no one.
SHOW: named noun → über den · alles / whole clause → worüber
Flip for the remaining relative specialists.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Whose, what, whoever
REF: 5.4.1, 5.4.3, 5.4.5

The genitive relative marks an owner and does not itself decline further. The neuter relative follows broad indefinites, nominalized superlatives and whole clauses. The personal interrogative can open a free relative with the meaning whoever.
TL: Die Expertin, deren Gutachten wir zitiert haben, widersprach der Schlussfolgerung.
EN: The expert whose report we cited disputed the conclusion.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Das Klügste, was wir jetzt tun können, ist abzuwarten.
EN: The wisest thing we can do now is wait.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wer sorgfältig prüft, entdeckt den Widerspruch.
EN: Whoever checks carefully will discover the contradiction.
SHOW: whose → dessen/deren · broad neuter → was · whoever → wer
Sixteen focused choices now contrast named antecedents, broad indefinites and propositions.

[CARD]
TITLE: Build from the gap inward
REF: 5.4.1, 5.4.4

To combine two sentences, replace the repeated noun inside the second sentence, not the noun in the first. Determine the missing noun's role, choose the relative form, then move the finite verb to the end.
TL: Wir besuchten das Labor. Das Labor war erst kürzlich eröffnet worden.
EN: We visited the laboratory. The laboratory had only recently been opened.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wir besuchten das Labor, das erst kürzlich eröffnet worden war.
EN: We visited the laboratory, which had only recently been opened.
[PAUSE:1100]
The first occurrence remains the antecedent. The second becomes a nominative neuter relative pronoun because it is the subject of the inserted clause.
SHOW: find repetition → solve its role → replace → send verb to the end
Flip for a C1 embedding trap.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reported thought needs two links
REF: 5.4.1c

English can insert a reporting clause inside a relative clause. German normally completes both dependencies. One clause links back to the antecedent; a second clause carries the reported proposition.
TL: Das ist die Kandidatin, von der du gesagt hast, dass sie hervorragend verhandelt.
EN: That is the candidate who you said negotiates brilliantly.
[PAUSE:1100]
A lighter written alternative turns the report into a parenthetical phrase.
TL: Das ist die Kandidatin, die deiner Einschätzung nach hervorragend verhandelt.
EN: That is the candidate who, in your assessment, negotiates brilliantly.
SHOW: reporting verb + dass-clause · or a parenthetical reformulation

[CARD]
TITLE: All is not one German pattern
REF: 5.5.1

For a plural set, the inflected quantifier is the default. Before an article or another determiner, the uninflected form is common. With one complete collective, stretch of time or place, the adjective meaning whole is usually the idiomatic choice.
TL: Alle eingereichten Anträge werden geprüft.
EN: All submitted applications are being reviewed.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Mit all diesen Einwänden hatte niemand gerechnet.
EN: Nobody had anticipated all these objections.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der ganze Ausschuss war anwesend.
EN: The entire committee was present.
TL: Trotz aller Sorgfalt blieb ein Fehler unbemerkt.
EN: Despite all due care, one error went unnoticed.
SHOW: plural set → alle · before a determiner → all · whole unit → ganz
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The quantifier can float
REF: 5.5.1

With a personal pronoun, the plural quantifier normally follows and agrees in case. It can also stand later in the clause with less emphasis. The neuter singular pronoun means everything and naturally takes the broad neuter relative.
TL: Wir haben sie alle gewarnt.
EN: We warned them all.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Sie sind alle rechtzeitig erschienen.
EN: They all arrived on time.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Das ist alles, was bisher feststeht.
EN: That is everything that has been established so far.
[PAUSE:900]
Two idiomatic extensions matter in the drill: the plural form can mean all gone in colloquial northern speech, and the neuter pronoun forms an idiom meaning anything but.
TL: Der Kaffee ist alle.
EN: The coffee is all gone.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Der Ton war alles andere als sachlich.
EN: The tone was anything but objective.
SHOW: pronoun + alle · alles + was · alles andere als = anything but
Twelve exercises now test the inflected, uninflected and pronominal forms.

[CARD]
TITLE: One without a noun
REF: 5.5.4

The indefinite article can introduce a noun, but the pronoun meaning one must stand on its own. It therefore takes the fuller ending pattern. In the neuter, the spoken and commonly written short form ends in s.
TL: Einer der Entwürfe ist bereits genehmigt.
EN: One of the drafts has already been approved.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir prüfen noch eines der Angebote.
EN: We are still reviewing one of the offers.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ein Gutachten war schlüssig; eines blieb widersprüchlich.
EN: One expert report was coherent; one remained contradictory.
SHOW: ein + noun · einer/eine/eines without a noun
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The same split runs through the family
REF: 5.2.1, 5.5.4, 5.5.16

The negative and possessive families copy the same contrast. A determiner can leave certain nominative forms endingless because a noun follows. A pronoun must complete the signal itself.
TL: Ich brauche einen Adapter, aber hier ist keiner.
EN: I need an adapter, but there is none here.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Mein Gerät funktioniert; deines nicht.
EN: My device works; yours does not.
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat these not as isolated vocabulary but as one morphological architecture.
SHOW: ein/mein/kein + noun · einer/meiner/keiner alone
Sixteen exercises now connect one, none and the possessive pronouns.

[CARD]
TITLE: Negate the noun or the claim
SVG: de_determinative_c11f.svg
REF: 5.5.16

Negation begins with the positive structure. If the noun phrase would have an indefinite article or no article, negate that noun phrase with the negative determiner. If it is definite or possessive, or if you reject the event as a whole, use sentence negation.
TL: Wir haben keinen Beleg gefunden.
EN: We found no evidence.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir haben den Beleg nicht gefunden.
EN: We did not find the evidence.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first denies that any evidence was found. The second refers to an identifiable piece of evidence and denies finding it.
SHOW: ein/zero noun phrase → kein · definite or whole claim → nicht
Flip for contrastive stress.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Not one means not a single one
REF: 5.5.16

The negative determiner is the ordinary choice. Keep the indefinite article under direct stress only when you mean not a single one or when you contrast one category with another.
TL: Sie hat keinen Einwand erhoben.
EN: She raised no objection.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Sie hat nicht einen, sondern drei Einwände erhoben.
EN: She raised not one objection but three.
[PAUSE:900]
Fixed noun-and-verb combinations vary, so learn their internal structure rather than translating the English negative.
TL: Der Zeuge hatte keine Angst, sagte aber nicht die Wahrheit.
EN: The witness was not afraid, but he did not tell the truth.
SHOW: ordinary absence → kein · stressed count or contrast → nicht ein
Seven full-answer exercises now force the choice from the positive sentence.

[CARD]
TITLE: Some has no single translation
REF: 5.5.7, 5.5.8, 5.5.9, 5.5.26

German chooses by noun type and discourse status. Unspecified mass nouns and plurals often need no determiner. A small mass can take the invariable quantity word. A limited plural set takes an inflected quantifier. To resume a mass noun or a plural already mentioned, colloquial German can use the indefinite pointing pronoun.
TL: Wir brauchen Geduld und zuverlässige Daten.
EN: We need some patience and reliable data.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ich habe Kaffee gekocht. Möchtest du welchen?
EN: I made coffee. Would you like some?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Einige Ergebnisse waren unerwartet.
EN: Some results were unexpected.
SHOW: new mass/plural → zero · small mass → etwas · resumed noun → welcher
Flip for the many meanings of any.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Any splits by logic
REF: 5.5.11, 5.5.12, 5.5.16, 5.5.19, 5.5.26

In a negative noun phrase, any becomes the negative determiner. For an unknown member with no restriction, use the indefiniteness prefix. For free choice, use the distributive meaning every. For a limited but non-total subset, the stressed plural quantifier is available.
TL: Falls irgendeine Frage auftaucht, ruf mich an.
EN: If any question at all comes up, call me.
[PAUSE:900]
In a neutral question, no special determiner may be needed; the word meaning at all can make the open scope explicit.
TL: Hat er überhaupt eine Begründung genannt?
EN: Did he give any reason at all?
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Jeder vernünftige Einwand wird berücksichtigt.
EN: Any reasonable objection will be considered.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Manche Details bleiben absichtlich offen.
EN: Some details are deliberately left open.
SHOW: negative → kein · unknown → irgendein · free choice → jeder
