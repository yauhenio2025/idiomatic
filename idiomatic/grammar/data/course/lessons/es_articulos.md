## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Articles package reference and agreement
REF: 3.1, 4.1

Spanish articles do two jobs at once. Their form carries gender and number, while their presence helps present a noun as identifiable, as one member of a class, or as an unbounded type.
TL: La investigadora presentó un informe preliminar.
EN: The researcher presented a preliminary report.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Los resultados justifican una revisión completa.
EN: The results justify a complete review.
[PAUSE:1000]
Treat the article as part of the noun phrase, not as a mechanical translation of an English word.
SHOW: form = gender and number · choice = reference
Flip for the stressed-a pattern.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Stressed initial a changes the neighbor, not the noun
REF: 3.1.2, 4.1.1

A feminine singular noun beginning with a stressed a sound normally takes el or un when the article stands immediately before it. The noun remains feminine everywhere else.
TL: El acta definitiva quedó aprobada por unanimidad.
EN: The final record was approved unanimously.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Las actas definitivas quedaron archivadas esa misma tarde.
EN: The final records were filed that same afternoon.
[PAUSE:900]
Insert another word and the ordinary feminine form returns.
TL: La última acta contiene una cláusula ambigua.
EN: The final record contains an ambiguous clause.
SHOW: el acta definitiva · la última acta · feminine throughout
Nine form decisions follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Definite articles can point to a whole class
REF: 3.2.3, 3.2.6

Spanish commonly uses a definite article when a subject denotes a species, a substance, or an abstract idea in general. English often uses a bare noun in the same place.
TL: La paciencia mejora la calidad de las decisiones.
EN: Patience improves the quality of decisions.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Los algoritmos también heredan los prejuicios de sus datos.
EN: Algorithms also inherit the biases in their data.
[PAUSE:1000]
The article does not make these nouns narrowly specific. It presents the class as a complete concept.
SHOW: generic subject → normally el, la, los or las
Flip for partial quantities.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Partial reference often has no article
REF: 3.2.8, 3.2.9, 3.2.10

An object or a noun after a preposition may denote an unspecified amount or only some members of a class. Spanish then often uses no article.
TL: Necesitamos paciencia para negociar este acuerdo.
EN: We need patience to negotiate this agreement.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: El laboratorio trabaja con cobre reciclado.
EN: The laboratory works with recycled copper.
[PAUSE:900]
A defining phrase can turn the same material into an identifiable quantity.
TL: Analizaron el cobre recuperado del cableado antiguo.
EN: They analyzed the copper recovered from the old wiring.
SHOW: whole class or identified set → definite · unspecified portion → zero
Thirteen mixed definite-article decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Un presents a bounded instance
REF: 4.1.2, 4.1.5

Use un or una when one countable instance matters as an individual, especially when it is introduced, contrasted, or characterized.
TL: Buscamos una solución que no dependa de financiación externa.
EN: We are looking for a solution that does not depend on external funding.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Se produjo un corte que afectó a todo el distrito.
EN: An outage occurred that affected the entire district.
[PAUSE:1000]
The modifier helps make each referent a bounded member of its class.
SHOW: one introduced or characterized member → un or una
Flip for type-like nouns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Roles and expected possessions favor zero
REF: 4.1.6, 4.1.8, 4.1.9

A profession after ser normally behaves like a classification, so no indefinite article appears. Typical possessions after verbs such as tener or llevar can behave the same way.
TL: Nuria es ingeniera y dirige el equipo de seguridad.
EN: Nuria is an engineer and leads the security team.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Óscar tiene coche, pero no tiene plaza de garaje.
EN: Óscar has a car, but he does not have a parking space.
[PAUSE:900]
Qualification can make an individual salient and restore the article.
TL: Nuria es una ingeniera que detecta riesgos antes que nadie.
EN: Nuria is an engineer who detects risks before anyone else.
SHOW: classify or name a typical possession → zero · individuate → un or una
Fifteen omission decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Unos and unas quantify without a number
REF: 4.2.1

The plural indefinite article can mean approximately, a limited set, a few, or one conventional pair. The interpretation comes from the noun and the context.
TL: Llegarán unos cuarenta participantes antes del mediodía.
EN: About forty participants will arrive before noon.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Compré unas tijeras pequeñas para el escritorio.
EN: I bought a small pair of scissors for the desk.
[PAUSE:1000]
In both sentences the plural form contributes more than a vague English some.
SHOW: approximation · limited set · one pair
Flip for the contrast with a bare plural.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Bare plural and unos do not frame the same set
REF: 4.2.1, 4.2.2

A bare plural can describe an open category or an unspecified supply. Unos or unas makes a limited group relevant to the conversation and can add an evaluative tone.
TL: Necesitamos consultores con experiencia internacional.
EN: We need consultants with international experience.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Necesitamos unos consultores que conozcan este mercado concreto.
EN: We need some consultants who know this particular market.
[PAUSE:900]
The second sentence asks for a bounded, contextually useful group.
TL: Son unos especialistas extraordinarios.
EN: They are extraordinary specialists.
SHOW: bare plural = open class · unos or unas = bounded or evaluated set
Ten plural-indefinite judgments follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Language names respond to syntax
REF: 3.2.16

Language names normally appear without an article after en and after everyday verbs of speaking or learning.
TL: La ponencia será en portugués, pero el debate continuará en español.
EN: The talk will be in Portuguese, but the discussion will continue in Spanish.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Marta habla japonés y está aprendiendo coreano.
EN: Marta speaks Japanese and is learning Korean.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not attach one permanent article pattern to the language name. Read its grammatical environment.
SHOW: en, hablar, aprender → usually zero article
Flip for subjects and qualified varieties.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Subjects and specified varieties take the article
REF: 3.2.16

A language used as the subject normally takes the definite article. The article also appears after many other prepositions and when a following phrase identifies a variety.
TL: El catalán conserva contrastes que otras lenguas han perdido.
EN: Catalan preserves contrasts that other languages have lost.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Tradujeron el contrato del árabe al español.
EN: They translated the contract from Arabic into Spanish.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Estudia el español del Caribe desde una perspectiva histórica.
EN: She studies Caribbean Spanish from a historical perspective.
SHOW: subject, governed preposition or qualified variety → definite article
Seven language-name decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Weekdays distinguish event from habit
REF: 3.2.20

A singular definite article locates one event on a particular weekday. A plural article presents that weekday as a recurring schedule.
TL: La comisión votará el martes.
EN: The committee will vote on Tuesday.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La comisión se reúne los martes.
EN: The committee meets on Tuesdays.
[PAUSE:1000]
The number on the article is therefore a temporal operator: one occasion versus a series.
SHOW: el martes = one Tuesday · los martes = every Tuesday
Flip for the zero-article environments.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ranges, predicates and dates reset the pattern
REF: 3.2.20

Weekdays normally lose the article in a range introduced by de, and after ser when the sentence simply identifies today's day. A full date uses the article before the weekday, but not before the month.
TL: Atendemos de lunes a jueves.
EN: We are open from Monday through Thursday.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Hoy es jueves.
EN: Today is Thursday.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: La auditoría comenzará el jueves 12 de marzo.
EN: The audit will begin on Thursday, March 12.
SHOW: el jueves · los jueves · de lunes a jueves · hoy es jueves
Nine weekday decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Proper names have lexical article behavior
REF: 3.2.17, 3.2.18

Most unqualified country and city names appear without an article. Some names conventionally include one, and a modifier can make an article necessary.
TL: Trabajó en Colombia antes de trasladarse a El Salvador.
EN: She worked in Colombia before moving to El Salvador.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: La Colombia que conoció de niño había cambiado profundamente.
EN: The Colombia he knew as a child had changed profoundly.
[PAUSE:1000]
Learn the lexical exceptions, then ask whether the name is being qualified as a particular version of the place.
SHOW: bare place name by default · lexical or qualified name may take article
Flip for people and titles.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Mentioned titles differ from direct address
REF: 3.2.21, 3.2.24, 3.2.25

A professional or official title normally takes the definite article when you speak about its bearer, but loses it when you address that person directly. Don and doña also stand without an article.
TL: La doctora Salas revisará el expediente esta tarde.
EN: Doctor Salas will review the file this afternoon.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Pase por aquí, doctora Salas.
EN: Come this way, Doctor Salas.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Doña Mercedes presidió la asociación durante una década.
EN: Doña Mercedes chaired the association for a decade.
SHOW: talk about the title → article · address the bearer → zero
This contrast feeds the full-sentence practice after the next card.

[CARD]
TITLE: Spanish often uses the article where English uses a possessive
REF: 3.2, 3.2.27

With body parts, clothing, and familiar personal possessions, Spanish often uses the definite article because the possessor is already recoverable from the verb or an object pronoun.
TL: Me lavé las manos antes de entrar en el laboratorio.
EN: I washed my hands before entering the laboratory.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Clara se puso el abrigo y guardó las llaves en el bolsillo.
EN: Clara put on her coat and put her keys in her pocket.
[PAUSE:1000]
Repeating a possessive would add contrast or emphasis that neutral narration does not need.
SHOW: recoverable possessor → definite article, not routine possessive
Flip for institutional place phrases.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Location phrases are learned as constructions
REF: 3.2.27, 3.2.28

Spanish place expressions do not obey one universal article rule. Some use a definite article, some use no article, and a few vary by region or meaning.
TL: Irene está en casa, pero su hermano sigue en la oficina.
EN: Irene is at home, but her brother is still at the office.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: El acusado permaneció en la cárcel hasta el juicio.
EN: The defendant remained in prison until the trial.
[PAUSE:800]
The same lexical discipline applies to activities.
TL: Después del trabajo, solemos jugar al ajedrez.
EN: After work, we usually play chess.
SHOW: learn the whole frame · en casa · en la oficina · jugar al ajedrez
Fourteen full-sentence article choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Contractions apply only to the ordinary article
REF: 3.1.3, 3.2.11

The sequences a plus el and de plus el contract to al and del. They stay separate when El belongs to an official proper name or title.
TL: Regresamos del archivo y entregamos la copia al secretario.
EN: We returned from the archive and handed the copy to the secretary.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: La columna apareció en El Observador, pero no en la edición digital.
EN: The column appeared in El Observador, but not in the digital edition.
[PAUSE:1000]
First decide whether el is grammatical or lexical. Contract only the grammatical article.
SHOW: a + el → al · de + el → del · lexical El stays intact
Flip for de compounds.

[SIDE]
TITLE: De can classify or identify
REF: 3.2.9, 3.2.11

A bare noun after de often classifies the first noun and creates a compound concept. A definite noun phrase identifies a particular source, owner, or domain.
TL: Instalaron una puerta de laboratorio resistente al fuego.
EN: They installed a fire-resistant laboratory door.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: La puerta del laboratorio quedó bloqueada durante el simulacro.
EN: The laboratory door remained blocked during the drill.
[PAUSE:900]
The surface sequence is similar, but the informational structure is different.
TL: El informe analiza los precios de la energía.
EN: The report analyzes energy prices.
SHOW: de + bare noun classifies · de + definite phrase identifies
Nine dense mixed decisions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Article choice is a reference diagnosis
REF: 3.2, 4.1

Before choosing a form, classify the noun phrase. Is the referent already identifiable, one bounded instance, a whole generic class, an unspecified portion, or a role-like type?
TL: La directora busca una traductora con experiencia jurídica.
EN: The director is looking for a translator with legal experience.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Buscamos personal con experiencia para ampliar el equipo.
EN: We are looking for experienced staff to expand the team.
[PAUSE:1000]
The first sentence introduces one countable professional; the second asks for an unbounded category.
SHOW: identified · bounded · generic · partitive · type
Flip for the editing routine.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Run the decision from discourse to form
REF: 3.2, 4.1, 4.2

First decide what the noun refers to in this sentence. Then check whether the construction imposes a conventional choice. Only after that select gender and number, contract al or del if required, and propagate agreement.
TL: Los expertos solicitaron información, pero una asesora pidió el informe completo.
EN: The experts requested information, but one adviser asked for the complete report.
[PAUSE:1000]
The bare noun denotes an unspecified resource, una introduces a participant, and el identifies the document already relevant to the discussion.
TL: Dentro de unos días, la asesora presentará las conclusiones al comité.
EN: In a few days, the adviser will present the conclusions to the committee.
[PAUSE:900]
Each article now follows from a reference decision rather than an English prompt.
SHOW: diagnose reference → apply construction → choose form → check agreement
Seven connected-text corrections finish the unit.
