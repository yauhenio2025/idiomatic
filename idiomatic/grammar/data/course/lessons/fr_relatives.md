## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Rebuild the clause before choosing the relative
REF: 15.1

A relative clause modifies a noun or pronoun. That antecedent also has a hidden grammatical job inside the relative clause: subject, direct object, prepositional complement, place, or time.
TL: Le dossier qui manque est sur son bureau.
EN: The file that is missing is on her desk.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Le dossier que Nora cherche est sur son bureau.
EN: The file that Nora is looking for is on her desk.
[PAUSE:900]
Recover dossier manque in the first sentence and Nora cherche le dossier in the second. The recovered role, not whether the antecedent is a person or thing, starts the choice.
SHOW: rebuild the simple clause -> identify the gap -> choose the relative
Flip for the main function map.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Map each hidden role to a relative family
REF: 15.1

Use qui for the subject gap and que for the direct-object gap. A preposition stays before qui for a person or an agreeing lequel form for a thing. De usually becomes dont, while a place or time relation often becomes où.
TL: la collègue qui répond · le message que je lis · la collègue à qui j'écris
EN: the colleague who answers · the message I read · the colleague I write to
[PAUSE:900]
TL: la règle à laquelle je pense · le projet dont nous parlons · la ville où elle vit
EN: the rule I am thinking about · the project we are discussing · the city where she lives
SHOW: subject qui · object que · preposition + relative · de -> dont · place/time -> où

[CARD]
TITLE: Qui fills the subject slot; que fills the object slot
REF: 15.2, 15.3

If the antecedent performs the relative-clause verb, choose qui. If another subject performs the verb on the antecedent, choose que.
TL: J'ai rencontré la médecin qui dirige le service.
EN: I met the doctor who runs the department.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: J'ai rencontré la médecin que le directeur a recrutée.
EN: I met the doctor whom the director recruited.
[PAUSE:900]
The contrast is la médecin dirige versus le directeur a recruté la médecin. Qui and que work with people and things alike.
SHOW: antecedent does the action -> qui · another subject acts on it -> que
Flip for two consequences of object que.

[SIDE]
TITLE: French keeps object que visible
REF: 15.3

English can omit an object relative, but French cannot. Que becomes qu' before a vowel sound, and a preceding direct object can trigger agreement on a compound-tense past participle.
TL: Voici les analyses qu'elle a publiées.
EN: Here are the analyses that she published.
[PAUSE:900]
The relative is still present in qu'elle, and publiées agrees with the feminine plural object placed before a form built with avoir.
TL: C'est une solution que nous avons proposée hier.
EN: It is a solution we proposed yesterday.
SHOW: never omit que · vowel -> qu' · preceding direct object may trigger agreement
Twenty-seven clean role, qui, and que items follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Keep the preposition before an animate relative
REF: 15.4

When a person or another animate antecedent is the complement of à, avec, pour, sur, or another preposition, keep that preposition and normally follow it with qui.
TL: La chercheuse à qui j'ai répondu travaille à Lyon.
EN: The researcher I replied to works in Lyon.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Le voisin sur qui nous comptions est déjà parti.
EN: The neighbour we were relying on has already left.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not strand the preposition at the end as English can. De has its own relative pattern, handled separately.
SHOW: recover répondre à / compter sur -> à qui / sur qui
Flip for an inanimate complement.

[SIDE]
TITLE: An inanimate complement takes an agreeing lequel form
REF: 15.5

After a preposition, an inanimate antecedent normally selects lequel, laquelle, lesquels, or lesquelles. The relative agrees with the antecedent.
TL: Le règlement auquel elle fait référence a changé.
EN: The regulation she refers to has changed.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Les conditions dans lesquelles nous travaillons sont exigeantes.
EN: The conditions in which we work are demanding.
[PAUSE:900]
À contracts with masculine le and plural les: auquel, auxquels, auxquelles. A feminine singular form remains à laquelle.
SHOW: thing + preposition -> agreeing lequel family · à + lequel/lesquels -> auquel/auxquels

[CARD]
TITLE: Parmi and entre select lequel even for people
REF: 15.4, 15.5

After parmi and entre, use an agreeing lequel form whether the antecedent is animate or inanimate. Bare qui is not the standard choice in this frame.
TL: Les candidates parmi lesquelles nous avons choisi avaient toutes de l'expérience.
EN: The candidates from among whom we chose were all experienced.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Voici les deux options entre lesquelles il faut décider.
EN: Here are the two options between which we must decide.
[PAUSE:900]
The same family appears in la raison pour laquelle. French does not build this meaning with la raison pourquoi.
SHOW: parmi / entre -> lequel family · reason why -> la raison pour laquelle
Flip for the rare non-prepositional use.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Plain lequel as subject or object is exceptional
REF: 15.5.1

Without a preposition, ordinary French normally keeps qui or que. A plain agreeing lequel form as subject or object is literary, legal, or used to disambiguate an antecedent.
TL: Il épousera la sœur d'un collègue, laquelle vit à Genève.
EN: He will marry a colleague's sister, who lives in Geneva.
[PAUSE:900]
Here laquelle explicitly points to sœur rather than collègue. In an ordinary unambiguous sentence, qui is the natural form.
TL: J'ai lu le rapport qui explique la décision.
EN: I read the report that explains the decision.
SHOW: ordinary subject/object -> qui/que · agreeing lequel only for marked clarity or register

[CARD]
TITLE: Dont replaces an ordinary de relationship
REF: 15.6.1

When the recovered clause directly contains de plus the antecedent, use dont for people and things. It covers verb complements, adjective complements, and possession-like noun complements.
TL: Voici le sujet dont nous parlions.
EN: Here is the subject we were talking about.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: J'ai rencontré une architecte dont les projets ont été primés.
EN: I met an architect whose projects won awards.
[PAUSE:900]
In the second sentence, recover les projets de l'architecte. Dont stays at the start of the relative clause even when the possessed noun is an object.
SHOW: parler de X -> X dont nous parlons · les projets de X -> X dont les projets
Flip when de is buried inside a larger phrase.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Do not use dont inside another prepositional frame
REF: 15.6.2

If de belongs inside a larger phrase such as à côté de, près de, autour de, or dans la poche de, preserve that whole frame and use de qui for a person or, more commonly, an agreeing duquel form.
TL: La maison à côté de laquelle nous avons déjeuné est à vendre.
EN: The house next to which we had lunch is for sale.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: L'écrivain à la vie duquel elle s'intéresse est peu connu.
EN: The writer whose life she is interested in is little known.
[PAUSE:900]
Duquel agrees with the antecedent, not with the intervening noun: écrivain controls duquel, while vie does not.
SHOW: direct de -> dont · larger preposition/de phrase -> de qui or duquel family
Thirty-one hygiene-gated prepositional and de-relative items follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Où packages a place relation
REF: 15.7.1

When the antecedent supplies the place in the relative clause, où is the most frequent modern choice. A preposition plus lequel may express the same underlying relationship more explicitly.
TL: Nous revoyons le quartier où nous habitions.
EN: We are revisiting the neighbourhood where we lived.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: C'est le bâtiment dans lequel les archives sont conservées.
EN: It is the building in which the archives are kept.
[PAUSE:900]
Où can retain a path preposition: d'où for source and par où for a route.
TL: Voici la porte par où ils sont sortis.
EN: Here is the door through which they left.
SHOW: place -> où · explicit relation -> preposition + lequel · source/route -> d'où/par où
Flip from place to time.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Definite time takes où; indefinite time can take que
REF: 15.7.2

For a definite time antecedent, où is the controlled form. Traditional French uses que with an indefinite time such as un jour or une fois, though colloquial que also spreads into some definite-time expressions.
TL: Je me souviens du jour où nous avons signé.
EN: I remember the day when we signed.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Un soir que je rentrais tard, le téléphone a sonné.
EN: One evening when I was coming home late, the telephone rang.
[PAUSE:900]
English when does not license relative quand here.
SHOW: definite time -> où · indefinite occasion -> que · never relative quand

[CARD]
TITLE: Quoi follows a preposition after a neutral antecedent
REF: 15.8

After rien, quelque chose, ce, or an entire preceding clause, a prepositional relative can use quoi. This pattern is especially at home in careful written French.
TL: Il n'y a rien sur quoi nous puissions nous appuyer.
EN: There is nothing we can rely on.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: C'est quelque chose à quoi il faut réfléchir.
EN: It is something that requires thought.
[PAUSE:900]
A whole event can also feed the pattern: terminez ce rapport, après quoi nous partirons.
SHOW: neutral antecedent + preposition -> quoi · rien sur quoi · quelque chose à quoi
Flip when the head is not stated at all.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A free relative supplies its own nonspecific head
REF: 15.9

A free relative means the person who or the thing that without naming a separate antecedent. For people, use an agreeing celui, celle, ceux, or celles plus the relative that matches the hidden role.
TL: J'ai appelé celle qui avait laissé un message.
EN: I called the woman who had left a message.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ils ont retenu celui que le jury préférait.
EN: They selected the person whom the panel preferred.
[PAUSE:900]
For an inanimate what meaning, French instead uses the ce-relative family.
SHOW: nonspecific person -> celui/celle/ceux/celles + relative · nonspecific thing -> ce family

[CARD]
TITLE: Ce qui is subject; ce que is direct object
REF: 15.9

In an inanimate free relative, choose ce qui when the missing thing performs the verb and ce que when another subject acts on it.
TL: Ce qui manque, c'est une date précise.
EN: What is missing is a precise date.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Je comprends ce que vous proposez.
EN: I understand what you are proposing.
[PAUSE:900]
With tout, ce remains obligatory: tout ce qui and tout ce que.
TL: Elle a vérifié tout ce que nous avions envoyé.
EN: She checked everything we had sent.
SHOW: something does -> ce qui · subject does something -> ce que · tout still keeps ce
Flip for governed complements.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Let the governing expression reshape ce
REF: 15.9

If the missing thing is governed by de, choose ce dont. With another preposition, put that preposition between ce and quoi.
TL: Ils ont apporté ce dont nous avions besoin.
EN: They brought what we needed.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Dites-moi ce à quoi vous pensez.
EN: Tell me what you are thinking about.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ne jetez pas ce sur quoi j'écrivais.
EN: Do not throw away what I was writing on.
SHOW: de complement -> ce dont · other preposition -> ce + preposition + quoi

[CARD]
TITLE: A whole preceding event also takes the ce family
REF: 15.9.1

When which comments on an entire preceding proposition rather than one noun, use ce qui, ce que, ce dont, or another governed ce form. Bare qui or que would attach to a noun instead.
TL: Léa a refusé l'offre, ce qui nous a surpris.
EN: Léa refused the offer, which surprised us.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Léa a refusé l'offre, ce que personne n'avait prévu.
EN: Léa refused the offer, which nobody had expected.
[PAUSE:900]
The first event is the subject of surprendre; in the second it is the object of prévoir.
SHOW: event does something -> ce qui · someone does something to event -> ce que
Flip for a verb governed by de.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Ce dont points back; de ce que builds its own complement
REF: 15.9.1

After a preceding event, ce dont links a de-governed verb or adjective back to that event. De ce que instead introduces the content directly when no separate antecedent is present.
TL: Vous avez réussi, ce dont je vous félicite.
EN: You succeeded, for which I congratulate you.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Je vous félicite de ce que vous avez accompli.
EN: I congratulate you on what you achieved.
[PAUSE:900]
Keep the two structures distinct: backward-linking relative versus a de complement with its own clause.
SHOW: stated event, then link -> ce dont · no stated head -> de ce que

[CARD]
TITLE: Whoever and whatever split by grammatical role
REF: 15.10

Subject whoever is quiconque plus the indicative. Object whoever is qui que plus the subjunctive. For whatever, use quoi qui when it is subject and quoi que when it is object, both with the subjunctive.
TL: Quiconque connaît le code peut entrer.
EN: Whoever knows the code can enter.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Qui que vous choisissiez, prévenez-moi.
EN: Whoever you choose, let me know.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Quoi qu'il propose, nous l'examinerons.
EN: Whatever he proposes, we will examine it.
SHOW: subject whoever -> quiconque + indicative · object whoever -> qui que + subjunctive · object whatever -> quoi que
Flip for a noun after whatever.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Keep quel que and quelque distinct
REF: 15.10

With être, use two-word quel que and make quel agree with its noun. Before a noun that serves as the object of another verb, use invariable singular quelque or plural quelques, followed later by que.
TL: Quelle que soit la réponse, nous continuerons.
EN: Whatever the answer, we will continue.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Quelques objections qu'il soulève, le projet avancera.
EN: Whatever objections he raises, the project will proceed.
[PAUSE:900]
Où que plus the subjunctive means wherever. Si, aussi, quelque, or pour plus adjective and que can mean however, also with the subjunctive.
TL: Où qu'elle aille · Si difficile que ce soit
EN: Wherever she goes · However difficult it may be
SHOW: quel que + être · quelque(s) + noun + que + verb · où que / si ... que + subjunctive

[CARD]
TITLE: Mood tells whether the antecedent is identified
REF: 15.11.2, 15.11.4

An ordinary relative about an identified person or thing takes the indicative. A relative describing only a sought, hypothetical, questioned, or nonexistent type usually takes the subjunctive.
TL: Je connais une entreprise qui offre ce service.
EN: I know a company that offers this service.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Je cherche une entreprise qui offre ce service.
EN: I am looking for a company that offers this service.
[PAUSE:900]
The first sentence identifies a real company. The second specifies the kind required without asserting that one has been found.
SHOW: identified antecedent -> indicative · open, sought, or absent antecedent -> subjunctive
Flip for first, only, and superlative descriptions.

[SIDE]
TITLE: First-ever and unique readings favor the subjunctive
REF: 15.11.3, 15.11.4

After le premier, le dernier, le seul, or a superlative, the subjunctive presents an absolute or evaluative extreme. The indicative can instead locate an item inside a known set.
TL: C'est le seul rapport que j'aie lu sur ce sujet.
EN: It is the only report I have ever read on this subject.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: C'est le premier rapport que j'ai lu ce matin.
EN: It is the first report I read this morning.
[PAUSE:900]
Questions and negation also favor the subjunctive when they leave the antecedent's existence open.
TL: Je ne connais personne qui puisse nous aider.
EN: I know nobody who can help us.
SHOW: absolute/unique/open set -> subjunctive · identified member of a set -> indicative
