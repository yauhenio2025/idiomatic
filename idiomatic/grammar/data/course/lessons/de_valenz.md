## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Every verb opens a frame
SVG: de_valenz_c01f.svg
REF: 16.1.1, 16.1.2

A verb does more than name an event. It opens a sentence frame with a particular number of slots. Some verbs need only a subject:
TL: Der Hausmeister gähnte.
EN: The caretaker yawned.
[PAUSE:900]
Others select an object:
TL: Die Gutachterin prüfte den Vertrag.
EN: The expert reviewed the contract.
[PAUSE:900]
And a three-place verb can select a subject, a receiver and a transferred thing:
TL: Die Kuratorin übergab dem Archiv ein seltenes Manuskript.
EN: The curator handed a rare manuscript over to the archive.
SHOW: the verb selects the number and type of slots
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Complement or extra information?
SVG: de_valenz_c01b.svg
REF: 16.1.3, 16.1.4

A complement belongs to the verb's frame. An adverbial merely adds circumstances such as time, manner or place. In the next sentence, the place completes the meaning of the position verb; the duration is extra:
TL: Die Forscherin wohnte drei Jahre lang in Basel.
EN: The researcher lived in Basel for three years.
[PAUSE:1100]
Now the same city phrase is only background, because the verb already forms a complete frame without it:
TL: Die Forscherin promovierte in Basel.
EN: The researcher earned her doctorate in Basel.
[PAUSE:1100]
Omission is a useful test, but not an infallible one. A complement may remain understood even when it is unspoken:
TL: Der Lieferwagen fährt schon.
EN: The delivery van is already on its way.
SHOW: complement = selected by the verb · adverbial = added context
The system is on the table. Next, the subject and its impostors.

[CARD]
TITLE: The subject — and sentences without one
REF: 16.2.1, 16.2.2

Most German verbs select a nominative subject, and the finite verb agrees with it. A whole clause can fill that slot:
TL: Dass der Vertrag scheitert, überrascht niemanden.
EN: The fact that the contract is falling through surprises no one.
[PAUSE:1100]
But a small, formal group of sensation verbs can build a clause with no subject at all. The experiencer is an object, and the verb stays third-person singular:
TL: Vor dem morgigen Verhör graut mir.
EN: I dread tomorrow's interrogation.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Mich friert trotz des dicken Mantels.
EN: I am cold despite the thick coat.
SHOW: usual frame: nominative subject · rare frame: object, no subject
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Three kinds of impersonal construction
REF: 16.2.4

First, weather verbs have a fixed impersonal subject:
TL: Es donnert über dem Tal.
EN: Thunder is rumbling over the valley.
[PAUSE:900]
Second, an indefinite agent can be packaged impersonally. Outside first position, the placeholder normally disappears:
TL: Im Sitzungssaal wurde bis Mitternacht gestritten.
EN: People argued in the meeting room until midnight.
[PAUSE:1100]
Third, a personal dative can name the experiencer with a linking verb and an adjective. Again, no placeholder is needed away from first position:
TL: Nach wenigen Minuten wurde mir plötzlich übel.
EN: After a few minutes I suddenly felt sick.
SHOW: weather · indefinite agent · dative experiencer
Ten construction drills follow this card.

[CARD]
TITLE: General existence
SVG: de_valenz_c03f.svg
REF: 16.2.5

German splits English there is into two different ideas. Use the fixed impersonal construction when you assert that something exists in general:
TL: In dieser Branche gibt es kaum unabhängige Gutachten.
EN: There are hardly any independent assessments in this sector.
[PAUSE:1100]
It also presents consequences and future events:
TL: Morgen gibt es eine entscheidende Abstimmung.
EN: There will be a decisive vote tomorrow.
[PAUSE:1000]
The impersonal subject in this construction never disappears, even when another element takes first position.
SHOW: general existence or an event → fixed impersonal frame
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Presence here and now
REF: 16.2.5

For presence at a definite place and time, use the ordinary linking verb. A dummy subject may hold first position while the real subject comes later:
TL: Es sind noch zwei Gäste im Foyer.
EN: There are still two guests in the foyer.
[PAUSE:1000]
Put the place first and the dummy vanishes:
TL: Im Foyer sind noch zwei Gäste.
EN: There are still two guests in the foyer.
[PAUSE:1000]
The same pattern works in the past:
TL: Vor dem Archiv stand noch ein Lieferwagen.
EN: There was still a delivery van outside the archive.
SHOW: definite presence → real subject · dummy only in first position

[CARD]
TITLE: Transitivity is a lexical choice
SVG: de_valenz_c04f.svg
REF: 16.3.1, 16.3.4

A transitive verb selects an accusative object. English often lets one verb alternate freely between causing a change and undergoing it. German more often chooses a different verb:
TL: Die Zentralbank senkte überraschend den Leitzins.
EN: The central bank unexpectedly lowered the key interest rate.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Leitzins sank überraschend stark.
EN: The key interest rate fell surprisingly sharply.
[PAUSE:1100]
One event has an external causer and an accusative object; the other has only the thing that changes as its subject.
SHOW: cause a change = transitive · undergo a change = intransitive
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reflexive and causative repairs
REF: 16.3.4, 16.3.5

German can also mark the intransitive side with a reflexive form:
TL: Seit dem Update hat sich die Benutzeroberfläche grundlegend verändert.
EN: Since the update, the user interface has changed fundamentally.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Das Entwicklerteam hat die Benutzeroberfläche grundlegend verändert.
EN: The development team fundamentally changed the user interface.
[PAUSE:1000]
And when no simple transitive partner exists, a causative construction supplies the causer:
TL: Der Techniker ließ das Kühlmittel vollständig ablaufen.
EN: The technician drained the coolant completely.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Das Kühlmittel lief vollständig ab.
EN: The coolant drained completely.
SHOW: separate verb · reflexive form · causative construction
Ten contrastive translations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: The receiver and the thing
SVG: de_valenz_c05f.svg
REF: 16.4.1

Many verbs of transfer open two object slots. The person or institution receiving the transfer is dative; the thing transferred is accusative:
TL: Der Verlag schickte der Gutachterin die korrigierten Druckfahnen.
EN: The publisher sent the corrected proofs to the reviewer.
[PAUSE:1100]
The same frame covers transfer by speech, concealment and permission:
TL: Die Redakteurin verschwieg dem Ausschuss einen wichtigen Einwand.
EN: The editor withheld an important objection from the committee.
[PAUSE:1100]
Do not insert a preposition merely because English can say to or from. The case is the relationship.
SHOW: person or recipient = dative · transferred content = accusative
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Content can be an entire clause
REF: 16.3.2, 16.4.1

The accusative content slot does not have to be a noun phrase. A subordinate clause or an infinitive clause can fill it:
TL: Die Sprecherin erklärte den Anwesenden, warum die Sitzung ausfiel.
EN: The spokesperson explained to those present why the meeting was cancelled.
[PAUSE:1100]
TL: Der Zeuge verschwieg der Ermittlerin, dass er den Fahrer erkannt hatte.
EN: The witness did not tell the investigator that he had recognized the driver.
[PAUSE:1100]
The whole clause behaves like the transferred thing; the person addressed stays dative.
SHOW: dative person + noun, infinitive or clause as content
Ten two-object frames follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Dative-only verbs
REF: 16.4, 16.4.2

Some verbs select a dative object and no accusative at all. The pattern must be learned with the verb:
TL: Der neue Beschluss widerspricht der bisherigen Praxis.
EN: The new decision conflicts with previous practice.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Zeugin wich der Frage geschickt aus.
EN: The witness skilfully evaded the question.
[PAUSE:1000]
Sometimes the German dative corresponds to the subject of the natural English translation:
TL: Der Prüferin fiel der Widerspruch sofort auf.
EN: The examiner noticed the contradiction immediately.
SHOW: dative can be the sole selected object
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reflexive case follows the open slot
REF: 16.3.5, 16.4.3

A reflexive pronoun is accusative when it occupies the verb's direct-object slot:
TL: Die Bewerberin stellte sich knapp vor.
EN: The applicant introduced herself briefly.
[PAUSE:1000]
If another accusative object already occupies that slot, the reflexive pronoun is often dative:
TL: Die Bewerberin stellte sich eine schwierige Frage.
EN: The applicant asked herself a difficult question.
[PAUSE:1000]
And a verb that governs the dative keeps that case when subject and object refer to the same person:
TL: In seiner zweiten Aussage widersprach er sich selbst.
EN: In his second statement, he contradicted himself.
SHOW: reflexive case is inherited from the verb's frame

[CARD]
TITLE: A preposition can be part of the verb
SVG: de_valenz_c07f.svg
REF: 16.5.1, 16.5.2, 16.5.3

In a prepositional object, the verb selects both the preposition and its case. Spatial logic cannot predict the choice:
TL: Wir warten auf die endgültige Freigabe.
EN: We are waiting for final approval.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Kommission zweifelt an der Belastbarkeit der Zahlen.
EN: The commission doubts the robustness of the figures.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Der Vorsitzende bestand auf einer schriftlichen Begründung.
EN: The chair insisted on a written justification.
SHOW: learn verb + preposition + case as one frame
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Prepositional objects can share the frame
REF: 16.5.1, 16.5.5, 16.5.7, 16.5.12

A prepositional object may be the verb's only object, or it can coexist with an accusative or dative object:
TL: Die Kanzlei warnte den Mandanten vor übereilten Schritten.
EN: The law firm warned the client against hasty steps.
[PAUSE:1100]
TL: Wir rechnen mit einer längeren Übergangsphase.
EN: We expect a longer transition period.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Arbeitsgruppe entschied sich für die riskantere Variante.
EN: The working group chose the riskier option.
SHOW: prepositional slot alone · or beside another object
Fifteen fixed-preposition and ending drills follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Change the preposition, change the meaning
SVG: de_valenz_c08f.svg
REF: 16.1.2, 16.5.3, 16.5.9

Valency can distinguish meanings of the same verb. One preposition points towards a future event; another reacts to an event already realized:
TL: Wir freuen uns auf die Veröffentlichung der Studie.
EN: We are looking forward to the publication of the study.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Wir freuen uns über die breite Resonanz auf die Studie.
EN: We are pleased about the broad response to the study.
[PAUSE:1100]
The contrast belongs to the whole verb frame, not to the dictionary headword alone.
SHOW: same verb + different preposition = different sense
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: One verb, three frames
REF: 16.5.3, 16.5.4, 16.5.6

The same principle can produce an entire family of meanings:
TL: Die Arbeitsgruppe besteht aus fünf Fachleuten.
EN: The working group consists of five specialists.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Ihre wichtigste Aufgabe besteht in der Prüfung der Rohdaten.
EN: Its most important task is to examine the raw data.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Vorsitzende besteht auf einer unabhängigen Kontrolle.
EN: The chair insists on an independent review.
[PAUSE:1100]
Composition, content, insistence: three different frames, three meanings.
SHOW: the selected preposition is part of the lexical entry

[CARD]
TITLE: Object or prepositional object?
REF: 16.3.4, 16.5.1

German often offers a transitive prefixed verb beside a simpler verb with a prepositional object. The event may be similar, but the syntax is not:
TL: Der Ausschuss beantwortete die Anfrage noch am selben Tag.
EN: The committee answered the enquiry that same day.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Ausschuss antwortete noch am selben Tag auf die Anfrage.
EN: The committee replied to the enquiry that same day.
[PAUSE:1100]
The first frame takes a bare accusative. The second keeps its preposition.
SHOW: prefixed verb → accusative · simple verb → prepositional object
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Near-synonyms keep separate frames
REF: 16.3.4, 16.5.1, 16.5.3

Do not let the shared English translation erase the German contrast:
TL: Wir besprachen den Entwurf ausführlich.
EN: We discussed the draft in detail.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir sprachen ausführlich über den Entwurf.
EN: We talked about the draft in detail.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Forschungsgruppe erhofft einen baldigen Durchbruch.
EN: The research group hopes for an early breakthrough.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Die Forschungsgruppe hofft auf einen baldigen Durchbruch.
EN: The research group is hoping for a breakthrough soon.
SHOW: synonymy does not cancel valency

[CARD]
TITLE: A prepositional object becomes a clause
SVG: de_valenz_c10f.svg
REF: 16.5.14

When a prepositional object expands into a subordinate or infinitive clause, German commonly leaves a prepositional adverb in the original slot:
TL: Wir rechnen mit einer Verzögerung der Lieferung.
EN: We expect a delay in the delivery.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Wir rechnen damit, dass sich die Lieferung verzögert.
EN: We expect the delivery to be delayed.
[PAUSE:1100]
The compound points forward; the following clause supplies its content.
SHOW: preposition + noun → forward pointer + clause
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The pointer may be optional, but it is safe
REF: 16.5.14

Written German includes the pointer more readily than casual speech. With some verbs it is optional, but including it is never wrong:
TL: Die Ministerin freute sich darüber, dass der Kompromiss hielt.
EN: The minister was pleased that the compromise held.
[PAUSE:1000]
It can also introduce an infinitive clause:
TL: Der Anwalt warnte davor, den Vertrag vorschnell zu unterschreiben.
EN: The lawyer warned against signing the contract hastily.
[PAUSE:1000]
TL: Die Prüferin bestand darauf, sämtliche Belege erneut zu sichten.
EN: The examiner insisted on reviewing all the supporting documents again.
SHOW: the pointer preserves the verb's prepositional frame

[CARD]
TITLE: Predicate complements identify the subject
REF: 16.6.1, 16.6.2

A linking verb does not act on an object. Its complement identifies or describes the subject, so a noun phrase stays nominative:
TL: Nora ist die leitende Ingenieurin.
EN: Nora is the lead engineer.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Nora blieb trotz des Drucks erstaunlich gelassen.
EN: Nora remained remarkably calm despite the pressure.
[PAUSE:1000]
A small set of naming verbs can instead describe an accusative object with an accusative predicate complement:
TL: Der Vorstand nannte Nora eine außergewöhnliche Vermittlerin.
EN: The board called Nora an exceptional mediator.
SHOW: linking verb → describes subject · naming verb → describes object
Flip the card.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The rare and the spatial slots
REF: 16.7, 16.8, 16.1.4

Genitive objects survive mainly in elevated, formal and specialized registers:
TL: Der Vorschlag bedarf einer gründlicheren Prüfung.
EN: The proposal requires a more thorough examination.
[PAUSE:1000]
Movement and position verbs select a direction or place complement:
TL: Die Delegation reiste nach Genf.
EN: The delegation travelled to Geneva.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Der Abschlussbericht liegt auf dem Konferenztisch.
EN: The final report is lying on the conference table.
[PAUSE:1000]
Compare a place that merely adds background to an otherwise complete event:
TL: Die Delegation tagte gestern in Genf.
EN: The delegation met in Geneva yesterday.
SHOW: genitive = formal object · direction/place = locative complement
You now have the whole system: learn each verb with its complete frame.
