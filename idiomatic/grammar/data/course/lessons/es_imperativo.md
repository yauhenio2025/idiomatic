## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A command chooses both an action and an addressee
REF: 21.1, 21.2, 21.3

Spanish commands are organized by who must act. The familiar singular, familiar plural, polite forms, and first-person plural do not all use one shared ending.
TL: Cierra la puerta, Ana.
EN: Close the door, Ana.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Cierren la puerta, por favor.
EN: Close the door, please.
[PAUSE:1000]
In most of Latin America, ustedes supplies the plural command for familiar as well as formal groups. Peninsular Spanish also has a distinct vosotros form.
SHOW: tú · vos · usted · nosotros · vosotros · ustedes
Flip for the one rule shared by every negative command.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Every negative command uses the present subjunctive
REF: 21.1, 21.3

Affirmative commands use several form-building routes. Once a command is negated, Spanish uses no plus a present-subjunctive form for every addressee.
TL: Abre la ventana, pero no abras la puerta.
EN: Open the window, but do not open the door.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Váyanse ahora, pero no se lleven los documentos.
EN: Leave now, but do not take the documents with you.
[PAUSE:1000]
Tone still matters. A bare imperative can be friendly or abrupt, and a question with poder or querer can soften a request when the social situation calls for it.
SHOW: affirmative = several paradigms · negative = no + present subjunctive
Flip to build the familiar singular forms.

[CARD]
TITLE: The affirmative tú form usually removes one s
REF: 21.2.2, 21.3

For most verbs, take the second-person singular present indicative and remove its final s. This gives habla, come, escribe, and the stem-changing forms piensa and vuelve.
TL: Piensa en la propuesta y vuelve mañana.
EN: Think about the proposal and come back tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Eight common verbs use short affirmative forms that need their own anchor.
SHOW: di · haz · ve · pon · sal · sé · ten · ven
Flip to contrast them with negative tú commands.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Negative tú commands return to the subjunctive
REF: 21.2.2, 21.3

Do not negate an affirmative form mechanically. Build the present subjunctive and place no before it.
TL: Haz la copia, pero no hagas cambios todavía.
EN: Make the copy, but do not make changes yet.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Ven conmigo; no vengas solo.
EN: Come with me; do not come alone.
[PAUSE:900]
When prefixes extend poner or tener, the affirmative keeps the family pattern and may require a written accent.
TL: Propón una fecha, pero no la impongas.
EN: Suggest a date, but do not impose it.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: haz ≠ no haz · haz → no hagas · ven → no vengas
Nine form-table completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Vosotros replaces the infinitive r with d
REF: 21.2.4, 21.3

The familiar plural form used in Spain is completely regular: remove r from the infinitive and add d.
TL: Hablad más despacio y escribid vuestros nombres.
EN: Speak more slowly and write your names.
[PAUSE:900]
With a pronominal verb, the d normally disappears before os. Irse is the traditional exception.
TL: Sentaos aquí y luego idos a casa.
EN: Sit here and then go home.
[PAUSE:1000]
For a negative command, os moves before a present-subjunctive form.
SHOW: hablar → hablad · sentad + os → sentaos · no os sentéis
Flip for the two major American address patterns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Vos and ustedes reorganize the address grid
REF: 21.2.3, 21.2.5, 21.3

The affirmative vos command usually removes r from the infinitive and stresses the final vowel. Pronominal verbs attach te without preserving the standard tú accent pattern.
TL: Vení mañana y traé el informe.
EN: Come tomorrow and bring the report.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Lavate las manos y sentate aquí.
EN: Wash your hands and sit here.
[PAUSE:900]
Usted and ustedes use third-person present-subjunctive forms. Across Latin America, ustedes normally addresses any plural group, not only a formal one.
TL: Pasen, siéntense y díganme sus nombres.
EN: Come in, sit down, and tell me your names.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: vos cantá · usted cante · ustedes canten · vosotros cantad
Eighteen vosotros transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: An affirmative command carries its object pronouns after it
REF: 21.2, 21.4

With an affirmative imperative, unstressed object and reflexive pronouns attach to the end of the verb as one written word.
TL: Cierra la carpeta y guárdala en el cajón.
EN: Close the folder and put it away in the drawer.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Póngase el abrigo y llévese el paraguas.
EN: Put on your coat and take the umbrella with you.
[PAUSE:1000]
The resulting word follows ordinary stress rules, so attachment often creates a written accent that preserves the command's original stress.
SHOW: guarda + la → guárdala · ponga + se → póngase
Flip to attach two pronouns in the correct order.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Two pronouns keep the ordinary clitic order
REF: 14.2.4, 21.4

An indirect-object or reflexive pronoun comes before a direct-object pronoun. Le and les become se before lo, la, los, or las.
TL: Explícame el problema y luego explícamelo por escrito.
EN: Explain the problem to me and then explain it to me in writing.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Entregue la llave a la directora; entréguesela personalmente.
EN: Give the key to the director; hand it to her personally.
[PAUSE:1000]
Do not detach the pronouns or move them before a positive command. Treat the verb-plus-pronoun sequence as a single accent-bearing word.
SHOW: command + indirect/reflexive + direct
Fifteen affirmative pronoun placements follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Negation moves every object pronoun before the verb
REF: 21.3, 21.4

Negative commands reverse the positive placement pattern: no comes first, then the pronoun cluster, then the present-subjunctive verb.
TL: Dímelo ahora, pero no me lo repitas mañana.
EN: Tell it to me now, but do not repeat it to me tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Llévenselo, pero no se lo enseñen a nadie.
EN: Take it away, but do not show it to anyone.
[PAUSE:1000]
The pronoun order itself does not change; only its position relative to the verb changes.
SHOW: affirmative dámelo · negative no me lo des
Flip for commands that English frames as passive.

[SIDE]
TITLE: English passive commands need an active Spanish strategy
REF: 21.1, 21.3

Spanish normally avoids a personal passive imperative such as English do not be fooled. A common solution is dejar plus an infinitive or a que clause.
TL: No te dejes engañar por esa promesa.
EN: Do not let yourself be deceived by that promise.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: No dejes que te obliguen a firmar.
EN: Do not let them force you to sign.
[PAUSE:1000]
Choose the participant who can allow or resist the event, then build a normal negative command around that participant.
SHOW: do not be affected → no te dejes + infinitive · no dejes que + subjunctive
Sixteen positive-to-negative transformations follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Nosotros commands use the present subjunctive
REF: 21.5

The first-person plural imperative includes the speaker: use a present-subjunctive nosotros form for let's do or let us do.
TL: Empecemos por los datos y revisemos después la conclusión.
EN: Let us start with the data and review the conclusion afterwards.
[PAUSE:900]
In the negative, pronouns stand before the verb as expected.
TL: No nos precipitemos y no lo decidamos todavía.
EN: Let us not rush and let us not decide it yet.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: hablemos · comamos · escribamos · no nos vayamos
Flip for the spelling changes caused by attached pronouns.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Before nos, the command drops its final s
REF: 21.5

Attach nos to a positive nosotros command, but remove the final s of the verb first. Other pronouns normally keep that s.
TL: Sentémonos cerca de la puerta.
EN: Let us sit near the door.
[PAUSE:700]
TL: Digámosles la verdad y expliquémoselo con calma.
EN: Let us tell them the truth and explain it to them calmly.
[PAUSE:900]
Ir often uses the special everyday forms vamos and vámonos, although vayamos and vayámonos also occur.
TL: Vámonos ahora; vayamos directamente a la estación.
EN: Let us leave now; let us go directly to the station.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: sentemos + nos → sentémonos · digamos + les → digámosles
Ten first-person plural completions follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Que plus subjunctive directs an absent third person
REF: 21.6

To express let him, let her, or let them do something, use que followed by a third-person subjunctive. Any object pronouns precede that verb.
TL: Si Marta quiere participar, que presente una propuesta.
EN: If Marta wants to participate, let her submit a proposal.
[PAUSE:900]
TL: Si llaman los técnicos, que nos dejen un mensaje.
EN: If the technicians call, tell them to leave us a message.
[PAUSE:1000]
The construction often passes an instruction through the current listener to someone else.
SHOW: que + lo/la/los/las + present subjunctive
Flip for wishes and emphatic reminders.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Que can also reinforce a second-person command
REF: 21.6, 21.7

Que plus a second-person subjunctive can make an instruction emphatic, present it as a reminder, or express a wish.
TL: ¡Que cierres bien la puerta!
EN: Make sure you close the door properly!
[PAUSE:800]
TL: ¡Que tengan buen viaje!
EN: Have a good trip!
[PAUSE:900]
Context separates a relayed third-person order from a wish addressed directly to the listener. The verb remains subjunctive in both patterns.
TL: Que entren los siguientes candidatos.
EN: Let the next candidates come in.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: relayed order · emphatic reminder · independent wish
Ten third-person imperative choices follow.

[CARD]
TITLE: Passive se gives written instructions without an addressee
REF: 21.8

Formal instructions can use a passive-se imperative when the writer does not address a particular reader directly. The verb agrees with its grammatical subject.
TL: Rellénese el formulario con tinta azul.
EN: Fill in the form in blue ink.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: Tradúzcanse las frases siguientes.
EN: Translate the following sentences.
[PAUSE:1000]
The singular and plural forms reflect formulario and frases, not the number of people reading the instruction.
SHOW: singular subject → rellénese · plural subject → tradúzcanse
Flip for the modern infinitive alternative.

[SIDE]
TITLE: The infinitive is compact, impersonal, and register-sensitive
REF: 21.9

Notices, manuals, and recipes commonly use an infinitive for a brief instruction. Negative public notices use no plus the infinitive.
TL: Pulsar el botón rojo y esperar la señal.
EN: Press the red button and wait for the signal.
[PAUSE:800]
TL: No tocar. No asomarse a la ventanilla.
EN: Do not touch. Do not lean out of the window.
[PAUSE:900]
Informal speech in Spain also uses the infinitive instead of a familiar plural form, but careful learner usage keeps this apart from neutral impersonal instructions.
TL: A dormir; mañana salimos temprano.
EN: Off to bed; tomorrow we leave early.
[PAUSE:1000]
SHOW: public instruction → infinitive · direct careful address → personal command
Five impersonal rewrites follow.
