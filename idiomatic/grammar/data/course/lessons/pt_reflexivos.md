## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: A reflexive form links its pronoun back to the subject
REF: 22.1, 22.2

A reflexive verb carries an unstressed object pronoun whose reference returns to the subject. Match the pronoun to the person of that subject.
TL: eu me · você se · ele se · ela se · nós nos · vocês se · eles se · elas se
EN: myself · yourself · himself or herself · ourselves · yourselves or themselves
[PAUSE:900]
The phrase a gente has third-person grammar, so its reflexive pronoun is se even when it means we.
TL: Eu me cortei. · Nós nos comprometemos. · A gente se comprometeu.
EN: I cut myself. · We committed ourselves. · We committed ourselves.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: eu -> me · você/ele/ela/a gente -> se · nós -> nos · vocês/eles/elas -> se
Flip to place that matching pronoun around the verb.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Reflexive pronouns follow the ordinary clitic-position rules
REF: 22.2

In everyday Brazilian Portuguese, an unstressed reflexive pronoun normally stands before a finite verb. A negative or another attracting word keeps it there.
TL: Ela se levantou. · Eu não me lembro. · Quando nos encontramos, conversamos muito.
EN: She got up. · I do not remember. · When we meet, we talk a lot.
[PAUSE:900]
More formal writing can append the pronoun with a hyphen. When nos is appended to a first-person plural form, the verb loses its final s.
TL: Ele machucou-se. · Nós sentamo-nos. · Nós pusemo-nos a trabalhar.
EN: He hurt himself. · We sat down. · We set to work.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: everyday finite verb -> pronoun + verb · formal attachment -> verb-pronoun · -mos + nos -> -mo-nos

[CARD]
TITLE: True reflexives describe action directed at the subject
REF: 22.3.1

In the clearest reflexive pattern, the subject acts on the same participant named by the object pronoun. English may translate this with myself, yourself, or another self-form.
TL: Eu me queimei. · Ele se olhou no espelho. · Ela se aplicou uma injeção.
EN: I burned myself. · He looked at himself in the mirror. · She gave herself an injection.
[PAUSE:900]
Portuguese also uses the same form for actions that English usually expresses without an overt self-object.
TL: Ele se escondeu. · Eles se vestem bem. · Nós nos identificamos com os personagens.
EN: He hid. · They dress well. · We identify with the characters.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: subject = object participant · English self-form may be explicit or only understood
Flip because a plural subject opens a second interpretation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: With a plural subject, the meaning can be reciprocal
REF: 22.3.2

A plural subject plus a reflexive pronoun can mean that the participants act on one another rather than separately on themselves.
TL: Eles se beijaram. · A gente mal se conhece. · Nós nos compramos o mesmo presente.
EN: They kissed each other. · We hardly know each other. · We bought each other the same gift.
[PAUSE:900]
When context does not make the reciprocal reading clear, add um ao outro for two participants or uns aos outros for a larger group.
TL: Os dois se ajudaram um ao outro. · Os alunos se apresentaram uns aos outros.
EN: The two helped each other. · The students introduced themselves to one another.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: plural + reflexive pronoun -> each other is possible · make explicit with um/uns ao(s) outro(s)

[CARD]
TITLE: Some reflexive forms correspond to English intransitives
REF: 22.3.3, 22.3.4

Portuguese often uses se where English simply describes a change with an intransitive verb. The changed thing is the subject, not an agent acting on itself.
TL: A doença se espalhou. · O fogo se alastrou. · Uma mancha se formou.
EN: The disease spread. · The fire spread. · A stain formed.
[PAUSE:900]
Changes of emotional state form another important group. English often uses get or be plus an adjective or participle.
TL: Eu me surpreendi. · Ela se emocionou. · Não se estresse.
EN: I was surprised. · She became emotional. · Do not get stressed.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: reflexive form can mark a change · physical development -> English intransitive · emotion -> English be/get
Flip for physical position and verbs that are reflexive by convention.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Position verbs and conventional reflexives must be learned as patterns
REF: 22.3.5, 22.3.6, 22.5

Sentar-se, ajoelhar-se, deitar-se, and levantar-se describe a change of bodily position. Other verbs are reflexive by convention, including arrepender-se, recusar-se, and gabar-se.
TL: Ela se levantou. · Ele se recusou a sair. · Eu me arrependi.
EN: She got up. · He refused to leave. · I regretted it.
[PAUSE:900]
Everyday speech often drops se from position verbs and from verbs such as casar-se, divorciar-se, esquecer-se, and lembrar-se. Emotional verbs may instead use ficar plus a participle.
TL: Levantei da mesa. · Eu fiquei surpreso. · Não fique estressado.
EN: I got up from the table. · I was surprised. · Do not get stressed.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: dictionary pattern may include -se · informal speech often uses a plain intransitive or ficar + participle

[CARD]
TITLE: Impersonal se creates a general statement without naming an agent
REF: 22.4.1, 22.4.2

With an otherwise intransitive verb, se supplies a general people, one, or you reading. The verb stays in the third-person singular.
TL: Come-se bem aqui. · Não se saía à noite. · Não se pode fumar aqui.
EN: People eat well here. · People did not go out at night. · You cannot smoke here.
[PAUSE:900]
With a transitive verb, English commonly renders the same construction as a passive. The Portuguese sentence still avoids naming the agent.
TL: Aqui se fala inglês. · Essa fruta se chama graviola.
EN: English is spoken here. · That fruit is called graviola.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: se + third-person verb -> general unnamed agent · intransitive -> people/one · transitive -> often English passive
Flip for agreement when the transitive construction has a plural noun.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Formal agreement can make the verb track a plural grammatical subject
REF: 22.4.3, 22.4.4

In formal grammar, a transitive se-construction uses a plural verb when its grammatical subject is plural. A singular verb is nevertheless widespread in speech and writing.
TL: Vende-se um apartamento. · Vendem-se apartamentos.
EN: One apartment is for sale. · Apartments are for sale.
[PAUSE:900]
With a modal and a following infinitive, formal agreement can appear when a plural subject connects directly to that infinitive. A preposition before the infinitive, or an intransitive main verb, keeps the modal singular.
TL: Não se deve destruir as florestas. · Não se devem destruir as florestas. · Tende-se a subestimar as dificuldades.
EN: The forests should not be destroyed. · People tend to underestimate the difficulties.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: formal transitive + plural subject -> plural agreement · preposition/intransitive pattern -> singular modal

[CARD]
TITLE: The same se shape can carry three different relationships
REF: 22.3, 22.4

Ask first whether se refers back to a participant named as the subject. If one participant acts on that same participant, the use is reflexive.
TL: Ana se penteou.
EN: Ana combed her own hair.
[PAUSE:850]
With a plural subject, ask whether the participants act on one another; that is reciprocal. If the sentence makes a general claim and leaves the agent unnamed, the use is impersonal.
TL: Ana e Bia se abraçaram. · Aqui se consertam sapatos.
EN: Ana and Bia hugged each other. · Shoes are repaired here.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: same participant -> reflexive · participants act mutually -> reciprocal · unnamed general agent -> impersonal
Flip to see why person and register matter as much as translation.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Context decides whether spoken Portuguese keeps the pronoun
REF: 22.3.4, 22.3.5, 22.5

Formal reference patterns help identify a verb's reflexive meaning, but informal Brazilian speech may omit the pronoun from several change, position, marriage, memory, and forgetting verbs.
TL: Ela se casou no sábado. · Ela casou no sábado. · Eu não me lembro. · Eu não lembro.
EN: She got married on Saturday. · I do not remember.
[PAUSE:900]
Do not remove se mechanically. Truly reflexive, reciprocal, and impersonal meanings still need the relationship it signals; usage varies by verb and register.
TL: Eles se amam. · Só se pode entrar com ingresso.
EN: They love each other. · You may enter only with a ticket.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: learn verb + meaning + register together · omission is a usage pattern, not a universal rule

[CARD]
TITLE: After a preposition, general third-person reflexives use si or consigo
REF: 22.6.1, 22.6.2

The stressed reflexive form after an ordinary preposition is si. With com, use the fused form consigo. In current Brazilian usage these forms are most natural in impersonal or general statements.
TL: Deve-se questionar a si mesmo. · Cada passageiro leva consigo uma mala.
EN: One should question oneself. · Each passenger takes a bag with them.
[PAUSE:900]
For a specific third-person subject, Brazilian Portuguese normally uses the corresponding ordinary stressed pronoun, often reinforced by mesmo or mesma.
TL: Você deve cuidar de você mesmo. · Ela levou o documento com ela.
EN: You should take care of yourself. · She took the document with her.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: general/indefinite third person -> si / consigo · specific person -> você/ele/ela + mesmo when needed
Flip for first-person forms and a compact choice routine.

[SIDE]
TITLE: First-person stressed reflexives use mim, comigo, nós, and conosco
REF: 22.6.3

After a preposition, a first-person singular reflexive uses mim; with com, use comigo. The plural uses nós or conosco, with mesmo forms when emphasis or clarity calls for them.
TL: Fiquei decepcionado comigo mesmo. · Guardamos uma cópia para nós. · Levamos os documentos conosco.
EN: I was disappointed with myself. · We kept a copy for ourselves. · We took the documents with us.
[PAUSE:900]
Choose in three passes: identify the participant, decide whether the meaning is reflexive, reciprocal, or impersonal, then apply the pronoun and placement pattern appropriate to person and register.
TL: Eu me machuquei. · Eles se ajudam. · Procura-se uma tradutora.
EN: I hurt myself. · They help each other. · A translator is wanted.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: participant -> relationship -> pronoun form -> position/register

[CARD]
TITLE: The dictionary form in -se must be adapted inside the sentence
REF: 22.2, 22.3.6

A dictionary lists a reflexive verb with se attached to the infinitive, but a sentence must match that pronoun to its subject and conjugate the verb for person, tense, and mood.
TL: arrepender-se -> eu me arrependi · recusar-se -> eles se recusaram
EN: to regret -> I regretted it · to refuse -> they refused
[PAUSE:900]
In a verb phrase, the reflexive pronoun still refers to the subject rather than to the nearest verb. Everyday Brazilian Portuguese often places it immediately before the infinitive.
TL: Você vai se arrepender. · Combinei de me encontrar com ela.
EN: You will regret it. · I arranged to meet her.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: citation -se -> choose me/se/nos by subject · then choose verb form and clitic position
Flip for the contexts in which impersonal se is especially at home.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Signs and formal exposition favor impersonal se
REF: 22.4.2, 22.4.3, 22.4.5

The impersonal construction has a formal tone and is especially common on signs, in advertisements, and in objective academic or scientific writing.
TL: Alugam-se quartos. · Consertam-se sapatos. · Procura-se professor de inglês.
EN: Rooms for rent. · Shoes repaired. · English teacher wanted.
[PAUSE:900]
Informal conversation uses it less broadly, but a few general expressions are completely ordinary and frequent.
TL: Nunca se sabe. · Como se diz isso em português?
EN: You never know. · How do you say that in Portuguese?
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: signs/formal prose -> impersonal se is productive · everyday speech -> common fixed general expressions remain

[CARD]
TITLE: Portuguese reflexives do not map word for word from another language
REF: 22.1, 22.5, 22.6

English may show a self-form where Portuguese prefers a stressed phrase, or omit one where Portuguese keeps an unstressed reflexive. The right choice belongs to the Portuguese verb and meaning pattern.
TL: Eles só pensam neles mesmos. · Ela se chama Lúcia. · Eu me machuquei.
EN: They think only of themselves. · Her name is Lúcia. · I hurt myself.
[PAUSE:900]
Portuguese also uses fewer reflexive verbs in informal speech than closely related Spanish does. Avoid transferring a reflexive pronoun merely because another language has one.
TL: Eu não lembro. · Ela casou no sábado.
EN: I do not remember. · She got married on Saturday.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: translate the relationship, not the visible pronoun · learn Portuguese register and verb pattern
Flip for the final diagnostic.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Diagnose se before deciding how to translate it
REF: 22.2, 22.3, 22.4, 22.5, 22.6

First identify the subject and ask whether the pronoun points back to it. Then distinguish self-directed action, mutual action, change or conventional reflexivity, and a general statement with no named agent.
[PAUSE:850]
Next choose the unstressed or prepositional form, match its person, place it for the intended register, and check whether a formal impersonal construction requires plural agreement.
TL: Ela se feriu. · Elas se abraçaram. · Aqui se fala inglês. · Vendem-se apartamentos.
EN: She hurt herself. · They hugged each other. · English is spoken here. · Apartments are for sale.
[PAUSE:950]
SHOW: subject/reference -> meaning pattern -> pronoun form -> placement/register -> agreement
