## SCRIPT

[CARD]
TITLE: Italian question words divide by the answer they request
REF: 8.1, 8.2, 8.3

Choose the interrogative from the missing information: chi for a person, che cosa, cosa, or che for a thing, perché for a reason, and come for manner or kind.
TL: Chi ha telefonato e che cosa voleva?
EN: Who called, and what did they want?
[PAUSE:900]
Use dove for place, quando for time, quale for identification, and quanto for quantity or degree. Most question words are invariable; quale and adjectival quanto can change form.
TL: Dove andate, quando partite e quante notti restate?
EN: Where are you going, when are you leaving, and how many nights are you staying?
SHOW: person -> chi · thing -> che cosa/cosa/che · reason -> perché · manner -> come · place -> dove · time -> quando · identity -> quale · quantity -> quanto
Flip for the rule English speakers most often miss when a preposition is involved.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Put a governing preposition before the interrogative
REF: 8.1, 8.2

Italian does not leave a preposition at the end of a question. Recover the relationship required by the verb or expression and place that preposition immediately before the question word.
TL: Con chi lavori e di che cosa state parlando?
EN: Who do you work with, and what are you talking about?
[PAUSE:900]
The same order holds in an indirect question. The interrogative phrase stays intact even when a reporting clause comes first.
TL: Vorrei sapere a chi hai scritto.
EN: I would like to know who you wrote to.
SHOW: work with whom -> con chi · talk about what -> di che cosa · write to whom -> a chi · no stranded preposition

[CARD]
TITLE: Yes-or-no questions usually keep statement word order
REF: 8.12

Italian normally marks a yes-or-no question through rising intonation in speech or a question mark in writing, without the auxiliary inversion used in English.
TL: Marta viene domani?
EN: Is Marta coming tomorrow?
[PAUSE:900]
A formal or literary question can place the subject after the verb, but this is a stylistic option rather than the ordinary question-building rule.
TL: Potrà il direttore approvare la proposta?
EN: Will the director be able to approve the proposal?
SHOW: statement Marta viene domani. -> question Marta viene domani? · no English-style do-support
Flip for questions that ask for a specific missing constituent.

[SIDE]
TITLE: A question phrase normally stands next to its verb
REF: 8.12

In a direct information question, the interrogative element is normally followed immediately by the verb, so an expressed subject usually comes after that verb.
TL: Dove sono andati i tuoi amici?
EN: Where did your friends go?
[PAUSE:900]
Perché and per quale motivo can be followed by subject plus verb. A detached topic may also stand before the interrogative phrase.
TL: Perché Luca non ha risposto?
EN: Why did Luca not answer?
SHOW: dove + verb + subject · quanto + noun + verb + subject · perché + subject + verb is possible

[CARD]
TITLE: Chi asks about people; che cosa asks about things
REF: 8.2, 8.3

Use invariant chi when the unknown participant is human. It can be the subject or another complement, and any required preposition comes before it.
TL: Chi ti ha invitato e da chi hai ricevuto il messaggio?
EN: Who invited you, and who did you receive the message from?
[PAUSE:900]
Use che cosa, cosa, or che for a non-human unknown. Cosa is especially common in much of central and northern Italy, while che is more common in the south.
TL: Che cosa è successo? Cosa vuoi?
EN: What happened? What do you want?
SHOW: unknown person -> chi · unknown thing -> che cosa/cosa/che · governed complement -> preposition first
Twenty-two chi-versus-che-cosa completions follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Bare che must remain immediately before the verb
REF: 8.3

When che means what as a pronoun, it normally stands directly before the verb; an attached clitic may intervene. Che cosa and cosa allow more material between the question word and the verb.
TL: Che vuoi? Che gli hai detto?
EN: What do you want? What did you tell him?
[PAUSE:900]
If negation must intervene, prefer che cosa or cosa. The restriction does not apply when a preposition comes before che.
TL: Che cosa non capisci? Di che non sei sicuro?
EN: What do you not understand? What are you not sure about?
SHOW: che + verb · che + clitic + verb · che cosa/cosa + non + verb · preposition + che is allowed

[CARD]
TITLE: Come asks how or what something is like
REF: 8.6

Use come for manner: how something happens, how an action is performed, or what method is used.
TL: Come hai risolto il problema?
EN: How did you solve the problem?
[PAUSE:900]
English what is ... like also calls for come, not che cosa. The same word appears in the fixed question for a person's name.
TL: Com'è il nuovo ufficio e come si chiama il direttore?
EN: What is the new office like, and what is the director called?
SHOW: manner -> come · what ... like -> come · name -> come si chiama?

[SIDE]
TITLE: Perché asks why; come mai makes the surprise stronger
REF: 8.4, 8.9

Use perché or the fuller per quale motivo to ask for a reason. Perché can introduce both direct and indirect reason questions.
TL: Perché sei partito presto? Non so perché sia partito.
EN: Why did you leave early? I do not know why he left.
[PAUSE:900]
Come mai means how come or why on earth, not however. It commonly signals surprise at the situation.
TL: Come mai siete ancora qui?
EN: How come you are still here?
SHOW: neutral reason -> perché/per quale motivo · surprised reason -> come mai · manner or kind -> come

[CARD]
TITLE: Quale identifies; quanto measures
REF: 8.5, 8.8

Quale asks which one or requests a specific identity. It has singular quale and plural quali, and is often shortened to qual before a form of essere beginning with e.
TL: Quale autobus prendi? Quali fermate conosci? Qual era il numero?
EN: Which bus are you taking? Which stops do you know? What was the number?
[PAUSE:900]
Do not write an apostrophe in qual è or qual era. Qual is a shortened form, not an elision marked by an apostrophe.
TL: Qual è la risposta corretta?
EN: What is the correct answer?
SHOW: singular -> quale · plural -> quali · before è/era -> qual · never qual'
Fourteen quale-and-quanto inflections follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Adjectival quanto agrees; adverbial quanto is invariant
REF: 8.8

When quanto modifies a noun, it agrees as quanto, quanta, quanti, or quante. It asks how much or how many of that noun.
TL: Quanta acqua serve e quanti bicchieri preparo?
EN: How much water is needed, and how many glasses should I prepare?
[PAUSE:900]
When quanto modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb, it is invariant. Before a vowel it may lose its final vowel, especially in quant'è.
TL: Quanto costa e quanto velocemente arriva?
EN: How much does it cost, and how quickly does it arrive?
SHOW: quanto + noun -> agree · quanto + verb/adjective/adverb -> invariant · quanto è -> often quant'è

[CARD]
TITLE: Quale seeks identity; che cosa can seek an explanation
REF: 8.3, 8.5

Use quale when the answer identifies or selects an item, often by a name, a demonstrative, or pointing. English what can still correspond to quale in this job.
TL: Qual è la capitale del Portogallo? È Lisbona.
EN: What is the capital of Portugal? It is Lisbon.
[PAUSE:900]
Use che cosa, cosa, or che when the answer defines or explains what something is. Context can allow overlap, so attend to the answer the speaker is requesting.
TL: Che cos'è un arcipelago? È un gruppo di isole.
EN: What is an archipelago? It is a group of islands.
SHOW: identify/select -> quale/qual · define/explain -> che cosa/cosa/che · answer type decides
Eleven identification-versus-definition choices follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Che can also modify a noun to ask which or what kind
REF: 8.5

Before a noun, che is an invariable interrogative adjective. It may ask for a selection or characterize the kind of thing under discussion.
TL: Che libro stai leggendo? Che musica ascolti di solito?
EN: What book are you reading? What music do you usually listen to?
[PAUSE:900]
Quale also modifies a noun but changes to quali in the plural. Prefer it when the alternatives are being identified as members of a known set.
TL: Quali documenti devo firmare?
EN: Which documents do I have to sign?
SHOW: che + noun -> invariant · quale + singular noun · quali + plural noun · known alternatives -> often quale

[CARD]
TITLE: Dove covers location and destination
REF: 8.7

Use dove both for where something is and where someone is going. Italian does not need a separate equivalent of English where to.
TL: Dove abiti e dove vai dopo il lavoro?
EN: Where do you live, and where are you going after work?
[PAUSE:900]
Add a preposition when the relation requires one: da or di for origin, fino a for an endpoint, and da for a starting time with quando.
TL: Da dove vieni e fino a dove arriva questo treno?
EN: Where do you come from, and how far does this train go?
SHOW: location/destination -> dove · origin -> da/di dove · endpoint -> fino a dove · time -> quando
Fourteen dove-or-quanto completions follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Quanto can ask quantity, duration, or degree
REF: 8.8

Use agreeing quanto before a noun and invariant quanto with a verb, adjective, or adverb. The noun can name a quantity of substance, a count, or a span of time.
TL: Quanto tempo rimane e quante persone aspettano?
EN: How much time remains, and how many people are waiting?
[PAUSE:900]
With an adjective or adverb, quanto asks to what degree. Do not make it agree with the subject in that use.
TL: Quanto è lontana la stazione?
EN: How far away is the station?
SHOW: how much time -> quanto tempo · how many people -> quante persone · how far -> quanto + adjective

[CARD]
TITLE: Mai, altro, and come mai reinforce a question
REF: 8.9

Add mai after a question word to express on earth or ever. Come mai has the specialized meaning how come or why on earth.
TL: Che mai sta facendo? Dove mai saranno andati?
EN: What on earth is he doing? Wherever can they have gone?
[PAUSE:900]
Use altro after the question word for else. Stronger colloquial words such as cavolo or diavolo exist, but they carry an informal tone.
TL: Chi altro viene e dove altro possiamo cercare?
EN: Who else is coming, and where else can we look?
SHOW: on earth/ever -> question word + mai · why on earth -> come mai · else -> question word + altro
Twelve mixed translations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Questions can use niente or nessuno with a non-negative meaning
REF: 8.10, 8.11

In colloquial questions, niente and nessuno can mean anything and anybody. Use qualcosa or qualcuno when the expected meaning is the more positive something or someone.
TL: Hai visto nessuno? C'è niente da bere?
EN: Have you seen anybody? Is there anything to drink?
[PAUSE:900]
What about plus a noun is often simply E plus that noun. For a proposed action, use che ne dici or che ne dite di plus the infinitive.
TL: E il preventivo? Che ne dite di parlarne domani?
EN: What about the estimate? What about discussing it tomorrow?
SHOW: anybody/anything in a colloquial question -> nessuno/niente · what about a noun -> E ...? · what about doing -> Che ne dici di + infinitive?

[CARD]
TITLE: Indirect yes-or-no questions begin with se
REF: 8.12

To embed a question whose answer is yes or no, introduce it with se. There is no equivalent of English auxiliary inversion inside the indirect question.
TL: Partiranno domani? Mi chiedo se partiranno domani.
EN: Will they leave tomorrow? I wonder whether they will leave tomorrow.
[PAUSE:900]
Se can also introduce two stated alternatives or an infinitive question. Mood and tense follow the meaning and the governing construction.
TL: Non so se aspettare o partire.
EN: I do not know whether to wait or leave.
SHOW: direct yes/no question -> ordinary question order · indirect yes/no question -> se + clause · alternatives -> se ... o ...
Twelve direct-to-indirect and indirect-to-direct transformations follow.

[SIDE]
TITLE: Indirect information questions retain their question phrase
REF: 8.12

When the original question contains chi, che cosa, perché, dove, quando, come, quale, or quanto, keep that interrogative phrase after the reporting expression. Do not add se.
TL: Dove abita? Non so dove abiti.
EN: Where does she live? I do not know where she lives.
[PAUSE:900]
Turning an indirect question back into a direct one removes the reporting frame and restores direct-question punctuation and intonation. The interrogative still remains beside its verb.
TL: Vorrei sapere quanto costa. Quanto costa?
EN: I would like to know how much it costs. How much does it cost?
SHOW: yes/no indirect -> se · information indirect -> retain question phrase · direct question -> question mark and question intonation
