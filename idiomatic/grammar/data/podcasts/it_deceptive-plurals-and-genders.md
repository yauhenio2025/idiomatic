## SCRIPT

Italian nouns look as if they advertise their gender and number on the final vowel. Often they do. Then a problem arrives, ends in a, and turns out to be masculine. A city refuses to change at all in the plural. An egg begins masculine and becomes feminine when there are two of it. The endings have not really lied; we asked a spelling pattern to carry more information than it can.

This topic comes directly from your current self-report: articles, gender, and especially gendered plural endings are a weakness. The old Italian corpus is too thin for frequency claims, but it contains one verbatim agreement error that is almost a perfect miniature of the system. The recorded form was:

TL: un rapporto sulle città intellegente

The target was:

TL: un rapporto sulle città intelligenti

There is a spelling correction inside the adjective, but the structural issue is number agreement. The noun does not change from singular to plural, yet the plural contraction and the adjective reveal that several cities are meant.

The solution is to stop storing a noun as one naked citation form. Store a three-part identity: the singular phrase, the plural phrase, and one agreeing neighbor. For example:

TL: il problema; i problemi; problemi complessi

TL: la città; le città; città intelligenti

TL: l'uovo; le uova; uova fresche

That package holds gender, article shape, plural, and agreement. It also keeps nearby Romance languages from supplying an article or plural by reflex.

Let us begin with the regular system, because exceptions only make sense against a strong default. Most masculine nouns in o change to i.

TL: il libro; i libri

Most feminine nouns in a change to e.

TL: la ricerca; le ricerche

Nouns in e can be masculine or feminine, but both normally change to i.

TL: il paese; i paesi

TL: la legge; le leggi

The e-class is an early warning: the singular ending does not determine gender. You must know whether the noun takes the masculine or feminine article, even though the plural ending is shared.

The article is not decoration. For masculine nouns, use the ordinary pair before most consonants.

TL: il libro; i libri

Use the special pair before s plus consonant, z, ps, gn, and a few similar onsets.

TL: lo stato; gli stati

TL: lo psicologo; gli psicologi

TL: lo gnocco; gli gnocchi

Before a vowel, masculine singular elides, while the plural still uses gli.

TL: l'articolo; gli articoli

For feminine nouns, the basic pair is la and le, with singular elision before a vowel.

TL: la legge; le leggi

TL: l'opinione; le opinioni

So every plural decision has two dimensions. What plural form does the noun take, and what article-and-agreement package goes with it?

Here is self-check one. Make the whole phrase plural: “the new system.”

TL: il sistema nuovo

[PAUSE]

TL: i sistemi nuovi

That example opens the first deceptive family: many high-frequency learned nouns in ma are masculine. A useful core is:

TL: il problema, il sistema, il programma, il tema, il clima

Their plurals end in i, and every agreeing word follows masculine plural.

TL: i problemi complessi

TL: due sistemi diversi

TL: nuovi programmi politici

Add other common members as complete packages.

TL: il panorama; i panorami

TL: il diploma; i diplomi

TL: il diagramma; i diagrammi

Many of these came into Italian through a Greek neuter class, which helps explain the surprising masculine a. But history is a memory aid, not a live suffix rule. Not every noun ending in ma is masculine, and not every masculine noun in a belongs to this learned family. Store the core nouns with their articles.

The practical repair is not just changing la to il. Gender radiates across the phrase.

TL: un problema serio

TL: questo sistema complesso

TL: due programmi nuovi

If you repair only the article and leave the adjective feminine, the noun phrase still disagrees with itself.

There are also masculine nouns in a outside the ma family, often referring to people.

TL: il poeta; i poeti

But human reference can make gender depend on the person and the lexical pattern, so do not turn “final a means feminine” into a law with a list of exceptions taped onto it. Treat it as a productive default with lexical identities stored on top.

Now reverse the trap. A few important nouns ending in o are feminine.

TL: la mano; le mani

The plural form mani looks like an ordinary i-plural, but agreement stays feminine.

TL: le mani fredde

Several common shortened words are also feminine and, unlike mano, invariant.

TL: la foto; le foto

TL: la radio; le radio

TL: la moto; le moto

TL: l'auto; le auto

The longer source words fotografia, motocicletta, and automobile help reinforce the gender of three abbreviations; radio is conventionally feminine in standard Italian. Etymology can support memory, but current article usage is the fact you need to retrieve.

Self-check two. Say, “Her hands are cold.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Le sue mani sono fredde.

Now the nouns that refuse to mark number on themselves. Final-stressed nouns are normally invariant.

TL: la città; le città

TL: l'università; le università

TL: il caffè; i caffè

The accent is not an ornament here. It helps identify a class whose final form stays fixed.

Many borrowed nouns ending in a consonant are invariant too.

TL: il film; i film

TL: il computer; i computer

TL: il podcast; i podcast

TL: lo smartphone; gli smartphone

Several feminine nouns ending in i are invariant.

TL: la crisi; le crisi

TL: l'analisi; le analisi

TL: la tesi; le tesi

TL: la sintesi; le sintesi

And two high-frequency e-nouns are invariant despite resembling the regular e-to-i class.

TL: la serie; le serie

TL: la specie; le specie

When the noun is invariant, number has not disappeared. It has moved into the article, the adjective, the verb, or the wider context.

TL: La città italiana cresce.

TL: Le città italiane crescono.

That is exactly what the recorded error tests. The form città cannot rescue you; you must follow the plural signal already present in the phrase.

TL: sulle città intelligenti

Prepositional contractions are part of the same article system. The article may be fused into a word such as del, dei, della, delle, sul, or sulle, but its gender and number information is still active.

TL: del problema; dei problemi

TL: della città; delle città

TL: sul programma; sui programmi

TL: sulla crisi; sulle crisi

This is especially useful with invariant nouns. In the recorded phrase, sulle already announces feminine plural before the unchanged noun arrives. The adjective must keep listening to that signal.

TL: sulle città intelligenti

TL: sulle crisi economiche

Do not restart the gender decision at the noun's last letter. Carry the features established by the article or contraction through the rest of the phrase.

Here is self-check three. Make this whole sentence plural: “The analysis is detailed.”

TL: L'analisi è dettagliata.

[PAUSE]

TL: Le analisi sono dettagliate.

The most theatrical group changes grammatical gender between singular and plural. Start with the indispensable example.

TL: l'uovo fresco

The singular is masculine. The ordinary plural is feminine and ends in a.

TL: le uova fresche

You therefore change not only the noun but also the article and adjective. The same historical pattern survives in several quantity and collective nouns.

TL: il paio; le paia

TL: il migliaio; le migliaia

TL: il centinaio; le centinaia

TL: il lenzuolo; le lenzuola

These are not invitations to guess that any masculine o-noun can make a feminine a-plural. They are a closed, high-value set. Learn the singular and plural as a contrast.

Self-check four. Say, “I bought six fresh eggs.” Make every word agree.

[PAUSE]

TL: Ho comprato sei uova fresche.

Body parts add meaning to the choice. Several masculine singular nouns have a common feminine plural in a when the parts are understood together as parts of a body or a natural set.

TL: il braccio; le braccia

TL: il dito; le dita

TL: il ginocchio; le ginocchia

TL: l'osso; le ossa

For ordinary human anatomy, these are the defaults to make automatic.

TL: Mi fanno male le braccia.

TL: Ha incrociato le dita.

TL: Si è fatto male alle ginocchia.

TL: Le ossa dello scheletro.

But some of these nouns also retain a masculine i-plural in special readings. The contrast is clearest with braccio.

TL: le braccia di una persona

TL: i bracci di una gru

The feminine plural is normal for human arms; the masculine plural is normal for mechanical arms and extensions. With osso, the feminine plural readily evokes the body's bones as a set, while the masculine form can treat bones as separate objects, including animal bones.

TL: le ossa del corpo

TL: gli ossi per il cane

With dito, the ordinary collective body plural is dita. The masculine diti exists in restricted contexts that single out individual fingers or types, but it is not the default you need in normal bodily descriptions. Ginocchia is likewise the everyday body plural; competing forms are marked or much less useful for routine speech.

This is why a one-word flashcard asking only for “the plural of braccio” can be badly designed. Meaning must force the answer. Is the sentence about a person's arms or a crane's arms? The plural is partly morphology and partly lexical meaning.

Here is self-check five. Say, “The robot has two mechanical arms.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Il robot ha due bracci meccanici.

There are spelling changes that look irregular but simply preserve sound. A feminine noun in ca or ga usually inserts h before the plural e.

TL: la banca; le banche

TL: la ricerca; le ricerche

Some masculine nouns in co or go take chi or ghi, while others take ci or gi; vocabulary history and stress matter, so this area is not safely predictable from the last two letters alone.

TL: il lago; i laghi

TL: l'amico; gli amici

TL: il medico; i medici

These are good examples of where disciplined lexical memory beats an overconfident rule. The broad ending system gets you most of the way; a compact exception package gets you the rest.

Here is the spoken routine to use when a noun feels suspicious. First, retrieve the singular with its article. Second, ask whether it belongs to a known class: masculine learned a, feminine o, invariant, or gender-changing a-plural. Third, retrieve the plural article and noun together. Fourth, propagate gender and number to adjective, determiner, participle, and verb where required. Finally, check whether meaning selects a special plural, as with body parts.

You can practice that routine without reciting disconnected lists. Put each noun in a sentence where number matters.

TL: Questo problema è complesso.

TL: Questi problemi sono complessi.

TL: Questa città è cambiata.

TL: Queste città sono cambiate.

TL: Il braccio del bambino è ferito.

TL: Le braccia del bambino sono ferite.

The noun phrase then becomes a network of mutually supporting cues instead of a final vowel followed by a guess.

Sixty-second recap. Start from the regular defaults: masculine o normally becomes i, feminine a becomes e, and either-gender e normally becomes i. But store every noun as a package: singular article, plural article and noun, plus one agreeing neighbor. Core learned nouns in ma are masculine and take i plurals: il problema, i problemi. Mano is feminine: la mano, le mani. Final-stressed nouns, many loans, and nouns such as crisi or analisi are invariant, so the article and agreement carry number. A small set changes from masculine singular to feminine plural: l'uovo, le uova; il paio, le paia. For body parts, meaning matters: human le braccia contrasts with mechanical i bracci, while le dita, le ginocchia, and le ossa are the everyday body plurals. Retrieve the package, choose the meaning, and make every neighboring word agree.
