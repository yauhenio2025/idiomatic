## SCRIPT

Italian nouns look as if they advertise their gender and number on the final vowel. Often they do. Then the noun for a problem arrives with the ending you expect on a feminine noun and turns out to be masculine. A city refuses to change at all in the plural. An egg begins masculine and becomes feminine when it becomes plural. The endings have not really lied; we asked a spelling pattern to carry more information than it can.

This topic comes directly from your current self-report: articles, gender, and especially gendered plural endings are a weakness. The old Italian corpus is too thin for frequency claims, but it contains one verbatim agreement error that is almost a perfect miniature of the system. The recorded form was:

TL: un rapporto sulle città intellegente

The target was:

TL: un rapporto sulle città intelligenti

There is a spelling correction inside the adjective, but the structural issue is number agreement. The noun for city does not change from singular to plural, yet the plural contraction and the adjective reveal that several cities are meant.

The solution is to stop storing a noun as one naked citation form. Store a three-part identity: the singular phrase, the plural phrase, and one agreeing neighbor. For example:

TL: il problema; i problemi; problemi complessi

TL: la città; le città; città intelligenti

TL: l'uovo; le uova; uova fresche

That package holds gender, article shape, plural, and agreement. It also keeps nearby Romance languages from supplying an article or plural by reflex.

Let us begin with the regular system, because exceptions only make sense against a strong default. Most masculine nouns follow the change heard in this pair.

TL: il libro; i libri

Most feminine nouns follow the change heard in the next pair.

TL: la ricerca; le ricerche

Nouns in a third common singular class can be masculine or feminine, but both normally share the same plural ending.

TL: il paese; i paesi

TL: la legge; le leggi

This third class is an early warning: the singular ending does not determine gender. You must know whether the noun takes the masculine or feminine article, even though the plural ending is shared.

The article is not decoration. For masculine nouns, use the ordinary pair before most consonants.

TL: il libro; i libri

Use the special pair before the consonant patterns heard at the start of the next four examples, and before a few similar onsets.

TL: lo stato; gli stati

TL: lo zaino; gli zaini

TL: lo psicologo; gli psicologi

TL: lo gnocco; gli gnocchi

Before a vowel, masculine singular elides, while the plural still uses the special plural article.

TL: l'articolo; gli articoli

For feminine nouns, the basic article pair appears in the next example, with singular elision before a vowel as shown after it.

TL: la legge; le leggi

TL: l'opinione; le opinioni

So every plural decision has two dimensions. What plural form does the noun take, and what article-and-agreement package goes with it?

Here is self-check one. Make the whole phrase plural: “the new system.”

TL: il nuovo sistema

[PAUSE]

TL: i nuovi sistemi

That example opens the first deceptive family: many high-frequency learned nouns sharing the same final syllable are masculine. A useful core is:

TL: il problema, il sistema, il programma, il tema, il clima

Their plurals change as shown next, and every agreeing word follows masculine plural.

TL: i problemi complessi

TL: due sistemi diversi

TL: nuovi programmi politici

Add other common members as complete packages.

TL: il panorama; i panorami

TL: il diploma; i diplomi

TL: il diagramma; i diagrammi

Many of these came into Italian through a Greek neuter class, which helps explain the surprising masculine gender despite the final vowel. But history is a memory aid, not a live suffix rule. Not every noun sharing that final syllable is masculine, and not every masculine noun with that final vowel belongs to this learned family. Store the core nouns with their articles.

The practical repair is not just changing a feminine article to a masculine one. Gender radiates across the phrase.

TL: un problema serio

TL: questo sistema complesso

TL: due programmi nuovi

If you repair only the article and leave the adjective feminine, the noun phrase still disagrees with itself.

There are also masculine nouns with the same final vowel outside that learned family, often referring to people.

TL: il poeta; i poeti

But human reference can make gender depend on the person and the lexical pattern, so do not turn “the final vowel determines gender” into a law with a list of exceptions taped onto it. Treat the usual ending pattern as a productive default with lexical identities stored on top.

Now reverse the trap. A few important nouns with the ending usually associated with masculine nouns are feminine.

TL: la mano; le mani

The plural form in the preceding line looks regular, but agreement stays feminine.

TL: le mani fredde

Several common shortened words are also feminine and, unlike the preceding noun, invariant.

TL: la foto; le foto

TL: la radio; le radio

TL: la moto; le moto

TL: l'auto; le auto

The longer source words help reinforce the gender of three abbreviations:

TL: fotografia; motocicletta; automobile

The remaining shortened noun is conventionally feminine in standard Italian. Etymology can support memory, but current article usage is the fact you need to retrieve.

Self-check two. Say, “Her hands are cold.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Le sue mani sono fredde.

Now the nouns that refuse to mark number on themselves. Final-stressed nouns are normally invariant.

TL: la città; le città

TL: l'università; le università

TL: il caffè; i caffè

The accent is not an ornament here. It helps identify a class whose final form stays fixed.

Many borrowed nouns ending in a consonant are invariant too.

TL: il film; i film

TL: il computer; i computer

TL: il podcast; i podcast

TL: lo smartphone; gli smartphone

Several feminine nouns with a final vowel that often signals the plural are invariant.

TL: la crisi; le crisi

TL: l'analisi; le analisi

TL: la tesi; le tesi

TL: la sintesi; le sintesi

And two high-frequency nouns in the otherwise regular third class are invariant.

TL: la serie; le serie

TL: la specie; le specie

When the noun is invariant, number has not disappeared. It has moved into the article, the adjective, the verb, or the wider context.

TL: La città italiana cresce.

TL: Le città italiane crescono.

That is exactly what the recorded error tests. The unchanged noun form cannot rescue you; you must follow the plural signal already present in the phrase.

TL: sulle città intelligenti

Prepositional contractions are part of the same article system. The article may be fused into forms like these, but its gender and number information is still active.

TL: del; dei; della; delle; sul; sulle

TL: del problema; dei problemi

TL: della città; delle città

TL: sul programma; sui programmi

TL: sulla crisi; sulle crisi

This is especially useful with invariant nouns. In the recorded phrase, the contraction already announces feminine plural before the unchanged noun arrives. The adjective must keep listening to that signal.

TL: sulle città intelligenti

TL: sulle crisi economiche

Do not restart the gender decision at the noun's last letter. Carry the features established by the article or contraction through the rest of the phrase.

Here is self-check three. Make this whole sentence plural: “The analysis is detailed.”

TL: L'analisi è dettagliata.

[PAUSE]

TL: Le analisi sono dettagliate.

The most theatrical group changes grammatical gender between singular and plural. Start with the indispensable example.

TL: l'uovo fresco

The singular is masculine. The ordinary plural changes gender as well as its ending.

TL: le uova fresche

You therefore change not only the noun but also the article and adjective. The same historical pattern survives in several quantity and collective nouns.

TL: il paio; le paia

TL: il migliaio; le migliaia

TL: il centinaio; le centinaia

TL: il lenzuolo; le lenzuola

For this noun, the feminine plural has a collective reading: it refers to the sheets used together to make a bed, typically the pair. Use the masculine plural when counting sheets as separate items.

TL: i lenzuoli

These are not invitations to extend the pattern to any masculine noun. They are a closed, high-value set. Learn the singular and plural as a contrast.

Self-check four. Say, “I bought six fresh eggs.” Make every word agree.

[PAUSE]

TL: Ho comprato sei uova fresche.

Body parts add meaning to the choice. Several masculine singular nouns have a common feminine plural when the parts are understood together as parts of a body or a natural set.

TL: il braccio; le braccia

TL: il dito; le dita

TL: il ginocchio; le ginocchia

TL: l'osso; le ossa

For ordinary human anatomy, these are the defaults to make automatic.

TL: Mi fanno male le braccia.

TL: Ha incrociato le dita.

TL: Si è fatto male alle ginocchia.

TL: Le ossa dello scheletro.

But some of these nouns also retain a masculine plural in special readings. The contrast is clearest in the first pair below.

TL: le braccia di una persona

TL: i bracci di una gru

The feminine plural is normal for human arms; the masculine plural is normal for mechanical arms and extensions. In the second pair, the feminine plural readily evokes the body's bones as a set, while the masculine form can treat bones as separate objects, including animal bones.

TL: le ossa del corpo

TL: gli ossi per il cane

For fingers, the ordinary collective body plural and the marked separate-item plural are:

TL: le dita; i diti

The masculine form singles out fingers considered separately, as in named finger types, but it is not the default in normal bodily descriptions. For knees, both of these plural forms exist:

TL: le ginocchia; i ginocchi

The feminine form is the safest everyday choice for human knees. Unlike the two finger plurals, an individual-versus-collective distinction is not reliable here, and the uses of the two knee plurals overlap.

This is why a one-word flashcard asking only for the plural of an ambiguous body-part noun can be badly designed. Meaning must force the answer. Is the sentence about a person's arms or a crane's arms? The plural is partly morphology and partly lexical meaning.

Here is self-check five. Say, “The robot has two mechanical arms.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Il robot ha due bracci meccanici.

There are spelling changes that look irregular but simply preserve sound. In a common feminine pattern, the plural spelling inserts a letter to preserve the hard consonant heard in the singular.

TL: la banca; le banche

TL: la ricerca; le ricerche

For corresponding masculine nouns, plural spelling is less predictable: some preserve the hard consonant while others switch to a soft one. Vocabulary history and stress matter, so this area is not safely predictable from the last two letters alone.

TL: il lago; i laghi

TL: l'amico; gli amici

TL: il medico; i medici

These are good examples of where disciplined lexical memory beats an overconfident rule. The broad ending system gets you most of the way; a compact exception package gets you the rest.

Here is the spoken routine to use when a noun feels suspicious. First, retrieve the singular with its article. Second, ask whether it belongs to a known class: the learned masculine family, a feminine exception family, an invariant class, or a gender-changing plural. Third, retrieve the plural article and noun together. Fourth, propagate gender and number to adjective, determiner, participle, and verb where required. Finally, check whether meaning selects a special plural, as with body parts.

You can practice that routine without reciting disconnected lists. Put each noun in a sentence where number matters.

TL: Questo problema è complesso.

TL: Questi problemi sono complessi.

TL: Questa città è cambiata.

TL: Queste città sono cambiate.

TL: Il braccio del bambino è ferito.

TL: Le braccia del bambino sono ferite.

The noun phrase then becomes a network of mutually supporting cues instead of a final vowel followed by a guess.

## 60-SECOND RECAP

Sixty-second recap. Start from the regular defaults, heard in these three pairs:

TL: il libro; i libri. La ricerca; le ricerche. Il paese; i paesi.

But store every noun as a package: singular article, plural article and noun, plus one agreeing neighbor. The learned family illustrated by the next pair is masculine; the noun for hand is feminine.

TL: il problema; i problemi. La mano; le mani.

Final-stressed nouns, many loans, and several nouns whose last vowel resembles a plural ending are invariant, so article and agreement carry number. A small set changes from masculine singular to feminine plural.

TL: l'uovo; le uova. Il paio; le paia.

For body parts, meaning matters: human and mechanical arms contrast.

TL: le braccia; i bracci.

The ordinary body plurals also include these forms:

TL: le dita; le ginocchia; le ossa.

Retrieve the package, choose the meaning, and make every neighboring word agree.
