## SCRIPT

Imagine four radio stations broadcasting from neighboring cities. They share some music, some presenters, and a great deal of vocabulary. You want Spanish, but a Portuguese word reaches the studio first. You want French, but Italian quietly edits the sentence. The result can feel like a failure to keep four languages in four sealed rooms. That diagnosis is wrong. Your brain is doing something useful too enthusiastically: it is exploiting similarity.

This walk is about how to keep the benefit and reduce the collisions. We will use your actual error record, but we will also keep the evidence honest. The Spanish profile contains sixty-two verbatim error pairs; twenty-six show cross-language or English interference. The much larger Portuguese error set classifies five hundred and eighty-eight of one thousand and ninety-eight errors as Romance interference. Those percentages are not directly comparable, because the teachers and capture systems differed. They do agree on the pattern: when a close relative offers a plausible word or structure, it often wins the retrieval race.

That race is not evidence that the languages are stored as soup. Multilingual speakers can choose one language while several remain partly active. A familiar-looking candidate receives a head start. Close relatives give one another especially strong starts because their sounds, meanings, and grammatical categories overlap. That is transfer. Transfer lets you understand a new headline, guess a cognate, and recognize a subjunctive construction without beginning from zero. The very system that accelerates comprehension can also produce a form that is legal in the wrong language.

Your clearest Spanish example is a software verb. Portuguese offers this perfectly good form:

TL: [pt] inserir

Spanish wants:

TL: [es] insertar

The intrusion was recorded twice, and the Spanish target was taught or retaught sixteen times from 2019 through 2024. That is not a random typo. Software is a familiar domain, so the meaning arrives quickly; the wrong language wins precisely because the concept is easy. High confidence is not always a sign that the form has been checked.

The useful response is not to distrust every cognate. It is to notice the small hinges that distinguish families. Portuguese exemplo and Spanish ejemplo differ by one consonant. Portuguese longo and Spanish largo share a history and a meaning neighborhood, but not the modern word. Portuguese conosco is one compact pronoun; Spanish says con nosotros. Store these as contrast pairs, not as isolated corrections.

TL: [es] por ejemplo

TL: [pt] trabalhar conosco

The label matters. If you merely repeat exemplo, ejemplo, exemplo, ejemplo, you may create a rhythm without a language decision. Give each form an address. Portuguese: exemplo. Spanish: ejemplo. The act of choosing the address is part of the memory.

Portuguese shows the same traffic in the other direction, and the scale is larger in the recorded data. Spanish todavía means still. Portuguese todavia, without the accent, normally means however. In the attested error, Spanish supplied todavia when Portuguese needed ainda. The correction was not a spelling adjustment; it was a change of word.

Contento is good Spanish and Italian; the recorded Portuguese repair is contente. Fato is a fact, while feito is done or a deed. Spanish and Italian make hecho and fatto feel dangerously available. Each collision has a different cure, so there is no general sound rule that can rescue you at the last second.

French adds another kind of border accident: the invented pan-Romance word. The record includes incluir, insérir, gestir, mostrer, and other forms that look as if a committee of Romance languages approved them. They are intelligible. They are also not the requested French verbs. Italian leaves structural fingerprints too. Missing pas after ne resembles Italian non. Using que as a subject relative is tempting when Italian che covers work divided between French qui and que.

TL: [fr] Ce n'était pas très simple.

TL: [fr] des partis qui critiquent le gouvernement

This is a useful distinction: sometimes interference imports a complete word; sometimes it imports a blueprint. A word intrusion sounds like inserir in Spanish. A blueprint intrusion preserves the foreign relationship while filling it with local material. French je déjà connais follows an English-like adverb slot even though every word is French. Portuguese eu vou a passar inserts the Spanish bridge a into an otherwise Portuguese future. Those errors survive spell-checkers because the pieces look legitimate.

Italian has the thinnest historical record, so we should not pretend to possess robust frequency estimates. The eleven recorded errors come from one lesson. Still, several are clean demonstrations of the same mechanism. Spanish columna entered Italian where colonna was required. French chercher à appears to have helped produce cercare a, but Italian requires cercare di. Spanish participar en or English participate in may have pushed partecipare in, while Italian uses partecipare a.

TL: [it] Cerco di mettere il problema nel contesto.

TL: [it] Partecipo a un convegno.

So should you separate the languages completely? Here the research gives a more interesting answer than folk advice. In a 2025 study by Pan and colleagues, learners practiced conjugation across seven tenses in two Romance languages. Interleaved practice improved learning across similar and dissimilar tense pairs. In Experiment Four, mixing two Romance languages improved the learners' ability to identify which language a form belonged to. Mixing did not simply make the languages melt together; under those experimental conditions, it trained discrimination.

That finding has boundaries. The study concerned two languages and controlled morphology practice, not four-language conversation, not every grammar system, and not an exact prescription for what to study on the same day. The research synthesis found no controlled evidence for a universal same-day schedule for cognate languages. So the honest conclusion is narrower: deliberate contrast can help you distinguish close alternatives. It does not prove that maximal chaos is optimal.

Why can interleaving work? One explanation is discriminative contrast. If you do twenty Portuguese items in a block, the language decision is made once at the beginning. Item twenty asks mainly, can you retrieve a Portuguese form? If Portuguese and Spanish alternate, every item asks two questions: which language is active, and which form belongs to it? The harder schedule practices the border itself.

Spacing is another part of the effect. Alternating items prevents one form from being repeated in a warm, recently activated loop. You must retrieve it after a small interruption. And the practice resembles the real problem. Your failures do not occur because you cannot produce inserir in a Portuguese block. They occur when several languages are available and you must select one. Practice that includes selection better matches the eventual task.

This creates a desirable difficulty. Interleaved practice often feels worse. You hesitate more and make more errors during training. Earlier research on Spanish preterite and imperfect found that blocking could look better during practice while interleaving did better on delayed tests. Immediate fluency can therefore be misleading. The goal is not a beautiful practice session; it is a cleaner decision tomorrow.

There is still a place for blocking. When a form is genuinely new, give it a short protected introduction. Hear it, understand it, and produce it a few times. Then bring in its competitor. For you, most of these systems are not new. The bottleneck is selecting among familiar relatives, so long protected blocks often remove the very decision you need to automate.

Grammar provides an even stronger contrast than vocabulary. Consider a future event after when. Portuguese uses the future subjunctive. Spanish normally uses the present subjunctive. Italian and French can use the future indicative. The shared meaning conceals four different instructions.

TL: [pt] Quando eu voltar, vou ajustar os cartões.

TL: [es] Cuando vuelva, voy a ajustar las tarjetas.

TL: [it] Quando tornerò, sistemerò le schede.

TL: [fr] Quand je rentrerai, je modifierai les cartes.

Do not memorize that as four translations to admire. Hear the hinge: voltar, vuelva, tornerò, rentrerai. The time clause is where the languages announce themselves.

Fourth self-check. You are speaking Portuguese about an open future event. Complete: quando eu blank, vou ajustar os cartões. The verb is voltar.

TL: [pt] Quando eu voltar, vou ajustar os cartões.

Now build a practical routine from the science and your evidence. First, name the target language before you retrieve. Not after the error: before the sentence. Second, contrast one meaning at a time. Put inserir beside insertar because they compete for the same job. Third, mark the hinge in your attention: the consonant, preposition, article, or mood that identifies the language. Fourth, retrieve inside a meaningful sentence. Mechanical chanting can preserve a paradigm without training selection. Fifth, attempt the answer before hearing it. Silent retrieval is useful; the strategy evidence indicates that covert recall can support retention, which suits a walk. Sixth, make feedback specific. Say Portuguese future subjunctive after a pending quando clause, not merely wrong tense.

One more self-check. You are speaking French about returning tomorrow. Complete this future time clause: Quand je blank demain. Use rentrer.

[PAUSE]

TL: [fr] Quand je rentrerai demain.

There is also an emotional adjustment here. A mixed form is not proof that studying several languages was a mistake. Often it is evidence of a large, highly connected Romance network. The task is to add sharper routing. Treat each error as a border sign: this meaning is shared, but this particular road belongs to Portuguese; that preposition belongs to Italian; this relative pronoun tells you the sentence is French.

Here is your sixty-second recap. Four Romance languages activate overlapping words and grammatical plans, so transfer is both your accelerator and your collision risk. Your records show the collisions clearly: inserir entering Spanish, todavía-shaped todavia entering Portuguese, pan-Romance inventions entering French, and columna or the wrong preposition entering Italian. The answer is not total separation. Research by Pan and colleagues shows that deliberate interleaving across two Romance languages can improve both conjugation learning and language identification, though it does not settle every scheduling question. Use a short block only for a truly new form, then practice contrasts. Name the language, retrieve one meaningful sentence, notice the identifying hinge, and explain the correction. If practice feels slightly slower, that may be the border becoming visible. You are not trying to build four sealed rooms. You are teaching one multilingual mind to choose the right door.
