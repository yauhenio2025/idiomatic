## SCRIPT

German adjective endings are a perfect example of knowledge that looks larger on paper than it feels in the language. A grammar book can show three declension tables, four cases, three genders, plural, and a swarm of endings. You see the swarm and postpone the noun phrase. Yet the whole system can be spoken as one decision tree.

The principle is this: a German noun phrase wants one clear public signal of gender, number, and case near its front. If the determiner displays that signal, the adjective can use a quiet ending. If no determiner displays it, the adjective has to speak more loudly. If an ein-type determiner displays only part of it, the adjective fills the gap.

This is the strongest verified German fossil in your record. Six adjective or determiner errors survive across 2019 to 2024, touching all three traditional patterns.

TL: Der härtester Konkurrent

TL: Er hat sehr tiefgründiger Schlüsse daraus gezogen.

TL: Sie haben keinen kohärentes Weltbild.

TL: Meine ultimative Ziel ist, daraus einen Film zu machen.

TL: Es kommen noch zwölf weiterer.

TL: Ich brauche ein größtmöglichstes Publikum.

We will repair every one, but not by assigning each to a separate table. We will send each phrase through the same tree.

The entrance has three questions. What noun is the adjective describing? What role gives that noun its case? What stands to the left of the adjective? Do not begin with the adjective ending. By the time those three questions are answered, the ending is usually the least interesting part.

Take the first recorded phrase.

TL: Der härtester Konkurrent

The noun is Konkurrent, masculine singular. It is presented as the subject, so nominative. To the left stands der, which openly announces masculine nominative. The adjective does not need to repeat the strong er signal. It takes the quiet ending.

TL: der härteste Konkurrent

This is the weak pattern: the determiner already does the identifying work. The adjective normally whispers e or en.

There is a compact way to navigate those two weak endings. After a definite article or another fully marked der-word, use e in five common light positions: all three nominative singular phrases, plus feminine and neuter accusative. Use en everywhere else.

TL: der neue Bericht

TL: die neue Methode

TL: das neue Programm

TL: die neue Methode prüfen

TL: das neue Programm testen

Now the other positions settle on en.

TL: den neuen Bericht

TL: mit der neuen Methode

TL: in dem neuen Programm

TL: die neuen Berichte

TL: mit den neuen Berichten

You do not need to visualize “five boxes” forever. High-frequency phrases make the rhythm automatic: der neue, den neuen, mit dem neuen; die neue, mit der neuen; das neue, mit dem neuen; plural die neuen.

Self-check one. Say, “The new report was convincing.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Der neue Bericht war überzeugend.

Now remove the determiner. The noun phrase still needs a public signal, so the adjective inherits the job. This is the strong pattern.

TL: kritische Analysen

TL: mit kritischen Analysen

In the first phrase, no article tells us that Analysen is plural nominative or accusative. The adjective ending e carries the strong plural signal. In the second, the preposition mit requires dative; the adjective shows em, and the plural noun adds n when possible.

TL: mit kritischen Analysen

The spelling happens to give the adjective en there, not em, because strong dative plural uses en. Compare singular masculine or neuter dative, where the strong signal is unmistakable.

TL: mit kritischem Blick

TL: mit klarem Ziel

This is why the decision must include number and gender rather than “no article means add er.” Strong endings distribute the same kind of information that a der-word would have shown.

Use four nominative anchors.

TL: guter Wein

TL: gute Musik

TL: gutes Essen

TL: gute Bücher

Then add a few role anchors.

TL: guten Wein bestellen

TL: mit gutem Wein

TL: wegen guter Musik

TL: mit guten Büchern

The system is not a perfect letter-for-letter copy of the definite-article table, especially in modern genitive masculine and neuter phrases, where an adjective often takes en and the noun carries the genitive s or es. But the information principle still predicts the useful core: without a marked determiner, the adjective must do much more signaling.

Now repair the second recorded sentence.

TL: Er hat sehr tiefgründiger Schlüsse daraus gezogen.

The verb phrase Schlüsse ziehen selects the conclusions as accusative plural. There is no determiner. The adjective must carry the strong plural accusative e.

TL: Er hat sehr tiefgründige Schlüsse daraus gezogen.

The same reasoning repairs the bare-numeral phrase. A number such as zwölf counts the noun but does not display case, gender, or plural agreement like a declined determiner.

TL: Es kommen noch zwölf weiterer.

The noun is understood as plural nominative: twelve more items are coming. The adjective-like word carries e.

TL: Es kommen noch zwölf weitere.

Self-check two. Say, “The editorial team publishes critical analyses,” with no article before the plural object.

[PAUSE]

TL: Die Redaktion veröffentlicht kritische Analysen.

The third branch begins with ein, kein, or a possessive such as mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, or euer. Traditional grammar calls this mixed declension. The name is less useful than the diagnosis: these determiners sometimes show the signal and sometimes leave a visible gap.

Listen to masculine nominative.

TL: ein neuer Bericht

The bare form ein does not carry the er that der would have carried. The adjective fills the gap with er.

Now neuter nominative or accusative.

TL: ein neues Programm

Again, ein has no visible es, so the adjective supplies es. Feminine ein already ends in e, and the adjective becomes quiet.

TL: eine neue Methode

Masculine accusative einen clearly marks the role, so the adjective also becomes quiet.

TL: einen neuen Bericht

Dative einem, einer, and plural possessives likewise display enough information for en on the adjective.

TL: mit einem neuen Programm

TL: mit einer neuen Methode

TL: meine neuen Projekte

This is not an arbitrary mixture. Ask whether the determiner's surface form has left the der-word signal blank. Bare ein, bare mein, and bare kein leave gaps in masculine nominative and neuter nominative or accusative. The adjective fills those gaps. Elsewhere the determiner is visibly marked, so the adjective usually uses en.

Now the third and fourth recorded errors become transparent.

TL: Sie haben keinen kohärentes Weltbild.

Weltbild is neuter. In the accusative, neuter looks like nominative. The correct determiner is bare kein, which leaves the es signal unstated. The adjective must provide it.

TL: Sie haben kein kohärentes Weltbild.

The other sentence contains both the wrong gender and the wrong distribution.

TL: Meine ultimative Ziel ist, daraus einen Film zu machen.

Ziel is neuter, even though the Russian cognate concept is carried by the feminine noun цель. Use bare mein. Because it leaves the neuter es signal blank, the adjective supplies it.

TL: Mein ultimatives Ziel ist, daraus einen Film zu machen.

Russian gives you excellent agreement instincts, but German distributes the visible information differently. In Russian, noun and adjective endings do much of the work and there is no article layer. In German, look left before you make the adjective strong. The determiner may already be carrying the package.

Self-check three. Say, “Our new program works reliably.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Unser neues Programm funktioniert zuverlässig.

We can now speak the complete decision tree. First identify the noun's gender and number. Second determine case from the clause or preposition. Third inspect the determiner. If it is a fully marked der-word—der, dieser, jeder, welcher, and their declined forms—the adjective is weak: e in the five light positions, en elsewhere. If there is no determiner, the adjective is strong and carries the missing signal. If it is an ein-word or possessive, ask whether that form visibly carries the signal. If not, the adjective fills the gap; if yes, it usually takes en.

Run a contrast through all three branches.

TL: der neue Bericht

TL: ein neuer Bericht

TL: neuer Bericht

The noun, gender, and case are identical. Only the information to the adjective's left changes. Definite der shows er, so the adjective whispers e. Ein leaves the er gap, so the adjective says er. With nothing to the left, the adjective also says er.

Now masculine accusative.

TL: den neuen Bericht

TL: einen neuen Bericht

TL: neuen Bericht

All three take en, but for different structural reasons. Den and einen visibly mark accusative. In the bare strong phrase, en itself is the masculine accusative signal. Surface sameness does not mean the decision tree disappeared.

Self-check four. Choose the correct form for “a coherent worldview.”

[PAUSE]

TL: ein kohärentes Weltbild

The final recorded phrase looks like agreement but contains word formation too.

TL: Ich brauche ein größtmöglichstes Publikum.

Größtmöglich already means “as large as possible.” Its stem contains the maximum idea. Adding another st creates a double superlative. Once the stem is corrected to größtmöglich, the noun phrase follows the ordinary mixed branch: Publikum is neuter accusative, ein leaves the es gap, and the adjective supplies es.

TL: Ich brauche ein größtmögliches Publikum.

This suggests one final safeguard: separate the adjective stem from its agreement ending. First choose the lexical adjective—groß, größer, größte, größtmöglich—then add only the grammatical ending required by the noun phrase. Do not make the agreement ending responsible for meanings already built into the stem.

Self-check five. Repair the recorded phrase and say the full sentence: “I need the largest possible audience.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Ich brauche ein größtmögliches Publikum.

Here is the sixty-second recap. Every German adjective phrase enters one decision tree. Identify noun, gender, number, and case. Then look left. A fully marked der-word already carries the signal, so use weak e in the five light singular positions and en elsewhere. With no determiner, the adjective carries a strong ending. With ein, kein, or a possessive, fill only the gaps: ein neuer Bericht, ein neues Programm, but einen neuen Bericht and mit einem neuen Programm. Numbers do not carry the case signal, so zwölf weitere. Russian case intuition helps you identify roles, but German shares the visible work between determiner and adjective. One noun phrase wants one clear signal. Find where it already lives, then add the quietest ending that completes the package.
