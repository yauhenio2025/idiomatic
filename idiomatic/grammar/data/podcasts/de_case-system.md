## SCRIPT

Welcome to episode five. Today we are going to treat German case as a story about jobs in a sentence: who does something, what is affected, who receives something, and what belongs to what. If Russian is your first language, none of that is alien. You already have deep intuitions that a noun phrase can change shape according to its role. The task is to transfer that intuition selectively, not to translate Russian endings into German one for one.

Russian has more cases than standard German, and it carries much of the information on nouns and adjectives without articles. German has four cases, and it often concentrates the clearest signal on a determiner whose form changes with case. Some meanings divided between Russian instrumental and prepositional cases are handled by German prepositions. Verb government also differs. So your transferable skill is not a table of equivalents. It is the reflex to ask, "What job is this noun phrase doing?"

The four German jobs are nominative, accusative, dative, and genitive. As a first map, nominative marks the grammatical subject. Accusative often marks the directly affected object. Dative often marks a recipient, addressee, experiencer, or the object selected by certain verbs. Genitive often marks possession or a close relationship between nouns.

Here is the simple three-person scene that gives this episode its title.

TL: Die Redakteurin gibt dem Autor den Vertrag.

The editor gives the author the contract. The editor phrase is nominative: the grammatical subject. The author phrase is dative: the recipient. The contract phrase is accusative: the thing transferred.

The English order helps here, but German case makes the jobs more durable than position alone. You can move an element for emphasis and keep the roles.

TL: Den Vertrag gibt die Redakteurin dem Autor morgen.

The contract is still accusative because its article marks it. The editor is still nominative. The author is still dative. Your Russian intuition that endings can preserve roles while word order changes is exactly the intuition to transfer.

Self-check one. In the next sentence, identify the recipient and say its German phrase.

TL: Die Redaktion schickt dem Journalisten den Entwurf.

[PAUSE]

TL: dem Journalisten

The recipient is dative. The draft phrase is accusative.

Now listen to the compact definite-article map. Do not try to chant it forever; use it as orientation. For masculine nouns, the sequence from nominative through accusative, dative, and genitive is:

TL: der, den, dem, des

For feminine nouns:

TL: die, die, der, der

For neuter nouns:

TL: das, das, dem, des

For plural nouns:

TL: die, die, den, der

Several cells repeat. That is why meaning and sentence structure must come before the ending. The feminine row repeats one form across its first two cases and another across its last two; the plural row repeats too. The dative masculine or neuter article is distinctive, while the masculine accusative article also serves as plural dative. A form is evidence, not always a complete answer.

The masculine row is the best audible landmark because all four forms differ.

TL: der Bericht

TL: den Bericht

TL: dem Bericht

TL: des Berichts

In the genitive, many masculine and neuter nouns also add the letter S or the ending E S. In the dative plural, a noun commonly adds the letter N when its plural does not already end that way and when the noun permits it.

TL: mit den Journalisten

TL: mit den Büchern

But the article decision comes from the job first. Do not begin by asking which ending sounds familiar.

The dative-plural letter N is not a free-floating signal that can be added whenever a noun phrase feels complicated. One retained line was:

TL: Damit kann man einen 2 Stunden langen Spielfilmen drehen

The article in that recorded line already identifies masculine accusative singular. The movie noun is not a weak noun, so it takes no extra letter N here.

TL: Damit kann man einen 2 Stunden langen Spielfilm drehen.

Case, number, and noun class all have to authorize an ending. This is particularly important when a strong case instinct starts to hypercorrect a noun that was already complete.

There are three gates to the case decision. Gate one is basic sentence structure. Find the finite verb and its grammatical subject. That subject is nominative, even when it is not a deliberate agent.

TL: Der neue Plan gefällt der Redaktion.

The new plan is nominative. The editorial team experiences the liking and is dative because the verb in the example selects that pattern. A rough "doer equals nominative" rule would fail here. Nominative means grammatical subject, not necessarily an intentional actor.

This matters because one retained error in the record was:

TL: Warum dieser Frau so viel Geld zur Verfügung hatte

In the intended clause, the woman is the subject, so the repair is nominative:

TL: Warum diese Frau so viel Geld zur Verfügung hatte

The fixed expression later in the clause can make dative feel contagious, but case does not spread across a sentence. Each noun phrase has its own job.

Gate two is verb government. Some German verbs assign a case that you must learn with the verb. Listen to seven common dative-selecting verbs.

TL: helfen, danken, folgen, gehören, gefallen, entsprechen, widersprechen

These take a dative object in their relevant common senses.

TL: Die Software hilft der Redaktion.

TL: Der Server gehört dem Ministerium.

TL: Das Verfahren entspricht den europäischen Standards.

Other verbs take an accusative object even when that object feels like a beneficiary or recipient in English. The learner record gives us an excellent contrast. The retained sentence was:

TL: Damit konnte er seiner Stadt mit Strom versorgen

It is tempting to treat the city as a recipient and therefore dative. But German uses the following frame, meaning "supply someone with something."

TL: jemanden mit etwas versorgen

The person or institution supplied is accusative. The preposition inside the frame then governs dative for the supplied resource.

TL: Damit konnte er seine Stadt mit Strom versorgen.

This is where your case intuition must become lexical. Semantic roles propose an answer; the German verb confirms or overrides it.

Self-check two. Correct the case in the recorded sentence.

TL: Damit konnte er seiner Stadt mit Strom versorgen

[PAUSE]

TL: Damit konnte er seine Stadt mit Strom versorgen.

The city is accusative because the verb selects it as its direct object. The supplied resource is dative because it follows the frame's preposition, although its case is not overtly visible here.

Ditransitive verbs make the who-to-whom structure especially clear. Listen to a useful group.

TL: geben, zeigen, schicken, erklären, erzählen, sagen

With these verbs, a recipient or addressee is normally dative, while a nominal thing communicated or transferred is accusative.

Two of the clearest retained errors land on this boundary.

TL: Du kannst den Programm sagen, was es hervorheben soll

The addressee selected by the verb is dative, and the noun meaning program is neuter. The repair is:

TL: Du kannst dem Programm sagen, was es hervorheben soll.

Another recorded line was:

TL: Sie tun damit jemanden einen Gefallen

The person receiving the favor is dative; the favor itself is accusative.

TL: Sie tun damit jemandem einen Gefallen.

Store that contrast as a rhythm.

TL: jemandem einen Gefallen tun

TL: dem Programm das Ziel erklären

TL: der Redaktion den Entwurf schicken

Self-check three. Say, "I explain the method to the committee."

[PAUSE]

TL: Ich erkläre dem Ausschuss die Methode.

The committee phrase is the dative addressee. The method phrase is feminine accusative; its form happens to match feminine nominative. Meaning and the verb frame distinguish it.

Gate three is the preposition. Some prepositions consistently govern a case. The following group normally governs dative.

TL: mit, nach, aus, zu, von, bei

The next group normally governs accusative.

TL: durch, für, gegen, ohne, um

Formal written German also uses genitive after the following prepositions, with register variation in speech.

TL: während, trotz, wegen

TL: mit dem Autor

TL: für den Autor

TL: wegen des Berichts

When a preposition is present, check it before applying a general recipient or object story. The preposition may settle the case immediately.

Then come the two-way prepositions. Here is the central set.

TL: an, auf, in, über, unter, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen

Their central contrast is location versus a change toward an endpoint. Dative presents the location where something is. Accusative presents the goal, placement, or boundary crossed. Mere physical movement is not enough; you can walk around within a location and still use dative.

TL: Wir arbeiten in der Türkei.

TL: Wir fliegen in die Türkei.

The first locates the work. The second gives the destination. The teaching record supplied that directional form several times. The profile identifies articleless country phrasing here as a Russian-L1 signature: Russian has no articles, while German feminine country names require one. This is a plausible transfer pressure, not proof that every such slip has one cause. Learn the place with its whole directional and locative pair.

TL: in die Türkei

TL: in der Türkei

Another retained error combines verb government with a two-way preposition:

TL: Sie verfüttern Menschen an Schweinen

The pigs are the endpoint selected by the verb-plus-preposition frame, so the record corrects the phrase to accusative plural:

TL: Sie verfüttern Menschen an Schweine.

Self-check four. You are already at the conference, not heading into it. Say, "We are at the conference."

[PAUSE]

TL: Wir sind auf der Konferenz.

For the destination, you would say:

TL: Wir gehen auf die Konferenz.

Genitive is the fourth job. It links one noun phrase closely to another and is especially useful in the written, analytical register that dominates your German lessons.

TL: die Größe des Binnenmarktes

TL: die Aufgabe meiner Presseagentin

TL: der Kollaps des Kommunismus

The profile found about fifteen teacher-supplied genitive constructions and a tendency to use analytic prepositional phrases meaning "of" instead. Such a phrase is not automatically wrong; it is normal and often preferable in speech. The issue is availability and register. In compact formal prose, the genitive often gives you the idiomatic noun chain.

TL: die besten Verteidiger des Kapitalismus

So treat genitive as an additional professional tool, not as a moral upgrade over the prepositional alternative.

Now for the Russian transfer warning. Existing case instincts are an advantage, but gender and articles can smuggle in the wrong form. The record preserves:

TL: Meine ultimative Ziel ist daraus einen Film zu machen

The profile notes that the Russian word meaning goal is feminine, while its German counterpart is neuter. The repair must update the whole noun phrase.

TL: Mein ultimatives Ziel ist, daraus einen Film zu machen.

That is a documented Russian-shaped signature, but it does not license a broad claim that Russian causes every German agreement error. It tells you where to be alert: familiar concepts with different lexical gender, and noun phrases where German requires an article that Russian does not.

The full production routine is now five steps. First, locate the noun phrase's structural job: subject, affected object, recipient, or possessor. Second, check whether the verb assigns a lexical case. Third, check whether a preposition governs the phrase, including the location-versus-endpoint choice for two-way prepositions. Fourth, retrieve the German noun's gender and number. Fifth, express the case on the determiner, adjective, and where required the noun itself.

Self-check five. Replace the conversational prepositional phrase with a compact genitive phrase.

TL: die Verteidiger vom Kapitalismus

[PAUSE]

TL: die Verteidiger des Kapitalismus

Do not expect this routine to remain slow. At first, explicit questions make the hidden choices visible. With repeated meaningful production, common frames become chunks.

TL: jemandem einen Gefallen tun

TL: dem Ausschuss etwas erklären

TL: jemandem widersprechen

TL: jemanden mit etwas versorgen

TL: in der Türkei; in die Türkei

The point of the analysis is to build fast retrieval, not to perform a grammar seminar before every noun.

## 60-SECOND RECAP

German case marks the job of a noun phrase. Nominative is the grammatical subject, accusative often the directly affected object, dative often a recipient or verb-selected object, and genitive a possessive or noun-to-noun link. Transfer your Russian instinct that roles can survive word-order changes, but do not map Russian endings directly onto German. German puts the clearest signal on the determiner. Hear the masculine row again.

TL: der, den, dem, des

Decide in order. Find the structural job. Check the verb: the verb meaning "say" gives its addressee dative, but the verb meaning "supply" takes the supplied person in the accusative. Check the preposition: the one meaning "with" takes dative, the one meaning "for" accusative, and two-way prepositions contrast location with an endpoint. Then retrieve German gender and number, because Russian gender may differ and Russian has no articles. Your anchor contrast is:

TL: Die Redakteurin gibt dem Autor den Vertrag.

Who acts, what changes hands, and to whom: nominative, accusative, dative.
