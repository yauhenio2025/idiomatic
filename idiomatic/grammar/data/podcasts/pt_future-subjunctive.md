## SCRIPT

Portuguese has a tense that can make a Spanish speaker feel as if the floor has shifted. You are talking about the future. The main clause can use the familiar spoken future. Then, inside a clause beginning with “when” or “if,” Portuguese suddenly asks for a form that looks like an infinitive, a past-tense stem, or both.

That form is the future subjunctive. It is not a museum piece. In Brazilian Portuguese it appears in ordinary plans, conditions, deadlines, promises, and instructions.

The title calls it the tense only Portuguese kept. Let us make that memorable claim linguistically precise. Older Spanish had a future subjunctive too, and remnants still appear in legal formulas and fixed expressions. Galician and Spanish retain fossilized or restricted remnants; Portuguese keeps the tense productive in ordinary modern use. So “only Portuguese” is a useful family portrait, not a claim that no neighboring variety preserved a trace of it.

For you, this is not an abstract curiosity. The Portuguese profile found seven future-subjunctive selection errors in twenty twenty and twenty twenty-one, followed by eight remediation notes in twenty twenty-three and twenty twenty-four. The teacher was still packaging forms such as “when you arrive” and “if they manage” years later. This is a persistent system, not a one-day slip.

Here is the first recorded error.

TL: quando eu vou voltar, eu vou ajustar os cartões

The phrase meaning “I am going to return” is a perfectly good main-clause future. But here it sits inside a pending time clause. Portuguese removes the little future construction from that clause and uses the future subjunctive.

TL: quando eu voltar, eu vou ajustar os cartões

Picture a gate. The clause after the gate sets the pending circumstance under which the main clause is evaluated. It need not happen first in chronological time. The event is future, but it has not yet been realized at the moment of speaking. Portuguese marks that pending status inside the gate.

Pending does not mean unlikely. You can be completely confident that the event will happen and still use this form because the event has not happened yet. Sunrise gives us a useful extreme case: the probability is not in doubt, but the time clause is still future-facing.

TL: Quando o sol nascer, a cidade vai acordar.

TL: Quando eu voltar, vou ajustar os cartões.

The main clause stays ordinary.

TL: vou ajustar os cartões

The special marking belongs to the dependent event.

TL: quando eu voltar

This is why the rule is not “use the future subjunctive whenever you talk about tomorrow.” A standalone prediction does not need it.

TL: Amanhã vou voltar.

Nor is it “use it after every occurrence of the word for when.” A direct question is not a dependent future gate.

TL: Quando você vai voltar?

But once that return becomes the condition in a larger sentence, the gate opens.

TL: Quando você voltar, me avise.

Self-check one. Repair the recorded sentence about adjusting the cards.

TL: quando eu vou voltar, eu vou ajustar os cartões

TL: Quando eu voltar, eu vou ajustar os cartões.

The central meaning is a future event still pending. Compare three views of the same activity. First, a repeated habit.

TL: Quando tenho tempo, leio.

Second, a completed past occasion.

TL: Quando tive tempo, li o relatório.

Third, a future occasion whose arrival is still open.

TL: Quando tiver tempo, vou ler o relatório.

The conjunction is the same. The temporal status changes. Present habit takes the indicative. Completed past takes a past indicative. Pending future takes the future subjunctive.

Your second recorded contrast makes the selection visible because the verb is irregular.

TL: quando eu vou ter mais tempo, vou testar

The repair is:

TL: quando eu tiver mais tempo, vou testar

With a regular verb, the first and third person singular future subjunctive look exactly like the infinitive.

TL: quando eu voltar

That surface resemblance can hide the grammar. The form is not a bare infinitive here. It belongs to a complete finite paradigm and can carry its own subject.

TL: quando eu voltar; quando você voltar; quando eles voltarem

Irregular verbs reveal the machinery.

TL: quando eu tiver; quando nós tivermos; quando eles tiverem

The most useful formation rule starts from a past form you may already know. Take the third-person plural of the completed past and remove the last two letters, A and M. The remaining stem is the first and third person singular future subjunctive.

TL: tiveram; tiver

TL: fizeram; fizer

TL: disseram; disser

TL: puderam; puder

TL: puseram; puser

TL: souberam; souber

TL: vieram; vier

TL: foram; for

Then add the personal endings. Hear one full high-frequency pattern.

TL: fizer, fizeres, fizer, fizermos, fizerdes, fizerem

In Brazilian conversation, the plural pronoun meaning “you all” normally takes the third-person plural form, which you will hear much more than the traditional second-person plural form.

TL: vocês fizerem

The full pattern still explains the system; your productive core can focus on the persons you actually use.

Regular verbs hide the historical stem because removing the past plural ending returns something that looks like the infinitive.

TL: falaram; falar

TL: chegaram; chegar

This also explains a common learning illusion. You may produce “when I arrive” correctly and think you have mastered the tense, while avoiding the forms that reveal it. The diagnostic forms are the irregulars: when I have, when I do, when I can, when I am, and when I come.

The resemblance creates one more trap: the future subjunctive is not the personal infinitive. Regular verbs make the two paradigms look alike, but an irregular verb separates them. Compare a personal-infinitive clause after an impersonal evaluation:

TL: É importante fazermos uma cópia.

Now compare a pending time clause:

TL: Quando fizermos uma cópia, vamos enviá-la à editora.

The first form keeps the infinitive stem.

TL: fazermos

The future subjunctive uses the stem recovered from the completed-past plural.

TL: fizeram; fizermos

Both forms can have an understood or explicit subject. Their shape is chosen by the construction, not simply by whether a subject is present. For your practical system, an evaluation with an infinitive and a future gate are two different machines.

The time-gate family includes the ordinary expressions meaning “when,” “as soon as,” and “whenever,” when they introduce a pending future event.

TL: quando

TL: assim que

TL: logo que

TL: sempre que

TL: Assim que eu receber o áudio, vou começar a edição.

TL: Logo que eles chegarem, vamos iniciar a reunião.

TL: Sempre que você tiver uma dúvida, pode me escrever.

Context still decides. The same expression can describe a habit with the present indicative.

TL: Sempre que tenho uma dúvida, escrevo para a editora.

Do not let a memorized trigger replace meaning. Ask whether the event is established and repeated, completed, or future and pending.

The second major gate is a genuine condition introduced by the word meaning “if.” Your profile preserves this Spanish-shaped future.

TL: se eu vou viajar em janeiro

An open future condition uses the future subjunctive.

TL: se eu viajar em janeiro

The full sentence can keep the spoken future in the main clause.

TL: Se eu viajar em janeiro, vou comprar a passagem em outubro.

Now compare a remote hypothesis. If the speaker imagines a less real or counterfactual situation, Portuguese normally changes to the imperfect subjunctive and the conditional frame.

TL: Se eu viajasse em janeiro, ficaria mais tempo.

For this future-facing contrast, keep two conditional frames apart. An open future condition takes the future subjunctive. A remote or contrary-to-fact condition takes the imperfect subjunctive. Other time frames can produce other patterns, so this is not a complete inventory of every conditional sentence.

There is also an important false trigger. The same short word can mean “whether” inside an indirect question. Then it does not create a condition.

TL: Não sei se ele vem amanhã.

That sentence means “I do not know whether he is coming tomorrow.” Compare the real condition.

TL: Se ele vier amanhã, vamos conversar.

The spelling of the gate is identical. Its job in the sentence is different.

TL: Se eles conseguirem financiamento, vão ampliar o projeto.

The future subjunctive also appears when a future person, place, or thing has not yet been identified. In these clauses, English often uses an ordinary present form.

TL: Quem chegar primeiro vai abrir a sala.

TL: O que você decidir vai estar ótimo para mim.

TL: Onde você estiver, vou ligar às oito.

TL: Qualquer pessoa que tiver o link poderá entrar.

These are relatives with an open slot: whoever arrives, whatever you decide, wherever you are, any person who has the link. The future form marks the eventual member of that set.

One high-value irregular deserves special attention. Both “to be” and “to go” share the same past plural form and therefore the same future-subjunctive form.

TL: foram; for

Context supplies the meaning.

TL: Quando o arquivo for enviado, vou confirmar o recebimento.

TL: Se eu for ao Brasil, vou passar alguns dias em São Paulo.

Your record contains the periphrastic future inside a pending passive clause.

TL: quando a pandemia vai ser controlada

The repair uses the future subjunctive of “to be.”

TL: quando a pandemia for controlada

Now for the boundary that prevents overuse. Future meaning alone does not choose this tense. Some conjunctions select the present subjunctive even when the action lies in the future. The expressions meaning “before,” “so that,” “in case,” and “although” have their own patterns.

TL: Antes que você publique, revise os dados.

TL: Vou mandar uma cópia para que ela possa comparar as versões.

TL: Caso você tenha tempo, leia o anexo.

The safe lesson is not “future equals future subjunctive.” It is “learn the future gates as constructions.” The strongest productive gates are pending time clauses, open conditions, and open future relatives.

Self-check five. The sentence begins with the phrase meaning “before,” so do not use the future subjunctive. Say, “Before she publishes the report, I want to check the figures.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Antes que ela publique o relatório, quero conferir os números.

One last production strategy will make this system faster. When you begin a sentence with a future gate, delay planning the main clause for half a beat. Complete the gate correctly first.

TL: Quando eu tiver o manuscrito...

TL: Se eles conseguirem financiamento...

TL: Assim que ela fizer a análise...

Then finish the main clause in whatever form the meaning requires. These three predictions use the ordinary spoken future.

TL: vou começar a revisão

TL: vão ampliar o projeto

TL: vai publicar o relatório

This chunks the decision at the point where your recorded errors occur. You are not failing to express future time. You already use the spoken future well. In these examples, the error is marking the future with the spoken construction in both clauses. Portuguese asks you to mark the subordinate event as pending and use the spoken future only in the main clause.

## 60-SECOND RECAP

Here is the future subjunctive in one minute. Use it in a dependent clause for a pending future event: after a time gate, in an open condition, or in an open future relative. Let the main clause follow its meaning—often future, but sometimes imperative or present.

TL: Quando eu tiver tempo, vou ler.

TL: Se eles conseguirem financiamento, vão ampliar o projeto.

TL: Quem chegar primeiro vai abrir a sala.

Build irregular forms from the completed-past plural: remove the last two letters, A and M.

TL: tiveram; tiver. Fizeram; fizer. Foram; for.

Future meaning alone is not enough. Established habits normally take the indicative; remote conditions take the imperfect subjunctive; and expressions such as “before” or “in case” take the present subjunctive. Your recurring trap is putting the spoken future in both clauses. Repair it: no “going to” inside the gate. Mark the event as pending, then let the main clause express a prediction, instruction, or present consequence.
