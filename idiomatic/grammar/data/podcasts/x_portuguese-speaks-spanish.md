## SCRIPT

Sometimes your Portuguese opens its mouth and Spanish comes out. Not a whole Spanish sentence. That would be easy to notice. What comes out is more dangerous: one familiar word, one tiny connector, or one cell from a Spanish-looking paradigm, fitted neatly into an otherwise Portuguese sentence.

This is not random. It is the most visible systemic weakness in your records. In Spanish, twenty-six of the sixty-two preserved learner-error pairs involve transfer from another language, with roughly fourteen pointing toward Portuguese. In Portuguese, five hundred and eighty-eight of one thousand and ninety-eight classified error rows involve Romance lexical or morphological transfer, mostly from Spanish and some from Italian.

Those percentages are not directly comparable. The Spanish record preserves only sixty-two wrong forms, while the Portuguese record preserves more than a thousand. The teachers also changed how they captured errors over time. But the directional pattern is unmistakable: forms associated with related languages repeatedly appear where the lessons expect a target-language alternative.

One plausible explanation is competition during retrieval rather than a simple knowledge gap. The records do not test recognition or distinguish that account from incomplete learning. Still, the pattern is consistent with several related candidates becoming active while you speak, allowing a familiar one to win before the language label has fully caught up.

Close relatives create this problem precisely because they help so much. Spanish and Portuguese share thousands of recognizable forms, similar sentence patterns, and a great deal of history. Similarity accelerates comprehension. It also lowers the alarm threshold. A word that sounds Romance-shaped feels licensed, even when it belongs to the neighboring system.

Your Spanish record gives a useful specimen from software talk. The lesson treats the ordinary Portuguese choice as the intrusion.

TL: [pt] inserir

The ordinary Spanish software target is different. A Spanish dictionary also records a separate verb with the same spelling as the Portuguese form. Its existence does not make that spelling the ordinary choice for this software context.

TL: [es] insertar

The intrusion itself was recorded twice in twenty twenty-two, and the Spanish target was taught or retaught sixteen times from twenty nineteen through twenty twenty-four. That span tells us this is not a rare word you once forgot. It is a stable contrast problem in a domain you discuss constantly, whether its source is retrieval competition, incomplete lexical learning, or both.

The solution is not to distrust all cognates. Most cognates are useful. The solution is to build a short watchlist for the forms that have actually crossed the border. Consider three more preserved Portuguese-shaped Spanish errors.

TL: [pt] longo

TL: [pt] isolado

Now hear the Spanish forms as complete lexical objects, not as spelling edits.

TL: [es] largo

TL: [es] aislado

If you treat the task as “make the Portuguese word look Spanish,” you invite productive guessing. Sometimes the change is one consonant. Sometimes Spanish chooses a different stem. Retrieve the target word as a whole.

Self-check one. In Spanish, say, “I want to insert the example.” Both risky words are in the sentence.

TL: [es] Quiero insertar el ejemplo.

That is layer one of interference: the lexical layer. Layer two is the paradigm. A verb may be shared, or nearly shared, while one conjugated cell belongs to only one language. Your Spanish record preserves this form for “we wanted.”

TL: [pt] quisemos

That is correct Portuguese. It is not the Spanish first-person plural preterite. Spanish requires:

TL: [es] quisimos

The error is revealing because no amount of changing the infinitive helps. You already know the verb. What crossed the border was one cell from a Portuguese paradigm. For high-risk irregular verbs, retrieve a short target-language sequence instead of an abstract instruction.

TL: [es] quise, quisiste, quiso, quisimos

You do not need to recite the whole table in conversation. A compact sequence gives the target form neighbors inside the right language and makes the intruder sound less at home.

Self-check two. Give the Spanish form for “we wanted,” then put it into “we wanted to publish the report.”

[PAUSE]

TL: [es] quisimos; quisimos publicar el informe

Layer three is the construction. Here every individual word may exist in both languages, but the bridge between them differs. The spoken future is the cleanest example.

In Spanish, the verb meaning “to go” takes a connector before an infinitive.

TL: [es] Voy a revisar el informe.

In Brazilian Portuguese, the corresponding spoken future has a zero-width bridge.

TL: [pt] Vou revisar o relatório.

Your Portuguese profile records the Spanish bridge inside a Portuguese sentence.

TL: [pt] Eu vou a passar quatro dias lá.

The teacher's repair removes the connector.

TL: [pt] Eu vou passar quatro dias lá.

This is why vocabulary review alone cannot solve interference. You can know every word and still assemble them with the neighboring language's syntax. Store the construction as a rhythm: Spanish has three beats around the infinitive; Portuguese has two.

Self-check three. Say “Tomorrow I am going to review the manuscript” first in Spanish, then in Brazilian Portuguese. Listen for the bridge.

[PAUSE]

TL: [es] Mañana voy a revisar el manuscrito.

TL: [pt] Amanhã vou revisar o manuscrito.

The same constructional transfer runs in the other direction. Portuguese commonly uses one destination pattern that tempts you in Spanish.

TL: [pt] viajar para um lugar

The Spanish profile reconstructs the attested fragment meaning “to travel toward” in this transferred frame.

TL: [es] viajar para un lugar

For the ordinary destination meaning practiced in your lessons, Spanish uses:

TL: [es] viajar a un lugar

Notice the right level of specificity. This does not mean the words for “to” and “for” map mechanically across the languages. It means the verb-and-destination chunk must be stored inside each language.

Pronouns can also cross intact. The Spanish record contains the Portuguese form:

TL: [pt] conosco

Spanish replaces it with a phrase.

TL: [es] con nosotros

Portuguese, of course, really does want the compact form. A useful contrast pair is therefore:

TL: [pt] Eles trabalham conosco.

TL: [es] Ellos trabajan con nosotros.

The danger is asymmetric. A form can be an error in one episode of speech and a target in the next. You do not want to suppress it globally. You want the correct language gate to release it.

What is a language gate? It is a small cue that establishes the target system before content retrieval begins. In deliberate practice, say the language name, hear one unmistakable anchor phrase, and only then produce the risky sentence. For Portuguese, an anchor might be:

TL: [pt] Agora vou falar em português.

For Spanish:

TL: [es] Ahora voy a hablar en español.

The anchors are similar enough to make the contrast audible, but distinctive enough to set the bridge. In real conversation, the setting itself becomes the cue. During practice, we manufacture that cue on purpose.

Your Portuguese record shows why the gate must include meaning, not just sound. One of the most persistent intrusions is a real Portuguese word used with its Spanish meaning.

TL: [pt] todavia

In Spanish, the accented cognate commonly means “still” or “yet.” In ordinary modern Portuguese, the unaccented word normally works as a relatively formal adversative connector meaning “however” or “nevertheless.” Your intended Portuguese word for continuing time was:

TL: [pt] ainda

So the repair is not pronunciation. It is a semantic border.

TL: [pt] Ainda não decidi.

TL: [pt] Todavia, precisamos decidir hoje.

The second sentence is grammatical Portuguese, but it belongs to a more formal register. This is an important principle: an intruder is not always nonsense. Sometimes it is a genuine target-language form doing the source language's job.

Another recorded case looks almost too small to matter.

TL: [es] contento

Spanish and Italian support that ending. Brazilian Portuguese uses a different final vowel in this adjective.

TL: [pt] contente

The Portuguese profile records the wrong form seven times and shows the target still being remediated later. Frequency turns one vowel into a priority.

Self-check four. In Brazilian Portuguese, say, “I am still happy with the plan.”

[PAUSE]

TL: [pt] Ainda estou contente com o plano.

There are also productive-looking Spanish intrusions that Portuguese rejects as lexical wholes. Your record includes the Spanish-shaped attempt for “I try.”

TL: [es] intento

Portuguese uses:

TL: [pt] tento

And it includes the Spanish existential form.

TL: [es] hay

Conversational Brazilian Portuguese commonly uses:

TL: [pt] tem

The record's correction includes this compact negative frame.

TL: [pt] não tem nada

Self-check five. Set the Portuguese gate, then say, “There is nothing here.”

[PAUSE]

TL: [pt] Não tem nada aqui.

How do you train all this without making the languages more confused? The research summary behind this project offers cautious support for deliberate interleaving, including practice that involved two Romance languages. That study tested French and Spanish tense rules, not this exact Portuguese-Spanish watchlist, so it does not prove that mixing always reduces interference. Use it as support for contrastive retrieval: choose the receiving language and produce its form before seeing the answer.

Use three kinds of contrast. First, same meaning, different whole word.

TL: [es] insertar

TL: [pt] inserir

Second, same verb family, different paradigm cell.

TL: [es] quisimos

TL: [pt] quisemos

Third, same communicative construction, different bridge.

TL: [es] voy a publicar

TL: [pt] vou publicar

Do not block fifty Portuguese items and then fifty Spanish items. Begin with a very short warm-up if necessary, then alternate directions. The switch itself is part of the skill.

There is one more practical rule: suspicious ease is a signal. When a sophisticated word arrives instantly and looks perfectly Romance, spend half a second checking its passport. Ask: have I retrieved this exact item in the target language, or have I generated it from a cousin? You do not need that pause for every common word. Use it for your documented watchlist: software verbs, recurring discourse words, destination frames, and irregular paradigm cells.

Variety labels require care because a teacher's preference is not always a grammatical correction. Your target is Brazilian Portuguese, and the Portuguese record consistently prefers the forms below. For accessing software, it redirects a form that is standard especially in European Portuguese:

TL: [pt] aceder

to the Brazilian target:

TL: [pt] acessar

For the verb meaning “to plan,” the record redirects this form:

TL: [pt] planear

to this one:

TL: [pt] planejar

The first alternative is standard in European Portuguese, and a Brazilian dictionary also lists it by referring readers to the second. Your teacher consistently preferred the more usual Brazilian form. These are variety-and-frequency targets, not claims that the alternatives are meaningless or ungrammatical. A language gate therefore needs a variety setting too: Brazilian Portuguese, with its preferred vocabulary, rather than an undifferentiated blend assembled from any available cognate.

When an intruder does escape, use a short repair sequence. First, stop without explaining the whole mistake. Second, say the correct target chunk once. Third, place it in a meaningful sentence. Finally, contrast it with the source-language form once, then move on. The aim is to strengthen the winning route, not to chant the error. For the software pair, the sequence ends with one Spanish sentence and one Portuguese sentence.

TL: [es] Quiero insertar el archivo.

TL: [pt] Quero inserir o arquivo.

That watchlist should stay directional. Portuguese to Spanish includes the recorded set around the software verb, the word for example, the adjectives for long and isolated, the pronoun meaning “with us,” and the form for “we wanted.” Spanish to Portuguese includes the spoken-future connector, the words for still and happy, and several Spanish-shaped lexical forms. Direction is part of the memory.

## 60-SECOND RECAP

Here is the one-minute version. A useful working explanation for these slips is that close languages activate one another, allowing a familiar candidate to reach speech before the target-language gate is secure; the records show the outputs, not the mental mechanism. Transfer appears at three levels: whole words, paradigm cells, and constructions. Do not repair a cousin by changing its ending. Retrieve the target word or chunk whole. Use a brief language anchor, keep a directional watchlist, and practice the dangerous pairs in alternating languages. Remember the core contrasts.

TL: [pt] inserir

TL: [es] quisimos

TL: [pt] quisemos

TL: [es] voy a publicar

TL: [pt] vou publicar

TL: [es] todavía

TL: [pt] ainda

Similarity is not the enemy; it is an accelerator that needs steering. The goal is not to silence Spanish while speaking Portuguese. It is to make the correct language win the retrieval race on purpose.
