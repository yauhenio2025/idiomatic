## SCRIPT

French noun gender invites bad philosophy. Learners search for a hidden reason that a period of time is feminine, a process is masculine, and a book is masculine. Perhaps shape, meaning, elegance, or some deep cultural essence explains it. Usually none of that helps. Gender is not a logical verdict about the object. It is stored grammar attached to a word.

That sounds disappointing until you look at your actual data. Gender is not an infinite fog in your French. Approximately 297 rows involve noun gender, but the errors concentrate around a small personal list. The profile explicitly names nineteen top offenders. A working bank extends that spine to forty using nouns attested elsewhere in the same profile. So the task is not “understand the gender of French.” It is “give forty recurring nouns a stronger memory address.”

The memory address should not be a bare letter m or f. Store a small audible package: demonstrative plus noun, article plus noun, or noun plus a visibly agreeing adjective. Gender matters because it radiates. It chooses ce or cette, un or une, mon or ma, and the ending of many adjectives.

What should you do with suffix rules? Use them as compression, never as a courtroom. Tion and sion strongly suggest feminine. Ment often suggests masculine. Age is often masculine. A final e says remarkably little. But your error list demonstrates the limit of logic: méthode and période are feminine despite shapes that fail to announce it; site, cadre, livre, téléphone, article, and magazine are masculine despite final e.

A better encoding routine takes five seconds. First hear the article. Second add a demonstrative or adjective that makes gender audible. Third place the phrase in your own domain.

Here is the sixty-second recap. French gender is lexical memory, not object logic. Your roughly 297 gender rows concentrate around a manageable personal core. The explicitly named nineteen begin with feminine période, fois, vie, carrière, présentation, leçon, méthode, question, ville, conférence, and région; and masculine mode, site, parti, moyen, processus, logiciel, cadre, and livre. The working forty adds frequent profile nouns from politique and décision to article and vaccin. Store each with an audible companion: cette période, un processus, ce logiciel, une politique indépendante. Use suffixes as hints, never verdicts. When an error occurs, repair the entire agreement radius. Forty strong lexical packages are worth more than a thousand guesses from spelling.
