## SCRIPT

German case is often taught as a table before it is taught as a story. Der becomes den, then dem, then des; die changes less visibly; adjective endings arrive; and the learner is asked to memorize sixteen boxes whose purpose remains obscure. Let us reverse the order. The story comes first: who does what to whom, and inside what relationship? The table is simply the signaling equipment.

As a Russian speaker, you already possess the central intuition. Noun phrases can carry roles. Word order helps, but it does not do all the work. A recipient is not the same participant as a transferred object; a destination is not the same scene as a location; possession can be built into a noun phrase. German does not ask you to invent case from nothing. It asks you to transfer the role instinct while learning a new set of signals.

That transfer has two traps. Russian puts much of the case signal on the noun and adjective, while German often puts the clearest signal on an article or determiner. And the two languages do not assign the same case to every verb, preposition, or noun. The instinct “roles matter” transfers beautifully. The answer to “which role does this German word govern?” must still be learned in German.

Begin with the cast. The nominative is the grammatical anchor, usually the subject: the participant whose person and number control the finite verb.

TL: Die Redaktion veröffentlicht den Bericht.

The editorial team is nominative. It controls veröffentlicht. The report is accusative: the directly affected object. Change the viewpoint to a passive, and the old object becomes the new nominative anchor.

TL: Der Bericht wird veröffentlicht.

So nominative does not mean “the active human.” It means the noun phrase functioning as grammatical subject in this clause.

One recorded sentence shows why this matters.

TL: Warum dieser Frau so viel Geld zur Verfügung hatte

The intended subject is the woman. Nothing has assigned dative to her; she is the nominative anchor.

TL: Warum diese Frau so viel Geld zur Verfügung hatte

The ending is the symptom. Role selection is the decision.

Self-check one. In “The agency publishes the report,” identify the subject by producing the full German sentence.

[PAUSE]

TL: Die Agentur veröffentlicht den Bericht.

The accusative is the next major role. With an ordinary transitive verb, it marks the entity directly affected, created, moved, perceived, or otherwise selected as the direct object.

TL: Die Autorin schreibt einen Artikel.

TL: Der Verlag veröffentlicht das Buch.

TL: Wir prüfen die Daten.

English word order often hides this relationship because subject before verb and object after verb already make it clear. German word order can move constituents for emphasis while case preserves the cast.

TL: Den Bericht veröffentlicht die Redaktion morgen.

The masculine article den tells you that the report is accusative. Die Redaktion remains nominative and controls the singular verb. Do not turn the first noun into the subject automatically.

The most efficient article landmarks are masculine because masculine shows all four distinctions clearly.

TL: der, den, dem, des

Hear them as a route: nominative der, accusative den, dative dem, genitive des. Feminine is die, die, der, der. Neuter is das, das, dem, des. Plural is die, die, den, der, and a dative plural noun normally adds n when it can.

You do not have to sing that matrix every time you speak. Use the masculine route as orientation, then retrieve frequent noun phrases. The next episode will show how this first signal controls adjective endings.

The dative introduces a second object role: recipient, addressee, beneficiary, possessor in some constructions, or the sole object selected by a particular verb. In a transfer scene, ask who receives or experiences something and what is transferred.

TL: Die Autorin schickt dem Verlag das Manuskript.

The publisher is the recipient and takes dative. The manuscript is what moves and takes accusative. Your record contains a classic version of this split.

TL: Sie tun damit jemanden einen Gefallen.

The favor is the accusative thing. The person receiving it needs dative.

TL: Sie tun damit jemandem einen Gefallen.

Store the whole rail.

TL: jemandem einen Gefallen tun

Another recorded sentence used the accusative-looking masculine article before a neuter program.

TL: Du kannst den Programm sagen, was es hervorheben soll.

The noun is das Programm, not der Programm, and the program is the addressee of sagen. Both gender and role point to dem.

TL: Du kannst dem Programm sagen, was es hervorheben soll.

Self-check two. Say, “We send the editor the manuscript.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Wir schicken der Redakteurin das Manuskript.

Not every English recipient-like idea becomes dative. German verb government is lexical. The record captured the reverse error with versorgen, to supply or provide.

TL: Damit konnte er seiner Stadt mit Strom versorgen.

German builds this as someone accusative plus mit something: supply someone with something.

TL: Damit konnte er seine Stadt mit Strom versorgen.

Russian intuition cannot settle this by itself, because languages package the same scene differently. Learn high-value verbs with their case frames. Helfen, danken, gefallen, gehören, folgen, antworten, widersprechen, and widerstehen select a dative participant in their core uses.

TL: Das Programm gehört diesem Unternehmen.

TL: Wir danken der Redakteurin.

TL: Der Journalist widerspricht dem Minister.

TL: Die Institution widersteht dem Druck.

The profile shows gehören taught at least three times, alongside widmen, widerstehen, and entsprechen. That is a signal of production avoidance or weak availability, even when a wrong form was not preserved. A short lexical frame is more useful than the label “dative verb.”

TL: jemandem gehören

TL: jemandem widersprechen

TL: jemandem Zeit widmen

Self-check three. Say, “The software belongs to this company.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Die Software gehört diesem Unternehmen.

Prepositions create another role system. Some always govern one case; others choose between accusative and dative according to how they frame a spatial relationship. The two-way prepositions include an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, and zwischen.

For a bounded destination or change into a new spatial relation, use accusative.

TL: Ich fliege in die Türkei.

For location within an established relation, use dative.

TL: Ich arbeite in der Türkei.

The Russian-speaker trap is revealing. Russian has no article in the equivalent country phrase, and the German profile records the articleless destination pattern.

TL: Ich fliege nach Türkei.

The correction was taught repeatedly.

TL: Ich fliege in die Türkei.

Use nach with most articleless cities and countries, but an article-bearing country keeps the article and enters the two-way-preposition system.

TL: nach Italien

TL: in die Türkei

TL: in der Türkei

Do not reduce the contrast to physical motion versus no motion. You can run around inside a room and remain in a dative location. You can put a stationary picture onto a wall and create a new accusative relation. Ask whether the phrase describes an established location or a destination/resulting relation.

The record contains a directional an phrase with the wrong dative plural.

TL: Sie verfüttern Menschen an Schweinen.

The people are being fed to pigs: the pigs are the goal of the transfer, and this construction uses accusative.

TL: Sie verfüttern Menschen an Schweine.

Self-check four. Contrast destination and location by saying, “I am flying to Turkey; I am working in Turkey.”

[PAUSE]

TL: Ich fliege in die Türkei; ich arbeite in der Türkei.

The genitive answers a relationship question such as “whose?” or “of what?” It remains productive in formal written German, the register of your books, podcasts, politics, and technology discussions. Your lessons supplied around fifteen genitive constructions, while your own accepted phrasing often used von.

TL: die Verteidiger vom Kapitalismus

That is common in colloquial German. In formal prose, the compact genitive is often preferable.

TL: die Verteidiger des Kapitalismus

Other supplied patterns included the size of the domestic market and the collapse of communism.

TL: die Größe des Binnenmarktes

TL: nach dem Kollaps des Kommunismus

The genitive is not morally superior to von. Register and noun-phrase density decide. The evidence points to avoidance, so the goal is to make the option available, not to purge spoken German of von phrases.

Case errors often arrive disguised as ending errors. A learner sees dem versus den and stares at the final letter. Reverse the workflow. First name the scene in plain language: publisher receives manuscript; manuscript moves. Then choose German lexical government: schicken gives recipient dative and thing accusative. Then retrieve gender and number. Only then choose the visible form.

Self-check five. Use formal written style to say, “the importance of the report.”

[PAUSE]

TL: die Bedeutung des Berichts

Here is the sixty-second recap. German case labels the cast. Nominative anchors the clause and controls the finite verb. Accusative normally marks the directly affected object. Dative marks a recipient or the participant selected by verbs such as helfen, gehören, and widersprechen. Genitive builds compact possession and “of” relations, especially in formal prose. Your Russian case intuition transfers at the level of roles, but German signals those roles mainly through determiners and assigns them lexically. Use the masculine landmark der, den, dem, des. With two-way prepositions, ask established location or destination relation: in der Türkei versus in die Türkei. Decide the scene, then the German frame, then gender and number, and only then the ending.
