## 1. Does drilling conjugation tables in Anki work? What do experienced users recommend?

**The dominant recommendation is "one card = one form, cued by a sentence context," not tables.**

- The most cited artifact in this space is **"Ultimate Spanish Conjugation" (KOFI method)** by Lisardo ([manual](https://www.asiteaboutnothing.net/w_ultimate_spanish_conjugation.php), [AnkiWeb deck](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/214482333), [announcement on Anki Forums](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/ultimate-spanish-conjugation/2839)). 4,239 cards, 72 verbs covering all 39 conjugation pattern groups, every tense/mood. Each card = one (verb, tense, person) combination presented as a cloze inside a short Spanish frame sentence. Explicit design rationale: whole-tense cards get memorized as "songs" — the French manual states: *"when I would need to produce the words 'they have', I would find myself mentally repeating that little song until getting to han"* ([KOFI French manual](https://www.asiteaboutnothing.net/w_kofi-french.php)). One-form-per-card also lets the scheduler target only the forms you actually miss: "You might master five conjugation forms but consistently miss a sixth — consolidating them wastes time." Tense/person are signaled by **verbal cues in the target language** ("Maintenant," / "L'année dernière" / "Fue sorprendente que") plus **symbols** (⊙ present, → future, 〰 subjunctive) and color, explicitly to avoid the meta-linguistic prompt "3rd person plural future." Reviewers of the Spanish deck "praised the use of sentences rather than asking for specific conjugation forms like 'ser 2pp'". KOFI ports exist for **French** ([AnkiWeb 1131659186](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1131659186)), **Italian** ([AnkiWeb 1891639832](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1891639832), [manual](https://www.asiteaboutnothing.net/w_ultimate_italian_conjugation.php)) and **Portuguese** (multiple source-language versions). Pacing advice: ~7 new cards/day, one full verb every 3 days, 6–10 months to finish. No typing — grade mentally.

- The **Anki Forums thread "How do you practice grammar, especially verb conjugations, with flash cards?"** ([forums.ankiweb.net/t/47795](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/how-do-you-practice-grammar-especially-verb-conjugations-with-flash-cards/47795)) shows the range of practice: user *Ammy* uses per-tense subdecks, separates regular vs irregular verbs grouped by pattern, and puts stem and suffix in separate fields to highlight the pattern (front: "Parler (speak) Imparfait, 1st person singular" → back: "Je parl*ais*"). *Matta* recommends **mixed question types** — translation cards with grammar hints (person/number/tense/mood) plus fill-in-the-blank sentences "forcing patterns into memory rather than requiring grammatical analysis." *Brumby* uses cloze with grammatical hints via the ["Fill the Blanks" add-on](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1933645497) (`{{c1::answer::(hint)}}` with multiple `type:cloze` blanks). Cautionary datapoint in the same thread: *Lagrave* reports **conjugation cards decay fast past ~1-month intervals**, "requiring restart despite consistent use" — i.e., conjugation seems to need higher review frequency than vocab.

- A Portuguese speed-run learner ([speedlang.substack.com](https://speedlang.substack.com/p/week-2-30-day-portuguese-language)) built their own deck from Wiktionary data because existing decks were "unusable": *"It asks for the correct conjugation in the context of a sentence (so no weird 'what is cantar in the 1st person singular present indicative' stuff)."* Cards have optional typed answer with spelling correction, audio, translation, and the full conjugation table on the back as reference. Motivation: *"I feel my lack of conjugation knowledge really limits the amount of speech I can produce myself."*

- **Fluent Forever (Gabriel Wyner)** is the most systematic pro-grammar-cards methodology. For German grammar ([blog](https://blog.fluent-forever.com/how-to-learn-german-grammar-with-anki/)): keep morphology cards atomic and separate — *"Every time I do that [combine forms], I regret it. You save a lot of time in the long run keeping them all as separate as possible."* Declension: "(der/die/das) Auto ist klein" → answer + grammatical function; one card per case/gender combination, "at least one model for each and every basic morphology challenge." Conjugation: "Ich ___ reich (sein)" → "bin", ~10 separate cards per verb so you "form 6 boxes in your head." Word order via **insertion** cards ("Erik, nach Hause (kommt)" → "Erik *kommt* nach Hause") and **transformation** cards (statement → Ja/Nein question). Prepositions get a 3-card treatment: which case it governs (in context), fill-in-the-blank, and meaning. Strong advice to **write your own sentences**: *"It turns the abstract, external idea of 'German Grammar' into your grammar and your examples"* — but verify them: "Make sure to double-check them on Lang-8 (or make your examples come from 100% proofread sources)." His general word-form card gallery ([New Word Form Flashcards](https://blog.fluent-forever.com/new-word-form-flashcards/)) uses production (sentence with blank → form), comprehension (form → think of any usage), and optional spelling cards, all **without translations on the card** (pictures + context instead; see also [Learning grammar without translation](https://blog.fluent-forever.com/learning-grammar-without-translation/)).

**The dissenting camp: input-based methods say don't SRS grammar at all (or barely).**

- **Refold** ([Grammar Study library page](https://refold.la/roadmap/library/grammar-study), [Stage 1C](https://refold.la/roadmap/stage-1/c/grammar/)): grammar study is only a *"priming activity"* to help you notice patterns in immersion. *"Traditional grammar drills are particularly inefficient because learners focus on the form of their production rather than on meaning"* (citing Wong & VanPatten 2003). *"Don't expect to 'learn' grammar from studying it. Real acquisition happens through immersion."* 10–15 min/day max; "just-in-time" lookup when you notice a pattern beats predetermined order. Their Anki usage is **sentence cards with one unknown element**, where the unknown may be a grammar structure ("1T sentences") ([Advanced Sentence Mining](https://refold.la/roadmap/stage-2/b/advanced-sentence-mining/)).
- **AJATT/Tatsumoto lineage** ([Discussing various card templates](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/discussing-various-card-templates.html)): *"There's sometimes a misunderstanding that SRS can be used to learn grammar and output; in reality the role of the SRS is to aid comprehension… Output can only come from lots of exposure."* Same site is openly hostile to cloze for language: *"there is always an infinite number of possible answers for any given 'fill in the blank' question. But the card only allows for one correct answer"*; cloze cards "train memorization rather than comprehension" and are *"generally considered ineffective for language study."* They recommend **Targeted Sentence Cards** (sentence with target word highlighted; up to 4x faster to review than plain sentence cards).
- A middle position from **A Mind for Language** ([Anki cloze cards for learning paradigms](https://amindforlanguage.com/blog/anki_tables.html)): memorizing tables "is probably not worth too much effort," but *"familiarity… has been helpful to me"* — paradigm familiarity aids recognition even if it doesn't create fluency, and *"Should creating and studying flashcards in Anki replace other learning activities that focus on comprehensible input? No."*
- On Hacker News' big FSRS thread, *jamager*: *"Language acquisition is not about memorizing words, but progressive familiarization (e.g. via retrieval practice)"* — consensus there was cards support immersion, don't replace it ([HN 39002138](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39002138)).

**Production vs recognition:** general community line is recognition-first (TL→NL), production later and sparingly. A recent guide summarizing the debate: *"For most learners, recognition cards are best… production cards can help later but are slower and can create frustration early; balanced approach: recognition-first, production-second"* ([wordy.info Anki guide](https://wordy.info/blog/anki-for-language-learning-guide)). On the Anki Forums ([Which direction for cloze cards?](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/which-direction-for-cloze-cards/65411)), user *sorata*: *"Bidirectional is only useful in the beginning. After that, you should use TL → NL. At higher levels this should become TL → TL"* — i.e., for an advanced learner, monolingual cards with TL synonym hints. Note the tension: conjugation/grammar repair is inherently a **production** task, and the pro-drill camp (KOFI, Fluent Forever, the Portuguese speedrunner) all use production-format cards for exactly that reason, while the input camp says SRS can't teach production at all.

## 2. Known failure modes

- **Interference between similar forms.** The Anki manual itself names it as a leech cause: *"an English learner may have recently learned the words 'disappoint' and 'disappear'… confusing the two when trying to answer"*; remedies = rewrite the card per the 20 rules, delete it, or *"leave one of the words suspended until you have learned the other one well, and then unsuspend it"* ([Anki manual: Leeches](https://docs.ankiweb.net/leeches.html)). SuperMemo's canonical [20 rules](https://www.supermemo.com/en/blog/twenty-rules-of-formulating-knowledge) (rules: minimum information principle, avoid sets/enumerations, combat interference) are the standard citation — complex cards asking for whole paradigms violate the minimum information principle; enumerations (= conjugation tables) are explicitly listed as material to avoid or convert to cloze. For heavily syncretic paradigms, Eric "Siggy" Scott's writeup ([When the Inductive Method Fails](https://medium.com/euthyphroria/when-the-inductive-method-fails-learning-very-complex-grammar-with-anki-4ea4c4174ee4)) is the best interference case study: endings like -u "do a lot of work" across paradigms so pure sentence exposure felt "largely random"; *"flash cards work best if I already understand the grammar well enough to know why I got a card wrong."* His fix: a two-tier system — a few **"landmark" overview-table cards** plus **targeted detail cards** drilling one confusable ending across its different functions; learning the structural system explicitly "reduced complexity back to a manageable (but non-zero) level." He explicitly rejected: direct factual cards ("what is the feminine genitive ending for ō-stems?"), chanting drills, full-table production cards, and "why does this word have this form" cards (rote memorization traps).
- **Leeches / rapid decay.** *Lagrave*'s report that conjugation cards collapse beyond 1-month intervals ([47795](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/how-do-you-practice-grammar-especially-verb-conjugations-with-flash-cards/47795)). On HN, *ekidd* auto-suspends after 3 misses and trusts that important items resurface in input ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39002138)).
- **"Pattern-matching the card, not the grammar."** Three variants reported: (a) the "song" problem — reciting the memorized table sequence instead of direct recall (KOFI manuals); (b) memorizing the specific sentence so the cloze answer pops out without processing grammar — the sentence-mining community's standard critique of cloze, and the reason Tatsumoto calls cloze ineffective; countermeasures people use are progressively-hiding-more card sequences and sourcing sentences from your own immersion ([workbookpdf cloze guide](https://workbookpdf.com/learn/anki-cloze), [Steven Legg](https://www.steven-legg.com/anki-cloze-cards.html)); (c) audio cards training recognition of *that recording*, not listening skill (Tatsumoto).
- **Ambiguity — multiple valid answers.** The single most repeated complaint about cloze grammar cards. Speakada's author abandoned cloze entirely over it: *"you could actually have multiple answers for this card… because you are learning a foreign language, I don't think there should be any room for error"*; verdict: *"No. I would suggest trying to create sentence cards instead"* ([Speakada cloze guide](https://speakada.com/cloze-deletion-anki-guide-my-experience-and-what-i-use-instead/)). Migaku's guide: *"If you delete the verb but the sentence could work with multiple tenses, you'll confuse yourself during review. The surrounding context should make the correct answer relatively clear"* ([Migaku cloze guide](https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/cloze-deletion-language-learning-guide)). This is why KOFI adds explicit tense/person cues rather than relying on the sentence alone.
- **Paradigm overload.** Both camps agree on not testing a whole paradigm at once (minimum information principle). Disagreement on whether the *whole table may appear*: Fluent Forever says keep forms maximally separate; A Mind for Language deliberately shows the full table with only a few cells blanked so *"we are only asking ourselves to recall a few forms at a time"* while "seeing the connections between the current form and the rest of the paradigm"; Scott's landmark cards similarly keep a few whole-table cards for orientation.
- **Boredom/burnout** shows up mostly as advice to keep grammar decks small and short-session (Refold's 10–15 min; HN *laurieg*: make cards *"slightly too easy, so that in a week or two it will be enough to tickle your memory"*; the orchard metaphor: *"grab one or two of the nicest apples… If you try to grab everything you're only going to slow yourself down"* — [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39002138)).

## 3. Popular shared grammar decks in the five languages

- **Spanish**: *Ultimate Spanish Conjugation (Vosotros)* — KOFI, cloze-in-sentence, one form/card, 4,239 cards ([AnkiWeb 214482333](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/214482333)); reviews praise sentence prompts over "ser 2pp"-style prompts and "only the 39 unique conjugation patterns with maximum efficiency." *Spanish Sentences* ([1169967797](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1169967797)) — EN/ES/cloze fields, reviewers complained cloze setup was unclear; *9000 Spanish sentences difficulty-sorted with native audio* ([1713698257](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1713698257)) — cloze convertible to basic; *575 Advanced Spanish Verbs with 3–5 usage sentences* ([1274644414](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1274644414)). Speakada sells [Spanish grammar bundles](https://speakada.com/spanish-grammar-bundle/) (comprehension + cloze + multiple-choice cards, rule on the back — "pop-up grammar", CEFR-graded, audio+IPA).
- **French**: *Ultimate French Conjugation (Lisardo's KOFI)* ([1131659186](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1131659186), collab with Moritz, released 2024) — all tenses incl. subjunctive, ~7 cards/day ≈ 6 months; *French Verb Conjugations* ([1856402207](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1856402207)) and *(Irregular)* ([695270790](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/695270790)); *70 French Verbs Conjugation Trainer in 10 Languages* ([1040931494](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1040931494)); Speakada [French grammar bundle](https://speakada.com/french-grammar-bundle/).
- **Italian**: *Ultimate Italian Conjugation (KOFI)* ([1891639832](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1891639832)) — "sixty verbs inside out, including the top fifty, and all the patterns," ~7 months. For clitics (ci/ne etc.) no notable shared Anki deck surfaced — community practice resources are exercise sites (italki drills, [Simone Pols exercises](https://simonepols.com/italian-grammar-en/clitics-in-italian-ci-and-ne/)) rather than decks.
- **Portuguese**: *Modern Brazilian Portuguese Verb Conjugations* ([698689428](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/698689428)) — 16 verbs, one form per card, EN→PT only; *Portuguese (BR) Verb Conjugation Trainer v0.9* ([1613320919](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1613320919)); *Comprehensive Brazilian Portuguese Verb Conjugation* ([1871075595](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1871075595)); [PT_ConjugationTrainer on GitHub](https://github.com/Troyciv/PT_ConjugationTrainer_Anki); the speedlang author's Wiktionary-generated 57-verb sentence-context deck (born of frustration that "available conjugation decks were unusable").
- **German**: *German Grammar Declension rules (Cases!)* ([1911029133](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1911029133)); *German Adjective Endings* ([896136496](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/896136496)); *German Grammar [@jackkinsella]* ([113138392](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/113138392)); *Practice Makes Perfect Complete German Grammar* ([1376959097](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1376959097)); *Der-Die-Das 4220 German Nouns Gender Trainer* ([anki-decks.com listing](https://anki-decks.com/anki-decks/german/der-die-das-4220-german-nouns-gende/)). Notable template rather than deck: **Monoglot Anxiety's German template** ([blog](https://www.monoglotanxiety.com/blog/german-anki-template/), [Gumroad](https://monoglotanxiety.gumroad.com/l/saHJaW)) — generates EN→DE, DE→EN, and **auto-cloze** cards from an example sentence, auto color-codes nouns by gender (detected from the article) and flags irregular verbs; documented failure mode: the auto-cloze script "mistakes words that begin with verb stems for a version of the verb, and deletes them." Community advice on gender generally: learn noun+article as one unit ("it's not Tisch, it's der Tisch"), color/icon coding as visual shortcut ([heylama](https://www.heylama.com/blog/der-die-das-mastering-german-noun-genders), [Speakada on gender](https://speakada.com/grammatical-gender-and-how-to-learn-gender-nouns-with-anki/)).
- Adjacent tool frequently recommended instead of building grammar decks: **Clozemaster's "Grammar Challenges"** (cloze sentences filtered by grammar point — Spanish subjunctive, German cases, etc.); reviews say it's great vocab-in-context practice but "fluency requires more than that" ([Clozemaster vs Anki](https://www.clozemaster.com/blog/clozemaster-vs-anki/), [alllanguageresources review](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/clozemaster-review/), [langoly](https://www.langoly.com/clozemaster-review/)).

## 4. Cloze format specifics

- **Hints in cloze**: widely used for grammar — `{{c1::form::(person/tense hint)}}`; the [Fill the Blanks add-on](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1933645497) supports typing into multiple clozes with hints (Anki Forums 47795). KOFI's alternative is in-sentence temporal cues + symbols instead of grammatical labels, to keep the prompt inside the language.
- **One vs multiple clozes per note**: Migaku: one deletion per card; multiple c1/c2/c3 per note is fine "just make sure each card focuses on one specific item"; "if you blank out half the words it stops being a focused retrieval exercise and becomes a frustrating puzzle" ([Migaku](https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/cloze-deletion-language-learning-guide)). Guides commonly cap at 2–3 deletions per note; paradigm-table cards use multiple clozes across cells (A Mind for Language). A progressive-hiding sequence over the same sentence is recommended specifically for grammar patterns ([workbookpdf](https://workbookpdf.com/learn/anki-cloze)).
- **Context requirements**: the sentence must uniquely determine the answer (tense-ambiguous sentences are the #1 complaint); 5–15 word sentences suggested; best sentences come from your own immersion/sentence mining because "you already have a memory attached to the source."
- **Translations**: Fluent Forever says no translations on cards at all (pictures + TL context). The sentence-mining camp says front = TL only; translation/definition belongs on the back or in the Extra field ("don't clutter the main sentence with meta-information that belongs on the answer side"). For advanced learners, forum advice is TL→TL with TL synonym hints ([65411](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/which-direction-for-cloze-cards/65411)). Speakada/Universe-of-Memory style rule decks put the rule explanation on the back only ("pop-up grammar").
- **Typing answers / mobile**: type-in is valued for conjugation spelling (accents!); the Portuguese speedrunner made typing optional with spelling correction; KOFI deliberately avoids typing (mental answer + self-grade) for speed. Known mobile friction: long type-in answers are hard to see/type on AnkiMobile ([forum thread](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/anki-mobile-typing-in-answer/11174)); general thread on the feature: [Type in the answer?](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/type-in-the-answer/17035). Community pattern: make typing optional so mobile reviews stay viable.

## 5. LLM-generated grammar cards

- Most-cited negative result: **"AI-generated Anki cards are not to be trusted"** ([denovo.substack.com](https://denovo.substack.com/p/ai-generated-anki-cards-are-not-to)) — 100 Claude-generated cards, 2 obvious errors plus 1 subtle-but-catastrophic one that passed initial review (reversed gradient direction); Gemini 2.5 Pro failed to spot it even when pointed at it. *"Learning incorrect things is really bad… at this point I don't think using LLMs to generate Anki cards is worth the risk."* (Biology, but the "subtle error survives review" failure mode is the community's core worry.)
- Positive/pragmatic camp: [Polyglossic's ChatGPT→CSV→Anki workflow](https://www.polyglossic.com/chatgpt-to-anki-vocabulary-decks/) — prompt engineering to force "accurate and colloquial, never bookish or unnatural" output, heavy emphasis on iterating prompts; [Traipsing About](https://www.traipsingabout.com/p/how-im-using-ai-to-turbocharge-my) (Italian/Spanish/Portuguese) generates "common mistakes intermediate-advanced learners make" cards and cloze cards from reading highlights (format: "(He visto) esa película…" → "he visto"), but *"I scan the results for quality control"* and a live teacher caught register problems (AI slang "carried Mexican gangster vibes"). HyperTTS for audio.
- Deck authors who ship AI sentences hedge: e.g., a Serbian deck README ranks AI-produced example sentences below human-sourced content because "AI-generated example sentences should not be solely considered to be the source of truth" ([github](https://github.com/sowcow/serbian-anki-deck)); AI-flashcard tools themselves tell users to review each batch before study ([studycardsai](https://studycardsai.com/blog/ai-flashcard-generator-for-anki)). Add-ons exist that generate fresh example sentences per review ([anki-ai-dynamic-cards](https://github.com/AleksandrFurmenkovOfficial/anki-ai-dynamic-cards)) — which incidentally attacks the "memorized the sentence" failure mode, at the cost of unverified content.
- Net community position: LLMs are accepted for *formatting/reorganizing verified material* and for high-volume sentence generation in big languages **if a human (or teacher) reviews**; unreviewed generation is considered risky mainly for subtle grammatical/register errors and, in morphology-heavy languages, wrong gender/agreement.

## 6. FSRS and grammar cards

- Consensus that **FSRS fixes "ease hell"** that language decks (with many similar, failure-prone cards) suffered under SM-2; moderator advice to language learners unhappy with default scheduling: *"Go ahead [switch to FSRS]. Make sure to optimise (and grade cards honestly)"* ([Advanced settings for language learning](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/advanced-settings-for-language-learning/48475)).
- **Mixed easy/hard content skews FSRS parameters.** *Oktoberpaard* (7,000-card typing-based language deck): excluding never-failed cards made RMSE jump ~3%→12%, and noted *"the use of 'again' for slight errors as well as for complete lapses of memory makes it harder for FSRS to accurately predict the optimal interval"* — i.e., grading a typo the same as a real lapse (exactly what happens with typed conjugation cards) degrades scheduling. Recommended fix from *Aleksej*: separate decks/presets for different card types ([FSRS on a deck with many easy cards](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/fsrs-on-a-deck-with-many-easy-cards/54281)). Implication repeated elsewhere: give grammar/conjugation cards their own preset optimized separately from vocab.
- FSRS gives longer first intervals but is more conservative on mature cards than SM-2 ([FSRS FAQ](https://faqs.ankiweb.net/frequently-asked-questions-about-fsrs.html)) — relevant given *Lagrave*'s observation that conjugation knowledge decays fast at >1-month intervals; the community answer is raising desired retention for such decks rather than fighting the algorithm.
- From the [HN FSRS thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39002138), the most-agreed point: scheduling is not the bottleneck for language cards — *ekidd*: *"I don't think the biggest improvements will come from better spaced repetition algorithms. I suspect the biggest wins will come from improved card formats"*; his own practice is paragraph-length cloze (hide one word in a pasted paragraph) plus aggressive auto-suspension of leeches.

## Cross-cutting disagreements worth keeping visible

1. **Cloze is simultaneously the most-recommended grammar format** (Fluent Forever, KOFI, Migaku, Speakada-the-store, Anki Forums users) **and declared "generally ineffective" by the AJATT/Tatsumoto camp and abandoned by Speakada-the-blogger** — the split hinges entirely on whether the card's context uniquely determines the answer.
2. **Isolated forms vs visible paradigm**: Wyner says never combine; A Mind for Language and Scott keep a few whole-table "landmark" cards and blank cells within them.
3. **Whether SRS can teach production/grammar at all**: KOFI/FF/forum practitioners say yes with the right format; Refold/Tatsumoto say SRS only aids comprehension and grammar is acquired from input; middle ground (Scott, A Mind for Language, HN) says explicit cards work as scaffolding for noticing, especially where paradigms are too complex for induction — which matches an advanced learner "repairing" grammar.
