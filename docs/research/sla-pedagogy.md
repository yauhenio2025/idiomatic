# SLA research: effective grammar practice and sequencing for proficient adult learners

> Commissioned research, 2026-07-28. Raw findings; synthesis lives in
> ../GRAMMAR_STRATEGY.md. "Strong" = multiple experiments and/or
> meta-analysis; "moderate" = several studies, some conflicting;
> "weak/emerging" = few studies or expert opinion only.

## 1. Retrieval practice for morphology

**Retrieval practice works for grammar, not just vocabulary — but production and recognition build partly different knowledge (strong evidence for skill specificity).**
- DeKeyser (1997) trained learners on an artificial language over a semester: learners who practiced production performed far worse when tested on comprehension and vice versa — declarative knowledge is shared, but procedural knowledge is skill-specific ([DeKeyser & Suzuki 2025 chapter, preprint PDF](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/PreprintDeKeyser-R.-M.-Suzuki-Y.-2025.-Skill-acquisition-theory.-In-B.-VanPatten-G.-D.-Keating-S.-Wulff-Eds.-Theories-in-second-language-acquisition-An-introduction-4th-ed.-pp.-157-182-.pdf)). Implication: if the goal is accurate production, practice must be productive; recognition-only cards will not transfer fully.
- The best single study for flashcard-style grammar practice: **Serfaty & Serrano (2024), Language Learning** — 119 adults typed translations of sentences into an artificial language on digital flashcards with feedback, over 1–4 relearning sessions on consecutive days. At 14 days, *receptive* knowledge was high for everyone, but *productive* knowledge required 3–4 relearning sessions; critically, learners attained durable productive knowledge once they completed **two error-free sessions**, regardless of total sessions — an empirically grounded "mastery criterion" for dropping an item ([Serfaty & Serrano 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12585)).
- Retrieval-format research in L2 (mostly vocabulary, cautiously extendable): recall formats beat recognition when productive/orthographic knowledge is the goal; recognition suffices when it isn't ([Nakata 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/294443124_Effects_of_retrieval_formats_on_second_language_vocabulary_learning)). A CUNY dissertation found retrieval practice benefits grammar even via recognition tests ([Retrieval Practice Promotes Learning of Turkish](https://academicworks.cuny.edu/gc_etds/5083/)).

**Typing vs. mentally recalling: covert retrieval is as effective as overt (strong, from cognitive psychology).**
- Smith, Roediger & Karpicke (2013, JEP:LMC): covert (silent, in-the-head) retrieval benefits retention as much as overt retrieval (writing/typing/speaking); a mini meta-analysis across four studies found comparable effect magnitudes ([PDF](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2013/2013_Smith_Roediger_Karpicke_JEPLMC.pdf); [Frontiers 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01018/full)). Caveat: declarative-memory materials; for L2 *procedural* skill, skill-specificity argues some practice should be in the actual output modality.

**Error-driven learning / pretesting (moderate-to-strong in cognitive psych).**
- Potts & Shanks (2014) and successors: errorful generation/pretesting improves subsequent memory relative to errorless study ([review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20445911.2020.1711766); [Pan & Sana 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348155988_Pan_and_Sana_2021_Pretesting_vs_posttesting_Errorful_generation_prequestions_and_retrieval_practice)). Caveats: improves memory for items more than associations ([Learning from failure](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328065843_Learning_from_failure_Errorful_generation_improves_memory_for_items_not_associations)); robustness varies by test format ([Memory & Cognition 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-025-01813-x)). Implication: attempting the conjugation before feedback is desirable; errors during practice are a feature, provided immediate corrective feedback follows.

**Desirable difficulties framework applied to L2.**
- Suzuki, Nakata & DeKeyser (2019, MLJ special issue): performance during practice does not predict retention; effortful conditions (spacing, interleaving, retrieval, generation) depress practice performance but improve long-term learning; difficulty must be calibrated ([intro paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334457807_Optimizing_Second_Language_Practice_in_the_Classroom_Perspectives_from_Cognitive_Psychology); [coda PDF](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Suzuki-Nakata-DeKeyser-2019-MLJ-Desirable-Difficulty.pdf)).

**Interleaving vs. blocking for confusable forms — the headline finding (strong and directly on preterite/imperfect).**
- **Pan, Tajran, Lovelett, Osuna & Rickard (2019, JEdPsych)**: 4 experiments, Spanish preterite vs. imperfect. Blocking beat interleaving *during training*; reversed on delayed tests — in the multi-session designs ([PDF](https://rickardlab.ucsd.edu/pdf/PTLOR-2019.pdf)).
- **Nakata & Suzuki (2019, MLJ)**: 115 learners, five confusable English structures. Interleaving produced the most training errors but beat blocking on the 1-week delayed test; lower-pretest learners benefited more ([PDF](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Nakata-Suzuki-2019-MLJ.pdf)). Mechanisms: spacing, Discriminative Contrast Hypothesis, transfer-appropriate processing.
- **Pan, Rodríguez Flores, Kaku & Lai (2025, Learning and Instruction)**: interleaving improved conjugation across *seven* tenses in *two* Romance languages, similar and dissimilar pairs; Experiment 4: **improved language identification when two Romance languages were mixed** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475224001725)). Most direct evidence that contrastive/mixed practice of confusable Romance morphology helps.
- Nuance: **Carpenter & Mueller (2013)**: blocking beat interleaving for French pronunciation rules ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234142482_The_effects_of_interleaving_versus_blocking_on_foreign_language_pronunciation_learning)). **Suzuki & Sunada (2020)**: "synergistic" schedule best ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2211368119301329)).
- Aside: Pan et al. analyzed 25 Spanish coursebooks; 24 introduce preterite and imperfect blocked — commercial sequencing contradicts the lab evidence.
- Consensus: block briefly when a form is brand-new, then interleave confusable forms; for an advanced learner, interleaving/contrastive mixing is the supported default.

**Spacing specifics for grammar (moderate; lag findings conflict).**
- Bird (2010), Rogers (2015): 7+ day gaps beat short gaps for syntax retention. **Suzuki & DeKeyser (2017)**: *shorter* spacing (1 day) better than 7 days for proceduralizing morphology ([PDF](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Suzuki-DeKeyser-2017-LTR.pdf)); Kasprowicz et al. (2019) failed to replicate long-lag advantages. Kim & Webb (2022) meta-analysis (48 experiments) confirms robust overall spacing benefit. Reconciliation: proceduralization benefits from short initial lags; retention of established knowledge from expanding long lags — essentially what an SRS does.

## 2. Explicit vs. implicit; metalinguistic explanation; focus on form; Processing Instruction

**Explicit instruction wins for adults in the meta-analyses (strong, with a measurement caveat).**
- Norris & Ortega (2000), Goo et al. (2015), Spada & Tomita (2010), Kang, Sok & Han (2019) all favor explicit; especially for low-salience, morphologically marked structures. Caveat: most outcome measures tapped explicit/controlled knowledge ([discussion](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1397178.pdf)); implicit training can yield more fluent production ([Applied Linguistics 2021](https://academic.oup.com/applij/article/42/4/668/6009751)). Reading: metalinguistic explanation on cards is supported, but explanation is not the endpoint; timed meaningful use is.
- ICALL: Heift — metalinguistic, error-specific feedback beats right/wrong in intelligent language tutoring ([Heift](https://www.collectionscanada.gc.ca/eppp-archive/100/201/300/zeitschrift/2002/02-05/heift2.htm)).

**Focus on form (moderate-to-strong).**
- Long/Ellis: reactive (error-triggered) or planned; supports error-triggered targeted mini-treatments over comprehensive re-teaching for an advanced learner ([Ellis review](https://scispace.com/pdf/focus-on-form-a-critical-review-1ttwercgnz.pdf)).

**Processing Instruction / structured input (strong within its paradigm).**
- VanPatten's PI: structured input where the learner *must* process the form to get the meaning. **Referential** activities (right/wrong, meaning depends on form — "Juan hablaba" → habitual vs completed) vs **affective** ([overview](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781118784235.eelt0094)). **Henshaw (2012)**, Spanish subjunctive: referential did the heavy lifting; referential+affective best ([LTR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1362168812436919)).
- Input-vs-output war ended in "both, matched to target skill" (Shintani, Li & Ellis 2013 meta-analysis).
- Takeaway: interpretation cards where the form is the only cue to meaning are a validated genre, complementary to production cards.

**Mechanical drills (strong consensus against).**
- Wong & VanPatten (2003) "The Evidence is IN: Drills are OUT" ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1944-9720.2003.tb02123.x)); DeKeyser: *meaningful* practice proceduralizes. Communicative intent during drilling improved oral proficiency gains ([Serfaty, TESOL Quarterly](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/tesq.70155)). Design rule: every drill item requires processing meaning.

**Corrective feedback (strong).**
- Li (2010), Lyster & Saito (2010): CF has medium, durable effects; **prompts** (elicit self-correction) outperform **recasts** ([SSLA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/oral-feedback-in-classroom-sla/4999EE1C8379B2BF026B148EAF373CA1)). Maps to: prefer "try again / self-correct" flows over immediately displaying the answer.

## 3. Skill acquisition theory (DeKeyser)

([DeKeyser & Suzuki 2025 chapter preprint](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/PreprintDeKeyser-R.-M.-Suzuki-Y.-2025.-Skill-acquisition-theory.-In-B.-VanPatten-G.-D.-Keating-S.-Wulff-Eds.-Theories-in-second-language-acquisition-An-introduction-4th-ed.-pp.-157-182-.pdf))
- **Declarative → procedural → automatized.** Declarative fast; proceduralization fast given a few meaningful practice trials with the rule available; automatization slow (power law, hundreds-thousands of retrievals).
- **Applies to**: adult, explicit-instruction contexts, learners with metalinguistic ability — precisely an advanced adult doing deliberate grammar repair.
- **Skill specificity**: procedural skill barely transfers comprehension↔production or untimed↔speeded; practice must mirror target use.
- **Implications**: (a) rule refresh right before initial practice; (b) meaningful items only; (c) short lags early, expanding later; (d) progressive time pressure; (e) procedural knowledge decays slower than declarative.
- **Measuring automatization**: RT + **coefficient of variation** (Segalowitz & Segalowitz 1993): falling CV alongside falling RT indicates genuine automatization ([CV paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228735660_Automatization_in_second_language_acquisition_What_does_the_coefficient_of_variation_tell_us); [Suzuki, Maie & Hui 2025 timeline](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/30C96770C708A4A9513570F47F40F054/S026144482500059Xa.pdf/research-timeline-automatization-in-second-language-learning.pdf)). Response latency on cards is a meaningful automatization proxy, distinct from accuracy.

## 4. Sequencing

**Developmental sequences / Processability Theory (moderate).**
- Pienemann's PT: fixed processing stages (German ZISA word-order sequence); **Teachability Hypothesis**: instruction cannot skip stages ([Pienemann 2015](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12095)). Caveats: PT covers a limited set of phenomena; says little about tense/aspect/mood *choice*, where advanced Romance gaps mostly live. For an advanced learner most PT stages are passed; residual lesson is diagnostic.
- Textbooks routinely ignore acquisition orders; the Spanish tense-aspect literature criticizes traditional preterite-then-imperfect blocked order ([JSLT 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23247797.2024.2434334)).

**CEFR-based ordering (descriptive convention, not acquisition evidence).**
- Grammar inventories are third-party reconstructions (Kwiziq's French: A1-A2 present/passé composé/imparfait; B1 future, conditional, present subjunctive; B2 pluperfect, fuller subjunctive; C1 full control + literary tenses). Consensus curricular sequences, not validated acquisition orders.

**Diagnostic-then-targeted vs. linear for an advanced learner (converging support).**
- Fossilization literature: persistent errors don't fix themselves with exposure; destabilization requires noticing, targeted CF, self-monitoring; diagnostic testing is the standard recommendation ([ERIC EJ1081500](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1081500.pdf)).
- DIALANG is the established CEFR-linked diagnostic instrument.
- ITS/mastery-learning tradition: adaptive ITSs approach human-tutoring effect sizes (VanLehn 2011: d≈0.76 vs ~0.79). Net: diagnostic-then-targeted architecture better supported than marching through a syllabus; CEFR inventories as a *checklist of candidate gaps*.

## 5. Adaptive/personalized learning

- BKT strains under language's huge overlapping skill inventory; DKT captures cross-skill transfer ([BKT-LSTM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.12218); [Variational DKT](https://hci.stanford.edu/research/smartprimer/pdfs/lak21-31.pdf)).
- **Duolingo Half-Life Regression** (Settles & Meeder, ACL 2016): memory half-life as a function of practice history and *lexeme/morphological features* — direct precedent for tracking morphological tags as skills rather than whole items ([paper](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/settles.acl16.pdf); [code](https://github.com/duolingo/halflife-regression)).
- Duolingo 2018 SLAM shared task: per-exercise error prediction feasible; best systems used token morphology + *response time* ([CLUF](https://sharedtask.duolingo.com/papers/xu.slam18.pdf)).
- **Informative signals**: binary correctness; response latency; error *type* patterns (Heift's E-Tutor kept per-learner grammar-error profiles, deployed 10+ years: [IJAIED 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-015-0061-0); TAGARELA did the same for Portuguese).
- **Anki-specific**: FSRS predicts recall better than SM-2 for ~99.5% of users, ~20-30% fewer reviews at equal retention ([srs-benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)). Caveat: FSRS models *item memory*, not *skill/rule mastery*; nothing in the SRS literature handles "many cards instantiate one rule" — KT-style skill features (HLR's tags) are the precedent.
- **LLMs as grammar tutors/diagnosticians (emerging, mixed)**: GPT-4 explains grammatical errors with ~90% accuracy in human evals ([GEC feedback](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.09565)); LLMs are SOTA *evaluators* of corrections. Pitfalls: systematic **overcorrection**, fluency edits erasing learner voice (worst for advanced learners); hallucinated corrections; ~35% of GPT-4 tutoring hints too general/wrong/answer-revealing; weak at *detecting whether an error occurred* ([math-tutor error study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.03238); [AIED 2026 GEC eval](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07635)). Consensus: constrain to minimal edits, use for explanation and item generation with verification, don't trust unaided error detection for learner modeling.

## 6. Multi-language simultaneous study (es/pt/it/fr + de)

- L3 transfer: prior *Romance* languages dominate as transfer source; Rothman's Typological Primacy Model ([Rothman 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0267658310386439)). es↔pt↔it interference is structural and expected.
- Contrastive/plurilingual pedagogy: intercomprehension (EuroComRom) exploits similarity; Woll & Paquet's plurilingual consciousness-raising tasks build crosslinguistic awareness ([LTR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/13621688211056544)). **Pan et al. 2025 Exp. 4: interleaving two Romance languages improved language identification, rather than increasing confusion.**
- **No controlled research on same-day scheduling** of cognate languages; "keep them separate" is practitioner lore. For an advanced learner the interleaving + discriminative-contrast evidence tilts toward deliberate contrastive exposure being helpful; separation matters most when one language is weak.

## 7. Audio-based grammar practice

- **Pimsleur anticipation drills**: mechanism = retrieval practice with immediate feedback under time pressure — well-supported; the specific graduated intervals were never empirically validated; no controlled product trials (Choe 2016, [PDF](https://www.hpu.edu/research-publications/tesol-working-papers/2016/08ChoeTaiAnn.pdf)).
- **Elicited imitation** (moderate): validated as a proficiency measure; promising as practice with caveats about parroting ([SSLA meta-analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/elicited-imitation-tasks-as-a-measure-of-l2-proficiency/C52297B9096EF1DA34FA28639242E3ED)).
- **Listening cloze / "beep" exercises**: no substantial direct experimental literature; theoretically grounded via structured-input listening tasks (robust) + auditory retrieval practice. Consistent with, not directly tested in, the retrieval literature.
- **Shadowing** (moderate for fluency/listening/pronunciation; weak for grammar): a fluency/prosody tool rather than grammar-repair ([2025 systematic review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/29984475.2025.2546827)).

## Cross-cutting consensus and controversy

1. **Consensus**: for adult, already-proficient learners: explicit rule access + meaningful retrieval-based production practice + immediate (preferably metalinguistic/prompt-style) feedback + spaced, interleaved scheduling with progressive time pressure.
2. **Consensus**: interleave confusable morphology rather than blocking — supported at the exact grain of Romance tense morphology (Pan 2019; Nakata & Suzuki 2019; Pan et al. 2025).
3. **Live controversy**: whether explicit-practice-derived automatized knowledge is "real" implicit competence. Practically, implement both camps: structured-input interpretation cards + timed production cards.
4. **Evidence gaps**: same-day scheduling of cognate languages; validated Pimsleur trials; rule-level (vs item-level) SRS scheduling; LLM error detection reliability.
