# Ordre des mots : placer l'information autour du verbe et du nom

French and English often contain the same pieces but arrange them differently.
Your recurring errors concentrate in three small zones: an adverb placed before
the finite verb, *premier/dernier/prochain* placed before a cardinal number, and
a superlative built without its second article. Stabilize the verb frame and
the noun frame separately.

## Put the neutral adverb after a simple finite verb

In an unmarked sentence with a simple tense, many common adverbs follow the
conjugated verb:

- *Je connais déjà ce dossier.*
- *La ministre soutient aussi cette proposition.*
- *Les journalistes vérifient souvent leurs sources.*
- *Le gouvernement veut clairement modifier la loi.*

> **You specifically do this.** You produced *je déjà connais, vous déjà
> savez, il aussi parvient,* and *le gouvernement clairement veut*. Move the
> adverb across the finite verb: *je connais déjà; vous savez déjà; il
> parvient aussi; le gouvernement veut clairement*.

An adverb can be moved to the beginning for discourse focus—*Déjà, plusieurs
experts contestent ce chiffre*—but that marked choice does not license the
English-like sequence *plusieurs experts déjà contestent*. First master the
neutral frame.

## Find the finite element in a compound verb

With a compound tense, short and frequent adverbs often sit between the
auxiliary and the participle: *j'ai déjà lu le rapport; ils ont souvent
critiqué la mesure; la plateforme a aussi supprimé le contenu*. Longer manner
adverbs comfortably follow the participle: *le porte-parole a répondu
prudemment*. Position can vary with focus and rhythm, so treat this as a strong
default rather than an absolute law.

Negatives also reveal the finite element: *on n'arrivera jamais; ils n'ont
jamais rencontré la ministre*. In the second example, *ne ... jamais* surrounds
the auxiliary and *jamais* precedes the participle.

> **You specifically do this.** Your *on ne jamais arrivera* and *ils n'ont
> rencontré jamais* become *on n'arrivera jamais* and *ils n'ont jamais
> rencontré*. Do not send *jamais* to the end by default.

With an infinitive, meaning decides which action the adverb modifies: *ils vont
probablement annoncer un accord* comments on the likelihood of the whole
announcement; *ils promettent de mieux expliquer le modèle* makes *mieux*
modify *expliquer*. Identify the finite verb, then the semantic target.

## Cardinal number before *premier, dernier, prochain*

In a numbered group, French normally places the cardinal immediately after the
determiner, followed by the ordering adjective:

- *les deux premières semaines*;
- *ces dix dernières années*;
- *les trois prochains épisodes*;
- *les soixante premiers sujets*.

> **You specifically do this.** This reversed number-plus-ordering pattern
> appears roughly 90–100 times in your record; attested shapes include *les
> premières deux semaines, les derniers dix ans,* and *les prochaines deux
> semaines*. Build **determiner + number + ordering
> adjective + noun**: *les deux prochaines semaines*.

Agreement still applies. *Semaines* and *années* are feminine plural, hence
*premières, dernières, prochaines*. *Épisodes* and *mois* are masculine plural,
hence *premiers, derniers, prochains*.

## Place common evaluative adjectives before the noun

Many adjectives can change position for meaning, emphasis, or style, so a
single universal rule would be misleading. Your specific recurring pair has a
clear neutral target: *la meilleure solution, la meilleure période*, not *la
solution meilleure* in an ordinary recommendation. Other frequent prenominal
forms include *un nouveau modèle, une grande réforme,* and *un mauvais
résultat*.

The adjective *meilleur* modifies a noun: *une meilleure méthode*. The adverb
*mieux* modifies a verb or adjective: *le système fonctionne mieux; il est
mieux conçu*. Neither normally takes an extra comparative *plus*: avoid *plus
mieux*. Likewise, *pire* already means worse: *une situation pire* or, more
naturally in many contexts, *une situation encore pire*—not *plus pire*.

## Repeat the article inside a postnominal superlative

When the adjective follows the noun, the superlative has its own definite
article:

- *la mesure la plus coûteuse*;
- *le risque le plus grave*;
- *les données les plus récentes*.

The first determiner introduces the noun; the second belongs to the frame
*le/la/les + plus + adjective*. With a possessive, the superlative article
remains visible: *sa décision la plus controversée*. A demonstrative phrase is
often clearer when recast: *parmi ces modèles, les plus récents*. When the
adjective normally precedes the noun, one article is enough: *la meilleure
solution*.

> **You specifically do this.** *La chose plus grave* and *le numéro plus haut*
> need the superlative article: *la chose la plus grave; le nombre le plus
> élevé*. The repair also selects the idiomatic noun *nombre* for a quantity.

Read this skeleton aloud before adding detail: *Ces deux dernières années, le
gouvernement n'a jamais clairement expliqué sa stratégie la plus
controversée.* The numbered noun phrase, finite auxiliary, negative adverb,
manner adverb, and superlative each occupy a motivated position.
