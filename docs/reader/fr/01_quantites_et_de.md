# Quantités : le petit mot *de* qui change tout

French articles tell you whether a noun is definite, indefinite, or partitive:
*les articles*, *des articles*, *du temps*. A quantity expression changes the
job of the noun phrase. Once *beaucoup, trop, assez, peu, combien,* or a measure
has already supplied the idea of quantity, the following link is normally the
invariable **de** (or **d'** before a vowel sound), not a partitive article.

Compare the two questions:

- *Est-ce qu'il y a des articles sur cette crise ?* — Are there any articles?
- *Y a-t-il beaucoup d'articles sur cette crise ?* — Are there many articles?

In the second question, *beaucoup* supplies the quantity, so *des* loses its
article role. The same logic gives *trop de désinformation, assez de preuves,
peu d'argent, combien de pays,* and *une série de podcasts*. Treat the whole
frame as one unit: **quantity + de + noun**.

> **Your attested pattern.** You produced *beaucoup des articles* and
> *beaucoup de l'argent*. Variants of this error appeared 138 times—the most
> repeated individual French pattern in your record. Say the repair as a
> single rhythm: *beaucoup d'articles; beaucoup d'argent*.

The definite article can return when the speaker means a specific, already
identified set. *Beaucoup des articles publiés hier contiennent la même
erreur* means “many **of the** articles published yesterday.” Here *des* is the
contraction of *de + les*, and the phrase after the noun identifies a bounded
set. A useful diagnostic is to expand the English: if “many of the…” makes
sense, *beaucoup des* may be right. If you simply mean “many…,” use *beaucoup
de*.

This distinction also explains:

- *La plupart des électeurs interrogés…* — most **of the** surveyed voters;
- *la plupart d'entre eux* — most of them;
- *beaucoup d'électeurs* — many voters.

With a pronoun, standard French uses **entre**: *plusieurs d'entre eux, la
plupart d'entre elles*. Your recorded *la plupart d'eux* omitted this element.

## Negation and *autres*

After a normal verbal negation, an indefinite or partitive article usually
becomes *de*: *Le rapport contient des chiffres* → *Le rapport ne contient pas
de chiffres*; *Il y a du consensus* → *Il n'y a pas de consensus*. Definite
articles remain definite: *Je ne critique pas les chiffres de l'ONU.* The verb
*être* is another important exception: *Ce n'est pas une preuve.*

Before plural *autres*, indefinite *des* normally reduces to **d'**: *d'autres
sources, d'autres solutions*. Your *des autres choses* should therefore become
*d'autres choses*. But a definite, identified set can retain the contraction:
*l'analyse des autres candidats* means the analysis of the other candidates.

## *An* and *année*: count versus lived span

Your quantity problem often intersects with a second fossilized contrast.
**An** is the neutral counting unit; **année** presents the span or its content.
Age and exact counts favor *an*: *il a quarante ans; un mandat de quatre ans;
trois ans plus tard*. Repeated or qualified periods often favor *année*:
*chaque année, cette année, une année difficile, les années 1990*.

> **Your attested pattern.** *Cet an, chaque an,* and *dans les ans 90*
> recurred across the record. Use the established frames *cette année, chaque
> année,* and *dans les années 90*. Your combined error *beaucoup des ans*
> becomes *beaucoup d'années*—both repairs at once.

The same “unit versus experienced duration” contrast helps with *jour/journée,
matin/matinée,* and *soir/soirée*. A schedule counts units: *deux jours, trois
matins par semaine*. A description fills the period: *une journée décisive, une
matinée de négociations, une soirée électorale*. The boundary is semantic, not
mechanical; learn frequent frames rather than translating “year/day” one word
at a time.

## A compact reading routine

When a sentence contains a quantity, pause at the noun and ask:

1. Is the quantity general? Use *de/d'*: *trop de promesses*.
2. Is it a subset of a definite group? Use *de + article*: *trois des cinq
   propositions*.
3. Is the complement a pronoun? Often use *d'entre*: *la plupart d'entre eux*.
4. Is a negative verb removing an indefinite amount? Usually use *pas de*.
5. Am I counting time, or describing its span? Choose *an* or *année*
   deliberately.

Read this paragraph aloud: *Cette année, beaucoup de gouvernements ont publié
des stratégies sur l'IA. La plupart d'entre elles promettent davantage de
transparence, mais peu de textes définissent des sanctions. Plusieurs des
propositions publiées en juin seront révisées au bout de deux ans.* Every
article now has a reason.
