# Genre et accord : faire circuler l'information

French gender is not merely a label attached to a noun. It is information that
travels through the noun phrase: article, possessive or demonstrative,
adjective, and sometimes past participle. If the noun is feminine plural, every
element that must agree should receive that signal: *ces politiques
indépendantes ont été adoptées*. The most efficient habit is therefore to learn
a noun with a small agreement frame, not in isolation.

Say *une période difficile, cette période, la période actuelle* rather than
memorizing bare *période*. Say *un processus long, ce processus, le processus
politique*. The repeated determiner turns gender into something audible and
retrievable.

> *le cadre*. You said *ce période, mon vie, une processus,* and *le première
> fois*. These are vocabulary-entry failures, not abstract agreement failures:
> relearn each noun with its article.

## Adjectives: attributive and predicate

An adjective normally agrees whether it sits beside the noun or after a linking
verb:

- *une politique indépendante*;
- *la politique est indépendante*;
- *des décisions importantes*;
- *ces décisions semblent importantes*.

Spoken French often hides final written *-e* or *-s*, so written production
requires a deliberate check. First locate the noun that the adjective actually
describes; then copy its gender and number. In *beaucoup de personnes
importantes*, *beaucoup de* does not block agreement: *importantes* still
modifies feminine plural *personnes*.

> **Your attested pattern.** You produced *beaucoup de personnes importants*,
> *une politique très indépendant,* and *Amérique Latin*. The repairs are
> *importantes, indépendante,* and *Amérique latine*. Your data show that the
> article may be correct while the adjective loses the signal, so check the
> right edge of the phrase separately.

Adjective position can change emphasis or meaning. Many descriptive adjectives
follow the noun: *une réforme controversée*. A common group—including *beau,
bon, grand, gros, jeune, joli, mauvais, nouveau, petit, vieux*—often precedes
it. *Meilleur* also normally precedes the noun in your intended neutral sense:
*la meilleure solution, la meilleure période*. Your repeated *la solution
meilleure* is grammatically interpretable under special contrastive emphasis,
but not the default you wanted.

## The *-al → -aux* family

Many masculine nouns and adjectives ending in *-al* form their plural in
*-aux*: *un enjeu principal → des enjeux principaux; un débat social → des
débats sociaux; un marché global → des marchés globaux*. The corresponding
feminine forms are regular: *une question principale → des questions
principales*.

This is a strong pattern, not an absolute law. Familiar exceptions retain
*-als*, including *banals, fatals, natals,* and *navals*. Some words permit
variation in specialized uses. The useful learner rule is to memorize the
plural with each high-frequency *-al* item, not to treat every exception as a
reason to ignore the pattern.

> **Your attested pattern.** About fifty errors used *-als* where *-aux* was
> required: *ennemis principals, marchés globals, groupes socials*. In your
> professional register, automate *principaux, sociaux, mondiaux, globaux,
> nationaux, internationaux*. Note that *international* becomes
> *internationaux* only in masculine plural; *des organisations
> internationales* retains *-ales*.

## Participles: do not overgeneralize

With *être*, a past participle agrees with the subject: *les mesures sont
adoptées; elles sont devenues nécessaires*. With *avoir*, it normally does not
agree with a following object: *le gouvernement a adopté les mesures*. A
preceding direct object can trigger written agreement: *les mesures qu'il a
adoptées*. This is real, but it is not the first repair priority in your data;
ordinary noun/adjective agreement is far more frequent.

Use a three-pass edit for a dense news sentence. First underline each noun and
recall its article. Second trace determiners and adjectives back to that noun.
Third inspect only the *-al* masculine plurals and participles. For example:
*Ces deux nouvelles politiques industrielles sont devenues les principaux
outils du gouvernement.* The feminine plural signal runs through *ces,
nouvelles, politiques, industrielles, devenues*; *principaux* instead agrees
with masculine plural *outils*.

The goal is not to “feel” gender globally. It is to maintain a small set of
high-risk lexical anchors and transmit their features locally. Your personal
noun list is short enough to master, and it will remove a disproportionate
share of the noise from otherwise sophisticated French.
