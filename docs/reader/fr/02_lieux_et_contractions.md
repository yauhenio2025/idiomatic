# Lieux : *à Berlin*, *au Chili*, *en France*

French does not choose a place preposition from the physical meaning alone.
The grammatical type of the place—city, country, continent, or named region—
also matters. The most useful first map is compact:

| Place type | “in/to” | “from” |
|---|---|---|
| city | *à Berlin, à Paris* | *de Berlin, de Paris* |
| feminine country/continent | *en France, en Asie* | *de France, d'Asie* |
| masculine country | *au Chili, au Brésil* | *du Chili, du Brésil* |
| plural country | *aux États-Unis* | *des États-Unis* |
| masculine country beginning with a vowel | *en Iran* | *d'Iran* |

The pattern is grammatical rather than geopolitical. Most country names ending
in written *-e* are feminine, but important exceptions include *le Mexique, le
Cambodge, le Mozambique,* and *le Zimbabwe*. Learn a country with its article,
then let the article predict the contraction.

> **Your attested pattern.** You repeatedly said *en Berlin, en Paris, en
> Rome,* and *en Barcelone*—36 instances of *en + city* were found. French
> cities take *à*: *le sommet a lieu à Berlin; la rédaction est à Paris*.
> You also said *en Chili* and *en Brésil*; both are masculine, hence *au
> Chili, au Brésil*.

Movement and location normally use the same geographic form: *Elle travaille à
Berlin* and *elle va à Berlin*; *il vit au Brésil* and *il retourne au Brésil*.
Origin switches to the *de* column: *un correspondant de Berlin, une enquête
venue du Brésil*. This is unlike German two-way prepositions, and it is worth
keeping the systems separate.

## Ordinary places are lexical choices

Outside proper geography there is no single translation of English “in,” “at,”
or “on.” French packages common activities with conventional prepositions:

- *à la télévision, à la radio,* but *sur Internet, sur Zoom*;
- *au bureau, à l'université, à la banque*;
- *dans un rapport, dans le journal, dans une vidéo* when the item is a
  container of content;
- *chez l'éditeur, chez le médecin, chez des amis* for a person's home or
  professional premises.

> **Your attested pattern.** *Dans la télévision* became *à la télévision*;
> *en Zoom* became *sur Zoom*; *chez dans* confusions also appeared. These are
> collocations, so store the whole scene—not an English-to-French preposition.

The contrast between *en* and *dans* often concerns how a location is
conceptualized. *En Asie* is the conventional continent form. *Dans l'Asie du
Sud-Est des années 1990* can be possible when the noun is heavily specified and
treated as a bounded setting, but the neutral answer remains *en Asie*. A
learner gains little from inventing such marked frames before the default is
automatic.

## Contractions are mandatory

When *à* or *de* meets a masculine or plural definite article, the forms fuse:

- *à + le = au*: *au parlement*;
- *à + les = aux*: *aux Nations unies*;
- *de + le = du*: *du gouvernement*;
- *de + les = des*: *des ministres*.

There is no fusion with *la* or elided *l'*: *à la conférence, de la crise, à
l'étranger, de l'université*. The contraction is not a stylistic choice. Your
recorded *autour de le concept* must be *autour du concept*, and *la chute de
la mur de Berlin* requires both gender and contraction: *la chute du mur de
Berlin*.

Do not confuse contracted *des* (“of/from the”) with indefinite *des* (“some”).
In *les décisions des ministres*, it is *de + les*. In *des ministres ont
protesté*, it is an indefinite plural article. The surface form is identical,
but the syntax differs.

## Time and duration

Place errors sometimes travel with duration errors because English uses the
same small prepositions broadly. For a measured span, *pendant* is the safest
neutral form: *les négociations ont duré pendant trois jours*. *Pour* can mark
an intended future duration: *je pars pour trois jours*. It is less natural for
a completed retrospective span.

> **Your attested pattern.** You said *je pars pour 2, 3 jours* where your
> teacher supplied *pendant deux ou trois jours*. Also distinguish *après deux
> ans* (“after two years,” relative to an event) from *au bout de deux ans*
> (“after two years had elapsed”), another recurring repair in your record.

The practical method is to learn bidirectional triples: *à Berlin / de Berlin*;
*au Chili / du Chili*; *en France / de France*; *aux États-Unis / des
États-Unis*. Then add activity chunks—*à la télévision, sur Internet, chez
l'éditeur*—as independent entries. This prevents Spanish *en*, Italian *a/in*,
and English “in” from voting on every French sentence.
