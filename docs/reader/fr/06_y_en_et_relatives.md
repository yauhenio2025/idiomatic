# *Y, en, qui, que, dont* : preserve the missing relation

French clitics let a speaker avoid repeating a complement, but the replacement
must preserve the relation introduced by the original verb or preposition.
**Y** normally replaces an inanimate *à + thing* complement or a place phrase;
**en** normally replaces *de + thing*, a partitive, or a quantity. They stand
before the finite verb or auxiliary.

- *Je participe au débat → j'y participe.*
- *Je retourne à Berlin → j'y retourne.*
- *Je parle de cette enquête → j'en parle.*
- *Nous avons trois sources → nous en avons trois.*

For people, use the appropriate personal form rather than automatically using
*y* or *en*: *je réponds à la journaliste → je lui réponds*; *je parle de ce
chercheur → je parle de lui*. Usage with people can vary in a few lexicalized
or informal contexts, but this distinction is the reliable standard baseline.

> **Your attested pattern.** You often used *là* or nothing where French
> requires a clitic: *quand je retournerai là* and *ils vont aller là* became
> *quand j'y retournerai* and *ils vont y aller*. You also said *nous avons
> déjà discuté* when the intended meaning was “we discussed it”: because the
> frame is *discuter de*, say *nous en avons déjà discuté*.

The clitic remains attached to its verb group. In a simple tense: *j'y pense,
j'en doute*. In a compound tense: *j'y ai pensé, j'en ai parlé*. With a
conjugated modal plus infinitive, it normally sits before the infinitive whose
complement it is: *je veux y participer; nous devons en parler*. In an
affirmative imperative it follows with a hyphen: *allez-y; parlez-en*.

Quantity *en* preserves the number or quantifier after the verb: *Combien de
rapports avez-vous lus ? J'en ai lu trois.* The clitic replaces *de rapports*;
*trois* remains. In *je n'en ai aucune idée*, *en* belongs to the fixed noun
phrase *avoir une idée de quelque chose*, while *aucune* supplies the negative
quantity.

## Relative clauses: find the role inside the clause

The choice between **qui** and **que** is not based on whether the antecedent is
a person or thing. Ask what role the missing element plays inside the relative
clause:

- **qui** is the subject: *les partis qui critiquent le gouvernement*;
- **que/qu'** is the direct object: *les partis que le gouvernement critique*.

The quickest test is to inspect what follows. If a finite verb arrives
immediately and lacks a subject, *qui* supplies it: *le magazine qui suit ces
sujets*. If an explicit subject follows, *que* can be the object: *le magazine
que les chercheurs lisent*.

> **Your attested pattern.** You repeatedly used Romance-wide *que/che* as a
> universal relative: *des partis que critiquent* and *le magazine que suit*.
> Both gaps are subjects, so French requires *qui*. This error appeared at
> least 26 times.

**Dont** preserves a relation with *de*. It can correspond to “whose,” “of
which,” or a complement required by a verb/adjective:

- *un rapport dont les conclusions sont contestées* — a report whose
  conclusions…;
- *la crise dont nous parlons* — the crisis we are talking about;
- *une réforme dont le pays a besoin* — a reform the country needs.

Do not add a second *de*: not *dont nous parlons de*. The relative already
contains that relation. **Où** handles place and time: *la ville où le sommet a
lieu; l'année où la loi est entrée en vigueur*. After other prepositions, use a
form of **lequel** when appropriate: *les mécanismes par lesquels la plateforme
classe les contenus*.

Your teacher later supplied many *dont/lequel* phrases, which suggests
avoidance even where no wrong form was preserved. Use a two-step repair. First
reconstruct the plain sentences: *Nous parlons de cette crise. Les mécanismes
fonctionnent par ces canaux.* Then replace the repeated complement while
preserving its preposition: *la crise dont nous parlons; les canaux par
lesquels les mécanismes fonctionnent*.

The common principle unifies the chapter: do not translate a small word;
preserve a grammatical relation. *À* points toward *y*, *de* toward *en* or
*dont*, a subject gap toward *qui*, a direct-object gap toward *que*, and an
explicit preposition toward *lequel* or another suitable relative form.
