# Régimes verbaux : apprendre le verbe avec sa préposition

A French infinitive does not select its own preposition. The word before it
does. That governing word may require **à**, **de**, or no preposition at all.
This is called its *régime* or government. Translation is unreliable because a
near-equivalent verb in Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, or English may govern a
different structure.

Learn each item as a frame with one realistic completion:

- *chercher à comprendre une décision*;
- *commencer à enregistrer un épisode*;
- *réussir à convaincre les électeurs*;
- *décider de publier le rapport*;
- *essayer de réduire les coûts*;
- *refuser de céder*;
- *pouvoir vérifier les chiffres* (no preposition);
- *vouloir participer* (no preposition).

> **Your attested pattern.** You said *je cherche de m'isoler* about ten
> times, *on a commencé de parler*, and *j'ai décidé à lire*. The repairs are
> *chercher à, commencer à,* and *décider de*. The infinitive stays the same;
> the governing verb decides the link.

There is no productive rule that predicts every verb. Meaning sometimes helps,
but memorized contrast sets are safer. *Penser à* means to think about or keep
in mind: *je pense à cette réforme*. *Penser de* appears when asking or giving
an opinion: *que pensez-vous de cette réforme ?* Your *quand je pense de ce
mot* needed *à* because the meaning was “when I think of this word.” Likewise,
*participer à, dépendre de, profiter de, renoncer à,* and *se souvenir de*
should be learned with their complement.

## Adjectives before an infinitive

An impersonal adjective frame usually takes **de**:

- *Il est difficile de mesurer l'influence d'un algorithme.*
- *C'est impossible de prévoir tous les effets.*
- *Il est important de distinguer les deux sources.*

When the grammatical subject is a noun that is itself the thing being acted
on, **à** can appear:

- *Ce rapport est difficile à résumer.*
- *Cette hypothèse est impossible à vérifier.*

The contrast is structural. In *il est difficile de résumer ce rapport*, the
dummy *il* introduces the activity. In *ce rapport est difficile à résumer*,
the report is the object understood after *résumer*. This explains why blindly
memorizing “difficult = difficile à” fails.

> **Your attested pattern.** *C'est difficile à parler* and *c'était
> impossible à imaginer* recurred roughly twenty times. With impersonal
> *c'est/il est*, say *c'est difficile de parler* and *il est impossible
> d'imaginer…*. Your other variant, *c'est très difficile trouver*, needs the
> same *de*.

## Noun complements and direct objects

French can also differ on whether a verb takes a direct object. *Discuter de
quelque chose* names the topic: *nous avons discuté de la stratégie*. In
careful standard use, *discuter une proposition* means to examine or debate the
proposal directly. *Répondre à une question, assister à une réunion,* and
*obéir à une règle* retain *à*, while *attendre une réponse, chercher une
solution,* and *écouter un podcast* take a direct object with no preposition.

These choices determine the later pronoun. A direct object becomes *le/la/les*;
*à + thing/place* often becomes *y*; *de + thing/topic* often becomes *en*:

- *J'attends le rapport → je l'attends.*
- *Je participe au débat → j'y participe.*
- *Je parle de la crise → j'en parle.*

This is why regime learning pays twice: it fixes the preposition now and makes
the correct clitic available later.

## A cross-language quarantine

Your languages contain dangerous near-matches:

| Meaning | French | Italian | Brazilian Portuguese | Spanish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| try to | *essayer de* | *cercare di* | *tentar* + infinitive | *intentar* + infinitive |
| begin to | *commencer à* | *cominciare a* | *começar a* | *empezar a* |
| decide to | *décider de* | *decidere di* | *decidir* + infinitive | *decidir* + infinitive |
| succeed in | *réussir à* | *riuscire a* | *conseguir* + infinitive | *lograr/conseguir* + infinitive |

The similarities are useful only after each language's frame is explicit. For
French, write or say the governing word and link before producing the
infinitive: *décider—de—publier*. Do the same with noun complements:
*dépendre—de—la plateforme; participer—à—la conférence*. A personal regime
bank of twenty high-frequency professional verbs will prevent more errors than
another broad conjugation review.
