# Quantités et articles : *beaucoup de*, sauf quand c'est “of the”

French articles mark whether a noun phrase is definite, indefinite, or
partitive: *les articles, des articles, du temps*. A quantity expression already
does another job: it tells the reader how much or how many. After most such
expressions, the noun is linked by the invariable **de**, shortened to **d'**
before a vowel sound.

Learn the complete pattern **quantity + de + noun**:

- *beaucoup de rapports, trop de désinformation*;
- *assez de preuves, peu de temps*;
- *combien de pays, davantage de transparence*;
- *un million de personnes, une série d'articles*.

> **You specifically do this.** The broader quantifier-plus-article error
> family recurs 138 times, making it your single most repeated French pattern.
> Within it, *beaucoup des articles* appears at least 10 times and *beaucoup de
> l'argent* 15 times. For a general quantity, say *beaucoup d'articles*
> and *beaucoup d'argent*. Rehearse *beaucoup-de-nom* as one rhythmic unit.

## When *des* really is correct

The form *des* can be the contraction of *de + les*. It returns after a
quantity when you select from a definite, already identified group:

- *Beaucoup de médias ont repris la dépêche.* — Many media outlets did so.
- *Beaucoup des médias présents à la conférence ont repris la dépêche.* —
  Many **of the** outlets in that defined group did so.

The safest diagnostic is to expand the intended meaning. If “many of the…” is
essential, *beaucoup des* can be justified. Otherwise use *beaucoup de*. With a
demonstrative, the distinction is especially clear: *beaucoup de ces médias*.

Related expressions have their own stable frames. Say *la plupart des
électeurs* and *la majorité des pays* because a selected population follows.
With a stressed pronoun, standard French inserts *entre*: *la plupart d'entre
eux, plusieurs d'entre elles*. A simple indefinite quantity remains *beaucoup
d'électeurs*.

> **You specifically do this.** Your *la plupart d'eux* omits a necessary
> element. Use *la plupart d'entre eux*. The same repair gives *plusieurs
> d'entre elles*.

## *D'autres*, not usually *des autres*

Before plural *autres*, an indefinite *des* normally reduces to *d'*: *d'autres
sources, d'autres options, d'autres modèles*. A real contraction survives when
the group is definite: *les propositions des autres partis* means the
proposals of the other parties. Ask again whether the phrase means unspecific
“other…” or definite “of the other…”.

> **You specifically do this.** Replace your general *des autres choses* with
> *d'autres choses*. In *je n'ai pas d'autres options*, both the indefinite
> plural and the negative construction point to *d'*.

## Articles after negation

After an ordinary verbal negation, indefinite *un/une/des* and partitive
*du/de la/de l'* normally become *de/d'*:

- *Le rapport contient des preuves* → *Le rapport ne contient pas de preuves*.
- *La chaîne diffuse de la publicité* → *La chaîne ne diffuse pas de
  publicité*.
- *Il y a un accord* → *Il n'y a pas d'accord*.

A definite article remains because the referent stays identified: *Je ne
critique pas les chiffres de l'ONU.* The verb *être* is the major exception:
*Ce n'est pas une preuve; ce ne sont pas des données vérifiées.* Contrast or a
deliberately preserved category can also retain an article, but *pas de* is the
working default after other verbs.

## Countries and languages still need their noun phrase

Quantifier errors belong to a broader habit of deleting articles. Country
names normally keep an article when they are direct objects: *visiter la
Turquie, connaître l'Italie, soutenir le Brésil*. Geographic prepositions may
absorb that article—*en Turquie, au Brésil*—but the country has not become a
bare noun.

Language names also commonly take the definite article as nouns: *apprendre le
japonais, étudier le français, traduire vers l'allemand*. After *parler*, the
neutral language name is usually bare: *parler japonais*. Record the verb and
noun together instead of treating the article as decoration.

## A five-question article audit

When revising a news or technology paragraph, stop at each quantity and ask:

1. Is this a general amount? Use *de/d'*: *trop de promesses*.
2. Is it a subset of a defined group? Use *de + determiner*: *trois des cinq
   propositions; beaucoup de ces articles*.
3. Is the complement a stressed pronoun? Often add *entre*: *la plupart
   d'entre eux*.
4. Does a negative verb remove an indefinite amount? Usually use *pas de*.
5. Is a country or language functioning as a noun? Restore its normal article.

Read the result aloud: *Beaucoup de gouvernements publient des stratégies sur
l'IA. La plupart d'entre elles promettent davantage de transparence, mais peu
de textes prévoient des sanctions. Plusieurs des propositions adoptées en juin
ne contiennent pas de calendrier.* Every small form now encodes a specific
relationship.
