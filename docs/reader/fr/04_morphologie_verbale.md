# Morphologie verbale : sécuriser la forme avant la phrase

Your French errors rarely show wholesale tense confusion. More often, the
tense is right but one high-information form is built from the wrong stem. The
main pressure point is the third-person plural of *-ir, -re,* and *-oir* verbs,
followed by a smaller set of imperfect and past-participle forms. A focused
family method is more useful than reviewing every conjugation from the start.

## Start from subject, lemma, family

Before producing a finite form, make three decisions in order:

1. Identify the exact subject and number.
2. Recover the infinitive, not a neighboring Romance form.
3. Recall a known verb in the same French family.

For regular *-er* verbs, third-person plural is usually transparent: *ils
publient, elles vérifient*. The written *-ent* ending is silent. Outside that
large group, however, the stem can change and may also change the sound. Do not
build every plural by attaching letters to the singular.

> **You specifically do this.** You produced *les images qui provient* and
> *ils comprend vraiment*. The plural forms are *les images qui proviennent*
> and *ils comprennent vraiment*. The subject of the relative clause controls
> the verb: plural *images* requires *proviennent*.

## Memorize high-yield third-person families

The following families cover several of your recurring forms:

| Family | Singular anchor | Third-person plural |
|---|---|---|
| *venir / tenir* and compounds | *il vient, elle provient* | *ils viennent, elles proviennent* |
| *prendre* and compounds | *il prend, elle comprend* | *ils prennent, elles comprennent* |
| regular *finir*-type *-ir* | *il finit, elle choisit* | *ils finissent, elles choisissent* |
| *préférer* | *il préfère* | *ils préfèrent* |
| *produire / conduire* | *il produit, elle conduit* | *ils produisent, elles conduisent* |

Not every verb ending in *-ir* follows *finir*. Compare *ils choisissent* with
*ils partent* and *ils viennent*. The infinitive ending is only a clue; the
lexical family decides the stem.

> **You specifically do this.** *Les régions choisent* and *ils préfièrent*
> should be *les régions choisissent* and *ils préfèrent*. In a policy
> sentence: *Les rédactions choisissent les sujets que leurs lecteurs
> préfèrent.*

The silent ending can hide useful structure in speech, so practice a pair where
the stem itself is audible: *il comprend / ils comprennent; elle vient / elles
viennent*. Then place it in a real clause: *Les entreprises comprennent les
risques que ces systèmes présentent.*

## Build the imperfect from the *nous* form

For most verbs, the imperfect stem comes from the present *nous* form after
removing *-ons*: *nous prenons → pren- → je prenais*; *nous choisissons →
choisiss- → ils choisissaient*. Add *-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient*.
The major exception is *être*, with the stem *ét-* throughout: *j'étais, nous
étions, ils étaient*.

> **You specifically do this.** Your *nous était* and *je prendais souvent*
> combine a correct time frame with a malformed verb. Use *nous étions* and *je
> prenais souvent*. Anchor the latter in *nous prenons*, not in the written
> infinitive *prendre*.

This method also prevents an unnecessary extra consonant. A news retrospective
can say: *À l'époque, nous prenions ces annonces au sérieux, mais les experts
étaient plus prudents.*

## Past participles are lexical forms

Do not force an *-é* participle onto every verb. Learn frequent infinitive–past
participle pairs:

- *produire → produit*: *la société a produit un nouveau modèle*;
- *décrire → décrit*: *le podcast a décrit la campagne*;
- *découvrir → découvert*: *les chercheurs ont découvert une faille*;
- *réussir → réussi*: *l'équipe a réussi son lancement*.

> **You specifically do this.** Replace *j'ai producé* with *j'ai produit* and
> *tu as décrivé* with *tu as décrit*. You also used *je suis réussi*; the verb
> *réussir* normally forms this compound tense with *avoir*: *j'ai réussi*.

With *avoir*, a following direct object does not normally trigger agreement:
*la société a produit des puces*. A participle used adjectivally does agree:
*des puces produites en Europe*. Keep form selection and agreement as two
separate checks.

## A four-line verification habit

For a verb that feels uncertain, write four items: subject, infinitive, family
anchor, final form. For example: *les plateformes — comprendre — ils prennent —
comprennent*. Then verify an unfamiliar form in a reliable conjugation
reference before publication. Finally, read the full sentence aloud; the
contrast *comprend/comprennent* is audible even though final *-ent* itself is
not.

This narrow routine matches the evidence: preserve your generally successful
tense choice, but stop irregular stems and participles from being improvised at
the last second.
