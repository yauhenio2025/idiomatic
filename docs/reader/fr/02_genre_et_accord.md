# Genre et accord : construire une chaîne visible

French noun gender is grammatical. It may agree with biological sex, but most
objects and abstractions have no meaning-based test that can reliably predict
*le* or *la*. The practical solution is to learn the noun with a determiner and
then make every dependent word agree with that stored unit: *une méthode
fiable*, *ce processus complexe*, *les décisions importantes*.

## Store the article, not a translation

Do not memorize *période = period*. Memorize *une période* or *la période*.
This makes gender audible and gives the adjective a model. For words beginning
with a vowel, an elided article hides gender, so add another clue: *l'analyse
est longue* or *une analyse*. Plural *les* also hides gender; learn the singular
at first contact.

Your highest-value personal list is small enough to rehearse in groups:

| Feminine | Masculine |
|---|---|
| *la période, la fois, la vie* | *le site, le parti, le livre* |
| *la carrière, la présentation, la leçon* | *le moyen, le processus, le logiciel* |
| *la méthode, la question, la ville* | *le cadre* |
| *la conférence, la région, la mode* | *le mode* (a mode or grammatical mood) |

The last pair shows why meaning still matters after gender is memorized: *la
mode* is fashion or what is fashionable; *le mode* is a method, mode, or mood.

> **You specifically do this.** Wrong gender accounts for roughly 297 items in
> your record. *Ce période, mon vie, une processus,* and *le première fois*
> should be *cette période, ma vie, un processus,* and *la première fois*.
> Rehearse the corrected noun phrase, not the noun alone.

## Agreement spreads through the phrase

A determiner, noun, and adjective form an agreement chain. Number and gender
must reach every variable member:

- *un rapport important* → *des rapports importants*;
- *une personne importante* → *des personnes importantes*;
- *un système indépendant* → *une agence indépendante*;
- *l'Amérique latine* → *les économies latino-américaines*.

In speech, some endings are silent, but they remain mandatory in writing. In
other cases agreement changes the sound: *indépendant / indépendante*, *actif /
active*, *européen / européenne*. Reading a sentence aloud is therefore useful,
but it cannot replace a visual check.

> **You specifically do this.** You wrote *beaucoup de personnes importants*
> and *une politique très indépendant*, and *Amérique Latin* recurred across
> several kinds of note. The noun controls the repair: *personnes importantes,
> politique indépendante, Amérique latine*.

Predicative adjectives agree too. The verb *être* does not break the chain:
*les plateformes sont puissantes; les résultats sont globaux*. A past
participle used as an adjective behaves the same way: *des données publiées,
des frontières fermées*. Participles inside compound tenses require additional
rules, so first ask whether the word describes a noun or forms the verb. In
*les données publiées hier*, it describes *données*; in *les chercheurs ont
publié les données*, *ont publié* is the verb and there is no agreement with
the following direct object.

## The high-frequency *-al* pattern

Many masculine adjectives and nouns ending in *-al* form their plural in
*-aux*: *principal → principaux, social → sociaux, global → globaux, journal →
journaux, hôpital → hôpitaux*. The spelling *-als* is not the default.

> **You specifically do this.** Your forms *principals, globals,* and *socials*
> account for a recurring family of about 50 errors. In a technology briefing,
> write *les principaux risques sociaux sont globaux*. You also need the exact
> noun spelling *les hôpitaux*, not *les hospitaux*.

There are exceptions, so treat *-al → -aux* as a strong pattern rather than a
machine. Common masculine plurals include *des bals, des carnavals, des
festivals, des récitals,* and adjectives such as *banals, fatals,* and *navals*.
The adjective *final* needs a separate lookup: modern references record both
*finals* and *finaux*, with preferences varying by source and usage.
Dictionaries remain the authority for an unfamiliar word.

## Agreement in information-dense sentences

News and technical prose often separates a noun from its adjective. Keep the
chain active across the intervening words:

*Les mesures annoncées par les autorités européennes sont difficiles à
appliquer.* Here *annoncées* tracks feminine plural *mesures*; *européennes*
tracks feminine plural *autorités*; *difficiles* again tracks *mesures* after
*sont*. In *les nouveaux modèles d'intelligence artificielle sont puissants*,
*nouveaux* and *puissants* track masculine plural *modèles*, not the nearby
feminine singular *intelligence*.

Use a backward check before publishing. Box each adjective or adjectival
participle, find the exact noun it describes, and mark that noun F/M and SG/PL.
Then rebuild the ending. For a new noun, record one useful phrase—*une méthode
fiable de vérification*, *un logiciel libre de transcription*—so that gender,
agreement, and register enter memory together.
