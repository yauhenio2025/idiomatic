# German source survey

License audit performed 2026-08-01. A source is **usable for adaptation** only when the page or publisher supplies a primary, traceable license that permits derivatives and commercial reuse: CC BY, CC BY-SA, or a verified public-domain dedication. “Free to read” is not enough. Third-party images, audio, linked videos, and embedded exercises are excluded unless their own licenses independently pass that test.

The seven German chapters adapt only Grimm Grammar and the author-written prose of the UNA Pressbooks sequence. Their examples, ordering, learner-error analysis, and politics/technology/media/publishing frames are newly written. No source image, audio, fairy-tale dialogue, H5P exercise, or linked video is reproduced.

## Rejected or quarantined

| Candidate | Decision and evidence |
|---|---|
| [A Foundation Course in Reading German](https://wisc.pb.unizin.org/readinggerman/front-matter/about/), Howard Martin, revised by Alan Ng | **Rejected: NC.** Its own “About this Book” page specifies CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. It is excellent and comprehensive, but the commission permits only CC BY, CC BY-SA, or public domain. “Open textbook” in a catalog does not override the NonCommercial term. |
| [Intermediate German: The world of work](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/languages/intermediate-german-the-world-work), The Open University | **Rejected: NC and third-party exceptions.** The course’s copyright panel displays the CC BY-NC license type and warns that third-party material is outside the course license. Free access is not adaptation permission under this commission. |
| Commercial grammars from Routledge, Hueber, Cornelsen, Klett, and Duden | **Rejected: paywalled/all rights reserved unless a specific page carries a qualifying open license.** A purchasable book, a Google Books preview, or an unlicensed PDF mirror is not evidence of remix permission. None is used. |
| Deutsche Welle’s *Nicos Weg*, Lingolia, Dartmouth’s German grammar pages, and miscellaneous “free German grammar” sites | **Quarantined: free-to-read or pedagogically useful, but no qualifying CC BY/CC BY-SA/public-domain grant was verified on the specific prose proposed for adaptation.** They may be linked for private study later, but no wording or examples are imported here. |
| Project Gutenberg’s *Kleine deutsche Sprachlehre* (1897) | **Not adapted.** The catalog verifies “public domain in the USA,” not a worldwide public-domain status, and the nineteenth-century orthography and native-school analysis lower its value for a modern international reader. A jurisdiction-limited notice is insufficient for this package. |
| Anonymous uploads, PDF mirrors, scraped “grammar books,” and AI summaries | **Rejected: unverifiable provenance or license.** They are neither evidence nor source prose. |

## Topic-to-source routing used

1. Adjective endings: Grimm Grammar’s adjective overview/three determiner patterns, checked against UNA German 102/201.
2. Gender and case: Grimm Grammar’s noun-gender and case overviews.
3. Dative governance: Grimm Grammar’s dative chapter, checked against UNA German 102 §4-7.
4. Passive: Grimm Grammar’s present/past passive and passive alternatives pages.
5. N-declension: Grimm Grammar’s weak-masculine-noun chapter, checked against UNA German 102’s N-noun unit.
6. Genitive: Grimm Grammar’s genitive chapter, checked against UNA German 102 §6-1.
7. Verb–preposition frames and da-compounds: Grimm Grammar’s verbal-phrases chapter, checked against UNA German 201 §§9-2–9-3.
