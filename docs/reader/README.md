# The personal grammar reader

This is a reading course built from the learner's recorded errors, teacher
corrections, and stated avoidance areas. It is not a general grammar sequence.
Start with one language, follow the evidence-priority order below, and read one
chapter at a sitting. On a second pass, say the examples in the shaded **You
specifically do this** callouts aloud and then replace one noun or verb with a
current-affairs, technology, media, or publishing example of your own.

Every chapter is 600–1,200 words and ends with its own attribution, source,
license, and change note. The underlying license verification and rejected
sources are documented in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). In particular, no prose
from a source carrying NC, ND, all-rights-reserved, paywalled, or unclear terms
was adapted.

## Reading order by language

### French

The French record is broad enough to rank by repeated corrections. Prepositions
account for roughly a quarter of classified errors; agreement follows, while
the unusually frequent *beaucoup des* and *an/année* patterns justify their own
early chapters.

1. [Prepositions and verb/adjective frames](fr/01_prepositions_et_regimes.md)
2. [Gender and agreement](fr/02_genre_et_accord.md)
3. [Quantities and articles](fr/03_quantites_et_articles.md)
4. [Verb morphology under production pressure](fr/04_morphologie_verbale.md)
5. [Word order](fr/05_ordre_des_mots.md)
6. [*An, année,* and duration](fr/06_an_annee_et_duree.md)
7. [*Y, en,* and relative pronouns](fr/07_pronoms_y_en_et_relatifs.md)
8. [Negation](fr/08_negation.md)

### German

Adjective endings lead because they are the strongest recorded production
cluster. The next chapter separates a noun's lexical gender from the case
assigned by syntax; the third then makes verb-governed dative frames explicit.
The passive follows because the record shows avoidance rather than only wrong
forms. The final three chapters target narrower noun-declension, formal-genitive,
and prepositional-complement gaps.

1. [Adjective endings as a system](de/01_adjektivendungen_als_system.md)
2. [Gender stays; case moves](de/02_genus_bleibt_kasus_bewegt_sich.md)
3. [Dative as a verb-frame decision](de/03_dativ_ist_eine_verbentscheidung.md)
4. [Building the passive with *werden*](de/04_passiv_werden_ohne_ausweichen.md)
5. [N-declension without stray *-n*](de/05_n_deklination_und_falsches_n.md)
6. [Genitive for written prose](de/06_genitiv_fuer_geschriebene_prosa.md)
7. [Prepositional verbs and da-compounds](de/07_verbpraepositionen_und_dakomposita.md)

### Spanish

Cross-Romance interference represents about 42% of the classified Spanish
sample. Read that diagnostic first, then the two seven-error clusters, before
moving into form retrieval and lower-frequency syntax.

1. [Keep Spanish in the Spanish lane](es/01_interferencia_romance.md)
2. [*Muy* or *mucho*](es/02_muy_y_mucho.md)
3. [Movement and location](es/03_movimiento_y_lugar.md)
4. [Strong preterites](es/04_preteritos_fuertes.md)
5. [Light verbs and fixed prepositions](es/05_verbos_ligeros_y_preposiciones_fijas.md)
6. [*Se lo* and two-clitic sequences](es/06_se_lo_y_dos_cliticos.md)
7. [Numbers and dates](es/07_numeros_y_fechas.md)

### Italian

Italian has the thinnest captured-error sample, so teacher assignments and the
learner's self-report carry more weight. Begin with the concentrated 74-item
*ci/ne* assignment, then cover the two self-reported structural weaknesses.
After those foundations, move from the productive subjunctive to reading-first
control of the remote past and finally the one attested past-viewpoint contrast.

1. [*Ci, ne,* and procomplementary verbs](it/02_ci_ne_e_verbi_procomplementari.md)
2. [Verb frames and prepositions](it/01_reggenze_verbali.md)
3. [Gender, articles, and plural](it/03_genere_articoli_e_plurali.md)
4. [The subjunctive as perspective](it/04_congiuntivo_come_prospettiva.md)
5. [*Passato remoto* for reading and narration](it/05_passato_remoto_per_leggere_e_narrare.md)
6. [Event or background](it/06_evento_o_sfondo.md)

### Brazilian Portuguese

Interference is the dominant category at roughly 54% of the classified sample,
so the contrastive chapter comes first even though its filename is 07. Gender
and articles form the next largest cluster. The remaining sequence moves from
verb frames and person agreement to three systems the learner tends to avoid or
replace with Spanish-shaped forms.

1. [Stop Spanish-izing Portuguese](pt/07_interferencia_espanhol_portugues.md)
2. [Gender, articles, agreement, and numbers](pt/01_genero_artigos_e_numerais.md)
3. [Regency and contractions](pt/02_regencia_e_contracoes.md)
4. [Preterite: *eu* or *ele*](pt/03_preterito_eu_ou_ele.md)
5. [*Ser, estar,* and *ficar*](pt/04_ser_estar_ficar.md)
6. [Future subjunctive](pt/05_futuro_do_subjuntivo.md)
7. [Brazilian clitics and formal infinitive forms](pt/06_cliticos_no_portugues_brasileiro.md)

## Podcast-conversion candidates

The strongest spoken episodes are chapters organized around audible contrasts,
short decisions, and recurring examples. Table-heavy paradigms can remain
reading supplements. A practical season-two shortlist is:

| Language | Best first candidates | Why they work aloud |
|---|---|---|
| French | [Quantities and articles](fr/03_quantites_et_articles.md); [*an/année*](fr/06_an_annee_et_duree.md); [negation](fr/08_negation.md) | Each has a repeated soundable contrast and a compact final audit. |
| German | [Dative verb frames](de/03_dativ_ist_eine_verbentscheidung.md); [passive](de/04_passiv_werden_ohne_ausweichen.md); [prepositional verbs](de/07_verbpraepositionen_und_dakomposita.md) | The frame-first method supports call-and-response examples without requiring a full declension table. |
| Spanish | [Romance interference](es/01_interferencia_romance.md); [*muy/mucho*](es/02_muy_y_mucho.md); [movement/location](es/03_movimiento_y_lugar.md) | Contrast pairs are easy to dramatize and repeat; the learner can answer before hearing the repair. |
| Italian | [*Ci/ne* verbs](it/02_ci_ne_e_verbi_procomplementari.md); [*passato remoto*](it/05_passato_remoto_per_leggere_e_narrare.md); [event/background](it/06_evento_o_sfondo.md) | Pronoun clusters and tense viewpoint benefit from rhythm, voiced minimal pairs, and short narrative scenes. |
| Portuguese | [interference](pt/07_interferencia_espanhol_portugues.md); [*ser/estar/ficar*](pt/04_ser_estar_ficar.md); [future subjunctive](pt/05_futuro_do_subjuntivo.md) | They naturally support pause-and-complete prompts and Brazilian/Spanish contrastive listening. |
