# The future subjunctive: the event is future, the form is not a future assertion

Brazilian Portuguese regularly uses the future subjunctive in subordinate clauses that point toward an unresolved future event. English often uses a present form there—“when I return”—and Spanish usually uses the present subjunctive—*cuando vuelva*. Portuguese has a dedicated form: *quando eu voltar*.

The construction divides labor between two clauses. The subordinate clause sets a condition or time whose realization is still open; the main clause states the projected consequence:

*Quando eu voltar, vou ajustar os cartões.*

Returning has not happened yet. *Vou ajustar* carries the main prediction. Portuguese does not normally put *vou* into both clauses.

> **You specifically do this:** you say *quando eu vou voltar*, *quando eu vou ter mais tempo*, *se eu vou viajar*, and *quando a pandemia vai ser controlada*. Your teacher’s targets were *quando eu voltar*, *quando eu tiver*, *se eu viajar*, and *quando a pandemia for controlada*—and the same construction was still being retaught years later.

## The two-clause test

Look for three ingredients:

1. a subordinate introducer such as *se, quando, assim que, logo que, depois que, enquanto*;
2. a situation located in the future;
3. an event that is conditional, pending, or not yet realized.

When all three are present, the future subjunctive is likely:

- *Se o Congresso aprovar a proposta, o governo publicará o decreto.*
- *Quando a equipe terminar a edição, vamos lançar o episódio.*
- *Assim que os dados forem públicos, os jornais farão novas análises.*
- *Depois que você ler o relatório, podemos conversar.*

Do not apply the form mechanically after every *quando*. A habitual present takes the indicative: *Quando tenho tempo, leio os jornais.* A completed past sequence also uses an indicative past: *Quando cheguei, a reunião já tinha começado.* The future meaning, not the conjunction alone, triggers the choice.

## Regular forms look deceptively simple

For regular verbs, the future subjunctive often looks exactly like the infinitive in the first and third singular:

| Person | *falar* | *receber* | *partir* |
|---|---|---|---|
| eu | *falar* | *receber* | *partir* |
| você/ele/ela | *falar* | *receber* | *partir* |
| nós | *falarmos* | *recebermos* | *partirmos* |
| vocês/eles/elas | *falarem* | *receberem* | *partirem* |

That resemblance can hide the mood. In *quando eu falar*, *falar* is not an infinitive; it agrees with the understood subject and belongs to a finite subordinate clause. The plural reveals the pattern: *quando eles falarem*.

## Derive irregular forms from the third-person plural preterite

A powerful formation rule starts from the *eles* form of the *pretérito perfeito*. Remove *-am*:

- *tiveram* → *tiver*;
- *fizeram* → *fizer*;
- *foram* → *for*;
- *disseram* → *disser*;
- *puderam* → *puder*;
- *quiseram* → *quiser*;
- *vieram* → *vier*;
- *puseram* → *puser*;
- *trouxeram* → *trouxer*.

Then add *-mos* or *-em* where needed: *se nós tivermos*, *quando eles fizerem*. This rule connects the mood to a form family you can verify, instead of treating every irregular stem as a separate surprise.

> **You specifically do this:** *quando eu vou ter mais tempo* avoids the irregular form *tiver*. Retrieve the pair *eles tiveram → se eu tiver*. Likewise, retrieve *eles foram → for* for the unresolved future: *quando a pandemia for controlada*.

## *Se* does not always mean future subjunctive

An open future condition takes this form: *Se houver acordo amanhã, os mercados reagirão.* A general truth uses the present indicative: *Se há inflação, o poder de compra cai.* A hypothetical or counterfactual condition uses other mood-and-tense combinations: *Se houvesse acordo, os mercados reagiriam.*

Your immediate target is narrower: real, open conditions about what may happen. Pair the subordinate form with an ordinary future expression in the main clause:

- *Se eu puder, vou gravar hoje.*
- *Quando o editor vier, vamos decidir.*
- *Assim que eles disserem a verdade, a crise vai mudar.*

## Read it as a hinge

When reading aloud, group the conditional clause as one hinge and pause at the comma: *Quando eu tiver o manuscrito | vou revisar as notas.* This discourages you from planning two independent future assertions. The first clause opens a door; the second tells what happens if or when the door opens.

For production, start with five personally frequent stems—*tiver, fizer, for, puder, quiser*. Add one topical sentence to each. Recognition will grow from the transparent regular forms, but these irregulars are where avoiding the construction becomes most tempting.
