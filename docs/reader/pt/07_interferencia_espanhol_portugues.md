# Do not “Spanish-ize” Portuguese: retrieve a Portuguese family

More than half of your recorded Portuguese errors are cross-Romance lexical or morphological transfers. That number changes the diagnosis. You do not lack enough Portuguese words to discuss technology, publishing, or geopolitics. Under speaking pressure, a Spanish or Italian neighbor wins the retrieval race, and you fit it into an otherwise Portuguese sentence.

The remedy is not a longer bilingual vocabulary list. It is deliberate separation: store contrast sets, notice which part differs, and retrieve a Portuguese word family rather than modifying the competing form on the fly.

> **You specifically do this:** recurring intrusions include *todavia* for *ainda*, *contento* for *contente*, *intento* for *tento*, *prefirem* for *preferem*, *hay* for *tem*, and forms from the *presentar* family without Portuguese *a-*.

## Sort interference by mechanism

### A real word with the wrong meaning

Some dangerous forms exist in both languages but do different work. Portuguese *todavia* means “however,” not Spanish *todavía* “still.” Thus:

- *Ainda não decidi* = I still have not decided.
- *A proposta é cara; todavia, pode funcionar* = the proposal is expensive; however, it may work.

Likewise, Portuguese *fato* means fact, while *feito* is the participle of *fazer* or a noun meaning deed. *É um fato comprovado* contrasts with *O estudo foi feito em 2024*. These pairs need meaning labels, not just spelling labels.

### A plausible form that Portuguese does not use

Other intrusions are understandable but not target forms: *tarea → tarefa*, *datos → dados*, *errores → erros*, *retraso → atraso*, *prensa → imprensa*, *olvidar → esquecer*, *cerrar → fechar*. Do not waste time deriving them through letter-substitution rules. Retrieve a full phrase:

- *cumprir uma tarefa*;
- *publicar os dados*;
- *cometer erros*;
- *o atraso do voo*;
- *a imprensa internacional*;
- *esquecer a senha*;
- *fechar a fronteira*.

Collocations create more retrieval hooks than naked equivalences.

### A Portuguese stem with foreign morphology

You also reshape a real Portuguese verb around a Spanish paradigm: *prefirem* instead of *preferem*, *quere* instead of *quer*, *ponemos* instead of *pomos*. Here the solution is a small family:

*preferir → eu prefiro, eles preferem*;
*querer → eu quero, ele quer*;
*pôr → eu ponho, nós pomos*.

The family blocks analogy from supplying a foreign diphthong or ending. Use the forms in contrastive headlines: *Os eleitores preferem estabilidade, mas eu prefiro uma reforma rápida.*

## Protect the high-frequency Portuguese prefixes and stems

The *apresentar* family is a major fossil. Portuguese normally has the initial *a-*:

- *apresentar um relatório*;
- *a apresentação do livro*;
- *o apresentador do programa*;
- *o governo apresentou a proposta*.

Similarly, Brazilian Portuguese uses *acessar um site* and *baixar um arquivo*, not **aceder** and not a Spanish-shaped **descargar** in this context. One coherent domain sentence can join the family: *A apresentadora acessou o site, baixou os dados e apresentou a análise.*

> **You specifically do this:** the *presentar* family was corrected roughly fifteen times, and *aceder* appeared repeatedly. These are daily technology/media words, so frequency alone has not extinguished the competing forms.

## Keep three-way contrasts when necessary

Because Italian also intrudes, a two-column Spanish–Portuguese card can hide the real competitor. Store all active rivals:

| Meaning | Portuguese | Spanish | Italian |
|---|---|---|---|
| while | *enquanto* | *mientras* | *mentre* |
| happy/content | *contente* | *contento* | *contento* |
| to try | *tentar* | *intentar* | *tentare* |
| to tell/recount | *contar* | *contar* | *raccontare* |
| press | *imprensa* | *prensa* | *stampa* |

The table is not for simultaneous translation practice. It tells you which boundaries need protection. Then practice only the Portuguese column in meaningful sentences.

## Sound contrasts can carry grammatical consequences

Some short pairs require different repair strategies. In much Brazilian speech, *mas* “but” may be diphthongized and sound like or nearly like *mais* “more,” so sound alone is not a dependable selector. Let syntax choose: *mas* links contrasting clauses, while *mais* modifies a quantity or comparison. By contrast, *sem* “without” and *sim* “yes” retain a useful vowel contrast. Your pronunciation record shows substitutions in both sets, so put them in clauses whose structure makes the intended word unavoidable:

- *O plano é caro, mas é mais seguro.*
- *Sem dados, não podemos dizer que sim.*

For *sem/sim*, slow the stressed vowel enough to choose the word, then speed up the whole phrase. For *mas/mais*, rehearse the complete syntactic frame instead of expecting pronunciation to rescue the choice. A spelling-only exercise will not fix errors that recur during speech.

## Retrieval protocol

When a tempting cognate appears, do not ask whether it “sounds Portuguese.” Pause and retrieve one of three things: a known collocation, a word family, or a contrast sentence. If none arrives, paraphrase with secure Portuguese. *Atraso* can be *demorou mais do que o previsto*; *imprensa* can be *os jornais e as emissoras*. A clean paraphrase is better than a confident invention.

Your advanced register is an advantage: the same concepts recur. Build clusters around *publicar dados, apresentar uma análise, cumprir uma tarefa, acessar o sistema,* and *ainda não decidir*. Repetition inside real arguments will separate the languages more effectively than memorizing hundreds of isolated cognates.
