# The preterite has a subject: *eu fiz*, *ele fez*

You do not mainly struggle to choose the Portuguese preterite. You struggle to keep its person attached to the subject while producing a dense sentence. The revealing pairs are *eu fiz / ele fez*, *eu tive / ela teve*, *eu escrevi / ele escreveu*, *eu criei / ele criou*. In each pair, the tense is right; the speaker or actor is not.

> **You specifically do this:** your records include *ele fiz*, *ela tive*, *ele escrevi*, *eu criou*, *eu começou*, and *um jornalista me diz isso ontem*. First-person and third-person forms swap in both directions, so this is an agreement problem under load, not one missing ending.

## Start with the regular contrast

For Brazilian Portuguese, the most useful production contrast is first-person singular versus third-person singular (*ele/ela/você*). Regular verbs make the difference clearly:

| Infinitive | *eu* | *ele/ela/você* |
|---|---|---|
| *publicar* | *publiquei* | *publicou* |
| *receber* | *recebi* | *recebeu* |
| *decidir* | *decidi* | *decidiu* |

The *-ar* pair contrasts *-ei* with *-ou*. The *-er* and *-ir* pairs contrast first-person *-i* with third-person *-eu/-iu*. Spelling may adjust to preserve the stem sound: *publicar → publiquei*, *começar → comecei*, *ficar → fiquei*. Those spelling changes do not create a new tense; they keep the pronunciation stable.

When narrating news, state the subject immediately before retrieving the verb: *eu publiquei*, *a agência publicou*; *eu recebi*, *o ministério recebeu*. Alternating the two forms is more useful for you than chanting a six-person table.

## Treat strong verbs as contrast pairs

Several high-frequency verbs have irregular preterites. Learn them in subject pairs:

- *eu fiz / ele fez* (*fazer*);
- *eu tive / ela teve* (*ter*);
- *eu estive / ele esteve* (*estar*);
- *eu disse / ela disse* (*dizer*);
- *eu fui / ele foi* (*ir* and *ser*);
- *eu pude / ela pôde* (*poder*);
- *eu vim / ele veio* (*vir*).

The accent in *pôde* distinguishes the third-person past from present *pode*. Context usually helps in speech, but the written accent matters: *O tribunal não pôde votar ontem; hoje pode votar.*

Do not overgeneralize a first-person form after a third-person subject just because it is the form you retrieve fastest. In *Um jornalista me disse isso ontem*, *disse* happens to look identical for *eu* and *ele*, but the surrounding pronouns still identify the roles. With *fazer* and *ter*, the difference is audible and must be selected.

## Perfective means bounded, not necessarily brief

The *pretérito perfeito* presents an event as completed or bounded in the narrative. It can last a second or ten years:

- *O parlamento aprovou a lei às duas da manhã.*
- *Ela dirigiu a organização entre 2015 e 2024.*
- *Eu gravei três episódios na semana passada.*

The imperfect instead opens a past situation from the inside: background, habit, or an unfolding state. *Enquanto eu gravava, o governo anunciou a decisão.* The recording supplies the background; the announcement advances the event line. This distinction matters, but it is not the center of your current preterite errors. Secure person agreement first.

## Beware the *nós* interference trap

Portuguese present and preterite forms can coincide for regular *-ar* verbs: *nós publicamos hoje* and *nós publicamos ontem*. Time expressions disambiguate them. For regular *-er* verbs, *nós recebemos* can likewise serve as present or preterite in standard Brazilian Portuguese.

Your data contains a different problem: **desenvolvimos** where the intended verb is *desenvolver*. Spanish and the visible *-ir* inside the spelling pull you toward *-imos*, but *desenvolver* is an *-er* verb: *nós desenvolvemos*. Segment it as *des-en-vol-ver*, then use the *-er* ending. By contrast, *decidir* genuinely gives *nós decidimos*.

## A production routine for long sentences

Before the verb, silently lock three features:

1. **Who?** *eu*, one named actor, or several actors?
2. **When?** Is the event bounded in the past?
3. **Which contrast pair?** *fiz/fez*, *tive/teve*, *criei/criou*?

Then say a short core before adding detail: *A empresa criou o sistema* … *durante a pandemia* … *para monitorar contratos públicos*. If you try to plan every modifier first, the subject fades and the most accessible verb form wins.

Practice with paired headlines: *Eu escrevi o roteiro; a jornalista escreveu a reportagem.* *Eu fiz a análise; o instituto fez a pesquisa.* *Eu tive acesso aos dados; a oposição teve acesso depois.* The repetition should train a subject-to-form link, not merely recognition of a correct conjugation.
