# Regência: decide what comes between the verbs

A Portuguese infinitive can follow another verb directly, with a preposition, or inside a fixed expression. The choice is part of the first verb’s lexical identity. It is not a freely selected bridge, and it is exactly where Spanish, Italian, and French compete in your speech.

The most efficient learning unit is therefore not *tentar* by itself. It is *tentar fazer alguma coisa*. Likewise, learn *conseguir fazer*, *decidir fazer*, *começar a fazer*, *precisar de alguma coisa* and *gostar de fazer*. A chunk stores both the meaning and the small grammatical word that follows it.

> **You specifically do this:** you say *tentar de instalar*, *conseguir a procurar*, *decidir de mandar*, and *vou a passar*. The corrections span several years. Each wrong preposition is grammatical in one of your other Romance languages, which makes guesswork especially unreliable.

## Four bare-infinitive traps

In Brazilian Portuguese, these four high-frequency verbs take a bare infinitive in the meanings you use most:

- *tentar + infinitive*: *O governo tentou conter a inflação.*
- *conseguir + infinitive*: *A equipe conseguiu publicar a reportagem.*
- *decidir + infinitive*: *Os editores decidiram adiar o lançamento.*
- *ir + infinitive* for the periphrastic future: *Vou gravar o episódio amanhã.*

There is no *de* after *tentar* or *decidir*, no *a* after *conseguir*, and no Spanish-style *a* between a conjugated *ir* and its future infinitive. Say the pair as one rhythm group: *tentou conter*, *conseguiu publicar*, *decidiram adiar*, *vai gravar*.

This does not mean Portuguese dislikes prepositions before infinitives. It means each governor makes its own choice. Compare:

- *começar a negociar*;
- *continuar a trabalhar* or, very commonly in Brazil, *continuar trabalhando*;
- *gostar de analisar*;
- *precisar rever o texto* (Brazilian Portuguese; compare *precisar de + noun*);
- *aprender a usar o sistema*;
- *parar de publicar*.

Do not create a universal rule from one familiar verb. Learn a small network of contrasts.

## When an object changes the frame

Some verbs can take a person and then an infinitive. The roles matter. In *O editor permitiu aos jornalistas publicar os dados*, the permission is given **to the journalists**, while *publicar* names the permitted act. In everyday Brazilian Portuguese, a reformulation such as *O editor deixou os jornalistas publicarem os dados* may sound more natural. The personal infinitive *publicarem* makes its subject explicit.

With obligation, your target frame is *ter que + infinitive*: *A plataforma tem que explicar a mudança*. Your attested **tem ser** omits the connector. In careful writing, *ter de + infinitive* also occurs, but *ter que* is an excellent stable default for the Brazilian variety you are learning.

## Preposition first, contraction second

Regência also governs noun complements. Once the verb or expression selects a preposition, Portuguese may fuse it with the following article:

- *participar de + o debate* → *participar do debate*;
- *pertencer a + a empresa* → *pertencer à empresa*;
- *depender de + a votação* → *depender da votação*;
- *chegar a + o aeroporto* → *chegar ao aeroporto*;
- *falar sobre + a crise* → *falar sobre a crise* (no fusion).

The contraction cannot repair a wrong preposition. First retrieve *pertencer a*; only then combine *a + a* into *à*. This two-stage logic also helps with places: Brazilian Portuguese normally says *na França*, *na Suíça* and *no Brasil*, because *em* combines with the country’s article. Modes of transport commonly take *de*: *viajar de carro*, *ir de trem*, *chegar de avião*.

> **You specifically do this:** *em França*, *no este*, *na uma*, *viajar em carro*, and *a le 5* appear in your data. The target forms are *na França*, *neste*, *numa*, *de carro*, and *às cinco*.

## Meaning contrasts worth keeping separate

*Por* and *para* are too broad for one English translation. For your error set, retain three anchors:

- purpose, destination, or beneficiary → *para*: *trabalhava para a prefeita*; *viajou para Brasília*;
- reason or exchange → *por*: *por duas razões*; *vendeu por cem reais*;
- rate or recurrence → *por*: *três aulas por semana*.

Similarly, memorize *ao mesmo tempo*, *de manhã*, *à tarde* and *às cinco* as time chunks. Their prepositions are not predicted safely from another Romance language.

## Build a personal regency ledger

For every correction, record four fields: the governor, its complement shape, one contrast language, and one sentence in your register.

| Governor | Portuguese frame | Interference trap | Your sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| *tentar* | *tentar Ø + inf.* | Italian *tentare di* | *Tentaram bloquear o acordo.* |
| *conseguir* | *conseguir Ø + inf.* | Italian *riuscire a* | *Consegui terminar o roteiro.* |
| *ir* (future) | *ir Ø + inf.* | Spanish *ir a* | *Vou publicar os dados.* |
| *pertencer* | *pertencer a* | guessed *de* | *A rede pertence ao grupo.* |

Review the frame, not the translation. The question is never “Which preposition feels Romance?” It is “What complete Portuguese chunk have I already stored?”
