# Gender propagates: articles, agreement, and numbers

Portuguese gender is not a label attached to a noun and then forgotten. It is a signal that travels through the noun phrase. Once you choose *o programa*, the same masculine choice normally appears in the determiner and adjective: *um programa novo*, *este programa europeu*, *os programas novos*. With a feminine noun, the chain changes together: *uma mensagem nova*, *esta mensagem urgente*, *as mensagens novas*.

That propagation is the useful way to think about gender. Do not try to memorize *programa = masculine* as an isolated fact. Memorize a short phrase that exposes the agreement: *o programa foi atualizado*; *a mensagem foi publicada*. The article makes the gender audible, and the participle or adjective gives you a second check.

> **You specifically do this:** you have repeatedly said *uma programa nova*, *as idiomas*, *um mensagem*, *este viagem*, *o lei*, and *na site*. These are not random slips. They cluster around a compact personal list and recur across years.

## The high-value exceptions

Endings are clues, not laws. Most nouns in *-o* are masculine and many in *-a* are feminine, but your most persistent errors sit precisely where that shortcut fails.

Several common nouns ending in *-ma* are masculine: *o problema, o programa, o sistema, o tema, o idioma, o clima*. Many came through Greek and do not follow the beginner’s “final *-a* means feminine” expectation. Use them in your own register:

- *O programa de monitoramento gerou um problema sério.*
- *O sistema eleitoral virou o tema principal do debate.*
- *O idioma do podcast depende do público.*

The reverse trap is the group in *-agem*, which is normally feminine: *a mensagem, a viagem, a reportagem, a sondagem, a abordagem*. So: *esta reportagem extensa*, not **este reportagem extenso**. One related word, *a ordem*, ends in *-em* but is also feminine. Your personal feminine set also includes *a lei, a voz, a equipe, a fonte* and *a ideia*.

Loanwords and technology terms need their own chunks. In Brazilian usage, learn *o site, o e-mail, o link, o aplicativo*. That gives *no site* (*em + o*), *do aplicativo* (*de + o*) and *pelo link* (*por + o*). The contraction is a visible consequence of the noun’s gender.

## Articles and contractions are one decision

Portuguese routinely combines a preposition with an article. First decide which preposition the meaning requires; then let the noun choose the article:

| Base | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| *em* | *no Brasil, no site* | *na França, na ordem* |
| *de* | *do programa* | *da mensagem* |
| *a* | *ao debate* | *à reunião* |
| *por* | *pelo sistema* | *pela imprensa* |

This is why **em França** and **na site** are different failures. In the first, Brazilian Portuguese normally requires the country’s article: *na França*. In the second, the article has the wrong gender: *no site*. Treat the complete forms as lexical frames, not as an abstract contraction exercise.

## Portuguese makes “two” agree

Like Spanish, Portuguese marks gender on one and on the hundreds from two hundred through nine hundred. Unlike invariant Spanish *dos*, however, Portuguese also distinguishes *dois/duas*. This is the cross-language contrast most likely to flatten under speaking pressure:

- *dois meses*, but *duas semanas*;
- *dois episódios*, but *duas entrevistas*;
- *duzentos artigos*, but *duzentas páginas*;
- *oitocentos eleitores*, but *oitocentas pessoas*.

> **You specifically do this:** *duas meses*, *dois telas*, *umas dias*, and *oitocentos pessoas* all appear in your correction history. The two-way errors show that you are not missing one noun’s gender; you are failing to carry known gender onto the numeral.

Make the number wait for the noun. Plan *meses* first, retrieve *o mês*, and only then say *dois meses*. For a long news sentence, build outward from the noun: *pessoas* → *as pessoas* → *oitocentas pessoas entrevistadas*.

## A compact checking routine

When a noun phrase matters, scan it in three passes:

1. **Noun:** which memorized chunk do you know—*o programa* or *a mensagem*?
2. **Left edge:** does the article, demonstrative, possessive, or numeral match—*este programa*, *a minha voz*, *duas viagens*?
3. **Right edge:** do adjectives and participles agree—*uma nova lei foi aprovada*; *os sistemas foram atualizados*?

Use contrast pairs to strengthen retrieval: *um programa / uma mensagem*; *dois temas / duas viagens*; *no site / na página*. The goal is not to recite a gender rule. It is to make one noun trigger an entire agreement package before Spanish or Italian supplies a competing ending.
