# Brazilian clitics: spoken placement and formal infinitive forms

Portuguese object pronouns are small, but they force several decisions at once: which object is being replaced, where the pronoun goes, and whether its shape changes beside an infinitive. Your corrections concentrate on a formal written pattern—*procurá-los, fazê-lo, tê-lo*—plus the fused stressed forms *comigo* and *conosco*.

Brazilian speech and formal writing do not always make the same placement choices. A practical reader must distinguish “what you will hear and naturally say” from “what edited prose may require,” without calling ordinary Brazilian usage wrong.

## First identify the object

Direct-object clitics agree in gender and number with the replaced noun:

- *o relatório* → *o*;
- *a reportagem* → *a*;
- *os arquivos* → *os*;
- *as entrevistas* → *as*.

In contemporary Brazilian conversation, a pronoun before the conjugated verb is a strong default: *Eu o vi* is formal; *Eu vi ele* is common in speech, while *Eu vi o relatório* avoids the issue. With two verbs, a clitic between them is extremely natural: *Vou te mandar o arquivo*; *Eles querem nos entrevistar*. This differs from Spanish placement and deserves its own rhythm.

## Infinitive plus *o/a*: drop the *r*, add *l*

In edited writing, when *o, a, os, as* attach after an infinitive, the final *-r* disappears and the pronoun gains *l-*:

- *procurar + os* → *procurá-los*;
- *colocar + as* → *colocá-las*;
- *fazer + o* → *fazê-lo*;
- *ter + o* → *tê-lo*;
- *integrar + os* → *integrá-los*.

The accent preserves the stressed vowel after the final consonant is removed. *-ar* gives *-á-lo*; *-er* gives *-ê-lo*. An *-ir* infinitive normally keeps stress naturally: *produzir + o → produzi-lo*, without an acute accent.

> **You specifically do this:** *procuraros, colocaros, fazero,* and *terlo* recur in your data. These spellings bolt the pronoun onto the untouched infinitive. The target process is morphological: remove *r*, adjust the stress mark, then attach *-lo/-la/-los/-las* with a hyphen.

In natural Brazilian speech, you may instead hear *para procurar eles* or a repeated noun, and in many contexts speakers omit an understood inanimate direct object. Your teacher’s *procurá-los* target remains valuable for formal production and careful narration. The goal is register control, not the claim that every Brazilian conversation uses enclisis.

## Indirect objects favor Brazilian proclisis

The indirect clitics most useful in your variety are *me, te, lhe, nos, lhes*. With a single verb, contemporary Brazilian Portuguese normally puts an unstressed pronoun before it: *Ela me disse a verdade*; *O editor nos enviou o contrato*. With a verb sequence, placement between the verbs is common: *Ela vai me dizer*; *Eles querem te mandar uma carta*; *A plataforma está nos cobrando uma taxa*.

This middle position is a useful contrast with Spanish, where a pronoun generally goes before the whole sequence or attaches to a nonfinite verb. Keep the Brazilian frame as a spoken chunk: *vai me dizer*, not a word-for-word transformation of *me va a decir*.

Formal European-influenced rules may prefer other positions in some written contexts, but this reader targets Brazilian Portuguese. Negative and other attracting words reinforce proclisis: *não me disseram*, *quando nos avisarem*, *ninguém o publicou*.

## *Comigo* and *conosco* are fused forms

After *com*, several stressed pronouns use special forms:

- *com + mim* → *comigo*;
- *com + ti* → *contigo*;
- *com + nós* → *conosco*;
- *com + vocês* → *com vocês*.

> **You specifically do this:** *trabalhar com nós* and *trabalham com eu* were corrected to *trabalhar conosco* and *trabalham comigo*. Do not produce **com mim** either. Store the complete forms as single lexical items.

Note that *com a gente* is also extremely common in Brazilian speech and takes third-person agreement elsewhere: *Eles querem trabalhar com a gente; a gente vai responder amanhã.* For careful prose, *conosco* is concise and unambiguous.

## Possessors stay before the noun

Your correction *o marido seu → o seu marido* belongs to the same broader problem: Portuguese clitic and determiner placement cannot simply copy Spanish or Italian. The neutral Brazilian pattern is article + possessive + noun: *o seu marido, a minha voz, os nossos arquivos*. A postposed possessive can occur for emphasis or in set expressions, but it is not the default you need.

## A register-aware checklist

Ask four questions:

1. Is the object direct or indirect?
2. Is this conversation or edited/formal prose?
3. Is a direct *o/a/os/as* attaching to an infinitive? If so, apply the *-lo* transformation.
4. Does *com* require *comigo, contigo,* or *conosco*?

Practice in paired registers: *Vou mandar os arquivos* → conversational *Vou mandar eles amanhã* / careful *Vou mandá-los amanhã*; *A editora vai me enviar o contrato* remains natural Brazilian placement. The best answer is not one universal position. It is the position that matches the object, construction, and register.
