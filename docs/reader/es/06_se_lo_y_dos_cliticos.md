# From *le lo* to *se lo*: package two object pronouns

Spanish can replace both the recipient and the thing transferred with short
unstressed pronouns. The order is indirect object first, direct object second.
But when indirect *le* or *les* would stand immediately before direct
*lo, la, los,* or *las*, Spanish changes *le/les* to *se*.

That gives the core formula:

**le/les + lo/la/los/las → se + lo/la/los/las**

> **You specifically do this:** in July 2022, your fourth year of recorded
> lessons, you said *quiero comprar le lo*. Your teacher supplied both
> *Quiero comprárselo* and *Se lo quiero comprar*, then worked through the
> direct- and indirect-object steps. It is only one verbatim error, but it
> revealed a whole late-stage paradigm gap rather than a spelling slip.

## Build the pair in two replacements

Start with a full sentence whose roles are visible:

*La productora envió el informe a la editora.*

The thing sent, *el informe*, is the direct object; masculine singular *lo*
can replace it. The recipient, *a la editora*, is the indirect object; *le*
can represent that role.

- recipient only: *La productora le envió el informe a la editora.*
- thing only: *La productora lo envió a la editora.*
- both roles: *La productora se lo envió a la editora.*

The last step is not **le lo**. As soon as *le* and *lo* meet, *le* becomes
*se*. This *se* still represents the recipient; it has not become reflexive
and it has not erased the person's identity. An optional clarifier can restore
it: *Se lo envió a la editora*; *Se las envié a las editoras*.

Your original *comprar* example follows the same route: *Quiero comprarle un
carro a mi hijo* can become *Quiero comprárselo a mi hijo*. The attached *le*
cannot simply remain beside *lo* as **comprarlelo** or **comprar le lo**.

The direct-object pronoun continues to show the gender and number of the
thing:

- *el informe* → *Se **lo** mandé a la periodista.*
- *la foto* → *Se **la** mandé a la periodista.*
- *los archivos* → *Se **los** mandé a la periodista.*
- *las fotos* → *Se **las** mandé a la periodista.*

Notice that *se* does not become plural for several recipients. *Se los mandé
a ellos* has singular-looking *se* and plural *los* because *los* agrees with
the files, not the people.

## Choose one of the two placement sites

With a single conjugated verb, the pronoun package goes before it and remains
written as separate words:

- *Se lo compré ayer.*
- *La plataforma se los envió a los suscriptores.*
- *No se la mostramos al ministro.*

A conjugated verb followed by an infinitive gives you two grammatical
positions. Put the entire pair before the conjugated verb, or attach the
entire pair to the infinitive:

- *Se lo quiero comprar.*
- *Quiero comprárselo.*

Do not split the package between sites: not **se quiero comprarlo** and not
**le lo quiero comprar**. When attached pronouns lengthen the infinitive, the
written accent preserves the original stress: *comprar* → *comprárselo*.

The same choice occurs with a progressive construction:

- *Se lo estoy enviando.*
- *Estoy enviándoselo.*

Again, attachment can require a written accent. With an affirmative command,
the pronouns attach—*Envíeselo*—while a negative command puts them before the
verb: *No se lo envíe*. The reusable skill is to move the pair as one unit.

## Recover meaning when *se* is ambiguous

Because recipient *se* no longer displays the gender or number expressed by
underlying *le/les*, a bare sentence such as *Se lo explicaron* may not identify
the recipient. Context normally does. When precision matters in a report,
retain or add the full phrase:

- *Se lo explicaron a la audiencia.*
- *Se lo explicaron a él, no a la directora.*
- *Se las enviamos a ustedes por correo.*

The clarifier does not license **le lo**; the replacement rule still applies.
Think of *a la audiencia* as an identifying label attached to indirect *se*.

## A four-question production routine

Before replacing both objects, ask:

1. What is transferred, shown, told, bought, or sent? Choose *lo/la/los/las*.
2. Who receives or benefits? That role would be *le/les*.
3. Are both pronouns present? Change *le/les* to *se* and keep indirect first.
4. Is there an infinitive or gerund? Move the whole pair before the conjugated
   verb or attach the whole pair, adding the required accent.

Apply it to a newsroom example: *La productora envió las transcripciones a
los suscriptores.* The thing is feminine plural, so *las*; the recipients
would be *les*, which becomes *se* before *las*: *La productora se las envió*.
With an infinitive: *Se las va a enviar* or *Va a enviárselas*.

Your correction is therefore not an arbitrary sound change to memorize. It is
a stable packaging rule: identify the roles, preserve indirect-before-direct
order, change the colliding *le/les*, and place the resulting *se lo* unit
together.
