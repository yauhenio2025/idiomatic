# Strong preterites: retrieve the stem before the ending

Most Spanish preterites let you start from an infinitive and attach a familiar
ending. A smaller, very frequent group changes the stem and uses its own set of
unstressed endings. These are traditionally called strong preterites. The
practical consequence is simple: with *poner* or *querer*, do not begin saying
the regular form and repair it halfway through. Retrieve *pus-* or *quis-* as
the first unit.

> **You specifically do this:** your corrections include *pune* → *puse* and
> *quisemos* → *quisimos*. You also produced *investieron* → *invirtieron* and
> *me fue* → *me fui*. These errors mix three related but distinct systems:
> strong stems, third-person stem changes, and the person forms of *ir*.

## The strong-stem package

For the Latin-American person set used here, attach this ending sequence to
the special stem: **-e, -iste, -o, -imos, -ieron**. (The *vosotros* form, when
needed, takes *-isteis*.) None of these endings carries a written accent.

| Infinitive | Strong stem | Selected forms |
|---|---|---|
| *poner* | *pus-* | *puse, puso, pusimos, pusieron* |
| *querer* | *quis-* | *quise, quiso, quisimos, quisieron* |
| *tener* | *tuv-* | *tuve, tuvo, tuvimos, tuvieron* |
| *poder* | *pud-* | *pude, pudo, pudimos, pudieron* |
| *venir* | *vin-* | *vine, vino, vinimos, vinieron* |
| *estar* | *estuv-* | *estuve, estuvo, estuvimos, estuvieron* |

This makes *quisimos* predictable once *quis-* is active: *quis-* + *-imos*.
The form **quisemos** is especially tempting because it is correct in
Portuguese. Tag it as a cross-language trap, then retrieve the Spanish stem
before adding the ending.

The same method prevents **pune**. Spanish does not take a present-tense form
and merely change its ending. The entire preterite family is built on *pus-*:
*El productor puso el enlace en la descripción*; *pusimos el episodio bajo
control*; *los medios pusieron el tema en portada*.

Two high-frequency families have an additional detail. *Hacer* uses *hic-* but
has *hizo* in the third-person singular. *Decir* and *traer* use *dij-* and
*traj-*; their third-person plural drops the *i* found in the usual ending:
*dijeron, trajeron*, not **dijieron, trajieron**.

## *Invertir* is a different pattern

*Invertir* is not a strong preterite. It belongs to the *-ir* verbs whose stem
vowel changes only in the third-person preterite forms. Its useful row is:

*invertí — invertiste — invirtió — invertimos — invirtieron*

The first and second persons keep *invert-*; the third persons change *e* to
*i*. There is no *ie* in this preterite. Thus: *El fondo invirtió en energía
solar* and *Los fondos invirtieron en la plataforma*, never **investieron**.
The inserted *s* in your error also reflects pressure from cognates such as
English *invest* and neighboring Romance verbs. Anchor the Spanish infinitive
and preterite together: *invertir → invirtieron*.

Other common *-ir* verbs follow the same third-person-only logic: *pedí,
pidió, pidieron*; *sentí, sintió, sintieron*; *dormí, durmió, durmieron*.
Calling this pattern “third-person change” keeps it separate from the strong
stem that appears across the whole preterite row.

## *Fui* and *fue*: person first, meaning from context

The verbs *ir* and *ser* share the preterite forms *fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos,
fueron*. Context tells you which verb is meant:

- *Fui a Francia para cubrir las elecciones.* (*ir*, first person)
- *La entrevista fue breve.* (*ser*, third person)
- *Los corresponsales fueron a la frontera.* (*ir*, third person plural)

With reflexive *irse*, the pronoun must agree with the person: *me fui, te
fuiste, se fue, nos fuimos, se fueron*. Your **me fue** combined a first-person
pronoun with a third-person verb. Retrieve *me fui a + place* as one frame:
*Me fui a Francia tres días antes de la votación.*

Do not let a form drill erase the tense contrast. The preterite presents a
bounded event: *No fui a la oficina ayer*. The imperfect presents a repeated,
ongoing, or background situation: *Antes no iba a la oficina los viernes*.
Your attested **yo no fue** needed both first-person morphology and, in its
original context, the imperfect *iba*. Ask first whether the scene is a
completed event or background pattern; then retrieve the correct person.

## A compact retrieval sequence

For a past-tense verb under speaking pressure:

1. Decide event versus background: preterite or imperfect.
2. If preterite, ask whether the verb has a strong stem.
3. If it is an *-ir* stem-changer, check whether the subject is third person.
4. Say the person explicitly in your head: *yo puse*, *nosotros quisimos*,
   *ellos invirtieron*, *yo me fui*.

Now one news sentence can rehearse all four targets: *Quisimos investigar por
qué el fondo invirtió allí, pero el director puso los documentos bajo control
y se fue a Berlín.* Each form comes from a different retrieval decision; none
needs to be guessed by resemblance to another language.
