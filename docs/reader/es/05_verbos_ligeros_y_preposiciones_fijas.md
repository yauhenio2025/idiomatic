# Choose the whole phrase: decisions, errors, photos, and control

A light verb such as *hacer, tomar,* or *cometer* contributes little concrete
meaning on its own. The noun carries the main idea, while Spanish convention
chooses the verb. That convention is why translating English “make” word by
word is unreliable: understandable **hacer una decisión** is not the
established neutral expression *tomar una decisión*.

Fixed prepositional phrases behave similarly. Knowing the individual meaning
of *control* does not tell you that the state “under control” is *bajo
control*. These are lexical frames: small pieces of grammar that must be
retrieved with their companions.

> **You specifically do this:** you said *hacer estas decisiones*, *hacer
> fotos*, and *hacer errores*. The corrected frames—*tomar decisiones, tomar
> fotos,* and *cometer errores*—were taught again across 2019–2022. More
> strikingly, you produced *sobre control* twice, 19 months apart, and *bajo
> control* was drilled again in 2024. That is your clearest fossilized fixed
> phrase.

## The three noun-and-verb frames

Use *tomar una decisión* when a person or institution decides:

- *El gobierno tomó una decisión después del debate.*
- *Los editores tendrán que tomar varias decisiones.*
- *La toma de decisiones fue poco transparente.*

The related noun phrase *la toma de decisiones* reinforces the same lexical
choice. In more specific contexts, other verbs are possible—*adoptar una
decisión* is formal, and *decidir* often makes the noun unnecessary—but
*tomar* is the safest neutral default. Do not let English “make a decision”
select **hacer**.

For an error, use *cometer*:

- *El algoritmo cometió un error de clasificación.*
- *Los medios cometieron varios errores al verificar las imágenes.*
- *Si cometo un error, publicaré una corrección.*

Other constructions express different perspectives: *hay un error en el
informe* locates it; *corregir un error* repairs it; *equivocarse* describes
the person making a mistake. But when the noun *error* is the direct object of
“make,” retrieve *cometer un error*.

For photography in Latin-American-neutral Spanish, use *tomar una foto*:

- *La corresponsal tomó fotos de la protesta.*
- *No se permitía tomar fotografías dentro del tribunal.*

There is a variety caveat. *Hacer fotos* is established usage in Spain; it is
not bad Spanish everywhere. *Tomar fotos* is the target in your
Colombian-teacher data and a strong neutral choice across Latin America. The
aim is variety control, not declaring another region's convention
ungrammatical.

## *Bajo control* is a stable unit

Spanish expresses the controlled state with *bajo*:

- *La policía afirmó que la situación estaba bajo control.*
- *Tenemos el calendario de producción bajo control.*
- *Los técnicos mantuvieron el incendio bajo control.*
- *La nueva política puso el gasto bajo control.*

Several verbs fit around the same core: *estar, tener, mantener,* and *poner*
something *bajo control*. The English idea “control over something” belongs to
a different construction: *tener control sobre los datos* or *ejercer control
sobre la plataforma*. This contrast explains why **sobre control** feels
tempting yet fails as the state expression.

Compare the structures directly:

- state: *El equipo tiene la crisis **bajo control***;
- domain of authority: *El equipo tiene control **sobre la publicación***.

Never choose the preposition from the isolated noun. Choose the intended
frame.

## *Al final*, *por fin*, and real *al fin*

Your teacher changed *al fin* to *al final* in a sentence meaning “in the
end”: *Al final va a costar mucho menos.* Use *al final* for the final result
of a process and *al final de* for the last part of a bounded period or object:

- *Al final, la empresa retiró la propuesta.*
- *La aclaración aparece al final del episodio.*

Use *por fin* when the speaker presents something as long awaited: *Por fin
publicaron los resultados.* Spanish *al fin* also exists, especially with an
“at last” flavor and in *al fin y al cabo* (“when all is said and done”). Your
recorded correction therefore identifies the meaning needed in that sentence;
it does not ban *al fin* from the language.

## Train retrieval, not translation

Make each target a reversible cue:

- decision → *tomar una decisión*;
- error → *cometer un error*;
- photo in your target variety → *tomar una foto*;
- controlled state → *tener algo bajo control*;
- eventual outcome → *al final*.

Then vary tense, number, and subject without breaking the middle of the chunk:
*tomamos decisiones, cometieron errores, había tomado varias fotos, seguirá
bajo control*. This preserves grammatical flexibility while making the lexical
choice automatic.

A final production check is enough: underline every generic English verb such
as “make,” “do,” “take,” or “have” in your planned script. Instead of
translating that verb, retrieve the Spanish noun's preferred partner. In a
single media sentence: *Al final, el equipo tomó la decisión de publicar las
fotos, reconoció que había cometido un error y afirmó que ya tenía la
situación bajo control.*
