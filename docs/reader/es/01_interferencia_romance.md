# Keep Spanish in the Spanish lane

Your largest Spanish pattern is not a missing tense or an obscure rule. It is
competition among languages you already know. Portuguese, Italian, French,
English, and Spanish offer related-looking words and paradigms; when Spanish
retrieval is a fraction of a second late, a neighboring language supplies a
plausible substitute. The result often *sounds* Romance, yet is not Spanish.

That distinction matters because a cognate-shaped invention can pass through
conversation unnoticed. A wrong irregular verb is conspicuous; *inserir* or
*similaridades* may feel so natural that you keep using it. For you, the best
defense is not another general cognate list. It is a compact quarantine list
built from forms you have actually produced.

> **You specifically do this:** cross-language interference accounts for
> **26 of the 62** verbatim error pairs in your 2019–2022 correction record.
> You said *inserir* instead of *insertar* twice, and *insertar* was taught or
> retaught 16 times from 2019 through 2024. This is a persistent retrieval
> competition, not a one-off typo.

## Three kinds of interference

The first kind is a complete import. Portuguese supplies *exemplo, longo,
chines, isolado, conosco* and *financeiras* where Spanish needs *ejemplo,
largo, chino, aislado, con nosotros* and *financieras*. Italian supplies
*pasaporto, Berlino, nostro* and *semplice* where Spanish needs *pasaporte,
Berlín, nuestro* and *simple*. These pairs are best learned as contrasts:

- *El ejemplo chino quedó aislado del conjunto de datos.*
- *Nuestro corresponsal viajó a Berlín con el pasaporte nuevo.*
- *Las autoridades financieras publicaron un informe largo.*

The second kind is paradigm transfer. The infinitive may already be correct,
but another language takes control during conjugation. Portuguese has
*quisemos*; Spanish has *quisimos*. Spanish *invertir* does not produce
**investieron**: the news report says *los fondos invirtieron en tecnología*.
This type of error belongs on both your interference list and your verb-form
list because it has two causes.

The third kind is derivational overconfidence. You recognize a Latinate base
and manufacture a noun or adjective with a productive-looking ending. Spanish
then asks for a different member of the word family:

| Tempting invention | Spanish retrieval target | Useful chunk |
|---|---|---|
| *dictación* | *dictado* | *el método del dictado* |
| *consumación* | *consumo* | *el consumo de energía* |
| *similaridades* | *similitudes* | *las similitudes entre los informes* |
| *profundido* | *profundo* | *un análisis profundo* |
| *mensales* | *mensuales* | *los pagos mensuales* |
| *extensiva* | *muy extensa* | *una investigación muy extensa* |

Spanish does have *consumación*, but in other senses, such as completion or
consummation. Likewise, *cualidad* is a real word for a property or positive
trait, while *calidad* is the target when discussing the quality of an audio
or product. The lesson is not “never trust cognates.” It is “retrieve the
whole phrase that matches the intended meaning.”

## Store islands, not isolated words

A single translation pair remains vulnerable: *insertar = to insert* can
still lose a race to Portuguese *inserir*. Store a short Spanish island with
syntax and context:

- *insertar un enlace en la descripción*;
- *insertar una tarjeta en el sistema*;
- *insertar una cita en la transcripción*.

The surrounding words make the Spanish form easier to recover. Do the same
with *investigar una denuncia* rather than the Italian-influenced
**resercar**, and *cancelar una reserva* or *anular una decisión* rather than
assuming that **restar** carries the meaning of another language's cognate.

Another high-yield tactic is a half-second language tag before a risky word:
“Spanish—*ejemplo*”; “Spanish—*con nosotros*.” Use it only for your quarantine
list, not for every cognate. The pause should disappear once the Spanish chunk
wins reliably.

## A production check that fits your register

Before publishing or recording, scan nouns, high-information verbs, and
country or nationality words. Ask three questions:

1. Have I heard this exact form in Spanish, or have I merely built it from a
   familiar Romance root?
2. Can I attach it to a Spanish phrase I know—*tomar una decisión*, *la
   calidad del audio*, *una periodista holandesa*?
3. Is the form on my personal quarantine list?

Consider: *El nostro equipo quiere inserir una análisis sobre las
similaridades.* Every item is interpretable, which is why the sentence is
dangerous. A Spanish-only rebuild is: *Nuestro equipo quiere insertar un
análisis sobre las similitudes.* Better still, choose a more idiomatic frame:
*Nuestro equipo quiere incluir en el episodio un análisis de las similitudes.*

Your multilingual knowledge is an asset: it gives you range and helps you
guess meaning while reading. The narrow goal is to stop a successful guess in
one language from masquerading as successful retrieval in another. Contrast,
chunk, and then reuse the corrected Spanish form in a sentence from your own
media or current-affairs domain.
