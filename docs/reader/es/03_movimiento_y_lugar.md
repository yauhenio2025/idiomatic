# Going *a* a destination, spending time *en* a place

Spanish keeps a useful contrast between movement toward a destination and
presence within a place. For the neutral patterns you need most often, a verb
of going, arriving, or returning selects *a*; a state or an activity located
somewhere selects *en*.

- *La corresponsal viajó **a** Colombia.*
- *Llegó **a** Bogotá el lunes.*
- *Regresará **a** Italia después de las elecciones.*
- *Pasó tres noches **en** Bogotá.*
- *La corresponsal está **en** Colombia.*

English often hides the distinction inside “to,” “in,” or no preposition, and
Portuguese can make *para* or *em* attractive. Build the Spanish preposition
as part of the verb-and-place frame.

## Destinations take *a* in the core pattern

Learn the following verbs with their destination slot attached:

- *ir a*: *El ministro fue a Bruselas.*
- *irse a*: *La editora se fue a Londres.*
- *viajar a*: *Viajaremos a Ucrania para cubrir la noticia.*
- *llegar a*: *Los observadores llegaron al centro electoral.*
- *regresar/volver a*: *El equipo regresó a México.*

When the following noun has the masculine article *el*, *a + el* contracts to
*al*: *llegar al aeropuerto, volver al estudio*. It does not contract before a
proper place name without an article: *llegar a Berlín*.

The preposition survives changes of tense and mood. Your correction *cuando
llegue a…* contains present subjunctive after future-oriented *cuando*, but
*llegue* still selects *a*: *Publicaré el episodio cuando llegue a casa.* The
grammar of the clause does not turn the destination into **llegar en**.

Keep person and reflexive form separate from the preposition. *Fui* is first
person; *fue* is third person. Both *fui a Londres* and *me fui a Londres* are
possible, but *irse* highlights departure or leaving: *me fui a Londres el
martes*. Your attested **me fue** mixed first-person *me* with third-person
*fue*. The complete retrieval frame is *me fui a + lugar*.

## Location and time spent take *en*

Once the sentence places a person or event inside a location rather than
directing motion toward it, *en* is the normal choice:

- *estar en*: *La periodista está en París.*
- *vivir en*: *Los expertos viven en la región fronteriza.*
- *quedarse en*: *Nos quedamos en el hotel.*
- *pasar tiempo en*: *Pasé una noche en París.*
- event + *en*: *La cumbre se celebró en Chile.*

The same place can therefore appear with either preposition as the scene
changes: *Llegamos a Bélgica el jueves y pasamos tres días en Bélgica.* The
first clause traces an endpoint; the second locates a duration.

Time expressions do not replace the destination marker. Say *En tres días voy
a Francia*: initial *en* means “in three days,” while *a* introduces France.
Keeping both phrases visible prevents the compressed error **me voy en
Francia**.

## What about *para*?

Do not turn the personalized correction into the false rule “Spanish never
uses *para* with movement.” Spanish does allow destination-oriented
expressions such as *salir para Bogotá*, *un vuelo para Lima*, and, in some
regional usage, *irse para casa*. *Para* can present orientation or intended
destination, and its distribution varies with the construction and region.

Your safer Latin-American-neutral default with the verbs in this chapter is
still *a*: *viajar a, ir a, llegar a, regresar a*. That is the target your
Colombian teacher consistently recorded, and it works across the formal news,
travel, and production contexts you use. Learn exceptional *para* patterns as
complete expressions later; do not use them to improvise **viajar para** when
you mean the ordinary destination construction.

One more contrast adds the route: *viajar a Colombia* names the destination,
while *viajar por Colombia* can describe movement through or around the
country. A concise planning question now resolves most cases:

1. Endpoint of going, arrival, or return? *a*.
2. Position, event, or time spent there? *en*.
3. Route through a region? often *por*.

Use the contrast in one connected report: *El equipo salió para el aeropuerto,
viajó a Chile, llegó a Santiago por la mañana y pasó dos semanas en la
capital.* Here even legitimate *para* belongs to *salir para*; the core
destination and location frames remain stable.
