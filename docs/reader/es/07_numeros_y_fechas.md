# Hundreds and dates: say the structure you can see

Long Spanish numbers are assembled in blocks, but a few hundreds have their
own forms. Dates then add conventions that English and other Romance languages
do not divide in exactly the same way. Your goal is not speed arithmetic in
Spanish. It is reliable retrieval of the high-risk block before you connect
it to the rest.

> **You specifically do this:** you once produced *cinco cientos* for
> *quinientos* and later recorded the misspelling *quinnientos*. Hundreds kept
> returning in lessons: *quinientos mil dólares, doscientas entrevistas,
> setecientas páginas,* and *mil novecientos sesenta y dos*. Dates such as *el
> primero de mayo/junio, el tres de octubre,* and *hasta mediados de octubre*
> were also retaught through 2024.

## The irregular hundreds are single words

The sequence begins *cien, doscientos, trescientos, cuatrocientos*, but three
forms must be retrieved rather than assembled mechanically:

- 500: *quinientos*, not **cinco cientos** or **quinnientos**;
- 700: *setecientos*, not **sietecientos**;
- 900: *novecientos*, not **nuevecientos**.

From 200 through 900, the hundreds agree with a following feminine noun:
*doscientas entrevistas, quinientas páginas, setecientas personas*. The ending
does not tell you the gender of the numeral in isolation; it agrees with the
noun being counted. Thus *quinientos episodios* but *quinientas grabaciones*.

Use *cien* for exactly one hundred and directly before a noun or the larger
units *mil* and *millones*: *cien páginas, cien mil votos, cien millones de
usuarios*. Use *ciento* when more digits follow within the same hundred:
*ciento uno, ciento treinta,
ciento noventa y nueve*. In a counted masculine noun phrase, final *uno*
shortens as usual: *ciento un episodios*.

Spanish inserts *y* between tens and units, not after the hundreds:

- 532: *quinientos treinta y dos*;
- 748: *setecientos cuarenta y ocho*;
- 905: *novecientos cinco*.

So **quinientos y treinta y dos** is not the ordinary cardinal structure.
Build the number aloud as [hundreds] + [tens and units].

## *Mil* and *millón* behave differently

For 1,000, say *mil*, normally not **un mil**. The numeral does not take a
plural ending in exact figures: *dos mil personas, quinientos mil dólares*.
The noun *miles* exists when you mean an approximate quantity—*miles de
personas*—but it is a different construction.

*Millón*, by contrast, is a noun: *un millón, dos millones*. Before the thing
counted, use *de*: *un millón de dólares, dos millones de usuarios*. This
produces a useful structural contrast:

- 500,000 dollars: *quinientos mil dólares*;
- 1,000,000 dollars: *un millón de dólares*.

For years, read the full cardinal without inserting *de*: 1962 is *mil
novecientos sesenta y dos*; 2024 is *dos mil veinticuatro*. In prose, *en 2024*
means “in 2024,” while *desde 2024* marks the starting point.

## Dates use *de* and lower-case months

In a full spoken or written date, put the day before the month and connect the
parts with *de*: *el tres de octubre de 2024*. Month names are lower-case in
ordinary Spanish prose: *mayo, junio, octubre*.

For the first day of a month, *el primero de mayo* is the conventional target
in much of Latin America and the form repeatedly used in your Colombian
teacher data. *El uno de mayo* also occurs across the Spanish-speaking world,
with regional preferences. For the remaining days, use cardinals: *el dos de
mayo, el tres de octubre*, not ordinals such as **el tercero de octubre** for
an ordinary calendar date.

Include the article when the date functions in a sentence: *La ley entrará en
vigor el primero de junio*. A heading or document field may omit it. With a
deadline extending to the middle of a month, the reusable expression is
*hasta mediados de octubre*. For an event located around that period, use *a
mediados de octubre*: *La campaña comenzó a mediados de octubre*.

All-numeric dates create international ambiguity: 03/10 can be read as 3
October or March 10 depending on convention. In scripts, captions, and
international reporting, spelling out the month—*3 de octubre de 2026*—is
often the clearest solution. Punctuation and digit grouping conventions vary
by editorial standard, so do not let a formatting rule replace the stable
spoken structure.

## A block-by-block reading routine

For a large number, locate the special block first. If the figure begins 500,
say *quinientos* before doing anything else. Then check noun gender, attach
*mil* or *millón de* as required, and only then say the noun:

500,000 interviews → *quinientas mil entrevistas*.

For a date, name the four slots: article + day + *de* + lower-case month,
optionally + *de* + year. That yields *el primero de junio de dos mil
veintiséis* and *el tres de octubre de dos mil veinticuatro*.

One sentence can combine your targets: *El primero de mayo, la plataforma
anunció que había alcanzado quinientos mil suscriptores; a mediados de octubre
ya tenía setecientas treinta y dos entrevistas publicadas.* The arithmetic is
secondary. The win is that every block—*primero de mayo, quinientos mil,
setecientas treinta y dos*—has been retrieved in its Spanish shape.
