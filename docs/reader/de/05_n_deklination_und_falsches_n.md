# N-Deklination und das falsche *-n*

The ending *-n* does several different jobs in German. Confusing them creates a revealing pair of errors: omitting *-n* where a weak masculine noun requires it, then adding *-n* to an ordinary singular because the phrase “feels oblique.” The cure is to identify the noun class and number before touching the ending.

## The small weak-masculine class

Most nouns do not change in the accusative or dative singular. A limited group of masculine nouns—the **N-Deklination**—does. Its nominative singular is the dictionary form; accusative, dative, and genitive singular add *-n* or *-en*:

| case | *Junge* | *Journalist* |
|---|---|---|
| nominative | der Junge | der Journalist |
| accusative | den Jungen | den Journalisten |
| dative | dem Jungen | dem Journalisten |
| genitive | des Jungen | des Journalisten |

The plural normally has that same *-(e)n* shape: *die Jungen, die Journalisten*. The article therefore carries crucial information.

Useful clusters include:

- masculine people and animals ending in *-e*: *der Junge, Kollege, Kunde, Zeuge, Löwe*;
- many international person nouns in *-ant, -ent, -ist, -oge* and related suffixes: *der Demonstrant, Präsident, Journalist, Soziologe*;
- several frequent items to learn individually: *der Mensch, Herr, Held, Prinz, Bär*.

The exact inventory has exceptions, so store a weak noun with a diagnostic pair: **der Journalist — mit dem Journalisten**. Do not infer that every masculine noun is weak.

> **You specifically do this — Du machst genau das:** *Es geht um einen Junge, der sehr innovativ ist* needs **einen Jungen**. *Um* selects accusative, and *der Junge* belongs to the N-declension. The relative pronoun *der* is nominative because the boy is the subject of *ist*; one referent can appear in different cases in the same sentence.

## Ordinary masculine nouns stay ordinary

*Der Spielfilm* is not an N-noun. Its singular is *der Spielfilm, den Spielfilm, dem Spielfilm, des Spielfilms*. Compare:

> Der Regisseur dreht **einen zweistündigen Spielfilm**.  
> Der Journalist interviewt **einen jungen Präsidenten**.

Both phrases are accusative masculine. The adjective takes *-en* in both; only the lexical class decides whether the noun itself changes.

> **You specifically do this — Du machst genau das:** *einen 2 Stunden langen Spielfilmen* was retained with a spurious noun ending. The target is **einen zwei Stunden langen Spielfilm**. The article and adjective mark accusative masculine; the strong noun remains singular and bare.

That contrast is worth saying aloud: *einen langen Film* but *einen jungen Journalisten*.

## Dative plural is a different rule

All plural nouns—not just N-nouns—normally add *-n* in the dative if the plural does not already end in *-n* or *-s*:

> die Filme → mit den Filmen  
> die Honorare → mit den Honoraren  
> die Journalisten → mit den Journalisten  
> die Teams → mit den Teams

This *-n* depends on **dative plural**, not on masculinity. Check both conditions: is the noun plural, and is the phrase dative? If either answer is no, this rule cannot justify an ending.

> **You specifically do this — Du machst genau das:** Your corpus contains both sides of the number problem: **höhere Honorare**, not *höhere Honorar*, because *Honorar* has plural *Honorare*; but **einen Spielfilm**, not *Spielfilmen*, because that phrase is singular. Number must be selected lexically before case morphology is added.

## Measures do not become dative plurals

After a precise cardinal number, many currency and measure names appear in an uninflected counting form:

> 50 Schilling pro Watt · 100 Euro Vorschuss · drei Kilo Papier

They are not dative just because a number precedes them, and an English plural does not force a German plural ending.

> **You specifically do this — Du machst genau das:** *50 Schillingen pro Watt* hypercorrects an *-n*. In the quantified measure expression, use **50 Schilling pro Watt**. By contrast, a true lexical plural in a different meaning can inflect normally; the construction decides.

## The mixed exceptions

A few nouns resemble weak nouns but add *-s* in the genitive. Learn the most useful as a separate mini-class:

> der Name — den/dem Namen — des Namens  
> der Gedanke — den/dem Gedanken — des Gedankens  
> der Glaube — den/dem Glauben — des Glaubens  
> der Wille — den/dem Willen — des Willens

*Das Herz* is the famous neuter special case: *das Herz, das Herz, dem Herzen, des Herzens*. Calling it simply another masculine N-noun would hide both its gender and its mixed forms.

## A three-question filter

Whenever you feel an urge to append *-n*, ask:

1. Is this a lexically weak or mixed noun in a non-nominative singular form?
2. Is this an actual dative plural whose plural lacks final *-n* or *-s*?
3. Is this instead an ordinary singular or a bare quantified measure?

In political and publishing prose, the contrasts recur constantly: *mit dem Präsidenten*, *gegen den Journalisten*, but *für den Verlag*, *über den Algorithmus*, *einen Spielfilm drehen*, *mit mehreren Spielfilmen werben*. The noun’s class and number come first; case supplies only the ending that class permits.
