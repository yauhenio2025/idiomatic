# Adjektivendungen als System: Wer trägt die Information?

German adjective endings look like a wall of sixteen cells only if you start with the wall. A better question is: **which word in this noun phrase tells the listener its gender, number, and case?** The article and adjective divide that signaling job between them.

First separate two uses of an adjective. A predicate adjective comes after verbs such as *sein, werden* or *bleiben* and stays bare:

> Das Programm ist **neu**. Die Strategie bleibt **kohärent**.

An attributive adjective stands before its noun and must take an ending:

> das **neue** Programm; eine **kohärente** Strategie

That first decision prevents a great deal of wasted computation. If the adjective is not attached to a following noun, do not decline it.

## Three distributions of the work

After a **der-word**—*der/die/das*, *dieser, jeder, jener, welcher* and similar determiners—the determiner already exposes most of the grammar. The adjective therefore uses the weak pattern. There are only five **-e** slots: nominative singular in all three genders, plus accusative feminine and neuter. Everywhere else the ending is **-en**.

> der kritisch**e** Journalist; die digital**e** Plattform; das neu**e** Buch  
> den kritisch**en** Journalisten; mit der digital**en** Plattform; die neu**en** Bücher

After an **ein-word**—*ein, kein, mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer*—the adjective sometimes has to reveal information that the determiner hides. The high-value pattern is small:

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | ein neu**er** | eine neu**e** | ein neu**es** | meine neu**en** |
| accusative | einen neu**en** | eine neu**e** | ein neu**es** | meine neu**en** |
| dative/genitive | **-en** | **-en** | **-en** | **-en** |

With **no article**, the adjective carries nearly the whole signal. In the nominative and accusative, its endings mostly resemble the distinctive forms of *der/die/das*: *kritischer Journalismus, digitale Politik, neues Denken, weitere Folgen*; accusative masculine alone has *-en*: *kritischen Journalismus*. Dative gives *-em/-er/-em/-en*: *mit neuem Geld, mit digitaler Technik, mit neuem Personal, mit internationalen Partnern*.

The point is not that the endings are interchangeable with articles. It is that German tries to make the grammatical features audible somewhere near the noun. Ask where that information already appears.

## A five-second diagnostic

When an ending stalls you, run this order:

1. Find the **head noun**, not the nearest attractive word.
2. Recall its gender, or identify that it is plural.
3. Determine its case from the clause: subject, object, verb government, or preposition.
4. Classify what comes before the adjective: der-word, ein-word, or no article.
5. Supply the ending, then read the entire noun phrase once.

Try it with *Die Redaktion veröffentlicht zwölf weiter__ Kapitel.* The head is plural *Kapitel*. It is the accusative object, but plural nominative and accusative have the same relevant form here. A bare numeral does not carry a declension ending, so the adjective must: *zwölf weiter**e** Kapitel*.

Now take *Sie haben kein kohärent__ Weltbild.* *Weltbild* is neuter and accusative. *Kein* is an ein-word and exposes no neuter ending here. The adjective supplies it: *kein kohärent**es** Weltbild*.

> **You specifically do this — Du machst genau das:** Your retained productions include *der härtester Konkurrent*, *tiefgründiger Schlüsse*, *keinen kohärentes Weltbild*, *Meine ultimative Ziel* and *zwölf weiterer*. These are not five unrelated mistakes. In each one, the phrase was assembled before gender, case, and determiner type had been resolved. Rebuild them as units: **der härteste Konkurrent**, **tiefgründige Schlüsse**, **kein kohärentes Weltbild**, **mein ultimatives Ziel**, **zwölf weitere**.

## The endings in your working register

Politics and publishing create long noun phrases, but length changes nothing:

> Die Regierung kündigte **ein neues digitales Transparenzgesetz** an.  
> Der Verlag sucht **einen erfahrenen politischen Lektor**.  
> Trotz **heftiger öffentlicher Kritik** wurde das Manuskript veröffentlicht.  
> Mit **neuen generativen Modellen** lassen sich Stimmen imitieren.

Do not assign one ending to the whole string. Each attributive adjective receives the ending required by the same noun: *ein neu**es** digital**es** Transparenzgesetz*; *einen erfahren**en** politisch**en** Lektor*. Coordinated adjectives behave likewise: *ein neues, umstrittenes Gesetz*.

Participles used attributively are adjectives too: *die veröffentlichte Studie, ein trainiertes Modell, die von der Opposition vorgeschlagene Reform*. The internal phrase may be long; the ending still sits at the right edge of the adjectival unit, immediately before the noun.

One separate trap is word formation, not declension. *Größtmöglich* already means “as large as possible”; adding another *-st* creates a double superlative. Say *ein größtmöglich**es** Publikum*, not *ein größtmöglichstes Publikum*.

## A final compression

Keep three sentences in memory rather than three full tables:

- After a der-word, use **-e in the five nominative/accusative singular slots; otherwise -en**.
- After an ein-word, the adjective fills the visible gaps: **-er, -e, -es**; accusative masculine and the oblique cases use **-en**.
- With no declined determiner, the adjective carries the stronger case/gender signal.

Then always verify the noun’s lexical gender. An ending system cannot rescue the wrong entry for *das Ziel* or *das Programm*.
