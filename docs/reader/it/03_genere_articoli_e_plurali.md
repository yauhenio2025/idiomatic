# Gender and plural: make the article carry the noun

Italian noun endings are highly informative, but your self-report identifies articles, gender, and especially plural endings as a weak area. The best response is to learn a noun with its article and its plural partner: *il problema / i problemi*, *la mano / le mani*, *l’uovo / le uova*. A bare noun hides precisely the information you need to retrieve.

Agreement spreads. Once *la città* is feminine singular, a following adjective must match: *la città intelligente*. In the plural: *le città intelligenti*. The noun *città* itself does not change, so the article and adjective do all the visible work.

> **You specifically do this:** the one attested agreement error is *un rapporto sulle città intellegente* for *un rapporto sulle città intelligenti*. The noun is plural even though *città* has the same written form in both numbers; the adjective must reveal *-i*.

## The productive core

Most nouns fit three useful patterns:

| Singular | Plural | Example |
|---|---|---|
| masculine *-o* | *-i* | *il libro / i libri* |
| feminine *-a* | *-e* | *la notizia / le notizie* |
| masculine or feminine *-e* | *-i* | *il giornale / i giornali*; *la legge / le leggi* |

The *-e* class does not reveal gender. Learn the article: *il paese* but *la fonte*; both form a plural in *-i*: *i paesi, le fonti*. In topical language, store *il dibattito/i dibattiti, la legge/le leggi, la crisi/le crisi, il giornale/i giornali*.

## Your Romance exception set

Several masculine learned nouns end in *-a*: *il problema, il programma, il sistema, il tema, il clima*. Their plural is *-i*: *i problemi, i programmi, i sistemi, i temi, i climi*. This happens in Portuguese too, which can help—provided French or an automatic final-*a* heuristic does not override it.

The reverse exception *la mano* is feminine and becomes *le mani*. Store both ends of the contrast: *il sistema italiano / la mano italiana*.

Nouns ending in an accented vowel are normally invariant: *la città/le città, l’università/le università, il caffè/i caffè*. Many recent loanwords ending in a consonant are also invariant: *il podcast/i podcast, il software/i software, il film/i film*. The article and agreement carry the number.

## Articles expose sound as well as gender

Definite singular articles divide the masculine class by sound:

- *il* before most consonants: *il governo, il podcast*;
- *lo* before *s + consonant*, *z, ps, gn, x,* and similar clusters: *lo Stato, lo scrittore, lo zaino*;
- *l’* before a vowel: *l’articolo, l’episodio*.

Their plurals are *i* for *il* and *gli* for *lo/l’*: *i governi, i podcast, gli Stati, gli scrittori, gli articoli*. Feminine *la* becomes *le*, while *l’* before a feminine vowel also becomes *le*: *l’agenzia/le agenzie*.

The indefinite forms parallel this distinction: *un governo, uno Stato, un articolo*; feminine *una legge, un’agenzia*. The apostrophe in *un’agenzia* marks elision of feminine *una*. Masculine *un articolo* has no apostrophe.

## Special plurals that change gender

A small but common inherited group has masculine singular *-o* and feminine plural *-a*:

- *l’uovo / le uova*;
- *il braccio / le braccia*;
- *il dito / le dita* (for the body-part collective sense);
- *il paio / le paia*.

Do not generalize this pattern to ordinary nouns. It is a lexical family. Agreement follows the plural gender: *le uova fresche, le braccia aperte*.

Some nouns allow two plurals with different meanings, but those are a later reference problem. Your high-value goal is immediate retrieval of the common set.

## Spelling-sensitive plurals

Nouns in *-ca/-ga* normally keep a hard sound with *h*: *la banca/le banche, la collega/le colleghe*. Words in *-cia/-gia* may retain or drop *i* depending on spelling and stress: *la camicia/le camicie* but *la provincia/le province*. These rules are real, yet they should not crowd out agreement. If the plural is uncertain, consult a dictionary and then store the entire pair.

## Build outward from a noun pair

For production, retrieve the plural noun phrase before the clause:

*la città → le città → le città intelligenti → le città intelligenti hanno approvato il piano.*

Do the same for an invariant loanword:

*il podcast → i podcast → i nuovi podcast → i nuovi podcast sono disponibili.*

This makes article and adjective mutually corrective. If you hear **le città intelligente**, the article has already warned you that the adjective cannot remain singular.
