# *Ci, ne,* and verbs that become a new unit

Italian *ci* and *ne* can replace complements, but in many frequent verbs they do more than save repetition. They fuse with a verb—and sometimes with another pronoun—to create a conventional meaning: *farcela, andarsene, prendersela, sentirsela, cavarsela*. These are often called *verbi procomplementari*. Their meaning cannot always be calculated word by word.

## Referential *ci* and *ne*

Start with their transparent uses. *Ci* can replace *a/in + place* or *a + thing* with certain verbs:

- *Vai a Roma? Sì, ci vado domani.*
- *Credi nel progetto? Sì, ci credo.*
- *Pensi alla proposta? Ci penso spesso.*

*Ne* commonly replaces *di + noun*, a source, or a quantity-linked noun:

- *Parliamo della crisi? Ne parliamo dopo.*
- *Che pensi del piano? Che ne pensi?*
- *Quanti episodi hai registrato? Ne ho registrati tre.*

The pronoun normally precedes a finite verb: *ci vado, ne parliamo*. It attaches to an infinitive after the final *-e* drops: *andarci, parlarne*. With another clitic, the order and shapes change: *me ne, te ne, se ne, ce ne, ve ne*.

## Lexicalized meanings

Now separate the common procomplementary verbs into meaning families.

### Success and coping

*Farcela* means manage or succeed: *Ce la faccio*; *non ce l’abbiamo fatta a finire il montaggio*. *Cavarsela* means get by, cope, or extricate oneself: *Se l’è cavata bene durante l’intervista*. The pronouns are not ordinary objects you should translate independently. Retrieve the whole infinitive and its mini-paradigm.

### Feeling willing or able

*Sentirsela di + infinitive* means feel up to doing something: *Non me la sento di pubblicare senza verificare i dati*. The reflexive pronoun changes with the subject, while *la* remains: *te la senti, se la sente, ce la sentiamo*.

### Taking offence or reacting personally

*Prendersela con qualcuno* means take it out on or get angry with someone: *Se l’è presa con il produttore*. The compound tense uses *essere* because the verb is reflexive, and the participle agrees with the fixed feminine *la*: *me la sono presa*, *se la sono presa*. This agreement is easy to miss because *la* has no concrete noun antecedent.

### Leaving and not caring

*Andarsene* means go away: *Me ne vado; se ne sono andati*. Here the finite compound takes *essere*, and the participle agrees with the subject: *Maria se n’è andata; i giornalisti se ne sono andati*. *Fregarsene* means not care, often informal or blunt: *Se ne frega delle critiche*. Use it with register awareness.

## The shape changes before the verb

When *mi, ti, si, ci,* or *vi* stands before another clitic, its vowel changes:

- *mi + la* → *me la*;
- *ti + ne* → *te ne*;
- *si + la* → *se la*;
- *ci + la* → *ce la*;
- *vi + ne* → *ve ne*.

Thus *farcela* becomes *ce la faccio*, not **ci la faccio**. *Andarsene* becomes *me ne vado*, not **mi ne vado**. In the infinitive, the pieces join the verb: *farcela, sentirsela, andarsene*. In a finite declarative clause, they normally stand before it: *non me la sento*.

## Compound tenses: two agreement logics

This area combines clitics with the past participle, so identify the auxiliary first.

- Reflexive procomplementary verbs use *essere*: *me ne sono andato/a; se la sono presa; ce la siamo cavata*.
- *Farcela* instead uses *avere*: *ce l’ho fatta; ce l’abbiamo fatta*. The fixed preceding *la* is reflected in *fatta*. Memorize this lexical pattern as a whole rather than transferring the auxiliary from *cavarsela*.

That last contrast is exactly why a general pronoun rule is not enough. Say the anchor forms aloud: *ce l’ho fatta; me ne sono andato; me la sono presa; me la sono cavata*. Then expand the subject and tense.

## Learn each verb as a four-part card

For every procomplementary verb, store:

1. infinitive: *sentirsela*;
2. plain meaning: “feel up to it”;
3. one present form: *non me la sento*;
4. one compound form: *non me la sono sentita*.

Use your register: *Il ministro non se l’è sentita di rispondere*; *la redazione ce l’ha fatta a pubblicare in tempo*; *gli investitori se la sono presa con il governo*. The pronoun cluster should become one audible launch sequence, not a puzzle solved midway through the sentence.
